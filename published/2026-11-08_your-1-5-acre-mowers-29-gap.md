Your mower's acreage badge hides a 29% gap in what two same-price machines cut per charge: one is rated for 1.5 acres, the other for 0.75, and the smaller one cuts more grass between charges. At $2,799.00, the Segway Navimow X350 is sold as a 1.5-acre machine [1]. The Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD 3000 — LUBA (the maker's mower line), an all-wheel-drive (AWD) unit — is sold as a 0.75-acre machine, yet it cuts 0.42 acre per charge against the Navimow's 0.30 [2]. Buy the Navimow for a fenced acre with open sky; it is the only one of the two rated for that much lawn, it runs at 60 A-weighted decibels (dB(A)), and it recharges in 80 minutes [1]. The Mammotion LUBA wins below three-quarters of an acre, or on a bank past 27 degrees, where its 38.6-degree drive and its light detection and ranging (LiDAR) sensor are the point [2].

## At a glance

- **The shift:** Two mowers at one price; the one rated for twice the lawn covers less per charge.
- **The number:** 0.30 acre a charge for the Navimow, 0.42 for the Mammotion.
- **The risk:** Neither runs antenna-free; the Mammotion's correction service is a paid line in year four.

## The badge is a work rate

The acreage number on either box is a daily work rate, not the amount of grass the machine cuts per battery charge. Navimow's own lab note attaches its run time to a smaller area, in a footnote below the headline [1]. Mammotion states its per-charge cut in a battery section further down its page [2]. I've been watching how easily those two numbers swap places in a reader's head.

| What to compare | Segway Navimow X350 [1] | Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD 3000 [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Price (2026-11-08) | $2,799.00 | $2,799.00 |
| Rated area | 1.5 acres | 0.75 acre |
| Cut per charge | 0.30 acre (maker's lab note) | 0.42 acre (maker's claim) |
| Slope ceiling | 50% (27°) | 80% (38.6°) |
| Edge slope limit | 25% | not stated |
| Noise | 60 dB(A) | ≤70 dB |

The table does not show the real story until you compare charge time. The duty-cycle math below shows how the larger badge hides the smaller tank.

| Duty-cycle math | Segway Navimow X350 [1] | Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD 3000 [2] |
|---|---|---|
| Run time per charge | 200 minutes | 215 minutes |
| Full charge time | 80 minutes | not stated |
| Cycles for rated pass | 5 | 1.8 |
| Full-pass time | about 23 hours (desk arithmetic) | not stated |
| Area per cutting hour | roughly 3,920 sq ft | roughly 5,105 sq ft |
| Price per rated acre | $1,866 | $3,732 |

Slope and navigation cut the two machines apart more than run time does. A bank down to the road points to the Mammotion LUBA for that reason alone. Both machines lean on a correction service rather than on open sky alone. The Navimow ships a real-time kinematic (RTK) antenna and a pole, and sends buyers to a network-coverage check [1]. The Mammotion LUBA pairs its LiDAR with a network RTK service over a built-in cellular module, free for three years [2].

The part I keep circling is how easily the two coverage numbers swap places in a reader's head. A published review reads the X350's 200 minutes as covering "appx 1.5 acres" [3]. The maker's own note attaches the same 200 minutes to 0.3 acre [1]. A second outside review lists the same 10-amp-hour pack and the same 200 minutes without reading them as a per-charge area [4]. Two sentences, one machine, and only one of them is about a single charge.

## By the numbers

- **5 charge cycles:** one full pass of the Navimow's rated area takes [1].
- **0.42 acre per charge:** the Mammotion LUBA's cut against its 0.75-acre rating, or 1.8 cycles a pass [2].
- **1.1 to 1.8 seasons:** the payback against a 26-visit season at $60 to $100 a visit [7].

## The hidden costs off the sticker

What I find hard to square is that neither antenna shows up on the sticker. Neither machine is free of one, whatever the marketing says. That antenna means a mast and a cable run in the yard. The network mode behind it depends on coverage at your address. On the Mammotion LUBA, the built-in cellular module receives the correction data, and the product page links a renewal page that prints no price [2]. That renewal is a real cost the sticker does not show, and this desk will not put a number on it.

Running parts are just as uneven. Navimow prices a replacement 10-amp-hour (10Ah) battery at $399.99 and an anti-clogging cutting disk at $59.99 for the X3 line [1]. Mammotion's LUBA page prints no battery or blade price at all, so its upkeep is unpublished rather than low [2].

Against the contract math in the box above, the mower is not cheaper in year one. One acre mowed weekly runs $60 to $100 a visit, so a 26-week season costs $1,560 to $2,600 [7]. At those rates the machine pays for itself in 1.1 to 1.8 seasons, and what it buys before then is hours and noise rather than money.

| Your situation | The pick |
|---|---|
| A fenced acre, open sky | Segway Navimow X350: the acre rating, 60 dB(A), an 80-minute recharge |
| 0.75 acre or less, or a bank past 27 degrees | Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD 3000: the 38.6-degree drive is the deciding figure |
| Heavy tree cover over the whole lawn | Mammotion LUBA, whose LiDAR does not wait on a satellite fix |
| Three-quarters of an acre, price first | A Kress dealer quote on the KR173, below |

That last row needs its own paragraph, because it holds the cheapest number in this piece and the least reliable one. Kress lists the KR173, a ¾-acre machine with obstacle avoidance, on two of its own pages [5]. The second page is the dealer promotion [6]. The table below shows why the quote matters.

| Kress KR173 page | Price shown | Reference price | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kress mower page | $1,499.99 | $2,999.99 | not stated |
| Dealer promotion page | $1,999.99 | $3,699.98 | dealer only, through 31 December 2026 while supplies last |

Two of the maker's own pages sit $500 apart, and neither carries a cart. The quote, not the page, is the price.

## What we did not test

My read stops where the hardware does. This review is built from the two makers' product, specification and accessory pages, one publisher's cost table and two outside reviews. The desk handled neither machine, installed neither dock and measured nothing. Every runtime, coverage and slope figure above is the maker's own claim, or a division of one. The all-day pass estimate is desk arithmetic on Navimow's lab note rather than a field result. Mammotion prints no full-charge time, so no dock-to-dock figure is given for it, and the contract math rests on a season length the publisher does not state.

On the data I could check, the Navimow is the pick for a full acre with open sky. The Mammotion LUBA is the pick for three-quarters of an acre or less, a bank past 27 degrees, or heavy tree cover, where [the canopy problem is its own argument](/mowers-2cm-dies-under-trees/).
