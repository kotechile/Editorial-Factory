<!-- lead -->
Oracle added $700 million to a layoff plan on 11 September 2026, and 19 days later ElevenLabs closed a $300 million employee tender that let staff sell shares at twice the value it set in February [1][4][5]. Both sums settle a promise made in stock, and both were decided by the employer alone. The difference is what reaches your side of the table: Oracle's exit pays a 26-week cash ceiling, then kills unvested awards that take 4 years to earn. The cliff is the door, not the vest date.

<!-- tension -->
My read starts with the paperwork, because one side is a filed number and the other is not.

## The big picture:

Oracle's quarterly report puts its 2026 job cut at "up to $2.1 billion" as of 31 August 2026, then adds "approximately $700 million" for steps the company expects to take [1]. It is paying cash to end a payroll it no longer wants, while the stock it cancels was granted over four years [2][7].

Severance pay and the vesting clock both count years of service, but they pay in units that never meet. Oracle gives four weeks of base pay for the first year and one extra week for each year after, stopping at a hard 26-week cap [7]. Staff spend about eight years at the firm on average, and 31% have stayed ten years or more [2]. Head count fell from about 162,000 to about 141,000 in fiscal 2026, a drop of 21,000 people or 13% [3][2]. A ten-year employee works out to 13 weeks of pay under this formula, and a worker only hits the cap after 23 years [7]. That leaves about three months of cash set against whatever portion of a four-year grant has not yet vested.

The filing is open about where the forfeited stock goes. Oracle says "we account for forfeitures of stock-based awards as they occur", and books the cost of service awards "over the service period of the award, which is generally four years" [2]. Cancelled units go right back into the plan pool, ready to grant to someone else. The company keeps the shares it had already promised, and the employee keeps the exit date.

ElevenLabs runs the mirror image. Outside investors bought stock from staff in a $300 million tender at a $22 billion value, double the price of its February round, led by Wellington and T. Rowe Price [4][5]. The firm calls liquidity a regular commitment for a team of more than 800, after a $100 million tender at a $6.6 billion value the year before [4][9]. That is a company making a market in its own paper once a year, at a price it agrees with chosen buyers.

## By the numbers

- **$2.8 billion — Severance plan ceiling:** $2.1 billion of estimated cost stood in the plan at 31 August 2026, plus about $700 million added for further steps; $324 million in cash went out in that quarter [1].
- **26 weeks — Exit pay cap:** four weeks of base pay for the first year and one more per year after, capped at 26 weeks, on a package reported to cancel unvested stock [6][7].
- **$300 million — Staff tender:** outside investors bought shares from staff at a $22 billion value, twice the February round, and the firm calls liquidity a regular commitment [4][5].
- **19 days — Event gap:** Oracle filed on 11 September 2026 and ElevenLabs posted on 30 September 2026, so two prices for one kind of asset landed in a single month [1][4].

## What I'd watch:

The people closest to this story are reading contract lines rather than arguing about market values.

- **The early-vesting line:** A contract settles whether a job cut triggers a vesting event or a cancellation, and the reported Oracle terms put layoffs strictly outside vesting [6][7]. I'd want to know how often workers read that line before signing an offer.
- **The notice dates:** Three Oracle notices filed on 14 September 2026 named a 13 November 2026 last day, giving 60 days of warning [8]. A public date that far out makes the vest dates inside the window highly visible.
- **The tender rhythm:** ElevenLabs moved from a $6.6 billion staff tender to a $22 billion one in twelve months [9][5]. If a yearly tender becomes normal, the question shifts from hoping for cash to picking which year's price to take.
- **The equity pool:** Forfeited units go back into the plan and get granted to someone else [2]. Who gets them, and at what price, is a quieter signal than any layoff headline.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->
## The catch

None of this is misconduct, and the honest version of plan design is duller than the outrage version.

Oracle's package lives plainly in the contract and, based on reporting, runs thinner than its peers. Salesforce's standard policy offers up to 30 weeks, while Microsoft's recent round went up to 39 weeks [6]. The cancelled stock is the larger financial hit and the least discussed, but it remains common plan design. Unvested simply means unearned until the worker meets the service test.

A tender is not a true market either. It features one buyer, one price, and terms the company never publishes. I could be wrong, but the useful read here is narrower than assuming private stock is safe. The price of a grant is an event, the event belongs to the employer, and the only thing you can size in advance is how much unvested pay sits in front of the next one.

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** One employer added $700 million to a layoff plan whose exit terms cancel unvested stock, while another closed a $300 million staff tender at double its last value, 19 days apart [1][4]. The same kind of asset was cancelled by one company and priced up by the other.
- **Why It Matters:** A layoff cash formula counts years of service in weeks, while the stock it cancels was granted over four years. For a long-serving worker the cancelled units are usually the larger sum, yet they never show up in the severance figure [2][7].
- **What I'd Watch:** Which event your employer is likely to schedule next, and what the contract says happens when the job ends.
  - **Early-vesting clauses:** Wording that treats a layoff as a vesting event instead of a cancellation, usually settled in the offer long before anyone needs it.
  - **Tender rhythm:** A yearly tender turns a single hope for cash into a series of priced windows, each carrying its own value [9][5].
  - **State layoff notices:** Public filings that set a last day weeks ahead, making the vest dates inside that window visible [8].
- **The Catch:** Forfeiture on a layoff is normal plan design, and a tender is a one-buyer arrangement the company can skip; the comparison here is structural, not a measure of any one person's loss.
