<!-- lead -->
Under Armour told investors on its November 6 earnings call that it will now cut its stock-keeping unit (SKU) count by 50% [2]. That doubles the 25% reduction it cleared earlier this year [2]. The same filing leaves $1.1 billion of stock standing — 4% more than the sales plan calls for [1]. Cutting SKUs makes the forecast simpler [3]. It does not fix the demand that left the stock there, because revenue fell 7% while inventory rose against it. The cost of clearing what the cut leaves hidden already shows in the company's trimmed margin guidance [1].

<!-- tldr -->
## At a glance
Under Armour is doubling its trim to 50% while stock still sits above its planned needs. This piece reads that call for operations leaders: a one-time purge simplifies the forecast once, and only faster calls on the remaining SKUs pay every cycle after.

<!-- tension -->
## The purge is a planning decision

The timeline runs in three beats. In the quarter ended September 30, Under Armour reported revenue down 7% to $1.2 billion, with stock above its own plan [1]. On the call that day, President and CEO Kevin Plank set the new goal — halve the SKU count, after clearing the earlier 25% target [2].

Chief financial officer Reza Taleghani added that the company is "reducing future buys and taking targeted sell-through actions to release working capital" [2]. The trade-press write-up landed November 24. By then Under Armour had already cut its margin outlook for fiscal 2027. The expected gain fell from 220 to 270 basis points to 170 to 220, "due largely to higher fuel and freight costs, impacts of planned inventory management actions, and foreign exchange headwinds" [1].

What strikes me is the machinery underneath. A demand plan has to predict every SKU it carries. Each added product multiplies the places a forecast can miss. Cutting the lineup in half is the blunt version of fixing that. You get fewer objects, fewer misses, and one clean quarter of cash back. The stock line is where the misses land.

Plank framed it the same way: "A smaller assortment lets us invest more deeply in our strongest products, simplify decisions, focus our storytelling and make the offer clearer for our teams, partners and consumers" [2]. What the frame leaves out is the clock. The purge pays once. The reason the stock sat above plan — revenue falling faster than buys — repeats every quarter [1].

## By the numbers
- **50% SKU cut:** The doubled trim Plank set on the earnings call [2].
- **$1.1 billion:** Quarter-end inventory, up 4% and above planned needs [1].
- **170 to 220 basis points:** The trimmed margin guidance, partly for inventory actions and freight costs [1].

## Where the decision actually lands

The people closest to the story are sequencing, not celebrating. Under Armour licensed its youth apparel line to Centric Brands. That deal starts cutting stock and complexity in fall 2027 [2]. Launches are also timed with wholesale partners, so shelf stock matches what each channel sells [2]. Taleghani's future buys are already reduced, and the margin cost is booked into the outlook, not promised for later [2].

What I find worth watching is where the freed time goes. Plank said the company sees "opportunities for AI to speed decisions and reduce repetitive work" [2]. Cisco described the same trade-off when its planning agent started saving each of 42 demand planners at least six hours a week [5]. Cisco kept every planner and spent the hours on analysis rather than fewer heads [5].

That is the fork for an operations leader sizing a planning upgrade. The purge shrinks the decision space; the sensing layer decides what each remaining product is becoming. A planning essay published October 26 argues the second half is where forecasting pays: "The profits from forecasting will only be gained if the forecast leads to a new decision relating to inventory management" [3]. The math behind the excess backs that warning, since [inventory carrying cost typically runs 20 to 30 percent of stock value a year](/inventory-carrying-cost-rate/), so a billion-dollar pile compounds while it waits [4].

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->
## The catch

What I read out of the filing is that the excess is a symptom, not only a mistake. Sales fell 7% in the quarter while stock rose against them. Two forces, not one, put the stock there. Part of the extra inventory is demand that moved faster than the plan, not products the plan added [1]. A halving cannot predict the next drop in sales. It can only shrink the blast radius when demand moves again.

The other limit is timing. The licensing move behind the deepest part of the cut starts in fall 2027, so most of the cleanup is a later-year story, not a next-quarter fix [2]. The numbers also stay tangled: the guidance change bundles inventory moves with fuel, freight and currency effects, so no single figure shows what the lineup work bought [1]. The purge and the sensing layer are not rivals. They are a sequence — and the second half is the part that repeats.
