Türkiye's new investor residence permit has a quoted price: $150k in Turkish deposits, bonds, fund units or pension money buys a three-year legal base, and the rule took effect the day it was published [1]. That is the cheap half of the move — where you are allowed to live. The other half, where you pay tax, has no price and no product, because in the US, the UK and Ireland even a careful day count below the famous number settles nothing [3].

<!-- tldr -->
## At a glance
Türkiye now sells a residence permit as a financial product, starting at $150,000. This piece covers what the permit prices, what it refuses to price, and why the day-count tests that decide your tax bill were never part of the sale.

<!-- tension -->
## Priced permit, unpriced test
The timeline is short. The decision was signed 17 November, printed in the Official Gazette on 18 November, and took effect that day [1]. Four kinds of assets qualify, each confirmed by its own regulator: bank deposits, government debt, units in property or venture funds, and private pension contributions [2]. Two price points carry the schedule: $150,000 buys three years, $200,000 buys five, and each level demands half its term as the minimum hold — 18 and 30 months [2]. After the hold, the capital can come out while the permit keeps running [2].

What I find hard to square is how differently the two halves of this deal are governed. The permit settles presence under Article 31 of Turkey's Law No. 6458, the short-term residence statute, and it is deliberately narrow [2]. It carries no right to work. Article 4 says permits issued under the decision do not count toward citizenship, so the $400,000 property route to a passport stays a separate product [2]. The thing many buyers think they are purchasing — a settled tax position — is decided by entirely other machinery in each country.

That machinery is the 183-day rule people plan around, and it is not one rule [3]. In the United States, tax residence turns on 183 weighted days spread over three years, and the year itself needs at least 31 days before the formula counts at all [4]. The UK makes 183 days final only as the first automatic test of its Statutory Residence Test; under that line, the other tests and the ties test decide [3]. Ireland counts 183 days in a tax year, or 280 across two tax years, and throws out any year with 30 days or fewer [3]. None of these tests looks at a residence permit, and no permit changes any of them.

## By the numbers
- $150,000 in Turkish financial assets is the floor of the new three-year residence permit [1].
- 18 months is the minimum hold on the three-year tier, after which the money can go [2].
- 280 days across two tax years is Ireland's other residence trigger, skipping any year with 30 days or fewer [3].

## What I'd watch
The paperwork layer is still unwritten. Article 3 leaves the document lists, the regulator letters and the renewal steps to the Ministry of Interior, so the first files will set the practice [2]. That guidance will also show how the holding period gets proved, which matters before any confirmation fee is paid. The family scope is narrower than a citizenship program's: a foreign spouse and dependent children, with parents and adult children left unnamed [2].

On the tax side, the line the permit leaves open is the one that matters. My reading of the permit's own design — no work rights, no citizenship step — is a seller content to price presence and stop there [2]. The forward trigger is where you plan to spend your days: the weighted US formula means 120 days a year for three years lands at 180 weighted days and still passes, while one heavy recent year can fail you [4]. Day counting is the load-bearing work, permit or no permit.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->
## The catch
This critique has a fair rebuttal, and I want to give it room: many buyers want presence and nothing more. A retiree or a mobile earner who stays under every country's day-count thresholds gets real value — a three-year legal base, money that stays liquid after the hold, no property purchase. The honest framing is not that the permit is a bad product. It is that the permit fee is the cheapest number in the decision. The tax position is decided later, in the day counts and tie tests of the countries you actually use, and nothing in the purchase changes that arithmetic [3]. Price the second half before you wire the first.
