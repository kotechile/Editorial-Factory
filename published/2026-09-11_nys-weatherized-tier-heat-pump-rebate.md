<!-- lead -->
On September 1, 2026, New York began paying up to double for heat pumps in homes that seal and insulate first [1]. In 20 months, this step stops being a bonus and becomes a hard rule.

For National Grid upstate, the rebate for a cold-climate heat pump jumped from $4,000 to $8,000 for a sealed home [1]. (These units heat and cool by moving outside air instead of burning gas.) Taking out the old furnace pushes that payout from $7,000 to $12,000 [1].

<!-- tension -->
**The big picture:** The two federal heat pump tax credits expired on December 31, 2025 [3]. The 25C credit covered home improvements; the 25D credit covered clean energy. Both are gone.

That leaves state and utility rebates as the only real cash help for a homeowner switching off fossil heat.

**Why it matters:** New York runs the largest heat pump rebate program in the country. In a May 2025 order, state regulators told the program to require sealing and insulating for any Clean Heat rebate by March 1, 2028 [1]. The September 1 tier is the bridge to that deadline [1][2].

Dropping a new heat pump into a drafty house wastes money. A tightly sealed house makes the same unit cheaper to buy, smaller to fit, and cheaper to run. The rule every serious contractor repeats: the envelope comes before the equipment.

<!-- tactical-insight -->
## How to get the cash

**What to do:** You can reach the higher rebate tier through one of three paths [1]. None of them need a big remodel.

- **Built after 2010:** Modern codes require tight insulation, so the program assumes your home passes. Show property records or a move-in permit to prove the build year [1].
- **Past utility work:** You qualify if you already finished a weatherization job under a known program [1]. That list includes Con Edison's Weather Ready & Sealed, National Grid's Total Home Comfort, and the New York State Energy Research and Development Authority (NYSERDA) Comfort Home [1].
- **Test your home:** This route measures the heating load — how hard the furnace works per square foot of living space. A home under 1,000 square feet passes at 30 or less on the program's scale (27 in New York City). Between 1,000 and 2,000 square feet the bar is 25 (22 in the city), and above 2,000 it drops to 20 (16 in the city) [1]. Con Edison checks it with a HomeBoost Pro home visit that includes heat photos; upstate utilities want a standard heat-load calculation backed by photos [1].

The bigger "with decommissioning" rebates carry one rule: the program wants proof the old gas or oil furnace is actually retired before it pays the higher amount [1].

**By the numbers:** The upgrade pays real cash. National Grid doubles the standard rebate from $4,000 to $8,000 [1]. Con Edison bumps its cold-climate rebate from $7,000 to $10,000 when you remove the old furnace [1], and raises its geothermal (ground-source) whole-home rebate from $25,000 to $30,000 outside lower-income zones [1]. In lower-income zones, Con Edison's cold-climate rebate rises from $8,000 to $11,000, and its geothermal rebate from $35,000 to $40,000 [1].

The play is the order: seal and insulate first, then claim the higher heat pump payout. You win twice — lower energy bills from the tight house, and a fatter rebate on the equipment.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->
## The hidden costs

**The catch:** This is one state, and an early sign. If you don't live in New York, none of this touches your rebate yet. But it is the clearest hint of where rebate design is heading.

Even inside New York, the test route isn't free. It means contractor time and paid checks before you see a single rebate dollar [1].

And "insulate first" is a bigger, slower project than a straight heat pump swap. The higher rebate helps pay for it, but it does not turn a full retrofit into pocket change.

<!-- tldr -->
- New York's heat pump rebate now pays up to double ($4,000 to $8,000 on National Grid) to sealed homes as of September 1, 2026.
- Sealing and insulating becomes a strict rule for any Clean Heat rebate on March 1, 2028.
- Homeowners qualify through a post-2010 build year, past utility work, or a home heat test.

New York just made the building envelope the gatekeeper for its biggest heat pump rebate.

On September 1, the state's Clean Heat program launched a new tier that pays homes that insulate and seal first a massive bonus. The rebate jumps from $4,000 to $8,000 for a cold-climate heat pump on National Grid turf. It bumps from $7,000 to $10,000 with furnace removal in Con Edison's.

The kicker: By March 1, 2028, sealing the house becomes a hard rule for any Clean Heat rebate, not an optional bonus.

With federal tax credits dead since the end of 2025, state rebates are the only game in town. New York is the first major program to make "envelope before equipment" a formal rule. Dropping a new heat pump into a leaky house wastes money, and now the rebate math finally reflects that.

If you are in NY, you can qualify in three ways:
1. Your home was built after 2010.
2. You completed past utility weatherization work.
3. You pass a home heat test.

If you are not in NY: Watch this space. It is the clearest signal yet of where heat pump rebates are heading nationwide.
