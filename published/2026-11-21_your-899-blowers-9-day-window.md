The snow-blower aisle has an expiry date this month. The $899 price on the EGO (the battery outdoor-tool brand) 21-inch kit, with two 6.0Ah batteries in the box, runs out on November 30, 2026 — nine days from the November 21 reading of the maker's own page. The footnote that sets that date ends on the words "Subject to availability," which means the same stretch of days can decide whether the kit is still on the shelf at all [1].

## At a glance

This article covers EGO's early-Black-Friday snow-blower discounts for homeowners weighing a battery kit this month. The finding: the maker's own footnote gives the offer nine days and warns that stock may not last that long.

## One deadline, two calendars

1. **The price calendar.** It comes from EGO's snow-blower page, where the promotion runs as a banner and the operative detail sits one line below it [1]. The footnote reads, verbatim: "1-Offer valid through 11/30/26 on select EGO tools at participating retailers. Subject to availability. U.S. Only." A second offer on the same page runs two days longer, to December 2, so the ladder has more than one cutoff [1]. I read that footnote twice before noticing the two deadlines were different dates — the maker has printed both halves of the trade in one sentence, because the price cut expires on a date and the stock it applies to may already be gone.

2. **The stock calendar.** It is being reported from the field. A best-electric-snow-blower guide, re-checked on November 11 and updated November 20, pulled two of its own prior picks because they were hard to find: a 22-inch corded Snow Joe whose Amazon listing had moved to a third-party seller, and an 18-inch SENIX (a budget cordless-tool brand) two-battery kit shown as limited stock [2]. The guide also passes along Consumer Reports' buying calendar, which names February, October, November and December as the best months to buy, and adds the clause that matters: "battery kits with packs in the box can sell out once a forecast turns, so check the listing on the day you buy" [2]. The machines easiest to buy are the ones a storm can empty out of the aisle first.

The price side opened before the season did. A deals piece published October 26 already listed the same EGO 21-inch kit at $899 against $999.99, framed as what to buy "before you wake up to a buried driveway" [4]. The fuller ladder followed on November 8 on the same deal page:

## What to watch

The gap between the banner and the per-kit markdowns is the tell. The verified kit discounts run from $51 to $151 [3]. The vendor's "up to $220" must attach to higher-priced setups that no deal piece has verified [1]. My reading, held loosely: a homeowner buying this week is buying at the top of the season's discount curve, and the four kits in the table above are the part the discount actually pays for. What the packs in the box are worth is its own arithmetic; the desk sized a battery-only purchase last month in [Your $550 Battery Spare Is Wrong](/your-550-battery-spare-is-wrong/). The forward trigger is weather. The guide's own warning is that a named storm turns the remaining stock into a queue, and the vendor's printed deadline sits in the same stretch of weeks when forecasters start naming storms [2].

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->
## The catch

None of this is a prediction that kits vanish on the deadline. The vendor's stock clause is a hedge, not a countdown, and the guide's sellout warning concerns storm-driven rushes, which nobody schedules. It is also possible the discount renews in December — the January 2026 clearance wave took the same 21-inch machine's tool-only body to $449.94, and that trough arrived too late for the first storm. What the evidence supports is narrower than a buying rule. The discount window and the stock risk close on the same kind of day, and I would put the weight on that overlap rather than on any single date. The buyer who wants one particular setup, not just any machine at a price, is the one whose choice the deadline binds.
