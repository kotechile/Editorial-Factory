Yarbo sells the same 24-inch snow blower on two base units, and the base unit sets the bill. The Y40 Core with the Snow Blower Module Gen 2 lists at $4,899. The Y40P Core with the identical module lists at $6,199 as of 2026-11-22 [2]. That $1,300 buys brushless hub motors, 1,000 extra square feet of range per charge and faster mapping [1]. The snow hardware does not change. So your mapped area — not the marketing — should pick the Core.

## At a glance

This is a comparison of Yarbo's two snow setups for homeowners weighing a buy before the season starts: the $4,899 Y40 Core and the $6,199 Y40P Core, both wearing the identical blower module. The finding is that the gap is motors and range, so map size decides it.

## Why the gap exists at all

The order of events matters here, so it comes first. Yarbo listed the Y40 Core with its snow module at $5,599 on October 25, when this desk swept the same store page. The price then fell $700 inside 30 days, and the setup now lists at $4,899, under a Black Friday banner, as of 2026-11-22 [1]. The Y40P Core with the identical module has held at $6,199 [2]. A $700 swing in under 30 days is itself a decision input: quote yesterday's page and you overpay.

What the step-up buys is stranger than the price tags suggest. Yarbo's own comparison table concedes the point an ad never would: on the rows that describe snow, the two Cores match line after line [1]. Both cut a 24-inch two-stage path. Both take a 12-inch intake and throw snow up to 40 feet [7]. Both stop at the same slope, rated 36% — a 20-degree grade — and both carry the same cold rating [5]. The rows that differ are the drive system, the range per charge, the mapping speed, and one box: the Y40 ships with a Data Center in the carton, and the Y40P does not [1]. I read that table three times before accepting that the entire premium is chassis, not clearing hardware.

## By the numbers

- **The chassis step:** the whole $1,300 difference between the two snow setups, none of it spent on the blower module [1].
- **1,000 ft² — The range step:** 6,000 against 7,000 square feet of one-inch clearing per charge, as the maker states it [1].
- **The visit Yarbo replaces:** contractor-side pricing puts a residential plow visit at $50-$150, so the Y40's tag equals 33 to 98 visits [9].
- **1.5 hrs — The recharge loop:** dock refill time behind both range ceilings, with automatic resume from where the unit stopped [8].

## What actually decides it

The people closest to this decision — the owners posting maps and storm logs on the Yarbo forum and r/Yarbo — all converge on the same first move: measure the hard surface before opening the store page. Both range ratings are stated in one inch of snow, and both shrink as depth grows. A heavier storm cuts the working range, and the machine answers by docking and resuming — that costs time, not cleared area [8]. The dock refill is fixed for both Cores: about 80% back in roughly 1.5 hours [1]. If your full map fits inside one charge of the cheaper Y40, you would pay the step-up for a battery band you never leave.

The hardware the gap does buy still matters in one common case: a long sloped drive where the surface glazes. Brushless hub motors hold grip and turn more smoothly on packed ice — a maker claim, not a test result — and the Y40P's faster mapping suits a lot with more zones [1]. The slope limit does not move. Both Cores stop at the same 36% grade, so a steeper drive rules out both machines at once [7].

Owner reports set the honest floor on what autonomous means in a storm. Yarbo's own setup guide allows that curb piles left by street plows may need an extra pass or a shovel, and that heavy wet snow is best handled with the optional plow blade [1]. That matches the [docking-and-coverage behavior](/snow-robot-stops-at-6000-ft2-it-docks/) this desk traced in October: the robot clears the storm, not the plow company's leavings. What I am watching next is whether the December storms push any owner past the 6,000-square-foot band on a single charge, because that report settles the range question faster than any spec page.

Two purchase notes. The Y40 Core with Snow Blower Gen 2 and Lawn Mower Pro lists at $6,499 — $1,600 over the snow-only setup — and that is this same choice stretched across a year, one Core carrying two modules [6]. The winter tracks ship studded, and the Gen 2 module's metal chute is rated to take stray gravel on an unpaved drive, in the maker's own words rather than a reviewer's [1]. For the payback side of either setup, the [true cost of ownership calculator](/calculators/true-cost-of-ownership/) runs the plow-visit count against the price tag, and the [yard robotics coverage](/categories/yard-robotics/) hub holds the rest of the field.

| The situation | The pick |
|---|---|
| Driveway, walks and paths inside one Y40 charge | Y40 Core — same blower, the step-up held back |
| Long or multi-zone map, glazing ice, hub-motor grip | Y40P Core |
| Slope past 36% (20°) | Neither — walk behind the machine |
| Snow plus next season's mowing, one machine | Y40 Core + Lawn Mower Pro bundle |

## What we did not test

This piece is built from Yarbo's live store pages, one independent dealer's spec guide and a contractor pricing guide, plus owner reports on the Yarbo forum and r/Yarbo. No unit was handled here, nothing was installed, and this desk did not clear a storm. Every range, slope, throw and runtime figure is a maker or dealer claim, labeled as such; the plow-visit range is contractor pricing, not a quote this desk obtained. The Y40P's smoother turning on ice is a maker claim no source here has measured.

My reading, held loosely: the verdict survives that gap in evidence, because the choice does not rest on an untested number. Both setups list the same clearing hardware, so the buyer's own map size decides against a published range band. Measure what you actually map — the drive, the walks, the path to the bins — and let it pick. Under 6,000 ft², the $4,899 Y40 Core is the buy. Above it, or on glazing ice, pay the step-up for the Y40P.
