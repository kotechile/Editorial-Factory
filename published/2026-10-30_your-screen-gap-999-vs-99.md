<!-- lead -->
Apple charges $999 to fix the inner screen on the iPhone Duo, because that display is a glued stack of layers rather than a part a shop can lift out and swap [1][5]. The fee lands at exactly half of the phone's $1,999 retail price, and $114 above the $885 top of the trade-in range Apple prints on that same buy page [3]. That spread, rather than the repair price on its own, decides whether a Duo owner comes out ahead, because the same screen costs $99 once a plan is attached to it.

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

The part I keep circling is that Apple prints two service lists, and only one of them behaves like a market price. The insured list barely moves: with AppleCare service options chosen, the repair page returns the same fees for the iPhone 12, the 15, the 17, the 18 Pro and the Duo, which is nothing for a battery, $29 for an outer screen, $19 for the Duo's top layer, $99 for an inner display and $149 for a stolen phone [2].

The cash list moves at every step. Battery service climbs from $89 on a 12 to $99, $119 and $129 on the newer slabs, then jumps to $229 on the Duo, while other damage walks from $599 to $779 to $899 and then to $1,499 [1]. Apple gives no reason for the spread. The gap is the whole point, because the riskier phone is no more costly to fix for anyone who holds the plan.

Only the plan is priced per model, and there the Duo admits what it is. Its yearly charge runs $50 above the 18 Pro's, about the price of a phone case, and that difference buys a far longer tail [1][4]. It is the one place in Apple's own numbers where the fold is treated as the riskier object.

iFixit opened the same phone last week and found the opposite of a cheap fix inside it. The teardown calls the Duo "the most repairable foldable smartphone we've ever taken apart," which is true and beside the point, because the inner screen stays a bonded stack you cannot service in place [5]. iFixit puts a similar inner-screen job on the Pixel 10 Pro Fold at three to five hours for a careful amateur, and says of its own new screens, "even on its own it won't be cheap" [5].

The independent parts trade cannot undercut the maker on this one, since the cost sits in the panel rather than the labor. That absence is what turns a monthly plan into the only stand-in for cheap parts, and it is why the flat insured list matters more than any single fee.

## By the numbers

- **$999 — Half the price of the phone:** Apple's cash price for an inner-display service on a device it sells from $1,999, quoted beside the trade-in range [1][3].
- **$19 — The top layer under cover:** the insured fee for the Duo's outer layer, against $49 as cash work on a part that sits above the display [1][2].
- **$50 a year — The plan-cost gap:** $199.99 on the Duo against $149.99 on the 18 Pro, standing for $600 more tail and $620 more screen risk [1][4].
- **$568 — The rival part price:** Google's inner display for the Pixel 11 Pro Fold against $579 on the Galaxy Z Fold 8, per iFixit's read of each maker's own quote page [6].

## What I'd watch:

My read starts with the fact that Apple's page carries no print date and no promise to freeze these figures. A fee list is a policy, revised when a company chooses, so the only honest use of one is as a snapshot taken the day you read it, which for this piece was 30 October 2026 [1].

The screen line is the quiet one. Apple prices an outer display at $379 on the Duo, matching the 18 Pro, the 17 and the 15, a flat number across four generations, while the battery line climbs at every step [1]. Screen damage has become the part of a repair bill Apple holds still, and the fold is where that stops.

The same sum that sets a repair against the years of use it buys is the one a [repair-or-replace calculator](/calculators/tech-debt-repair/) runs, and running it here has an awkward answer: one inner-display failure costs about five years of the Duo's yearly plan charge [1][3].

What the people closest to this are doing is worth noting without cheering. iFixit plans to sell new screens, priced as low as a bonded panel allows, and Apple's page states that its approved service shops set their own fees and give their own quote [1][5]. Those shops are not held to Apple's figure, which is the one pressure valve a self-insuring owner has.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->

## The catch

The listed fee is an American figure for out-of-warranty work, not a firm market rate, and Apple says as much on the page [1]. A local shop may quote less for the same job, and the insured rate stays flat only while the plan is active, since it renews until cancelled rather than for a fixed term.

The cover is not bottomless either. Accidental repairs are promised without a count, while theft and loss claims carry a cap of two in every twelve months [3]. The two lists read as a pair, then, not as a menu.

What I find hard to square is that the Duo really did get easier to fix. iFixit found the battery releases with electricity, the outer screen lifts off easily, and the top layer can be renewed for $49 without ruining the display beneath it [5]. All true, and none of it touches the thousand-dollar failure, because the cheap layer sits directly above the costly one.

Apple also publishes prices rather than failure rates. A plan that looks cheap against that long tail can still be a losing purchase for a careful owner, and someone who never cracks a screen keeps every dollar of the charge. The argument here is about how the risk is priced, never about how often it lands.
