Argentina will sell you a passport for $350,000. Your $350,000 passport isn't Europe, though. The money buys a nationality, not the right to live and work in the bloc. Portugal's own migration report, out three weeks earlier, prices that second thing in years instead of dollars.

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

I have had two documents open all week, and almost nobody puts them in one sentence.

The first is Argentina's. On 2 October, at an event in Paris, the economy minister, Luis Caputo, and the cabinet chief, Diego Santilli, opened a citizenship-by-investment programme. The price is $350,000 paid to the treasury and never returned, or $800,000 put into a government bond.

A spouse or a child aged 18 to 25 adds $100,000. Each younger child adds $25,000. A family of four reaches $500,000. Applications are due to open in the fourth quarter of 2026 [1][2][3].

The second is Portugal's. Its migration agency published its 2025 report on 22 September. The country counted 1,585,854 foreign residents at the end of the year. It issued 337,880 residence titles during 2025, up 55% on the year before.

The report says that jump came largely from finishing files that had sat for years. Permits for work were 71.1% of the total. Permits for investment, the route brokers sell as buying a way into Europe, came to 2,685 [4].

That last figure is the one I keep circling. Two thousand six hundred and eighty-five out of 337,880 is under eight-tenths of one percent. Europe's door is not a shop. It is a labour queue with a filing cabinet behind it.

The cabinet is where the waiting lives. Of the 1,585,854 residents counted, 261,443 hold no valid residence card. They are waiting on a decision, a renewal slot, or a temporary-protection status that is not a permit at all. One in six [4].

At the end of that queue sits the passport. Its price is time. Since 19 May 2026, most non-European applicants need ten years of legal residence before they can ask for citizenship. Europeans and nationals of Portuguese-speaking countries need seven. The old rule was five for everyone [5][6].

## By the numbers

- **$350,000 — Argentina's cash route:** a payment to the treasury that is never returned, or $800,000 in a government bond; a family of four pays $500,000 [1][3].
- **0.79% — Portugal's investment share:** 2,685 investment permits out of 337,880 residence titles granted in 2025 [4].
- **261,443 — residents without a valid card:** about one in six of the 1,585,854 people the report counts [4].
- **Ten years — the citizenship clock:** for most non-European applicants filing from 19 May 2026; seven years for Europeans and nationals of Portuguese-speaking countries [5][6].

## What I'd watch:

What I am watching is not the Argentine price. It is the paperwork standing between the price and the passport.

- **The programme's rulebook.** The Agency for Citizenship-by-Investment Programmes leads the screening. The state intelligence service, the financial information unit, and the security and interior ministries take part. The National Directorate of Migration approves or rejects each file [1]. The rules still to come will decide how dependents are priced, whether a refusal returns the money, and how long approval takes.
- **Portugal's investment share.** 2,685 permits out of 337,880 is 0.79% of one year. If that share keeps falling while the work route absorbs the capacity, the cash channel into Europe is smaller than the industry around it suggests.
- **The ticket and the tenancy.** A passport is a travel document and a claim on consular help. A residence card, held long enough, is a claim on living and working in 27 countries. I would want to hear a buyer say which one they were shopping for.
- **The tax that follows the passport.** A second nationality does little to a United States return filed by a citizen abroad. That part tends to stay on the shelf in the brochures.

## The catch

My read is that both numbers are honest, and neither means what it first appears to.

The Argentine figure is not a residence price. The release describes funds that go to shore up the country's fiscal position, screened by intelligence and financial-crime agencies, with the migration directorate holding the final word. The money is never returned. The approval is discretionary. What the buyer gets is a nationality: travel access, consular help, a second passport [1][3].

The Portuguese figure is not a count of people with a card in a drawer. The report says so itself. The total includes 66,740 people on temporary protection, 76,907 whose titles had lapsed and who had already paid to be seen, and 24,476 family cases still awaiting a decision [4]. The country is not filling up either. Its own summary calls the stock a sign that new arrivals have steadied.

So the comparison holds in one direction only. Cash buys the document and skips the clock. Time buys the right to live and work there, and no part of that is for sale. Whether the trade is worth $350,000 turns on a question the two announcements never ask together: which of the two things the buyer actually needs.

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** Argentina has put a public cash price on citizenship, at $350,000 to the treasury or $800,000 in a government bond, with applications from the fourth quarter of 2026. Portugal's 2025 migration report shows the European route running on residence and work instead.
- **Why It Matters:** For anyone weighing a second passport, the two prices are not comparable. Argentina sells a nationality in a quarter. Portugal sells a residence right over years, and since 19 May 2026 most applicants must wait ten of them before citizenship is even on the table.
- **What I'd Watch:** The detail that decides both outcomes is procedural, so I am watching the paperwork on each side.
  - **Argentina's rules:** the regulations behind the programme, which set how dependents are priced, whether funds come back after a refusal, and how long the migration directorate takes.
  - **Portugal's investment share:** 2,685 investment permits against 337,880 grants is 0.79% of the year, and the direction of that share says whether Europe's cash channel is shrinking or only quiet.
  - **The queue itself:** 261,443 of Portugal's 1,585,854 residents held no valid card at the reporting date, which is where a ten-year clock really bites.
- **The Catch:** $350,000 buys a passport, not a European residence right, and the money is non-refundable whether or not the screeners say yes. Portugal's ten-year clock applies to applications filed from 19 May 2026, and its headline count includes people still waiting for a decision.
