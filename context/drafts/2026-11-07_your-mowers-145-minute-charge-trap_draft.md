EGO (the battery mower brand) publishes an hour of cutting for its 7.5 amp-hour pack on a 21-inch walk-behind [4]. The same pack refills in 60 minutes on the company's rapid charger and 145 minutes on its standard one [7]. On the rapid charger the spare fills while the first pack drains, so two packs keep the machine cutting. On the standard charger the spare is still filling an hour and a half after the mower stopped. That is the trap: a second battery buys a place in line, not a second cut, unless the charger can fill it inside the run.

<!-- tldr -->

## At a glance

- **The shift:** A spare battery is a charge-time purchase, not a capacity one.
- **The number:** 145 minutes, the standard charger's refill for a pack that cuts for an hour.
- **The risk:** The rotation never closes, so the pack waits while the grass does.

<!-- tension -->

## The charger sets the pace

The part I keep circling is that the charger, rather than the pack, sets the pace. A pack stores energy and a charger moves it, so the charger's wattage sets the pace. EGO's 7.5 amp-hour pack holds 420 watt-hours [7]. Its 550-watt rapid charger could move that energy in about 46 minutes of perfect conversion, against the hour EGO publishes [6]. Battery University puts cell charge efficiency near 99 percent, so the lost time sits in the charger's own ceiling and in the slow finish of a full fill [8]. A third charger in the same line, the 320-watt unit, needs 100 minutes for the same pack [7].

The lot sets the other half of the ratio, and it is dull in the way good purchase math is dull. A 21-inch deck is 1.75 feet wide, and at 2 miles an hour the mower sweeps 176 feet a minute [4]. That covers 308 square feet before overlap. Knock off 15 percent for the strip each pass cuts twice, and a quarter acre of 10,890 square feet takes about 42 minutes. The slowest self-propel setting of 0.9 miles an hour needs 92 minutes for the same ground, and the fastest 3.1 miles an hour about 27 [5].

Put both halves together and the duty cycle falls out. An hour of cutting buys an hour of charging on the rapid unit, which is a rotation one spare pack can close. Two and a half hours of charging for the same output is a machine that finishes its grass and then sits. The useful output is set by whichever number is smaller, and for most buyers that will be the charger.

## By the numbers

- **45 to 50 minutes:** Consumer Reports' measured average run time per charge for the battery walk-behinds it tested [1].
- **7.0 foot-pounds:** the torque this mower publishes, which is not what decides whether a lot gets finished [4].
- **70 minutes:** the rapid charger's refill for a 10 amp-hour pack, so the ladder grows with pack size and not with the mower [6].

## What a rotation needs

My read on the accessory list is that it sells the upgrade in the wrong order. The people selling these machines treat the charger as an accessory and the pack as the upgrade, and that order is backwards for anyone cutting more than one charge of grass. A rotation needs the refill to finish inside the run, which is why one spare closes the loop on the rapid charger and not on the standard one. The same clock runs across the rest of this desk's [outdoor power and snow equipment](/categories/outdoor-power-equipment/) coverage, from snow blowers to trimmers, because every one of those machines pairs a pack with a charger.

The measured band leaves less room than the box suggests. Consumer Reports' testers found two-thirds of the electric walk-behinds they rated could clear a quarter acre on one charge [2]. That is the same quarter acre the deck math puts at 42 minutes on a two-mile-an-hour pass. Anyone whose lot runs past it has two honest routes: a wider deck that finishes inside one charge, or a rapid charger that finishes the refill inside one run.

Against gas, the same testers call run time the one arena gas still owns [2]. They also call electric mowers largely free of routine upkeep [3]. The trade, then, is a refill you wait for against upkeep you no longer do.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->

## The catch

EGO's hour is an "up to" figure at a load the company does not state, and a mulching pass through tall grass is not the load a spec sheet measures. Consumer Reports' 45 to 50 minutes is a cross-brand average, not this mower's own result. The real duty cycle therefore sits below the tidy one-to-one even on the rapid charger, closer to 43 percent of the clock spent cutting [1].

The 145 minutes in the first paragraph belong to the standard charger sold beside the pack, not to EGO's chargers as a class [7]. The units between those two land in between on the same ladder, which is why the number to check before buying a spare is the refill time printed on your own charger. What I keep circling is that none of this is a battery problem. The pack is rated for the same hour either way, and the number that decides whether a second one earns its place is printed on a different box.
