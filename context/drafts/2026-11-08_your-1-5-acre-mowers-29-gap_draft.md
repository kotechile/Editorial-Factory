<!-- lead -->
Your one-acre lawn now has two wire-free robot mowers at exactly $2,799.00, and the badge hides a gap of about 29% between them. The Segway Navimow X350 is sold as covering 1.5 acres [1]. The Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD 3000, an all-wheel-drive (AWD) unit, is sold as covering 0.75, yet it cuts 0.42 acre per charge against the Navimow's 0.30 [2]. Buy the Navimow for a full acre, because it is the only one of the two offered for that much lawn, it runs at 60 dB(A), and it recharges in 80 minutes [1]. The Mammotion wins below three-quarters of an acre, or on a bank past 27 degrees, where its 38.6-degree drive and its light detection and ranging (LiDAR) sensor are the point [2].

<!-- tldr -->

## At a glance

- **The shift:** Two mowers at one price, and the one rated for twice the lawn covers less per charge.
- **The number:** 0.30 acre a charge for the Navimow, 0.42 for the Mammotion.
- **The risk:** Neither is antenna-free; the Mammotion's service becomes a bill in year four.

## Three numbers that decide this buy

Navimow's own lab note says 80 minutes of charging buys 200 minutes of cutting and covers up to 0.3 acre [1]. Divide the 1.5-acre rating by that, and one full pass takes five charge cycles, or about 23 hours of cutting and recharging. A crew does the same lawn in under an hour. Mammotion puts 215 minutes and 0.42 acre a charge against a 0.75-acre rating, which is 1.8 cycles [2]. Per hour of cutting, that is roughly 3,920 square feet for the Navimow and 5,105 for the Mammotion. The cheaper machine per rated acre, at $1,866.00 against $3,732.00, is the slower one per hour.

1. **Coverage per charge.** The gap is not a defect in either listing. A rated area is a duty cycle, not a tank: it says how much lawn a maker expects the machine to keep up with when it runs most of the day. Coverage per charge is the tank. Navimow answers the second question in a footnote, well below the headline [1]. Mammotion answers it in a battery section [2].

2. **Slope ceiling.** The Navimow is rated to 50%, or 27 degrees, and its limit at the lawn's edge is lower still, at 25% [1]. The Mammotion is rated to 80%, or 38.6 degrees [2]. A lawn with a bank running down to the road is a Mammotion lawn for that reason alone.

3. **Navigation.** Both machines lean on a correction service rather than on the sky alone. The Navimow ships a real-time kinematic (RTK) antenna and a pole, and its page sends buyers to a network-coverage check for their address [1]. The Mammotion pairs its LiDAR with a network RTK service over a built-in cellular module, free for three years [2].

The part I keep circling is how easily the two numbers swap places in a reader's head. A published review falls into exactly that. The Gadgeteer reads the X350's 200 minutes as covering "appx 1.5 acres" [3]. The maker's own note attaches the same 200 minutes to 0.3 acre [1]. Two sentences, one machine, a factor of five apart, and only one of them is about a single charge.

## The trap the price tag hides

What I find hard to square is that neither antenna shows up on the sticker. Neither machine is free of one, whatever the marketing says. That antenna means a mast and a cable run in the yard. The network mode behind it depends on coverage at your address. On the Mammotion side the cellular module is the loading dock for its correction data, and the product page links a renewal page that prints no price [2]. That renewal is a real cost the sticker does not show, and this desk will not put a number on it.

The running parts are just as uneven. Navimow prices a replacement 10Ah battery at $399.99 and an anti-clogging cutting disk at $59.99 for the X3 line [1]. Mammotion's LUBA page prints no battery or blade price at all, so its upkeep is unpublished rather than low [2].

Against a contract, the sums are sobering rather than triumphant. One acre mowed weekly runs $60 to $100 a visit [7]. Assume a 26-week season, which is this desk's own number. At $1,560 to $2,600 a season, the mower takes about 1.1 to 1.8 seasons to match the bill it replaces. What it buys in year one is hours and noise, not money.

That last row needs its own paragraph, because it holds the cheapest number in this piece and the least reliable one. Kress lists the KR173 at $1,499.99 on its own mower page, against $2,999.99 regular [5]. The KR173 is a ¾-acre machine with obstacle avoidance. Its dealer promotion page lists the same model at $1,999.99 against $3,699.98, dealer channel only, through 31 December 2026 while supplies last [6]. Two of the maker's own pages sit $500 apart, and neither carries a cart. The quote is the price.

## What we did not test

My read stops where the hardware does. This review is built from the two makers' product, specification and accessory pages, one publisher's cost table and two outside reviews. The desk handled neither machine, installed neither dock and measured nothing. Every runtime, coverage and slope figure above is the maker's own claim, or a division of one, and the 23-hour pass is desk arithmetic on Navimow's lab note rather than a field result. Mammotion prints no full-charge time, so no dock-to-dock figure is given for it, and the contract sum rests on a 26-visit season that the publisher does not state.
