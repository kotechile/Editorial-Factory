Apple charges $999 to replace the inner screen on the iPhone Duo. The phone itself costs $1,999, so the failure that runs down the fold is priced at exactly half of the device, because the display beneath the cover is one bonded assembly rather than a part a shop can swap. Apple's own buy page also quotes a trade-in range that tops out at $885, which leaves the uninsured repair $114 above the best credit for a whole previous-generation handset. That spread, not the headline repair price on its own, is what decides whether a Duo owner comes out ahead.

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

The part I keep circling is that Apple publishes two service lists, and only one of them reads like a price. The insured one barely moves: with AppleCare service options chosen, the repair page returns the same fees for the iPhone 12, the 15, the 17, the 18 Pro and the Duo, which is nothing for a battery, $29 for an outer screen, $19 for the Duo's top layer, $99 for an inner display and $149 for a stolen phone [2].

The uninsured list tells a different story, model by model. Battery service climbs from $89 on a 12 to $99, $119 and $129 on the newer slabs, then jumps to $229 on the Duo, and other damage walks from $599 to $779 to $899 and then to $1,499 [1]. Apple gives no reason for the spread. The gap is the whole point, because it means the riskier phone is not more expensive to fix for anyone who bought the plan.

The plan itself is priced per model, and only there does the Duo admit what it is. Its yearly charge runs above the 18 Pro's by the price of a decent case, and that difference buys a much longer tail [1][4]. It is the one place in Apple's own numbers where the fold is treated as the riskier object.

iFixit opened the same phone a week ago and found the opposite of a cheap fix inside it. The teardown calls the Duo "the most repairable foldable smartphone we've ever taken apart," which is true and beside the point, because the inner display is a bonded stack of layers rather than a part you swap [5]. iFixit puts a similar inner-screen job on the Pixel 10 Pro Fold at three to five hours for a careful amateur, and says of its own new screens, "even on its own it won't be cheap" [5].

The independent parts trade, in other words, cannot undercut the maker on this one, because the cost sits in the panel rather than the labour. That absence is what turns a monthly plan into the only substitute on the menu, and it is why the flat insured list matters more than any single part price.

## By the numbers

- **$999 — Half the price of the phone:** Apple's out-of-warranty inner-display service on a device it sells from $1,999, quoted on the same page as the trade-in range [1][3].
- **$99 — The flat insured fee:** identical on the Duo, the 18 Pro, the 15 and the 12, beside a battery service that drops to nothing under cover [2].
- **$50 a year — The plan-cost gap:** $199.99 on the Duo against $149.99 on the 18 Pro, standing for $600 more tail and $620 more screen risk [1][4].
- **$568 — What the same part costs elsewhere:** Google's inner display for the Pixel 11 Pro Fold against $579 on the Galaxy Z Fold 8, per iFixit's read of each maker's own quote page [6].

## What I'd watch:

My read starts with the fact that Apple's page carries no publication date and no promise to hold these figures. A fee list is a policy, revised when a company chooses, and the only honest way to use one is as a snapshot taken the day you read it, which for this piece was 30 October 2026 [1].

The screen line is the quiet one. Apple prices an outer display at $379 on the Duo and a screen at $379 on the 18 Pro, the 17 and the 15, a flat number across four generations, while the battery line climbs at every step [1]. Screen damage has become the part of a repair bill Apple holds still, and the fold is where that stops.

The same sum that sets the repair against the years of use it buys is the one a [repair-or-replace calculator](/calculators/tech-debt-repair/) runs, and running it here has an awkward answer: one inner-display failure costs about five years of the Duo's yearly plan charge [1][3].

What the people closest to this are doing is worth noting without cheering. iFixit plans to sell new screens, priced as low as a bonded panel allows, and Apple's page states that its approved service shops set their own fees and give their own quote [1][5]. Such shops will not be held to Apple's figure, which is the one pressure valve a self-insuring owner has.

## The catch

The list price is an American figure for out-of-warranty work, not a market price, and Apple says as much on the page [1]. A shop that is not Apple may quote less, and the insured fee is only flat while the plan is in force, since it renews until cancelled rather than for a fixed term.

The plan is also not unlimited in every direction. Accidental repairs are promised without a count, while theft and loss carry a cap of two claims every twelve months [3]. The two lists read as a pair, then, not as a menu.

What I find hard to square is that the Duo's repairability really did improve. iFixit found the battery releases with electricity, the outer screen lifts off easily, and the top layer can be renewed for $49 without sacrificing the display beneath it [5]. All true, and none of it touches the failure that costs a thousand dollars, because the cheap layer sits above the expensive one.

There is also no failure rate in any of this. Apple publishes prices, not incident counts, so a plan that looks cheap against that long tail can still be a losing purchase for a careful owner, and a self-insurer who never cracks a screen keeps every dollar of the charge. The argument here is about how the risk is priced, never about how often it lands.

## At a glance

**The shift:** Apple's insured repair fees are flat across five iPhones while its uninsured fees run from $89 to $1,499, so the plan carries the risk the parts list prices.
**The number:** $999 on a $1,999 phone, against $99 with cover.
**The risk:** prices are undated and third-party shops set their own.
