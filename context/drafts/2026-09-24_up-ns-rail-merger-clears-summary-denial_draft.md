<!-- lead -->
On Sept. 18, the Surface Transportation Board (STB) — the federal body that decides whether big railroads may combine — unanimously refused to throw out the proposed Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger, pushing what the two companies bill as America's first transcontinental railroad into a full merits review [1]. Rival railroads BNSF and CSX and a coalition of chemical, fuel, and fertilizer shippers had asked the Board to kill the application outright, arguing the railroads never showed the deal serves the public interest [1][2].

<!-- tension -->
**The big picture:** A UP-NS combination would stitch Union Pacific's 23-state western network to Norfolk Southern's 22-state eastern network into a single coast-to-coast system — the first true transcontinental Class I railroad in the modern era [2]. The companies say a seamless single-line railroad eliminates the slow interchanges where freight changes hands between carriers, which is where cost and delay hide. That is the upside. The downside is what BNSF and the shippers fear most: one railroad controlling both ends of a lane can price captive customers — the shippers with no second rail option — with far less discipline than two railroads competing for the same move [3].

**Why it matters:** Most freight today still interchanges between railroads at gateways like Chicago or Kansas City. A single carrier that owns the whole route can undercut on price one year and reprice the next, once the interchange competition is gone. For an ops leader, this is not a railroad story — it is a freight-sourcing story. Which of your lanes are single-rail captive? Which contracts lock in a rate for how long?

**By the numbers:**
- **2 railroads, one network:** Union Pacific spans 23 western states and Norfolk Southern spans 22 eastern states, which together would form a single transcontinental system [2].
- **10,000 interline lanes:** the number of existing lanes the merger would convert from two-railroad handoffs to single-line service [2].
- **2 million truckloads:** the highway traffic UP and NS claim the combined railroad would remove from roads each year [2].
- **Under 1% of rail traffic:** what BNSF says the deal's only real competition fix — Committed Gateway Pricing — actually covers, and only for a limited time [3].

<!-- tactical-insight -->
**The playbook:** The record is open until Nov. 18, and the outcome is not decided. Three moves worth making now:
- **Map your captive lanes.** List every rail lane where you have only one serving carrier. Those are the lanes a coast-to-coast railroad could reprice. Pull the origin-destination pairs from your TMS and flag single-carrier routes.
- **File a comment by Nov. 18.** The STB is explicitly accepting shipper comments on the merits through Nov. 18, 2026 [1]. A short filing that names your lanes, your volumes, and your competitive-route options carries more weight than silence.
- **Check your rate protections.** Look at how long your contract rates are locked and whether you hold any 2-to-1 or gateway-access rights today. Those rights are what you negotiate from — and what this deal could quietly shrink [3].

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->
**The catch:** The Sept. 18 decision is not a win for the merger — it is the Board choosing to keep building the record rather than dismiss it [1]. STB member Richard Kloster concurred only to warn that the applicants "still have a long way to go" to prove the deal is in the public interest [3]. The STB said plainly the decision "does not reflect any determination on the merits" [1]. So the smart read is not "the merger will happen" — it is that a once-in-decades rail consolidation is now on a fixed clock, and the comments shippers file by Nov. 18 are part of what decides the outcome. If the deal clears, the final decision lands within 90 days of the record close, and UP and NS expect to close in the second half of 2027 [1][2].

- **The Big Shift:** On Sept. 18, the Surface Transportation Board unanimously refused to dismiss the Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger, sending America's first coast-to-coast Class I railroad into a full regulatory review on a fixed timeline.

- **The Winning Moves:**
  - **Map captive lanes:** Flag every lane where you have only one serving railroad; those are the routes most exposed to a merged carrier's pricing power.
  - **File a comment by Nov. 18:** The regulator is taking shipper input on the merits through Nov. 18, 2026 — a short, specific filing beats silence.

- **The Catch:** The Board's decision does not endorse the deal — it only keeps the record open. The newest STB member says the railroads "still have a long way to go," and the deal could still be conditioned or blocked before any 2027 close.
