<!-- lead -->
The state approved its climate reporting rule on 12 October. That makes 10 November 2026 the first date on which big firms doing business there must file a carbon report [2][7]. The report counts only a company's own fuel and power [1]. When the state adds supply-chain figures in 2027, its plan skips 2 of the 15 lines it could ask for. The two it drops are the ones that cover your trucks [3]. Two days after the state moved, the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) pushed its own date from 30 October 2026 to 10 March 2027 [4]. That is 131 days later, in a rule it issued with no notice or comment period [4].

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

I've read both notices now, and they pull in opposite directions.

The state rule is narrow and firm. It bites on firms formed under U.S. law with yearly sales above $1 billion that do business here. They must send the California Air Resources Board (CARB) their scope 1 and scope 2 figures by 10 November [1][2]. A firm that held no such data and was not collecting it by 5 December 2024 may send a one-page note instead, but it still pays a fee [1]. Bills go out by 10 December and are due within 60 days [1].

The federal rule that is slipping away is the one built on fuel. The Greenhouse Gas Reporting Program (GHGRP) makes plants and fuel sellers file every year [4][6]. It covers 47 kinds of source and more than 8,000 of them, including sellers of petrol products and of natural gas liquids [4][6]. EPA has now moved the 2025 date twice, first from 31 March to 30 October, then from 30 October to 10 March 2027 [4][5]. It says reporters need time to react to a plan that would cancel most of the program, and that its own web tool cannot be ready in time [4].

That is the crash. One agency nails a date to the wall. The other says its own filing portal will miss the old date, and that the whole program may end [4][6].

## By the numbers

- **10 November 2026 — First filing date:** 18 days out, counting scope 1 and scope 2 only. Fee bills follow on 10 December [1][2].
- **131 days — Federal date slip:** EPA moved 2025 reports from 30 October 2026 to 10 March 2027. The rule started the day it was issued [4].
- **5 of 15 — Planned supply-chain rows:** CARB staff would require lines 1, 3, 5, 6 and 7 from 2027. The two transport lines stay optional [3].
- **$1,000,000,000 — Sales cutoff:** the line that decides whether a firm files at all, no matter how much freight it moves [1].

<!-- tactical-insight -->

## What I'd watch:

What strikes me is that a carrier's number enters neither rule by itself. In the state rule the filer is the shipper with the sales. In the federal rule it is the plant or the fuel seller [1][4]. A fleet shows up only if its customer counts it. So the file arriving from purchasing this quarter matters more than the legal text.

- **The line question:** if CARB keeps the two transport lines optional in the final 2027 rule, a shipper's freight row becomes a choice rather than a duty [3]. That choice gets argued over at renewal.
- **The method question:** the same gallon makes a different number when it is counted from fuel burned rather than from miles and weight [3]. The buyer who writes "grams per shipment" also writes the answer.
- **The bill:** fee bills land on 10 December whether or not the data was filed [1]. The cash cost of being in scope is already set.
- **The comment file:** comments on the federal extension close 30 November [4]. A final word on the cancellation would settle whether the March date is real.
- **The quiet route:** the one-page note lets a first-year filer skip reporting with no fine [1]. I want to see how many big shippers take it.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->

## The catch

The idea has a hard edge, and it cuts both ways. A carrier with sales above $1 billion is a filer itself, so the split between filer and data seller is a sales test rather than a job title.

The optional status of the two transport lines is also a plan, not a settled rule. CARB showed it at a July workshop for the rule that follows the 2026 filing, and a final text could put transport back in [3].

I could be wrong about how fast buyers agree on a format, but the state's own pages already lag its rulemaking. CARB's page still describes the rule as waiting for approval, while five separate reads of the 12 October approval agree on the date [2][7]. So the code cite is better proof than any notice [1].

One more limit: the federal date has moved twice in eight months. A March 2027 date that exists to make room for a possible cancellation is not a date to build a system on.

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** The state fixed 10 November 2026 as the first carbon filing date for large firms doing business there, two days before EPA pushed its own federal date out to 10 March 2027 [2][4].
- **Why It Matters:** Neither filing is a carrier's. The state rule reaches shippers with sales above $1 billion, the federal rule reaches fuel sellers and plants [1][4]. The two transport lines that would count a fleet's miles are optional in the state's 2027 plan [3].
- **What I'd Watch:** How shippers define the freight number they ask for, now that no agency has defined it for them.
  - **Transport lines:** The two supply-chain rows in the Greenhouse Gas Protocol that cover the movement of bought goods and of sold goods. They are planned as optional from 2027 [3].
  - **Method choice:** Whether a load is counted from fuel burned or from miles and weight. The choice changes the number a carrier reports [3].
  - **The cancellation file:** The federal plan to drop reporting for most kinds of source, still not final [4][6]. It is the reason the March date exists.
- **The Catch:** The transport lines are optional only in a plan, a carrier above the sales cutoff files on its own account, and the federal date has already moved twice [1][3][4].
