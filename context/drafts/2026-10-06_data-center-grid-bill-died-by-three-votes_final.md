On Sept. 16, the U.S. House voted 417-3 to make large data centers pay the full cost of the grid upgrades their servers need [1][3]. Two weeks later, the Senate killed the exact same bill [3]. The 57-43 test vote fell three votes short of the 60 it needed to pass [3].

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

The Ratepayer Protection Act (H.R. 9340) asked for very little. It told states to look at rules for large power users. It wanted them to pay for the poles and wires they force utilities to build, even if the user later walks away [2]. The bill defined a large-load customer as a data center using 100 megawatts (MW) or more at one site [2].

The bit that stuck with me is the vote count. A bill that won the House 417-3 could not pass a basic test vote in the Senate [3]. Senate Democrats called it a weak message bill that failed to shield homes from rising costs [3]. 

Analysts said the bill would only back a shift that is already happening [4]. States are writing their own rules for large power users anyway [4].

Congress does not decide if a server rack gets power. That call belongs to state utility boards and grid operator waitlists.

## By the numbers

- **417-3 — House vote:** The bill passed the House with huge support on Sept. 16 [1][3].
- **57-43 — Senate vote:** The bill failed a test vote on Sept. 30, falling short of the 60 votes it needed to move on [3].
- **100 MW — The cutoff:** The bill defined a large-load customer as a data center using at least 100 MW at one site [2].
- **194 gigawatts (GW) — Texas load:** On Sept. 3, the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) gave 204 projects (66.4 GW) base-load status and 158 projects (127.9 GW) studied-load status, while cutting 302.2 GW entirely [6].

<!-- tactical-insight -->

## What I'd watch:

The people closest to this issue are not lobbying in Washington. They are reading the calendar. ERCOT paused grid hookups for new large data centers of 75 MW or more. It will wait until it checks every project in its waitlist [6].

- **The audit gate:** ERCOT graded its whole queue on Sept. 3 and launched a check of every large load [6]. It owes reports to the state utility board by Dec. 10 [6]. A draft yes does not mean a final connection.
- **The switch-on freeze:** ERCOT will block new large data centers from drawing grid power until the check and a local review finish [6]. Galaxy Digital holds draft status for 4.2 GW across five Texas sites. The company told investors the check will take several months [7].
- **The state rulebooks:** California signed seven data-center bills on Sept. 21 to make sites pay their fair share of grid costs [5]. The failed federal bill just asked states to look at steps they are already taking [4].
- **The behind-the-meter option:** As grid dates slip, the urge to stop waiting grows. ERCOT's studied load faces the highest risk of delays [6]. This group must still wait for scarce power slots [6].

I'd want to know how many operators now build an off-grid power option into their core plan. Many used to treat it just as a backup.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->

## The catch

I could be wrong to call a failed vote the main event. The bill carried its own flaw by telling states to consider a standard, not enforce one. Critics called it a message rather than a strict law, so analysts expect little real change [3][4].

The harder catch cuts the other way. State checks and new rules are less about blocking data centers. They are more about deciding who pays and who actually builds. 

ERCOT kept about 194 GW of power load in its queue but cut 302.2 GW outright [6]. This shows most proposed projects were never going to be built [6]. 

Grid operators are rationing connections. That rationing now runs through state boards rather than federal law.

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** The U.S. House passed a bill 417-3 to make large data centers pay for the grid upgrades they cause. The Senate killed the measure two weeks later on a test vote.
- **Why It Matters:** State utility boards and grid operators now control the true timeline and costs for data center power. This turns a build team's power plan into a strict regulatory game.
- **What I'd Watch:**
  - **The audit gate:** Texas grid operator ERCOT put every large power load on draft status and launched a deep check. Early approval is not a final connection.
  - **The switch-on freeze:** New large data centers pulling 75 MW or more cannot draw grid power until ERCOT finishes its checks and local reviews.
  - **The state rulebooks:** States like California are writing their own rules for large power users. They are setting the terms without federal help.
- **The Catch:** The failed federal bill only asked states to "consider" a standard. Its defeat changes little while grid operators actively ration power connections anyway.
