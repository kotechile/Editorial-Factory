The $125 exam fee is the smallest cost on the bill, because the real price of a new skill is your time. Six courses in Google's Information Technology (IT) Support certificate take 122 hours of study time, which costs $3,628 at the median wage for a help-desk worker [3][4]. Set that beside the $125 fee on a cloud certification and the exam looks like a rounding error [1].

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

What I keep coming back to is that course sellers never send the real bill. A course page counts time in months, while a wage table prices a year in 2,080 working hours. Put the two numbers together, and the upfront fee quickly stops mattering.

The Google IT Support program lists its six courses at 19, 22, 28, 24, 23, and 6 hours of work, which adds up to 122 hours [3]. Divide that total by the 2,080 hours a working year contains, and it equals 5.87% of a year's paid time. Because both numbers are fixed, that share holds steady at every single wage.

At the help-desk median of $29.74 an hour, 122 hours costs $3,628, and at the software developer median of $65.38, the same block of time reaches $7,976 [4][6]. Add three months of Coursera Plus at $59 to take the program, and the total lands near 6% of a year's pay either way [2][3].

The exam fee sits completely outside that daily math. Associate Cloud Engineer is $125 for a two-hour paper, and the badge is good for three years [1]. Renewing it by exam costs $75 for one hour, which works out at $25 a year across each three-year term, against $41.67 a year for the first term [1]. The two hours in the chair are worth $59 at the help-desk median, $95 at the systems-administrator median and $131 at the developer median.

## By the numbers

- **122 hours — Course load:** The Google IT Support program's six course cards list 19, 22, 28, 24, 23, and 6 hours of work, against a stated design of three months at ten hours a week [3].
- **$3,805 — Total cost:** 122 hours at the $29.74 help-desk median ($3,628) plus three months of Coursera Plus at $59 a month ($177) [2][4].
- **6% — Share of pay:** 122 divided by 2,080 paid hours is 5.87% before the subscription, and 6.15% with it at the help-desk median [3][4].
- **1.2 months — Payback period:** The gap between the help-desk median ($61,860) and the systems-administrator median ($99,130) is $37,270 a year, or $3,106 a month, which repays a $3,805 cost in 1.2 months [4][5].

## What I'd watch:

I have been reading course pages next to wage tables all week, and the pattern is clear. The badge is priced like a purchase, but it is paid for like a job search.

- **The rung test:** The badge only pays if it moves you into a better-priced job. Computer user support workers median $61,860, while network and computer systems administrators median $99,130 [4][5]. That step is $37,270 a year, and against it a $3,805 cost repays in about five weeks.
- **The break-even raise:** Swap the job change for a 5% raise and the math flips. Five percent of $61,860 is $3,093 a year, so the same badge takes nearly 15 months to break even [3][4]. At the developer median, the break-even raise for 12 months is 6.0%.
- **The renewal clock:** Associate Cloud Engineer runs three years, then a $75 renewal exam of one hour [1]. That hour is worth $48 at the systems-administrator median, so the renewal costs more in time than in cash.
- **The vendor's score:** Coursera reports that 75% of certificate graduates report a positive career outcome, such as a new job, a promotion or a raise, within six months, from a 2022 survey of its own graduates [3]. A survey like that cannot tell a $500 bump from a real promotion, which is why I would not trust the math to it.
- **The third-party exam:** The same program page says it readies learners for the Computing Technology Industry Association (CompTIA) A+ exams [3]. That is a second badge with its own fee and renewal, so the whole stack has to be priced as a stack.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->

## The catch

My read is that the method is sound and the medians are not an offer.

The 122 hours is a sum of six course cards, not a hard number the page prints [3]. A learner who already knows the facts finishes faster, while a learner who fails a test finishes slower. The three-month design assumes ten hours a week, and the page's own finish guess runs to six months.

The $37,270 rung step is the gap between two job medians, not a sure return on a badge [4][5]. Nobody hands over that raise just because a badge exists; the next employer prices the next job. If the move never happens, the $3,805 buys nothing but a line on a resume.

Employer-paid training changes the whole sum, because the worker's own cost drops. When a company covers the course and the study happens on its time, the rung test becomes the only thing left to check.

Some credentials act as gates rather than raises. A help-desk role that filters on CompTIA A+ is not paying for the badge, but simply refusing people without one [3]. That presents a different math problem, and it usually argues for finding the cheapest route to the gate.

The fees are current as of October 2026, and the wages reflect the 2025 figures from the federal Occupational Information Network wage database. Both sets of numbers will move over time [1][2][4][5][6].

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** Course sellers price their goods by the exam fee, while the real cost is the study time. The Google IT Support program's 122 hours of course work is worth $3,628 at the 2025 help-desk median, against the $125 fee on a cloud certification [1][3][4].
- **Why It Matters:** 122 hours is 5.87% of a year's paid hours at any wage, so a badge is roughly a 6% bet on your own pay. It clears only if it moves you into a better-paying job, where the median step from help desk to systems administration is $37,270 a year [4][5].
- **What I'd Watch:**
  - **The rung step:** The gap between the median pay of the job you hold and the job you want. Against a $3,805 cost, a $37,270 step repays in about five weeks [4][5].
  - **The break-even raise:** The yearly raise a badge needs to repay itself inside a year. It is 6.0% to 6.2% of pay at the medians here, which is a bigger jump than most yearly cycles give [3][4][6].
  - **The renewal:** Associate Cloud Engineer is valid three years and renews by a one-hour, $75 exam [1]. The cash is $25 a year after the first term, while the hour costs more than that in time.
- **The Catch:** The rung step is a job median gap, not a certified premium, and a badge nobody hires for returns nothing. The course hours are a sum of six course cards rather than a page total, and every fee here is dated to a single day.
