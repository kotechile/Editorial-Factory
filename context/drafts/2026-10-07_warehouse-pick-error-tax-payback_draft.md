<!-- lead -->
A single wrong pick looks like a rounding error until it is priced. One operator's own math puts a building at 60 bad lines a day when it ships 4,000 lines at a 1.5% error rate [1]. At $25 to sort out each return and re-ship, that is about $390,000 a year [1]. At higher volume the rate hardly has to move. Pick 6,000 items a day at a 1% error rate and the bill is $1,560,000 a year at $100 each [3]. The error rate is not a quality score. It is a capital-budgeting input.

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

Picking is where the hours are. One vendor cites a 2024 study that puts order picking at up to 55% of a warehouse's running cost [1]. Manual picking runs at a 1% to 3% error rate [1]. Scan-and-light systems hold above 99%, and voice-directed work reaches 99.99% [1].

Those small shares sit inside a market that still moves goods by hand. In the 2026 Automation Study, only 12% of operations say picking is fully automated [6]. Another 33% say picking is mostly or fully manual, with no plans to change [6].

My read: the number that matters is not the error rate. It is the cost of one error, and many teams never work it out. Nearly 20% of the firms in one study did not track mispick cost at all [2].

## By the numbers

- **33% — Still picking by hand:** A third of surveyed operations say picking is mostly or fully manual with no plans to automate, against only 12% fully automated [6].
- **99.5% — The new floor:** Modern eCommerce operations are expected to hold 99.5% picking accuracy, while most centers run at a 1% to 3% error rate [5].
- **$22 to $300 — What one error costs:** Published estimates of the average cost of a mispick run from $22 each [2] up to a $50 to $300 band by industry [4]. That spread is the whole argument.
- **1% — The silent bill:** At a 1% error rate over 6,000 items a day, one vendor's own math lands at 60 wrong orders daily and $1,560,000 a year at $100 each [3].

<!-- tactical-insight -->

## What I'd watch:

What strikes me here is that two teams with the same error rate can face very different bills. The missing input is their own cost per error.

- **The four-way cost of one mispick:** A bad pick is paid for four times — the pick, the return, the re-ship and the credit [1]. Each line lands in a different budget. That is why the total rarely shows up in one place.
- **The checking layer:** Scan-at-pick-face tools confirm the item as it is pulled, and vision checks add a second look at packing [1]. Teams moving toward the 99.5% floor add these checks rather than more robots [5].
- **What one error does downstream:** About 23% of eCommerce returns happen because the customer got the wrong product. And 58% of shoppers say they would drop a brand after one poor experience [5]. A small error can cost far more than its headline figure.
- **The signals I'm watching:** which teams start to price their own cost per error, and whether the checking gear is bought on its own or inside a goods-to-person deal.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->

## The catch

The part I keep circling is that the cost of an error is not fixed. It swings ten-fold across the sources — $22 in one study [2], $50 to $300 by industry in another [4], $100 in a third [3]. It depends on the value of the goods, the return policy, and how much support time a bad order eats.

The evidence has a second limit. Five of the six sources here are vendors, not outside auditors, and they gain from a bigger error figure. The one survey that sells nothing, the 2026 Automation Study, measures how manual the work still is — not what an error costs [6].

The math also cuts both ways. A building with a high error rate on costly goods can justify a checking layer fast. A low-volume or low-value site may never clear the payback. Its savings run through the same small volume that made the small leak.

<!-- tldr -->

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** Picking accuracy is being re-priced from a quality statistic into a capital-budgeting input, as operators attach a dollar figure to each mispick.
- **Why It Matters:** At consumer-scale volumes a 1% error rate is a seven-figure annual leak, so the payback on automated pick checking turns on one number most teams never measure — their own cost per error.
- **What I'd Watch:** How teams price a bad pick before they buy checking gear:
  - **Cost per error:** The full cost of one mispick, including the re-pick, the return, the re-ship and the credit.
  - **Checking layer:** Scan-at-pick-face and vision checks that push accuracy toward the 99.5% floor.
  - **Downstream cost:** The returns and lost customers that turn a small error into a large bill.
- **The Catch:** Cost per error swings ten-fold across the sources, most of which are vendors, and a low-volume or low-value site may never clear the payback.
