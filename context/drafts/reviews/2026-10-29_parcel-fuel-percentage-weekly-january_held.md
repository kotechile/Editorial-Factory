Your 8 per cent charge on base postage shifts every week starting in January. The United States Postal Service (USPS) is trading it for a fuel surcharge on competitive parcels, reset weekly off diesel and jet fuel prices [1]. The notice came on 26 October. A day later United Parcel Service (UPS) reported U.S. domestic revenue up 4.5 per cent on a 6.1 per cent rise in revenue per piece, while average daily volume fell 3.3 per cent [2][3]. One document sets out a new pricing rule, and the other shows what that rule does when package volume falls.

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

USPS stated in March that it had avoided surcharges, noting its 8 per cent transport rise was less than a third of what rivals charge for fuel alone [4]. The agency called that rise a bridge to a permanent mechanism [4]. The bridge now ends at midnight Central time on 17 January 2027 [4]. The replacement arrives with only a month attached, as the agency question sheet answers January 2027 without giving a specific day [1]. 

I read this timeline as a direct transfer of pricing risk to the shipper. A flat charge on base postage is a fixed number a logistics team can put in a budget and defend during contract talks. A floating share is a number that arrives from the carrier just three to five days before it hits diesel products, and three to four days before it hits jet fuel products [1]. No buyer signs off on it.

UPS demonstrates why a carrier wants this exact rule. Its U.S. domestic revenue reached $14,865 million against $14,220 million a year earlier, and the release gives the 6.1 per cent lift in revenue per piece the credit [2]. During the earnings call, finance chief Brian Dykes divided that gain. Base rates and customer mix provided half the growth, while higher fuel rates supplied the rest [3]. The half a buyer agrees to was matched by the half that floats.

The Postmaster General treats this dynamic as deliberate policy. Revenue rising while volume falls "has been true for 14 out of 16 quarters", David Steiner said in August, and "we have more price to take in the marketplace" [5]. A pattern he traces back sixteen quarters now gains a part that moves on its own. However, signed contracts may have customized language regarding the fee, moving the carve-out back into the room where the base rate was agreed [1].

## By the numbers

- **8% — The bridge being replaced:** charged on base postage for Priority Mail Express, Priority Mail, USPS Ground Advantage and Parcel Select from 26 April 2026 until midnight Central time on 17 January 2027 [4].
- **11.5% — USPS revenue per piece:** the June quarter's shipping revenue of $8,250 million on 1,554 million pieces against $7,662 million on 1,609 million a year earlier, or about 11.5 per cent more per piece. The release gives the two ends, not the ratio [6].
- **6.1% — UPS revenue per piece:** U.S. domestic growth in the third quarter, won on a 3.3 per cent fall in average daily volume, with fuel behind about half of it [2][3].
- **3 to 5 days — Notice window:** how far ahead the agency says the diesel figure will be posted, and three to four days for jet fuel products [1].

## What I'd watch:

- **The first number:** I am watching the January calendar to see how the agency handles the transition. The question sheet names a month, and the charge it replaces ends on a stated date, leaving a potential overlap that no published document resolves [1][4].
- **The contract carve-out:** The agency notes that Negotiated Service Agreements (NSAs) may have customized language [1]. For a large shipper, this is the single part of the decision that remains open for negotiation.
- **The postage-due answer:** Mail bearing the previous week's share becomes postage due, and all payment systems will apply the fee [1]. Testing of the change becomes available about two weeks before the price change date [1].
- **UPS's fourth quarter:** The carrier guides U.S. revenue up 3 per cent on a slightly lower daily volume, with a little more than 100 customers behind about 80 per cent of the seasonal spike [3]. Another quarter of price outrunning volume turns a peak-season quirk into a regular policy.

## The catch

My view is that the base matters just as much as the moving rate. The first percentage remains unpublished, and a flat charge is not automatically larger or smaller than a weekly share of base postage. A January bill could easily move down instead of up at the start [1]. The base itself is narrow, as signature, insurance and nonstandard handling sit outside the share, meaning a heavy add-on mix dilutes the impact [1].

The truckload form of this tool is a formula rather than a share, built from a base peg, an assumed miles per gallon figure and a rate table. Parcel is reaching the same place from the other side. The carrier posts a number, and a shipper reads it days before it is charged.

For an operations team, the residue is a weekly task forced inside a monthly process. A rate card that changes four or five times a month must now show up in label software, invoice audits and any customer promise priced off postage [1].

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** USPS is swapping a flat 8 per cent transport rise for a fuel surcharge calculated as a share of base postage and updated weekly from January 2027. In the same quarter, UPS booked a 6.1 per cent gain in revenue per piece as daily volume fell 3.3 per cent.
- **Why It Matters:** The line that moves a parcel budget is now the one nobody bargains over. Base rates get agreed in a contract, but the share arrives from the carrier days before it applies to both commercial and retail parcels.
- **What I'd Watch:** The small print that decides how much of a bill floats.
  - **The first share:** The number itself decides whether January brings a rise or a fall against the 8 per cent bridge.
  - **The contract carve-out:** Signed deals may carry customized language, allowing the biggest shippers to sit outside the fee entirely.
  - **The postage-due rule:** Mail bearing the prior week's share is short-paid, turning a pricing change into a daily mailroom check.
- **The Catch:** The share covers base postage only, leaving add-on services outside it. Because the initial number is unpublished, the January change is not automatically a price hike.
