Yarbo sells the same 24-inch two-stage snow blower on two chassis, and the chassis decide the invoice: the Y40 Core with the Snow Blower Module Gen 2 lists at $4,899, and the Y40P Core with the identical module lists at $6,199 as of 2026-11-22 [1][2]. That $1,300 buys brushless hub motors, 1,000 extra square feet of clearing per charge and faster mapping — nothing about the snow itself changes [1]. For a driveway and walkway map inside about 6,000 ft², the Y40 is the right Core; step past that figure or onto rutted ice, and the Y40P stops being an upsell.

This is a buyer's comparison of Yarbo's two snow setups for the 2026-27 season, aimed at homeowners weighing the $4,899 Y40 Core against the $6,199 Y40P Core. Both carry the identical Snow Blower Module Gen 2, so the mapped area — not the marketing — picks the Core.

## The table that argues against its own upsell

Yarbo's own comparison table makes the honest argument better than a marketing page would. On the rows that describe snow — clearing width, intake height, throw distance, deflector and chute range, slope rating, operating temperature — the two Cores are listed as the same, line after line: a 24-inch two-stage cut, a 12-inch intake, throw up to 40 feet, and a slope ceiling both share at 36% (20°) [1]. The differing rows are the drive system, coverage per charge, mapping speed and one box: the Y40 ships with a Data Center in the carton, the Y40P does not [1]. I read the parity rows twice before believing the entire $1,300 sits under the chassis, because every row about snow says "same."

The price has also moved under this comparison. The desk's sweep of the same Y40 setup on 2026-10-25 recorded a list of $5,599; the store now shows $4,899 for the Core plus module [1]. A $700 swing in under 30 days on a machine sold with a Black Friday banner is itself a decision input — the number is volatile, and the buyer who quotes yesterday's page overpays.

## What I'd watch: your map, your ice, your slope

Map your hard surface before you look at the checkout. Coverage per charge is measured in one inch of snow, and both ratings shrink as depth grows — a 4-inch storm cuts the effective range hard, and the machine answers by docking and resuming, which costs time, not coverage [1][8]. The recharge math is fixed for both Cores: about 80% restored in roughly 1.5 hours [1]. A property whose full map fits inside one charge of the cheaper Y40 therefore pays $1,300 for a battery band it never exits.

The hardware the gap does buy still matters in one common case: a long sloped driveway where the surface glazes. Brushless hub motors hold traction and turn more smoothly on packed ice, and the Y40P's faster mapping suits a lot with more zones [1]. The slope ceiling does not — both Cores stop at the same 36% (20°) grade, so a steeper drive rules out both machines equally [1][7].

Owner reports set the honest floor on what autonomous means in a storm. Yarbo's own setup guidance concedes curb piles left by street plows may need an extra pass or a shovel, and that heavy wet snow is best handled with the optional plow blade [1]. That matches the [docking-and-coverage behavior](/snow-robot-stops-at-6000-ft2-it-docks/) this desk traced in October: the robot clears the storm, not the plow company's leavings.

Two purchase notes. First, the Y40 Core with Snow Blower Gen 2 and Lawn Mower Pro lists at $6,499 — $1,600 over the snow-only setup — which is the year-round version of this same decision, one Core carrying two modules [6]. Second, the winter tracks ship studded, and a dealer guide notes the intake must stay above the surface it eats; gravel driveways want the metal-chute tolerance the Gen 2 module claims, read in the maker's own words rather than a reviewer's [1][7]. What I am watching next is whether the $4,899 list holds past the Black Friday banner, because a repeat of October's $700 move changes the payback count. For that arithmetic, the [true cost of ownership calculator](/calculators/true-cost-of-ownership/) runs the plow-visit count against the invoice, and the [yard robotics coverage](/categories/yard-robotics/) hub holds the rest of the field.

## The catch

This comparison is built from Yarbo's live store pages, one independent dealer's spec guide and a contractor pricing guide, plus owner reports on the Yarbo forum and r/Yarbo; no unit was handled here, nothing was installed, and no storm was cleared by this desk. Every coverage, slope, throw and runtime figure is the maker's or the dealer's claim, labeled as such; the plow-visit range is contractor-side pricing, not a quote obtained. The Y40P's smoother turning on ice is a maker claim no source here has measured.

My reading, held loosely: the verdict survives that gap, because the decision does not rest on an unmeasured number. Both products list the same clearing hardware, so the choice is the buyer's own map size against a published range band. Measure the area you actually map — driveway, walks, the path to the bins — and let it pick. If the answer is under 6,000 ft², the $4,899 Y40 Core is the buy; above it, or on glazing ice, pay the $1,300 for the Y40P.
