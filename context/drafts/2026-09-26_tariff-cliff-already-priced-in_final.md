Washington just delayed the United States–China tariff truce to Jan. 10, 2027, but American companies are not buying the calm [1]. A new Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta report shows 70% of firms are hoarding their tariff refunds as cash [2]. Chief Financial Officers (CFOs) are building a massive buffer instead of spending the money on new growth. This huge gap between the official timeline and actual corporate behavior is the real story.

<!-- tension -->
## A Pause, Not A Fix

**The big picture:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the United States and China will stretch the "Busan Agreement" from Nov. 10 to Jan. 10 [1]. This deal suspends key import taxes for American buyers. Bessent was blunt about the future: "I don't know whether a bigger deal can be done. I don't know whether we will just roll the current deal" [1]. This two-month delay is just a brief pause, not a real fix.

**Why it matters:** The Atlanta Fed found firms sitting on a massive pool of cash [2]. The total refund pool is nearly $170 billion. The government paid out roughly $100 billion of that by late July [2]. Rather than spend it, companies are locking it away. 

When companies choose cash over new investments, they expect the suspended tariffs to snap back. They are pricing in the risk of sudden cost hikes. For operations leaders, the Jan. 10 deadline is an immediate problem to solve today.

**By the numbers:**
- **2 months:** The length of the new truce, pushing the deadline to Jan. 10, 2027 [1].
- **70%:** The share of firms keeping at least some of their refund as cash [2].
- **~$100 billion:** The exact amount already refunded to companies by late July [2].
- **17.2% / 14.8%:** The share of firms planning customer rebates (17.2%) or lower prices (14.8%) — the minority passing the windfall through [2].

<!-- tactical-insight -->
## Prepare For January Now

**The playbook:** Treat Jan. 10 as a hard deadline and lock in your plans today.
- **Stress test your landed costs:** Model your exact import bills if the suspended taxes return. Find the specific Stock-Keeping Units (SKUs) that hurt your profit margins most. You must re-price or shift these items first.
- **Match cash to exposure:** The companies hoarding cash are hedging their bets against Washington. Compare your working capital buffer against your total tariff risk. If you have low cash reserves and rely heavily on imports, you are badly exposed.
- **Lock in suppliers early:** If tariffs return, every rival will hunt for the same alternative factories. Start these talks now while factory capacity remains cheap and available. Do not wait for the frantic December rush.
- **Watch pricing signals:** The firms issuing customer rebates (17.2%) or cutting prices (14.8%) are showing their hands [2]. Track your rivals to see who truly believes the truce will last. Their daily pricing moves reveal their secret tariff strategy.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->
## Cash Is Not Proof

**The catch:** Hoarding cash does not guarantee a sudden tariff shock. The Atlanta Fed notes this cash retention perfectly matches standard textbook rules for handling one-time windfalls [2]. Bessent also left the door open for a larger trade deal before January [1]. 

The data simply shows firms refuse to bet blindly on peace. They are playing it safe. Plus, one-third of these refunds go to cash-strapped firms who need the money to pay basic bills [2]. For them, the cash is just survival, not a grand strategy.

Treat Jan. 10 as a live deadline. Not a December problem.
