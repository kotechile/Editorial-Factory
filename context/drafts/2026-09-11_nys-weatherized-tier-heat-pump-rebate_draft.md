<!-- lead -->
On September 1, 2026, New York's flagship heat pump rebate started paying homes that insulate first up to double — and in 20 months, that insulation stops being a bonus and becomes the price of admission [1]. On National Grid's upstate territory, the rebate for a cold-climate air-source heat pump (a unit that heats and cools by moving heat rather than burning fuel) jumped from $4,000 to $8,000 for a single-family home that already meets a weatherization standard — and from $7,000 to $12,000 when the old furnace is removed [1].

<!-- tension -->
**The big picture:** The federal heat pump tax credits — the 25C home-improvement credit and the 25D clean-energy credit — expired on December 31, 2025 [3]. That left state and utility rebates as essentially the entire savings stack for a homeowner who wants to electrify. **Why it matters:** New York runs the country's largest heat pump rebate program, and its utility regulator has now told it to make weatherization — air sealing plus insulation — a formal gatekeeper. The September 1 tier is the bridge: the New York Public Service Commission's May 2025 order requires the program to make weatherization a *prerequisite* for any Clean Heat rebate by March 1, 2028 [1]. The message to every homeowner is the one the building-science crowd has been shouting for a decade: **the envelope comes before the equipment.** A high-efficiency heat pump dropped into a leaky, uninsulated house is throwing money out the window; a tight house makes the same unit cheaper to buy, smaller to size, and cheaper to run.

<!-- tactical-insight -->
**What to do:** A home qualifies for the higher "Weatherized Tier" through any one of three pathways, and none of them require a gut remodel [1].

- **If your home was built after 2010, you likely auto-qualify.** Modern code-level air sealing and insulation are assumed; you just document the build year (property records, permits, or a certificate of occupancy) [1].
- **If you already had a weatherization job done, you may already be in.** Past participation in Con Edison's Weather Ready & Sealed, NYSEG/RG&E's Home Insulation and Air Sealing, NYSERDA's Comfort Home, National Grid's Total Home Comfort, or O&R's Sealed Comfort Plan counts [1].
- **Otherwise, prove the envelope performs.** The Performance Pathway measures the home's heating load per square foot — roughly, how hard the furnace has to work per square foot of conditioned space. A home at or under 999 square feet needs a score of 30 or better upstate (27 in Con Edison's New York City territory), and the bar tightens for larger homes [1].

**By the numbers:** the upgrade is worth real money. National Grid doubles the cold-climate full-load rebate ($4,000 → $8,000); Con Edison raises its cold-climate rebate with furnace removal from $7,000 to $10,000, and its ground-source (geothermal) whole-home rebate from $25,000 to $30,000 outside disadvantaged-community zones [1]. The play: get the air sealing and insulation done first, then claim the higher tier on the heat pump — you capture both the lower long-run bills from the tight envelope and the fatter rebate on the equipment.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->
**The catch:** this is one state, and a leading indicator, not a national rule yet. If you don't live in New York, the Weatherized Tier doesn't touch your rebate — but watch it, because it is the clearest signal yet of where rebate design is heading. Even inside New York, the Performance Pathway is not free: it requires documentation (a Manual J load calculation, plus for Con Edison a third-party HomeBoost on-site assessment with thermal imaging), which means contractor time and cost before you see a dollar [1]. And "insulate first" is a bigger, slower first project than a straight heat pump swap — the higher rebate helps, but it does not turn a full envelope retrofit into pocket change.

<!-- tldr -->
- New York's heat pump rebate now pays up to double ($4,000 → $8,000 on National Grid) to homes that are already weatherized, as of September 1, 2026.
- Weatherization becomes a hard prerequisite for any Clean Heat rebate on March 1, 2028 — the envelope, not the equipment, is now the first dollar to spend.
- Qualify via three paths: built after 2010, past weatherization work, or a heating-load performance test.

New York just made the building envelope the gatekeeper of its biggest heat pump rebate.

On September 1, the state's Clean Heat program launched a "Weatherized Tier" that pays homes that insulate and air-seal first a materially higher rebate: $4,000 → $8,000 for a cold-climate heat pump on National Grid territory, and $7,000 → $10,000 with furnace removal in Con Edison's. And the kicker — by March 1, 2028, weatherization becomes a hard requirement for any Clean Heat rebate, not a bonus.

With the federal 25C/25D tax credits dead since Dec 31, state rebates are the whole game now. New York is the first major program to make "envelope before equipment" a formal rule — a high-efficiency heat pump in a leaky house is money out the window, and now the rebate math finally says so.

If you're in NY: built after 2010 → you likely auto-qualify. Already weatherized → you may be in. Otherwise, a heating-load test gets you there.

If you're not in NY: watch this. It's the clearest signal yet of where heat pump rebates are headed.
