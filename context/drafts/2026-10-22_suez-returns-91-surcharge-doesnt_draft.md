<!-- lead -->
Suez returns, and your invoice doesn't follow. Carriers are moving services back through the Red Sea, with transits expected to recover fully in the first quarter of 2027. Yet the one number that changed on an Asia–Mediterranean shipment this month is a surcharge: $91 more per twenty-foot equivalent unit (TEU), live since 19 October, with no end date on the notice. Capacity moves the base rate. The add-on doesn't.

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

**My read starts with the two clocks in a freight contract.** The base rate is repriced once a year. A surcharge is repriced whenever the carrier says so. This one says "until further notice" [2].

The return is no longer a forecast. Container tonnage through the Suez Canal hit 72.1 million tonnes in the first eight months of 2026. That is up 54.2% on the same stretch of 2025, per the canal authority's 16 September communique [4]. Freightos' head of research expects full recovery in the first quarter of next year [1]. He also adds a line carriers will not enjoy reading: the return "increases the overcapacity risk for carriers in 2027" [1]. Drewry's base case, as reported by The Loadstar, has transits back to 80% of their pre-diversion level by February 2027 [3].

So the base rate heads down, in time, as more ships make more round trips. The surcharge heads nowhere. It is flat and open-ended. Mediterranean spot rates rose 3% in the week to 20 October, on general rate increases aimed at the 2027 contract round [1]. Carriers are pushing the rate up while they restore the capacity that will pull it down. The per-unit line is how both can be true at once.

## By the numbers

- **$91 per TEU — Surcharge switch:** a piracy risk charge of $55 plus a canal charge of $36, applied from 19 October 2026 "until further notice" on Asian cargo bound for the listed Mediterranean and Black Sea ports [2].
- **$3,927 per FEU — Mediterranean spot:** Asia–Mediterranean rates rose 3% in the week to 20 October, and Asia–North Europe rose 6% to $3,442 per forty-foot equivalent unit (FEU) [1].
- **$1m a voyage — Canal saving:** Drewry's slot cost comparison puts the Suez saving at about $1m per round voyage, or $43 per slot, against the Cape route [3].
- **$2.18m a ship — Surcharge ceiling:** The Loadstar's own sum for a fully loaded 24,000 TEU vessel, which is about 2.2 times the saving above (desk arithmetic on rows two and three) [3].

<!-- tactical-insight -->

## What I'd watch:

**Where this bites is the bid document, and the people closest to it are already splitting the two numbers apart.** A rate sheet that lists one all-in figure hides which half expires.

- **The split:** a lane charge with no end date has to be tendered as its own line, with its own expiry and its own trigger for a fresh talk.
- **The routing question:** shippers on the Asia–Europe call last week told the Journal of Commerce that some carriers switch to Suez at short notice, and that not every insurer carries Suez cover, so cover has to be arranged fast [3].
- **The corridor count:** the extension names Derince and Port Said West plus a long list of North Africa, West Mediterranean and Adriatic ports, so the same per-unit charge now reaches ports that no canal routing serves [2].
- **The next signal:** whether the 2027 bids closing this quarter carry the $91 as a standing line or a time-boxed one. That tells you more about next year's cost than the spot index does.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->

## The catch

**Between the lines, the surcharge is not a canal fee.** Mediterranean Shipping Company (MSC) puts both charges down to "the security situation of piracy risk around the Arabian Peninsula", and applies them to all cargo moving from Asia to the listed ports [2]. Nothing in the notice ties either amount to a ship that actually transits Suez. A shipper whose boxes go round the Cape pays the same $91. That makes it a lane charge, not a share of a routing cost.

The arithmetic that makes this vivid is also the soft part. Drewry's $1m saving covers slot costs only, and the analysis says it leaves out war-risk premiums and canal discounts [3]. The $2.18m is The Loadstar's own sum for a full 24,000 TEU ship, and it is offered as a possible maximum [3]. Set them side by side and the charge is roughly 2.2 times the saving, but each figure is a ceiling or a floor of a different thing.

One carrier's schedule is not the market. I have MSC's notice and nobody else's, so how far the pattern travels is an open question. A fast fall in base rates next year would also give shippers a reason to raise the add-on in the same talk.

<!-- tldr -->

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** Carriers are moving services back through the Suez Canal, with transits expected to recover fully in the first quarter of 2027, while a $91 per-TEU surcharge that began on 19 October applies to Asian cargo bound for Mediterranean and Black Sea ports with no end date.
- **Why It Matters:** The return adds capacity and should push the base rate down. The surcharge sits on top of that rate, is charged by lane rather than by routing, and does not fall with it. A 2027 freight budget built on the direction of spot rates can miss the line that actually grew.
- **What I'd Watch:** Next year's contract bids, and how they treat the two halves of the price.
  - **Split line items:** a base rate and a surcharge listed apart, each with its own expiry date.
  - **Suez insurance cover:** whether cargo cover for Red Sea routings is arranged before a carrier switches a service.
  - **Carrier followers:** whether other lines publish the same per-TEU charges on the same lanes.
- **The Catch:** The charge is put down to piracy risk, not to the canal, so a shipper routing round the Cape still pays it. The gap between the canal saving and the surcharge bill also rests on an analyst's slot-cost model and a full-ship maximum.
