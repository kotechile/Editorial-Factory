<!-- lead -->
Your 8 per cent charge on base postage shifts every week starting in January. The United States Postal Service (USPS) is replacing it with a fuel surcharge on competitive parcels, reset weekly off diesel and jet fuel prices [1]. The notice came on 26 October. A day later United Parcel Service (UPS) reported U.S. domestic revenue up 4.5 per cent on a 6.1 per cent rise in revenue per piece, while average daily volume fell 3.3 per cent [2][3]. One document sets out a new pricing rule, and the other shows what that rule does when package volume falls.

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

USPS said in March that it had "steadfastly avoided surcharges", and that its 8 per cent transport rise was "less than one-third of what our competitors charge for fuel alone" [4]. The agency called that rise a "bridge to a permanent mechanism" [4]. The bridge now ends at midnight Central time on 17 January 2027 [4]. The replacement has only a month attached, because the agency's own question sheet answers "January 2027" and gives no day [1].

I read that as a transfer of pricing risk. A flat charge on base postage is a fixed number a logistics team can put in a budget and defend in a contract talk. A floating share is a number that arrives from the carrier three to five days before it hits diesel products, and three to four days before it hits jet fuel products [1]. No buyer signs it.

UPS shows why a carrier wants this rule. Its U.S. domestic revenue reached $14,865 million against $14,220 million a year earlier, and the release gives the 6.1 per cent lift in revenue per piece the credit [2]. On the earnings call, finance chief Brian Dykes split that gain: base rates and customer mix provided about half, and "higher fuel rates made up the remainder" [3]. The half a buyer agrees to was matched by the half that floats.

The Postmaster General treats this as policy, not luck. Revenue rising while volume falls "has been true for 14 out of 16 quarters", David Steiner said in August, and "we have more price to take in the marketplace" [5]. A pattern he traces across sixteen quarters now gains a part that moves on its own. Signed contracts "may have customized language" on whether the fee applies at all, which puts the carve-out back in the room where the base rate was agreed [1].

## By the numbers

- **8% — The bridge being replaced:** charged on base postage for Priority Mail Express, Priority Mail, USPS Ground Advantage and Parcel Select from 26 April 2026 until midnight Central time on 17 January 2027 [4].
- **11.5% — USPS revenue per piece:** the June quarter's shipping revenue of $8,250 million on 1,554 million pieces against $7,662 million on 1,609 million a year earlier, or about 11.5 per cent more per piece. The release gives the two ends, not the ratio [6].
- **6.1% — UPS revenue per piece:** U.S. domestic growth in the third quarter, won on a 3.3 per cent fall in average daily volume, with fuel behind about half of it [2][3].
- **3 to 5 days — Notice window:** how far ahead the agency says the diesel figure will be posted, and three to four days for jet fuel products [1].

<!-- tactical-insight -->

## What I'd watch:

- **The first number:** I am watching the January calendar, because the question sheet names a month and the charge it replaces ends on a stated date, leaving an overlap that no posted document clears up [1][4].
- **The contract carve-out:** the agency says Negotiated Service Agreements (NSAs) may carry customized language, which is the one part of this decision still open for a large shipper [1].
- **The postage-due answer:** mail bearing the previous week's share becomes postage due, and all payment systems will apply the fee [1]. Testing opens about two weeks before the price change date [1].
- **UPS's fourth quarter:** the carrier guides U.S. revenue up 3 per cent on a slightly lower daily volume, with a little more than 100 customers behind about 80 per cent of the seasonal spike [3]. A second quarter of price outrunning volume would make the pattern a policy, not a peak-season quirk.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->

## The catch

My view is that the base matters as much as the moving rate. The first percentage is unpublished, and a flat charge is not clearly bigger or smaller than a weekly share of base postage, so a January bill could move down as easily as up [1]. The base is narrow too. Signature, insurance and nonstandard handling sit outside the share, so a heavy add-on mix dilutes it [1].

The truckload form of this tool is a formula rather than a share, built from a base peg, an assumed miles per gallon figure and a rate table. That is why [the per-mile fuel tables](/fuel-surcharge-peg-mpg-audit/) can move a shipper's bill while the diesel price stands still. Parcel is reaching the same place from the other side: a number the carrier posts, and a shipper who reads it days before it is charged.

For an ops team, the residue is a weekly task inside a monthly process. A rate card that changes four or five times a month has to show up in label software, in bill audit and in any customer promise priced off postage [1].

<!-- tldr -->

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** USPS is swapping a flat 8 per cent transport rise for a fuel surcharge set as a share of base postage and reset weekly from January 2027. In the same quarter, UPS booked a 6.1 per cent gain in revenue per piece as daily volume fell 3.3 per cent.
- **Why It Matters:** The line that moves a parcel budget is now the one nobody bargains over. Base rates get agreed in a contract, but the share arrives from the carrier days before it applies to both commercial and retail parcels.
- **What I'd Watch:** The small print that decides how much of a bill floats.
  - **The first share:** the number itself, which nobody outside the agency has seen, decides whether January brings a rise or a fall against the 8 per cent bridge.
  - **The contract carve-out:** signed deals may carry customized language, letting the biggest shippers sit outside the fee entirely.
  - **The postage-due rule:** mail bearing the prior week's share is short-paid, turning a pricing change into a daily mailroom check.
- **The Catch:** The share covers base postage only, leaving add-on services outside it. Because the first number is unpublished, the January change is not automatically a rise.
