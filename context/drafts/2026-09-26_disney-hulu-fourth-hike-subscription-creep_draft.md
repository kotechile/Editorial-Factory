On September 23, Disney+ and Hulu each pushed their ad-free tier up $2.50 — 13 percent — to $21.49 a month, the fourth price increase in four years [1][3]. The same morning, the two streamers had a full quarter of doubled profits to point at.

<!-- tension -->
## A Bill That Compounds

**Why it matters:** Streaming has been getting more expensive roughly three times faster than the broader inflation rate since 2022 [4]. This is not a one-off. It is the same mechanism behind every $9-to-$29 line item that auto-renews without anyone noticing.

**The big picture:** Disney did not raise prices because its costs spiked. It raised them because profitability did. Disney+ and Hulu's combined operating income jumped 116 percent year over year to $712 million, and their revenue rose 11 percent [6][7]. The part I keep circling is that the hike landed right after that result, not before it — the price follows the profit, and the bill follows the subscriber.

**By the numbers:**
- **13% / $2.50:** The ad-free Disney+ and Hulu jump, taking each to $21.49 a month [1].
- **4% / $0.50:** The ad-supported tier's increase, to $12.49 a month — Disney steering people toward ads [2].
- **+116%:** Disney+ and Hulu's combined operating income, year over year, to $712 million [6].
- **~3×:** How much faster streaming prices have risen than the broader inflation rate since 2022 [4].

<!-- tactical-insight -->
## Where This Bites

**Where this bites:** The people closest to the story are already routing around the $21.49 sticker, and their moves say more than the press release does.

- **The bundle is the bait:** The ad-free Disney+/Hulu bundle rose only $2, to $21.99, which is almost half the price of buying the two services separately [1][5]. Disney has spent years steering subscribers into bundles because those households cancel less often.
- **Ads are the soft landing:** The ad-supported tier moved just 50 cents, to $12.49 [2]. That is a deliberate funnel toward ad revenue, not an accident of rounding.
- **Netflix sets the ceiling:** Netflix's premium tier still sits at $26.99 a month, the priciest individual plan [8]. Disney at $21.49 is now chasing that number, not resisting it.

What I'd watch next: whether the households doing a quarterly audit — the ones who cancel anything unused for 30 days — treat this hike as the trigger to kill a second or third dormant subscription. The tools with no logins in a month are the first to go, and a 13 percent bump is the nudge that usually starts the count.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->
## The Catch

**The catch:** The $21.49 headline overstates the real hit for most people. If you are already on the bundle, your bill moved $2, not $2.50, and the ad tier barely moved at all [1][2][5]. Disney is raising the list price partly to make the bundle and the ad tier look like the sensible option.

My read: none of this makes streaming a bad deal. Even at $21.49, a service watched for a few hours a week is cheap entertainment per hour compared to a cable bill or a movie ticket. The thing worth watching is the habit, not the dollar amount — four hikes in four years is a cadence, and it is the cadence, more than any single increase, that quietly compounds.

- **The Catch:** The headline number overstates the real cost for bundle and ad-tier subscribers, and streaming is still cheap per hour of use. The real risk is the recurring cadence, not any single hike.
