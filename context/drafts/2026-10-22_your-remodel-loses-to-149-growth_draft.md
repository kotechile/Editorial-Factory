<!-- lead -->
The electrician who wires your kitchen and the one who wires a data center are the same person. This fall, the data center is winning. Crews under contract for that work carried 11.3 months of signed backlog in September, a full three months more than crews without it [1]. Over the same stretch, home improvement spending fell 7.4% from a year earlier [4]. Your remodel loses to 149% growth in data center work, and what it loses is not price. It is the calendar [2].

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

**My read starts with two gauges of one trade, read from opposite ends.** One counts work already signed. The other counts what builders think of their own market.

Associated Builders and Contractors (ABC) asks its members each month how many months of work they hold. The plain answer was 8.7 months in September. The 15% of members who do data center work averaged 11.3 months, "a full three months more" than the rest [1].

The National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) asks remodelers a different question. Its market index scored 62 in the third quarter, up a point [3]. A reading over 50 means more call conditions good than poor [3].

So both surveys say busy. The dollars say something thinner. Home improvement spending rose 2.5% in August and is still down 7.4% on the year [4]. Data center building rose 7.5% that month. It sits more than 73% above a year ago, and it has run at a 149% annualized pace since March [2].

NAHB's chief economist draws the line himself. Shortages of labor, he says, "exacerbated by immigration enforcement and competition from data center construction, are extending the time it takes to complete projects" [3]. That clause is the one place either release admits the two markets touch.

The split is lopsided. Of contractors with more than $100 million in sales, 39% hold data center work now. Below that line, it is about 1 in 10 [1]. A small remodeler does not bid against another small remodeler for wire pullers. He bids against a jobsite that signs year-long schedules.

## By the numbers

- **11.3 months — Data center backlog:** crews under contract for that work averaged 11.3 months of signed jobs in September, a full three months more than members without it [1].
- **149% — Pace of the boom:** data center building rose 7.5% in August, sits more than 73% above a year ago, and has grown at a 149% annualized pace since March [2].
- **7.4% — Remodel dollars:** home improvement spending rose 2.5% in August but is down 7.4% on the year, in a Census series that is not adjusted for prices [4].
- **62 — Remodeler mood:** the market index rose a point, and the backlog of remodeling jobs rose two points to 56 [3].

<!-- tactical-insight -->

## What I'd watch:

**Where this bites is the schedule page of the contract, which is usually the shortest part of the bid.** A price is a number. A finish date is a claim about someone else's crew.

- **The crew named in the bid:** the question I would want answered is whether the people priced into week one are the people who arrive in week six. On an 11.3-month backlog, crews get moved.
- **The size split:** large jobs rose two points to 66, while mid-size fell to 71 and small fell to 73 [3]. The biggest jobs are holding their ground. That fits a labor story better than a demand story.
- **The margin line:** more than 1 in 4 contractors expect profit margins to shrink over the next six months, the highest share since November 2025 [1]. Cost pain runs both ways.
- **The next print:** October's backlog figure lands in November, then the fourth-quarter mood index. If data center backlog climbs again while remodel backlog goes flat, the split is here to stay.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->

## The catch

**Between the lines, both headline numbers are opinions gathered by trade groups.** ABC polls its own members. NAHB polls remodeler members. Neither is a count of the work for sale in your town. An 11.3-month average also says nothing about the one builder who quoted your kitchen.

The mechanism is about time, not money. NAHB says competition from data center work is stretching out finish dates [3]. Nothing in this pair says data centers make a remodel cost more. ABC names materials, labor and borrowing costs as the squeeze on contractors. It names them for big commercial jobs [2].

My hesitation about the tidy version is simpler than that. Falling dollars and rising confidence can sit together for dull reasons. NAHB's own remodeling chair points to high material costs and to customers holding back [3]. Part of that 7.4% is nerves, not capacity. The two stories are not exclusive, and this data cannot split them.

What I keep circling is that the schedule is the one variable a homeowner can see. A builder's calendar is hidden before you sign. It shows up in a single line of the contract. That line is where the next year of this market will land.

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** Crews doing data center work carried 11.3 months of signed backlog in September, three months more than crews without it. Home improvement spending fell 7.4% on the year, even as remodeler mood rose to 62.
- **Why It Matters:** The scarce good in a 2026 remodel is not the quote. It is the crew's calendar, and the best crews are booked on longer jobs with bigger budgets.
- **What I'd Watch:** Whether the gap between data center work and remodel work widens in the next two surveys.
  - **Backlog gauge:** a monthly poll of how many months of signed work crews hold, split by data center work.
  - **Mood index:** a quarterly survey of remodeler opinion, where 50 is the break-even mark.
  - **Remodel spending:** the Census dollar series, which shows what was actually put in place.
- **The Catch:** Both headline surveys poll trade group members, and the clash they describe is about time rather than price.
