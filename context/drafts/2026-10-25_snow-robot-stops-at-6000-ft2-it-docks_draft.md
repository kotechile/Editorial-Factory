The Yarbo Y40 Core with the Snow Blower Module Gen 2 is listed at $5,599 as of 2026-10-25, and the number that decides the purchase sits one line under the price, because the page has to soften it. Yarbo puts capacity at 6,000 ft² per charge, then names the basis: one inch of snow [1]. That is where the robot stops and heads for its dock.

| Product | Price (2026-10-25) | What the listing says |
|---|---|---|
| Yarbo Y40 Core with Snow Blower Module Gen 2 | $5,599 | $6,199 regular; two-stage, 12-inch intake, 24-inch width [1] |

Buy it if the same Core will also mow: the bundle with the Lawn Mower Pro is $7,399 [1], and that second job is what pays for a platform. Buy it for snow alone and you are paying platform money for one job.

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

Yarbo sells one Core and a rack of modules, so the sticker is an entry fee, not a total. The mower bundle runs $7,399, which makes mowing an $1,800 add-on [1], and the snow module alone lists at $2,000, or $1,299 refurbished [2]. My read: the price list is the most honest page on the site, and it says the snow job alone does not carry the Core.

## By the numbers

- **$1,800 — The second job:** the Y40 with blower and mower lists at $7,399, which is $1,800 over the snow-only setup, so mowing is the cost that has to justify the Core [1].
- **$2,000 — The wear part:** no auger, scraper or pin price appears on the storefront. The snow module is sold as one unit at $2,000, so a February fault is a part price [2].
- **1.5 hours — The dock return:** at about 20% battery the machine docks, charges to about 80% in roughly 1.5 hours, and carries on [1].

## What the spec sheet hides

- **40 feet claimed, 23 to 26 feet seen.** The page says the snow flies "up to 40 ft" [1]. The independent review of the earlier module watched it land 7 to 8 meters away, or 23 to 26 feet [5]. Different generation, so read the gap as a direction, not a verdict.
- **A 12-inch mouth, a one-inch number.** The auger takes a foot of snow while the area claim is quoted at an inch [1]. Storms run deeper, so the charge divides by depth: 1,000 ft² at 10 inches is 10,000 square-foot-inches, or 167% of a charge, and that is a dock return before the driveway is clear.
- **134 pounds, and two water ratings.** The reviewer's table puts the Core at 134 pounds (61 kg) [5], fine until a track buries itself and someone has to dig it out. Yarbo says IPX5, the same table says IPX4 [1][5]; neither is a lab test I can point to, so read the machine as built for spray, not for being dunked.

## What I'd watch:

Owners on Yarbo's own forum are the closest thing to a field record, and they agree on where it struggles. Nine inches of dry snow came off cleanly; wet, heavy snow clogged the auger until one owner parked the blower and finished with the plow blade, and another had a module swapped for a wiring fault [8]. The forum belongs to the vendor, so treat that as owner reports, not testing.

- **Wet snow, not deep snow:** the depth figure is the easy case. Complaints gather around slush, which is the weak spot of most two-stage blowers too [8].
- **Cold charging:** in freezing weather the battery can want 20 to 30 minutes of warm-up before it takes a charge, stretching every mid-storm return past the 1.5 hours the page quotes [5][1].
- **Curb piles:** the page admits plowed-in snow at the driveway mouth may need a person — the job most owners hire out [1].

## The catch

Both ends of the thermometer are hard stops. The page rates the machine to 36% slope (20°) and stops the operating range at -13°F [1], so a steeper driveway or a colder snap sits outside the promise. My view: in a snowbelt town those two lines matter more than anything in the marketing, and an owner with a two-car driveway and a walk-behind in the garage should keep pushing it. A mainstream 24-inch two-stage claims an 18-car driveway at 8 inches on one charge, in its own words [7] — a different unit with no published area, so it cannot be set against the square footage above, but it shows the trade: less money, more of your Saturday.

## Ownership cost

The consumable is the module, not a blade. Yarbo publishes no auger or scraper price: the snow module is a $2,000 part, $1,299 refurbished, and the plow blade owners reach for in slush is $159 [2][3]. The 38.4Ah battery is $1,199, about a fifth of the machine, with no cycle life published, so that replacement is real but undated [4]. Track grease is $49 a tub [3].

## Verdict

Buy the Yarbo Y40 Core with Snow Blower Module Gen 2 only if the Core will also mow. Snow alone at $5,599 is a platform you will not use; the mower module is what turns an expensive snow blower into a machine that works most of the year. Snow-only buyers on a normal driveway should spend less and push. If you do buy, run your own numbers on the [true cost of ownership calculator](/calculators/true-cost-of-ownership/) and read the [yard robotics coverage](/categories/yard-robotics/) before the first storm — the module prices are the part nobody quotes at the counter.

## What we did not test

This review is built from Yarbo's own spec and price pages, one independent measured review of the previous generation of the snow module, Consumer Reports' preview (which states it has not lab-tested the product) and owner reports on Yarbo's forum. The unit was not handled here, nothing was run in snow, and every capacity, throw and charge figure belongs to the maker or to the reviewer named beside it.

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** Yarbo's winter setup is priced as a modular platform — Core and snow blower at $5,599, the same Core with the mower at $7,399 — and its published capacity is 6,000 ft² per charge at one inch of snow.
- **Why It Matters:** the snow job alone does not carry a five-figure platform. The mowing module spreads the cost across a season, and clearing snow is the smallest part of what the machine does.
- **What I'd Watch:** where the field reports land.
  - **Wet, heavy snow:** owners report clogging in slush and reach for the plow blade, while dry snow clears cleanly.
  - **Dock returns:** a mid-storm recharge runs about an hour and a half, longer in freezing weather.
- **The Catch:** capacity is quoted at one inch of snow, the machine stops at 36% slope and -13°F, and the battery's replacement interval is not published.
