<!-- lead -->
Your 8 per cent charge on base postage shifts every week from January. The United States Postal Service (USPS) is swapping it for a fuel surcharge on competitive parcels, reset weekly off diesel and jet fuel prices [1]. The notice came on 26 October. A day later United Parcel Service (UPS) reported U.S. domestic revenue up 4.5 per cent on a 6.1 per cent rise in revenue per piece, while average daily volume fell 3.3 per cent [2][3]. One page sets out a pricing rule, and the other shows what that rule does when volume falls.

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

**My read starts with the word the agency used in March.** It had "steadfastly avoided surcharges", it said then, and its 8 per cent transport rise was "less than one-third of what our competitors charge for fuel alone" [4]. That rise was, in the agency's own words, a "bridge to a permanent mechanism" [4]. The bridge now ends at midnight Central time on 17 January 2027 [4]. What replaces it has only a month attached: the agency's own question sheet answers "January 2027" and gives no day [1].

That gap matters because of what gets swapped. A flat charge on base postage is a number a shipper can put in a budget and defend in a talk with a carrier. A share reposted every week is a number that comes from the carrier, three to five days before it is charged on diesel products and three to four days before it is charged on jet fuel products [1]. No buyer signs it.

**UPS then makes the case for wanting such a rule.** Its U.S. domestic revenue reached $14,865 million against $14,220 million a year earlier, and the release gives the 6.1 per cent lift in revenue per piece the credit [2]. On the call, finance chief Brian Dykes split that gain: about half came from base rates and customer mix, and "higher fuel rates made up the remainder" [3]. The half a buyer agrees to was matched, roughly, by the half that no one does.

The Postmaster General calls this trade deliberate. Revenue rising while volume falls "has been true for 14 out of 16 quarters", David Steiner said in August, and "we have more price to take in the marketplace" [5]. A pattern he dates to those sixteen quarters now gets a part that moves on its own.

Two details decide where the risk lands. The share applies to base postage only, not to extra services or nonstandard fees, so the part of a bill that floats is smaller than the part of postage that floats [1]. And signed contracts "may have customized language" on whether the fee applies at all, which puts the carve-out back in the room where the base rate was agreed [1].

## By the numbers

- **8% — The bridge being replaced:** charged on base postage for Priority Mail Express, Priority Mail, USPS Ground Advantage and Parcel Select from 26 April 2026 until midnight Central time on 17 January 2027 [4].
- **11.5% — USPS revenue per piece:** the June quarter's shipping revenue of $8,250 million on 1,554 million pieces against $7,662 million on 1,609 million a year earlier, or about 11.5 per cent more per piece. The release gives the two ends, not the ratio [6].
- **6.1% — UPS revenue per piece:** U.S. domestic growth in the third quarter, won on a 3.3 per cent fall in average daily volume, with fuel behind about half of it [2][3].
- **3 to 5 days — Notice window:** how far ahead the agency says the diesel figure will be posted, and three to four days for jet fuel products [1].

## What I'd watch:

- **The first number:** the question sheet names a month, the charge it replaces ends on a stated date, so the two notices overlap for a stretch of January that no document fixes [1][4].
- **The carve-out:** "NSAs may have customized language" is, for a large shipper, the one part of this decision still open in a contract [1].
- **The postage-due answer:** mail bearing the prior week's share becomes postage due, per the agency, and testing of the change opens about two weeks before the price change date [1].
- **UPS's fourth quarter:** the carrier guides U.S. revenue up 3 per cent on a slightly lower daily volume, with a little more than 100 customers behind about 80 per cent of the seasonal spike [3]. A second quarter of price outrunning volume would make the pattern a policy, not a peak-season quirk.

## The catch

**What I keep circling is the base, not the rate.** The first percentage is unpublished, and a flat charge is not clearly bigger or smaller than a weekly share of base postage, so a January bill could move either way at the start [1]. The base is narrow too: signature, insurance and nonstandard handling sit outside the share, so a heavy add-on mix dilutes it [1].

The truckload form of this tool is a formula rather than a share, built from a base peg, an assumed miles per gallon figure and a rate table. That is why [the per-mile fuel tables](/fuel-surcharge-peg-mpg-audit/) can move a shipper's bill while the diesel price stands still. Parcel is reaching the same place from the other side: a number the carrier posts, and a shipper who reads it days before it is charged.

For an operations team the residue is a weekly task inside a monthly process. A rate card that changes four or five times a month has to show up in label software, in invoice audit and in any customer promise priced off postage [1].

<!-- tldr -->

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** The Postal Service is swapping a flat 8 per cent transport rise for a fuel surcharge charged as a share of base postage and updated weekly from January 2027, in the same quarter UPS booked a 6.1 per cent gain in revenue per piece as daily volume fell 3.3 per cent.
- **Why It Matters:** The line that moves a parcel budget is now the one nobody bargains over. Base rates get agreed in a contract; the share arrives from the carrier days before it applies, and it applies to both commercial and retail parcels.
- **What I'd Watch:** The small print that decides how much of a bill floats.
  - **The first share:** the number itself, which nobody outside the agency has seen and which decides whether January is a rise or a fall against the 8 per cent bridge.
  - **The contract carve-out:** signed deals may carry customized language, so the biggest shippers may sit outside the fee.
  - **The postage-due rule:** mail bearing the prior week's share is short-paid, which turns a pricing change into a daily mailroom check.
- **The Catch:** The share covers base postage only, so add-on services stay outside it, and because the first number is unpublished the January change is not automatically a rise. What is certain is the weekly cadence, not the direction.
