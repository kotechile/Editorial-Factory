The spare battery is the wrong buy, because the hardware in every box on this page is identical and the packs are the only thing that changes. EGO (the cordless outdoor power brand) sells its 24-inch two-stage blower in two kits that sit $50 apart as of 2026-10-25: $1,699 with two 10Ah packs and $1,649 with two 7.5Ah packs [1][2]. That $50 buys 280 more watt-hours, while a single spare 10Ah pack lists at $549.99, about $550, on its own [9]. Buy the bigger box and skip the spare.

| EGO Power+ SNT2405, 24-inch two-stage | $1,649 | Two 7.5Ah packs, 840 Wh, same machine [1][5] |

## The big picture:

My read starts with how two purchases are being sold as one. The blower is a steel auger, an impeller and a pair of brushless motors; the battery is a separate product with its own price, and the maker's headline capacity moves with whichever box you buy. EGO says the same 24-inch machine clears up to an 18-car driveway on the 7.5Ah packs and 24 cars on the 10Ah packs [1][3][4].

The reviewer who took both packages apart said the quiet part out loud: "The performance and features are the same, with the only difference being run time" [5]. That is not a knock on the machine. It is the buying instruction, because it says energy is the only variable left.

## By the numbers

- **46.7 Wh — one car-driveway, either box:** 840 Wh (2 × 56 V × 7.5 Ah) divided by 18 cars is 46.7, and 1,120 Wh (2 × 56 V × 10 Ah) divided by 24 cars is 46.7. EGO's two claims are one claim divided by pack energy [1][2].
- **$0.18 — a watt-hour, bought as an upgrade:** 18 cents a watt-hour is what the step between kits costs. The same watt-hour bought later as a pack runs $0.98, since 560 Wh of spare capacity sells for about 5.5 times that price [2][9].
- **$2.87 — a watt-hour, bought as a Toro:** the Toro's 540 Wh works out to $2.87 per watt-hour in the box, against $1.52 for the EGO 10Ah kit and $1.96 for the 7.5Ah kit [1][2][7][8].

## What I'd watch:

Independent testing is where a spec claim meets resistance. Three results here matter more than any spec block.

- **Throw distance:** EGO publishes up to 50 feet [3]. The reviewer's sawdust went "over 30 feet," and he added his own caveat that wet sawdust never throws as far as snow [5]. Both facts belong together, because the second is why the first is not a lie.
- **Throughput:** the same test cleared 22 cubic feet, or 462 pounds of wet sawdust, in about four minutes [5]. That is the only hard throughput figure this desk could verify for any machine in this group, and it is a volume rather than a car count.
- **Power draw, implied:** Toro publishes 60 minutes of run time on 540 Wh, which works out to 540 watts of average draw. EGO's claimed 25 minutes on 840 Wh works out to 2,016 watts [6][8]. If both figures describe the same job the EGO is pulling 3.7 times the power, and if they do not, neither number tells you how much snow either machine moves.
- **The powder caveat:** Good Housekeeping notes that EGO's 18-car claim held in testing only with light, dry powder [6]. A snowbelt driveway in a wet February is the case the number does not cover.

## The catch

My read: the Toro is not cheaper by accident. It clears up to 20 car spaces in up to 10 inches of snow on the pack in the box [8], so its claim is written at a deeper snow than EGO's and in a unit nobody publishes a size for. EGO's unit converts, once, to about 1,560 square feet at 18 cars [6]. Toro's 20 spaces cannot be set against that without assuming both makers mean the same car. Do not rank the car counts; rank the energy.

What the Toro buys with the gap is flexibility. It has three battery ports instead of two, and Toro's own listed spare is a 7.5Ah pack at $509.29 [8][10], so a second pack extends a run rather than ending it. It also carries 25.7 more pounds than the EGO, which is the difference you feel on a slope at the end of a driveway.

There is a model-number trap too. EGO's newer 24-inch kit, the SNT2425, lists at $1,749 for the same 7.5Ah pack tier that the SNT2405 sells at $1,649 [1][12]. That premium is a series name, not capacity. And an owner who already has EGO 10Ah packs should skip every kit here: the bare 24-inch tool is $939 [11].

EGO covers the tool for 5 years and the packs for 3; Toro covers its machine for 3 [3][8]. On a purchase this size that gap is worth reading before the price.

## Verdict

Buy the EGO Power+ SNT2406 if you are buying packs and machine together, because the step to the 10Ah box is the cheapest energy in the class and the only box that reaches EGO's own 24-car figure [2][4]. Buy the Toro Power Max e24 instead if you already own Toro 60V packs or you want three on-board ports and local dealer service, and accept that the included battery is the smallest of the three [7][8]. Skip both if EGO packs are already in your garage, because the bare tool does the same work without them [11].

## What we did not test

This review is built from manufacturer and authorized-dealer price pages, one independent measured test of the EGO SNT2406 in wet sawdust, and a magazine review that restates the maker's capacity claim with its own caveat. The units were not handled here, no snow was cleared, and every runtime, throw distance, capacity and price figure belongs to the maker, the dealer or the tester named beside it in the sources. If you buy, run your own driveway size and storm depth through the [true cost of ownership calculator](/calculators/true-cost-of-ownership/), and read the rest of the [outdoor power and snow coverage](/categories/outdoor-power-equipment/) before the first storm.

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** the 24-inch battery two-stage is now one machine sold in several battery boxes, and the capacity claim moves with the box rather than the hardware [1][3][4].
- **Why It Matters:** a $50 step between EGO kits buys 280 Wh, while buying that energy later as a spare pack costs $549.99 — so the cheaper box is the expensive decision [2][9].
- **What I'd Watch:** whether the makers ever publish the size of a "car space," because until they do, the headline capacity of every machine in this class is unfalsifiable.
  - **Measured throw:** the independent test reached just over 30 feet against a 50-foot claim [5].
  - **Snow depth:** Toro writes its claim at 10 inches, EGO at 8, which makes the two car counts incomparable [2][8].
- **The Catch:** prices are dated 2026-10-25, dealer stock moves, and nothing here was handled or run in snow.
