<!-- lead -->
Your vested options carry a deadline you probably never negotiated. Three months after the job ends, an incentive stock option (ISO) loses that label under Section 422. In practice, most employees get about 90 days to decide [4][5].

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

The strike price is the part everyone sees. The tax rules are the part that decides whether the exercise paid off.

Exercising an ISO adds nothing to regular taxable income. That is the whole point of the label.

But it can still trigger the alternative minimum tax (AMT) in the year of exercise. The reason: the gap between the price paid and the share value that day becomes a preference item [1][3].

I keep coming back to how quietly the rules stack. Section 422 carries two limits most holders meet too late.

The first is the three-month rule. Exercise past it and the option is no longer an ISO, so the spread is taxed as ordinary income [4].

The second caps how much stock can be granted as an ISO at all. The limit is $100,000 of value a year, and anything above it is not an ISO either [4].

Then there is the holding test. To keep the gain as capital rather than wages, the shares must clear two years from grant and one year from exercise [3]. An early sale turns the bargain into ordinary income.

## By the numbers

- **3 months — The ISO cutoff:** Past three months after the job ends, an option is no longer an ISO, so the spread is taxed as ordinary income [4].
- **26% — The AMT rate on the spread:** In tax year 2025 the AMT is 26% on the first $239,100, then 28%, minus $4,782 [2].
- **2 years — The holding test:** Shares must be held two years from grant and one year from exercise to keep capital-gain treatment [3].
- **0 — Taxable income at exercise:** Exercising an ISO adds nothing to regular income, which is why a tax bill months later can still surprise the holder [1][3].

<!-- tactical-insight -->

## What I'd watch:

What strikes me is that the rules do not care whether the equity is worth anything yet. The tax question lands in the year of the exercise, not the year of the exit.

- **The early sale:** Sell before the two marks and the bargain becomes wages, reported on the form the company files [3].
- **The AMT credit:** Tax paid on a spread that later falls can return as a credit in a year when regular tax tops tentative minimum tax. It is a slow refund few holders track [2].
- **The three-month reset:** Leaving does not just start a countdown. It can turn ISO grants into ordinary-income options, so the same shares carry a different tax bill by calendar [4][5].
- **The paper trail:** The adjustment the agency wants on Form 6251 line 2i is the exercise spread. The form that ties to it is Form 3921 [3].

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->

## The catch

On paper the AMT item is not always a cash bill. It raises tentative minimum tax, and only the excess over regular tax is owed. A holder with a low regular-tax base can owe; one with a high base may not.

I could be wrong, but the honest read is that 90 days is convention, not law. Carta reports that employees typically have about 90 days to decide whether to exercise after leaving [5].

The statute is the three-month rule that strips ISO status [4]. The two numbers rhyme, but they are not the same rule.

The other constraint is liquidity. Exercising means paying the strike and the tax at once, often on shares that cannot be sold for years.

<!-- tldr -->

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** The choice to exercise a vested option gets priced against the strike price, when the real cost is the tax. That means the AMT on the ISO spread and the three-month cutoff that ends ISO status after the job ends.
- **Why It Matters:** A holder can owe tax in the year of exercise on shares that cannot yet be sold. A late exercise or an early sale turns a capital gain into ordinary income.
- **What I'd watch:** Whether holders are pricing the exercise against the calendar, not the headline exit.
  - **The three-month rule:** ISO status ends three months after the job ends [4].
  - **The $100,000 cap:** ISO grants are capped at $100,000 of stock value a year [4].
  - **The holding test:** Two years from grant and one year from exercise keep the gain as capital [3].
- **The Catch:** The AMT item only bites when tentative minimum tax tops regular tax, and the 90-day window in the platform data is convention, not law.
