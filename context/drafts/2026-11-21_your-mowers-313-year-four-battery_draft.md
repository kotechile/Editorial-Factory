Husqvarna sells its 420 iQ robot mower for $2,599.99 in a current sale [6]. Its battery "typically lasts between 2 and 5 years" [1]. One genuine battery kit for the recent generations lists at $313.36 [5]. The sticker price is the machine. The upkeep is a separate bill nobody advertises.

My read of the two spec pages: four years of owning a robot mower costs a quarter of the machine price again in blades and battery — more if the battery dies twice inside the window. Almost every payback comparison online leaves that line out.

## At a glance

A robot mower's upkeep bill is published by the maker, not buried: blades wear out in weeks and the battery in years. Budgeting for both before you order turns one $2,599.99 purchase into a four-year figure worth dividing against your lawn contract.

<!-- tension -->
## The clocks nobody prices

The payback math that sells robot mowers is one division: machine price against the lawn crew's annual bill. I've run that division with regional season lengths, and it holds as far as it goes. What it leaves out is the wear schedule the maker prints in plain sight.

The blade clock is the fast one. Husqvarna's own guidance prints the full sentence: "Husqvarna Long Life Safety Blades should be replaced every 6-8 weeks and Husqvarna Endurance Safety Blades every 3-4 months" [2]. On a 28-week mowing season, that means two to four blade changes a year. The Endurance six-pack lists at $26.99 [3]. The Classic pack lists at $64.99 [4].

The battery clock is the slow one, and it lands inside the years you own the machine anyway. Husqvarna's support article puts it plainly: "The battery life of Automower® typically lasts between 2 and 5 years" [1]. A buyer who keeps the machine four years should expect to buy one battery, maybe two on daily duty. The kit for the 430X/440/450X generation lists at $313.36 at one mower-parts dealer — currently sold out there, which is itself a signal about demand [5].

## By the numbers

- **$313.36 battery kit:** One genuine replacement for the 430X/440/450X generation, listed while marked sold out [5].
- **6-8 weeks:** The replacement window Husqvarna publishes for its Long Life blades [2].
- **$26.99 blade six-pack:** The Endurance pack's list price, against $64.99 for the Classic pack [4].

## Where this bites

The dealers closest to these machines treat the battery as planned service, not a failure. I would read the app's own counters before I trust any second-hand robot mower. Husqvarna's support article tells owners to have a dealer swap the battery, and points at the app's statistics screen — running distance, charging cycles — as the way to watch for the early sign: the mower "returning to the charging station too often" [1]. That is a wear signal you can read at home, years before the machine stops on the lawn.

The blade side costs less but is easier to get wrong. Each blade change wants new screws, because Husqvarna says to swap them every time and never reuse them [2]. The Endurance pack's pitch is "more than twice the lifetime compared to classic blades" [3], which is why the cheaper-looking pack wins on anything but the lightest duty.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->
## The catch

I read that 2-to-5 band as the maker saying one thing clearly: no one can quote your battery's death year in advance. The battery band is the maker's own spread across duty cycles — a small lawn mowed twice weekly ages a battery far slower than a one-acre lot mowed daily — so one owner buys a battery in year four and another never does [1]. The blade windows are product-specific too: 6-8 weeks belongs to the Long Life blade, 3-4 months to the Endurance blade, and gravel or sand in the lawn shortens both [2]. So divide your own mower's duty into those windows rather than quoting anyone's four-year total. A middle case with three or four blade packs a year lands near $330 of blades. Add one battery kit inside those four years and the upkeep total runs near $640 to $745 against a $2,599.99 machine. That is the line the shopping-guide articles never print, and it is the one the year-four battery bill makes too big to ignore.
