The cart charges $199.00 for the RISION (the phone thermal-camera brand) Magic Pro as of 2026-11-22, and $109.00 for the RISION Magic, its entry sibling [1]. The Thermal Master P3 Lite closes the set at $239.00 [2]. All three plug into a phone's universal serial bus (USB-C) port and read surface heat with an infrared (IR) sensor. The count that separates them is not printed on the front of any box.

<!-- tldr -->
## At a glance

Three phone-plug thermal cameras advertise upscaled headline resolutions, but the cart buys only two detectors: 19,200 native pixels at the floor, 49,152 one $90 step up. Every dollar above that step buys kit, a Lightning adapter and shipping weeks, not sensing.

<!-- tension -->
## Why the headline resolution is the decoy spec

My read of the spec war is simple: ignore every resolution on the front of the box, and buy the detector instead.

1. **The headlines are software.** The Magic advertises 320×240 [1]. The Magic Pro advertises 512×384 [1]. The P3 Lite advertises "512 × 384 Razor-Sharp X³ IR resolution" [2]. All three are upscales over smaller detectors. RISION's own pages concede it: "native 160×120 resolution super-resolved to 320×240" [1]. Thermal Master's spec table names a "VOx 256×192 @12μm" detector [2].

2. **The $90 step is the hardware ladder.** A 160×120 detector holds 19,200 pixels [1]. A 256×192 detector holds 49,152 — 2.56 times the count [1]. On a wall bay between 16-inch-on-center studs, the bigger detector gives each tile about 2.6 vertical inches of wall against 3.6. Both makers rate the same 40 mK sensitivity floor, per their own pages [1], so the sharper image is a pixel count, not a better core.

3. **The money above the step buys nothing a wall can see.** The Magic Pro and the P3 Lite share the same 256×192 detector class [2]. Both claim ±2°C accuracy [3]. The differences are kit and timing. The P3 Lite's macro lens is a separate $50.00 variant [2]. Its shells ship in December batches, per the maker's own notice [2].

## Where each camera wins

My read of the set: the Magic Pro is the unit a first-time scanner should buy, and the other two serve readers who already know their own answer. The case is arithmetic. It is the cheapest camera here with a 49,152-pixel detector [1]. It ships today [1]. It sits $40.00 under the top unit's figure while doing the same sensing job [2]. One owner on the Magic Pro's page puts the use case plainly: "Find the cold spots on your house. So simple to use, download app, plug it in, see the images." [4]

The Magic wins on temperature span. Its 600-degree span [1] covers a cold baseboard and a hot breaker lug with room to spare. The catch is honest: 120 tiles across the detector can put a cold corner and the stud beside it in one tile. A reader who needs to know where a leak line runs will pay for the step after one frustrating scan.

The P3 Lite wins two narrow cases. A reader on a pre-2018 Lightning iPhone: its box includes a USB-C to Lightning adapter [2], while both RISION units need a Type-C port. And a reader who wants the $50.00 macro lens for circuit-board work and can wait for December shipping [2]. For a wall scan, neither applies.

## The catch

The price in the cart is not always the price on the page. RISION's rendered Magic page shows a sale display of $119.00, and its Magic Pro page $229.00 [1]. The cart record both pages reach charges less, which matches the maker's own November 19 announcement of a sale running through November 30 [5]. That deadline is a real cliff. Once it closes, the rendered banner figures become the honest expectation, the step between the two RISION units widens, and this verdict weakens a grade. A buyer who wants these two at today's figures should not shop the set in December.

What I find hard to square is that every number separating the three is one the makers rate and nobody has measured per unit. The sensitivity, the accuracy, the temperature spans — all manufacturer claims [3]. No independent measured review of these specific units was fetchable on the day this set was priced. The makers' own guidance concedes the instrument's limit: a thermal image shows surface patterns, not a confirmed diagnosis. This comparison can rank the detectors, because the detectors are printed. It cannot rank the images, because no hand has held the cameras.

## What we did not test

This review is built from three manufacturer specification pages, two cart records and one vendor announcement. The desk did not run, hold or measure any of the cameras — no unit was handled, no wall was scanned, and every sensitivity, accuracy and range figure is a manufacturer claim, labelled as such above. The RISION cart figures were read on the day this set was priced and are sale-window figures promised through the end of November; the rendered page banners differ and may revert. The P3 Lite's December shipping notice is the maker's own, and its macro lens is priced separately.
