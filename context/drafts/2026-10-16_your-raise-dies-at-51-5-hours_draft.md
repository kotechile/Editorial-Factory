<!-- lead -->
Your raise dies at about 51.5 hours a week. That is the point where the median computer and information systems manager — $175,140 a year, or $84.20 an hour [1] — drops back to what the median software developer already earns at 40 hours: $65.38 an hour, or $135,980 a year [2]. The same federal survey says 65% of the manager job works more than 40 hours a week [1].

<!-- tension -->

## The big picture:

The part I keep circling: the pay table and the work-week table sit on the same page. Only one of them sets the price.

O*NET (the Occupational Information Network) OnLine is the Labor Department's job database. It prints a median hourly wage for each job [1][2]. That hourly figure is the yearly median spread over 2,080 hours: 40 hours a week, 52 weeks a year.

The same page holds what people in the job said about a typical week. Managers in computer and information systems work split 65% "more than 40 hours" against 33% "40 hours" [1]. Software developers answer 40 hours nine times out of ten [2].

Then the law stops paying for the rest. The Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) sets a salary floor at 29 CFR § 541.600: pay an employee a salary of $684 a week or more, and the job can be exempt from overtime [4].

The floor is only half of it. The executive test at 29 CFR § 541.100 also asks that the main duty is management, and that the role directs two or more workers [5]. Meet both, and hour 41 costs the employer nothing.

## By the numbers

- **$175,140 — Manager median:** The 2025 median pay for computer and information systems managers, shown as $84.20 an hour because the yearly figure is divided by 2,080 hours [1].
- **65% — Managers over 40 hours:** The share of that job reporting a typical week longer than 40 hours. Among general and operations managers, 70% say the same [1][3].
- **$65.38 — Developer rate:** The 2025 median hourly wage for software developers, and 90% of that job reports a 40-hour week [2].
- **51.5 hours — Break-even:** The point where the manager median stops beating the developer rate: 175,140 ÷ (52 × 65.38) [1][2].

<!-- tactical-insight -->

## What I'd watch:

I have been reading pay tables next to offer letters this week. The hours are the line nobody prices.

- **The band premium:** The manager median is 1.288 times the developer median, or about $39,160 a year more [1][2]. That gap is the whole raise. The extra hours have to fit inside it.
- **The second job:** General and operations managers show the same split, at $50.85 an hour with 70% over 40 hours [3]. The 40-hour week is a guess built into the table, not a fact about the job.
- **The bonus slice:** Bonus, commission and restricted stock unit (RSU) pay counts as supplemental wages. It is withheld at a flat 22% up to $1 million a year, then 37% [6]. Those last hours are withheld like a bonus, not like a salary step.
- **The state columns:** Each O*NET page carries median wages by state [1]. The national median is not a local one. The work-week share is national too.
- **The duties line:** I would want to know if the main duty is really management [5]. A salaried worker who still spends the week building is not exempt.

<!-- nuanced-takeaway -->

## The catch

My read: the math holds, but it covers less ground than it looks like it covers.

O*NET reports the share of people in a group, not hours per person [1]. So 51.5 hours is math on two medians. It is not a stopwatch on a real week. The manager band also buys scope, title pull and a different next job, and a rate per hour cannot price any of that.

The duties test matters as much as the salary floor [5]. A salaried worker whose main duty is not management can still be owed overtime. Pay above $684 a week does not switch that off by itself.

The medians describe jobs, not offers. One package carries a strike price, a vesting schedule and a bonus target. Those set the cash flow long before a rate per hour does [1][2].

## At a glance

- **The Big Shift:** The federal job database prices manager pay as if every week were 40 hours, while its own survey reports that 65% of computer and information systems managers work more than 40. Past the $684-a-week salary floor, US law stops paying for the extra hours.
- **Why It Matters:** The median manager earns $39,160 a year more than the median software developer, and that gap is gone at roughly 51.5 hours a week. An executive band can pay less per hour than the band it replaces.
- **What I'd Watch:**
  - **The band premium:** The distance between the two medians, 1.288 times or about $39,160 a year, is the raise the extra hours must justify [1][2].
  - **The duties test:** Whether the main duty is management and whether the role directs two or more workers. That is the second half of the exemption [5].
  - **The withholding rate:** How the bonus slice is paid, since supplemental wages are withheld at a flat 22% up to $1 million a year [6].
- **The Catch:** The survey reports shares, not hours, so 51.5 hours is math on medians rather than a real week. The band also buys scope and a different job market that a rate per hour cannot price.
