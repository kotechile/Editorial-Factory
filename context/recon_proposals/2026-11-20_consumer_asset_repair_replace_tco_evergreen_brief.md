## Arithmetic the article performs (recomputed from the rows above, not copied from a draft)

- Pooled premium per device: Individual $19.99/mo covers up to 3 products → $239.88/yr ÷ 3 = $79.96 per device-year = $6.66 per device-month. Each extra product adds $5.99/mo = $71.88/device-year, so per-device cost falls toward $71.88 as the pool grows: 4 devices $311.76/yr = $77.94 each; 5 devices $383.64/yr = $76.73 each; 6 devices $455.52/yr = $75.92 each.
- Family tier: $49.99/mo = $599.88/yr. It beats the Individual path only past 7.5 devices (599.88 ÷ 79.96); a 3-person household each owning 2 devices pays $99.98 per device-year on Family — 25 percent more than Individual pooling. Family is a big-household tier, not a household default.
- Battery-only risk (the documented leg): Apple's ladder $69–$99, midpoint $89, one swap per phone roughly every 2 years (an assumption the reader sets — no source states a swap rate) → 3 phones ≈ 1.5 swaps/yr ≈ $133.50/yr uninsured, or 1.5 kits ≈ $75/yr DIY. The plan's $239.88/yr with the battery free is 1.8x the uninsured battery spend and 3.2x the DIY spend.
- The plan's own add-on price reads the same figure back: $5.99/mo = $71.88/device-year ≈ one $69 out-of-warranty battery swap a year — Apple prices each additional device at about one battery service annually.
- Break-even: the plan pays when the household's expected uninsured accidental-damage spend exceeds $239.88/yr minus the $29–$99 fee it still pays per event. The uninsured accidental-damage ladder is exactly the number Apple does not publish to a non-browser fetch (the live repair page renders "Pricing failed to load"), so the reader's own quote is the variable the honest article names instead of inventing.
- Coverage cap: two theft-and-loss claims a year (AppleCare+) or three total (AppleCare One Individual) — an insurance contract with a ceiling; a self-insurer's ceiling is the household's own savings, but pays no premium in a year with zero events.
