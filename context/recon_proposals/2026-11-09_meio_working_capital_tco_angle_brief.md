**Emergent Collision Point:** The carrying-cost model every inventory policy is built on assumes the goods lose value while they sit. The 20-30%-a-year convention is 5-7.5% a quarter, and against a purchased input whose contract price rose 80%, then 40%, then about 20% a quarter, the hedge earns several times its own storage bill — so the same arithmetic that says "cut the buffer" says "buy forward" once the input's own price curve enters the formula. Neither source says this: Apple reports a balance sheet and TrendForce reports a price forecast, and the decision they collide on belongs to the reader.

**Hook:** Apple finished its fiscal year holding $14.5 billion of stock, two and a half times the year before, and added $32.7 billion of purchase commitments in thirteen weeks — and its product margin still rose, because in a market where memory reprices 20% a quarter the stock you already own is the cheapest memory you will buy all year.

**Tension:** The hedge decays on a schedule the buyer can see. Apple guided holiday-quarter gross margin to 45-46% from 47.9% and said the benefit of carry-in inventory declines "over time" as purchase prices keep rising, so the working-capital win is a bridge of about one quarter, not a strategy. Meanwhile the whole manufacturing base is doing the same thing at once — ISM's customers' inventories index has read 41.6, "too low", for 25 consecutive months while its prices index has risen for 25 — so the pre-buy is rational per buyer and self-reinforcing in aggregate, and it becomes excess stock at peak prices the moment end demand cracks.
