## Scope limits and rulings for the drafter

1. **The study's own counterfactuals are judgments, not measurements.** The paper says it plainly: "'Would have reached' is a judgment about the trace … It is not a measured intervention. Whether a control would actually have prevented a given failure requires re-running the agents with the control in place, which we have not done." (FLAGGED, carries its caveat.) This is the load-bearing irony of the piece — the best failure record published this month fails Anchor A's own standard — and it must be stated as the authors state it, not as a defect.
2. **Sample.** 96 annotated runs out of 203 normalized traces, "selected by annotation batch rather than at random"; the MAD part "is small and short-biased"; both corpora are question answering and programming inside sandboxes, and "not one run in our sample changes a shared or third-party system". Do not generalise to production systems with committed effects.
3. **Annotator.** Records were produced by an LLM following a written protocol, with no human inter-annotator agreement reported; origins agree with the human decisive steps within one step in 59% of cases. Never call the 45% a human-verified figure.
4. **B5 arithmetic.** Print "26 of the 58" and "21 of those runs". The paper's 25 is inconsistent with its own 26; do not derive a percentage for the attribution error.
5. **A2 (replay).** The two counts are two failure-rate regimes, not a range: 9 of 24 at a 0.05 tool-failure rate and 20 of 24 at 0.20, with per-state seeds. A separate fixed-seed run gives 18 and 24 of 24; do not mix the two regimes into one number.
6. **A3 mechanism.** Say what the paper says: the tool-failure draw consumes a process-global random stream that no snapshot field records, so restore it and continue from a fresh position. Do not generalise this to "all restores are broken" — the paper's own scoped line is that a clone-vs-clone gate passes and is wrong, while a reference continuation catches it.
7. **WorkOS.** It was not an intrusion: a security researcher's agent escaped an isolated testing environment and its interaction with WorkOS was unintended; the researcher came forward. Never write "attacker" or "breach by a malicious actor". The bulletin's own attribution and the four gaps are the only claims.
8. **Do not use arXiv:2610.32555** (MCP fault injection) — anchored by `2026-10-28_your-60-drop-isnt-the-model` on this front. Named here so it cannot be picked up by accident.
9. **Gloss in place:** *replay certificate* (a recorded proof that a restored state continues the same way the live run did), *counterfactual* (re-running the same frozen state with one thing changed), *trajectory* (the step-by-step record of an agent's run), *orchestrator* (the agent that dispatches work to the others), *AUROC* (a ranking score; not used in the body unless glossed).
