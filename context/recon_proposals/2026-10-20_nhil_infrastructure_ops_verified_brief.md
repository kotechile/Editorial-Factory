## Discrepancies and traps the drafter must not walk into
- **"Up to 40%" is the vendor's marketing ceiling; the measured result is 37.1%.** Use 140 → 192 and +37.1% for the GPUs and reserve "up to 40%" for a direct quote. Do not write "40% more GPUs" as the finding.
- **The 49.2% is throughput, not power and not efficiency of the facility.** It is aggregate tokens/s on a fixed provisioned-power denominator. Never rephrase as "49% less energy" or "49% more efficient".
- **Mean GPU power went UP, not down.** A controller that fits more GPUs in the same budget raises draw inside the budget (166.2 → 198.9 kW measured). Any sentence implying the site drew less power is wrong.
- **P99 latency got worse.** Omit the 17% and the piece is dishonest; include it and the trade-off is visible.
- **The evaluation is one workload on one campus, in a vendor's own joint test.** No generalization to a typical enterprise site.
- **HPE's 10,000 actions are campus and branch networking,** not computational-load protection settings; the article may use them only as evidence that unattended change is routine at volume, with that scope stated in the same sentence.
- **NERC has not said a GPU power cap is a "setting change" under R11.2.2.** The article says the operator has to answer that question, not that NERC has.
- **The threshold change direction:** NERC raised it (20 MW/60 kV → 50 MW/100 kV) in response to comments, which makes the in-scope population smaller, not larger. Do not write that the rule was tightened downward.
- **"Ten calendar years" is between transmittals, not between verifications** — a change resets the 180-day clock regardless.
- **Do not imply the standards are in force.** Board adoption is Q4 2026, then a FERC filing; the July 16 order set 2026-12-31 as the filing deadline.
