## Flags carried into drafting

- **FLAGGED — California runs three clocks, not one.** § 678(a)(1) sets a 45-day deadline before expiration for either an offer of renewal or a notice of nonrenewal; § 678(c)(1) sets the 75-day deadline for the nonrenewal notice itself; § 678(c)(2) sets the 75-day continuation. The drafter must keep 45 and 75 in their own sentences and must never present 75 days as "the notice period for everything".
- **FLAGGED — "cancellation" and "nonrenewal" are different events in all three states.** Florida carves nonpayment out at ten days; California's cancellation bar (§ 676) applies to mid-term cancellation and does not govern expiry; Washington's 60 days is a cancellation clock. Do not total or average the three states' figures.
- **FLAGGED — a missed clock is time, not free coverage.** Florida keeps the policy at the same premium only until replacement coverage begins; California's continuation is 75 days from mailing. The article must not imply an indefinite extension.
- **No figure in this article is a market condition.** There is no premium, loss ratio or claim count in the evidence set, so the article carries no such number; the only figures are statutory periods.
