**Archetype:** evergreen

**Decision the reader is facing:** When a model-written agent workflow fails partway through — after paid calls have already committed — what is the team allowed to change in the repair: redeploy the script and re-pay every call that already succeeded, edit a committed payload in place and let replay return the replaced artifact, or gate the rewrite at the call identity so the frozen prefix replays for free and only new work is spent. It bites the first time a production run dies on call 4 of a 6-way fanout and the natural fix ("show the model the error and let it rewrite") re-invoices calls 1–3, and again the first time someone edits a prompt or seed in place and the replay silently serves the old artifact.
**Durability:** The two failure modes are properties of replay semantics, not of a release: a durable engine re-executes recorded history and does not compare activity payloads, so "pay again for calls that succeeded" and "accept a recorded result whose payload changed" are true on every durable runtime until a payload check is added at the call boundary. The measured contract costs are **as of** arXiv:2611.06967v1 (4 Nov 2026), the documented engine limits are as of the vendors' own pages retrieved 2026-11-25 (Temporal Event History 51,200 Events / 50 MB; AWS Step Functions 90-day history retrieval and its exactly-once/at-least-once split), and the Worker-Versioning removal date is as of the Temporal versioning page as of 2026-11-25. The exact percentages will drift with the study's task mix; the rule — identity the committed call, freeze the prefix, digest the payload, ledger the occurrence, fail closed on an unresolved attempt — does not.

**Thesis:** A durable engine's replay guarantees history, not payload, so the two cheap repairs both lose money in a fixed direction — the redeploy re-pays every committed call, the in-place edit silently returns a replaced artifact — and the fix is a contract, not a habit: give each capability call a structural identity (callsite, fanout position, occurrence count), freeze committed identities into a prefix, reject a changed committed payload at a static gate before a container starts, digest payloads at runtime, ledger occurrences, and fail closed on an unresolved crash-window attempt — 22 gate-passed repairs then replayed 258 committed identities with no duplicate, and a live six-way fanout paid for seven calls instead of twelve.
