## Gate rulings carried to the drafter
1. **Never format a diffusion reading as a percentage.** Warehousing Capacity 43.5 means capacity is contracting; Inventory Costs 88.8 means holding costs are rising fast. Writing "43.5% of capacity" or "88.8% more expensive" is a factual error.
2. **Never say vacancy is at a record.** Flexe says vacancy kept climbing and sits above 7.4% nationally, and CoStar expects it to peak in early 2027. "Record vacancy" is not in the source.
3. **Do not compare $14.14 with the survey's 43.5.** One is a price per pallet for short-term space; the other is a diffusion reading. They may be described side by side, never subtracted, divided or plotted on one axis.
4. **Name Flexe's commercial interest once, plainly.** It is a marketplace publishing an index of its own market; the figures are transaction data and the caveat is the publisher, not the arithmetic.
5. **Do not use diesel, and do not use customers' inventories.** Diesel at about $6.20/gallon has anchored two prior articles on this front and $6.352 is the same series; ISM's customers'-inventories 41.6 was the load-bearing leg of this vertical's 2026-10-19 piece.
6. **No hands-on claim.** This desk has no warehouse, no shipper panel and no index of its own; "we tested", "our data shows" and "in our experience" are barred.
7. **The synthesis margin is the argument, not a market call.** The article's claim is that committed and flexible capacity now clear at different prices and that an OTIF target buys the dearer one; it may not predict where prices go.
8. **Attribute the CoStar expectations to CoStar via Flexe**, not to Flexe's own forecast, and do not present a forecast as a measurement.
