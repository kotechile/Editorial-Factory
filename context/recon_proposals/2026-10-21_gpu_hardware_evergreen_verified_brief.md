## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Two different baselines, never one number.** The Joule Point (rows 1-3) is a fraction of a card's **rated** power. The calibrated setpoint (rows 5, 7) is a fraction of a lane's **natural draw** under load. 43-46% of rated and 80% of natural draw are not the same fraction and must never be presented as one. State each with its own base.
2. **Scope the big numbers to mixture-of-experts serving.** Row 9 caps the claim at roughly a fifth of the gain on a dense model. Do not extend the 20.4% or 22.4% to a dense stack.
3. **The vendor profile is the counter-example, not the recipe.** Row 6.
4. **The gotcha is latency, not energy.** Row 7 is the one actionable threshold: the cap's target is a latency cliff, and a cap can hold throughput while breaking the tail.
5. **The money is the small half of the argument.** Row 14 is authorised arithmetic only, and the article should say plainly that a node-year of power at the U.S. commercial average is a rounding error against the hardware — the reason to cap is capacity at fixed power, not the bill.
6. **Attribution read from the pages:** Joule Point — Apartsin & Aperstein, submitted 23 Aug 2026. Phase-decoupled control — Kim, Yoo, Ryu, Ha, Lee & Ryu (Xenoscube), 10 Sep 2026, arXiv:2609.11133v1. PALS — Hankendi, Shahout, Yu & Coskun, submitted 20 May 2026.
7. **No dense-model, training or multi-node claim.** Row 4 is the papers' own framing and may be attributed as such; it is not a measured result of either paper.
