# Angle Brief: enterprise_build_vs_buy — 2026-11-11
**Angle Type:** Single-Signal
**Winner:** Your ticket bill falls 44x above 64 an hour (working title; final headline scored at drafting)
**Scores:** N=8.8 A=9.2 S=9.0 → Composite=9.0
**Hook:** Two Amazon engineers moved their internal ticketing system's 20-label classification off Claude Sonnet 4.6 and onto a 4-billion-parameter decision model they run themselves, and published both bills: $0.0222 a ticket against about $0.0005, or about $22,200 per million tickets against about $500. The cheap side of that comparison is a rented GPU-hour at $1.408, so the 44x is not a price. It is arithmetic on how full the box is.
**Tension:** A per-token bill grows with demand; an instance-hour bill does not — it idles, at full price, all night. The swap therefore moves a variable cost onto a fixed one and hands the buyer a utilisation forecast in place of a quote. Break-even against Sonnet is about 63 tickets an hour on one `ml.g5.xlarge`; the run's own published $500 per million works out to roughly 2,800 tickets an hour, about half of the 5,600 one instance sustains at 640 ms a call. So the number that decides whether to build is not the discount, it is the floor: about $12,300 a year for one box that must never be idle for long, plus the accuracy you gave up to get a model that answers a closed question instead of writing one.
**Target reader:** eng_leader
**Single claim to defend:** The build side of an agent's per-decision spend is now priced in instance-hours, which turns a 44x token saving into a utilisation bet the buyer can compute before committing — the saving is real above about 63 decisions an hour on one box, and the risk that eats it is idle capacity, not model quality.
