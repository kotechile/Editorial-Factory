**Signal A (Anchor 1) — the physical calendar:** the Vincent Thomas Bridge, the road link between San Pedro and Terminal Island, closed in both directions at 12:01 a.m. on 7 November 2026 for a deck replacement Caltrans expects to run 16 months, to 1 March 2028, a $753 million project (https://portoflosangeles.org/community/vtb-deck-replacement). The Port of Los Angeles states on the same page that only **8%** of port-complex drayage truck traffic uses the bridge, that every Los Angeles terminal stays open with "no expected impact to port operations, cargo terminal productivity, flow or capacity", and that rail cargo is unaffected. Two weeks before the closure the Port of Long Beach began evening and Saturday gates, "a programme of six weeks excluding Thanksgiving week", run with the terminal operators, the ILWU and the Harbor Trucking Association (https://container-mag.com/update/port-of-long-beach-sets-all-time-monthly-record-with-997-177-mva7qo9n). The complex it is mitigating was at a record: Long Beach moved 997,177 TEU in September, up 25%, and Los Angeles 1,042,652 TEU, up 18.07%, for a combined record 2.04 million TEU (https://gcaptain.com/strike-threat-at-los-angeles-long-beach-ports-delayed-as-union-and-employers-extend-talks). The bridge carries about 53,000 vehicles a day, nearly 9% of them heavy trucks (https://abc7.com/post/vincent-thomas-bridge-san-pedro-closes-16-months-750-million-renovation-project-begins/20022363), and at least one Southern California drayage provider has already announced a congestion surcharge, with truckers estimating 45 to 60 extra minutes per Terminal Island trip at peak (https://www.falconfreight.ca/vincent-thomas-bridge-closure-drayage-surcharges).

**Signal B (Anchor 2) — the policy calendar:** on 10 November 2026 USTR published two Federal Register notices that moved the next US–China cliff from 9 November to early January: all 178 Section 301 product exclusions now run "through 11:59 p.m. eastern time on January 9, 2027" (91 FR 72610) (https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/10/2026-22925/notice-of-product-exclusion-extensions-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to-technology-transfer-intellectual-property-and-innovation), and the Section 301 maritime service fees and crane duties stay suspended "until January 10, 2027", with HTS headings 9903.91.12 through 9903.91.16 amended to replace "November 10, 2026" with the new date (91 FR 72608) (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-11-10/pdf/2026-22868.pdf). USTR took roughly 60 unique comments on its 26 October proposal to suspend again.

**Emergent Collision Point:** A cargo owner reads two calendars that were set by two agencies for two different reasons, and they do not overlap. The road is shut for roughly 70 weeks; the port's answer to it is six weeks of extra gate hours, ending around mid-December; the next date on which 178 exclusions and a vessel-fee suspension lapse is 9 January 2027, about four weeks after those gates close. Neither document says this, because neither agency is measuring the other's clock: Caltrans and the port measure terminal productivity (where the closure genuinely costs nothing) and USTR measures tariff liability (where the closure is invisible). The importer pays in the one quantity nobody published — the number of turns a drayage truck can make in the weeks when the extra gates are gone and the deadline is close.

**Hook:** The bridge that carries 53,000 vehicles a day is shut until March 2028, the port added six weeks of night and Saturday gates to absorb it, and the tariff deadline those boxes will be racing is 9 January — four weeks after the extra gates stop.

**Tension:** Who this empowers: the shipper who books the December gate slots and prices the drayage surcharge into the landed cost now; the forwarder who can show a customer why the January arrival is the expensive one. Who it threatens: the planner who reads "no expected impact" as a statement about their own cost, and the importer who treats the two-month truce extension as a reprieve rather than as a rescheduled cliff.
