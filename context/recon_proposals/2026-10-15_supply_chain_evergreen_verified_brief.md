## Status notes carried into drafting
- **Coordinate with the neighbouring evergreen, do not repeat it.** `published/2026-10-06_demurrage-invoice-30-day-clocks` (control_tower) covers 46 CFR Part 541 — the ocean billing clocks that decide whether a *terminal fee the shipper owes* is payable. This piece covers 49 CFR Part 370 / 49 CFR Part 1005 and 49 U.S.C. 14706, 11706 and 46 U.S.C. 30701 — the *cargo-loss* rules that decide whether money the carrier owes is recoverable. Do not cite 46 CFR Part 541, the Federal Maritime Commission, or any demurrage or detention figure anywhere in this draft.
- **The statute's floors are minimums, not maximums.** Both the motor and rail provisions say a carrier may not provide a *shorter* period. A bill of lading or tariff may allow longer, and a reader who files inside 9 months keeps the benefit of whatever longer window their contract gives. State this in the caveat section rather than writing "the deadline is 9 months" without qualification.
- **The three-day ocean notice is not a bar.** The statute says the failure to give it does not affect the right to sue within the year — so the one-year suit date is the hard limit and the three-day notice is a separate, softer step. Do not write that a late notice forfeits the claim.
- **The claim is not the liability finding.** Nothing in these rules decides who is at fault for the shortage or damage. They decide whether a demand exists and whether the carrier must act on it. Do not imply that filing a proper claim means getting paid.
- **Do not use COGSA as a bare acronym.** Write the Carriage of Goods by Sea Act, or "the sea-carrying statute"; if the acronym is needed, expand it at first use.
