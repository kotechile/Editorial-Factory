**Durability:** The claim is channel topology, not a model version: an output filter cannot inspect an inter-agent message or a memory write, so raising per-channel safety on the answer leaves those channels at the same rate forever, whatever the model. The measured rates are as of the three studies cited — the AgentLeak benchmark (arXiv:2602.11510v2, February 2026 vintage, five models over 1,000 scenarios), its counterfactual replay under transition-level admission (arXiv:2605.10481v1, 11 May 2026), and Vulcan's revocation-enforcement study (published 2026-09-11, 1,620 trials across five memory systems and nine models). The percentages re-index as models improve; the ordering (output-only < prompt-rule < transition/store-level) held in two independent studies on two different failure classes, which is the part that does not expire.
**De-dup:** `2026-10-14_gate-the-step-not-the-model` is this vertical's nearest prior artifact (7 days, same vertical): it argues that an agent's end-to-end reliability is set at the boundaries between steps because *errors* compound 11–14x, and it prices containment controls (circuit breakers 89%, isolation 64%, output gates catching 73% of silent degradation). This brief is not about fault propagation at all — it is about *constraint enforcement and its measurement surface*: which of a run's three channels a policy check can even see, what a per-channel exposure number does to an output-only filter's apparent success, and what transition-level admission costs in audit volume. It also differs from `2026-10-07_agent-memory-poisoning-write-path` (same vertical, 180-day window), which puts the control on the *write* path against attacker-authored content via provenance; here the offending record is the defender's own policy and the control point is admission at each transition plus per-channel measurement. Cross-vertical, `2026-10-14_one-approval-two-payments` (multi_agent_enterprise_fabric) covers effect identity at a checkpoint and `2026-10-19_your-agent-obeyed-the-data-left` (enterprise_ai_governance) covers permissions the actor already held — neither measures the gap between channels or prices the audit bill.
**Thesis:** A multi-agent system's safety number is usually measured on the one channel that does not leak: inter-agent messages leak the sensitive content at 68.8% against 27.2% on the final answer, so an output filter drives the visible rate from 27.2% to 2.9% while internal exposure stays at 68.8% and remains inadmissible at 32.8% forbidden exposure — the check has to sit at the transition (each message, memory write and tool argument), the metric has to be reported per channel, and the real bill for that is audit volume, 1,556 decisions becoming 19,916 over the same 4,979 traces.
