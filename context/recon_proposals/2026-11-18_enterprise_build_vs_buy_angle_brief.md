**Signal B (Anchor 2):** An operator's 2026-11-15 build of a self-hosted world map: one 139 GB PMTiles file behind a CDN, about $5/month in storage at 5x replication and $0.01/GB egress, a city view moving about 5 MB which he prices at $0.05 per 1,000 map loads against Mapbox's $5 per 1,000 — and an explicit list of what keeps it there (CORS referrer allow-list, large-object caching, a 10 kB/s throttle after 10 MB in one request, a monthly bandwidth cap), corroborated by the two price lists it rests on: Mapbox's own table ($5.00 per 1,000 loads in the 50,001-100,000 band, first 50,000 free, and "a map load includes unlimited Vector Tiles API and Raster Tiles API requests") and bunny.net's ($0.01/GB Europe and North America, $1 monthly minimum, no request fees) (https://bruceroettgers.eu/blog/protomaps/ and https://www.mapbox.com/pricing and https://bunny.net/pricing/)
**Emergent Collision Point:** The two stories are the same invoice read at two layers. Mapbox meters the **map load** — one priced unit that contains unlimited tile requests, so the vendor carries the tail of a heavy session and the buyer's bill is flat per visitor. OpenAI and Anthropic meter the **token** — a unit that is exactly the work, so every retry and every extra 100,000 tokens of context lands on the buyer, which is why Revolut's engineers were rationed by budget caps ("$1,000 a month, $10,000 a month with not so much ROI") until they bought GPUs. Self-hosting does not remove the tail; it transfers it. Both of this window's cheapest self-host stories therefore end in the same artefact — a list of caps the operator had to write himself — and neither source calls that list the cost, because it is labour, not a line on a price sheet.
**Hook:** Mapbox sells a map load, and its own price list says that one load "includes unlimited Vector Tiles API and Raster Tiles API requests" — a fixed price for unbounded work. That is the product a self-hoster stops buying, and the reason a 139 GB world map serves for $5 a month is that he then wrote the caps Mapbox used to write for him: throttles, referrer lists, bandwidth ceilings. Revolut did the same thing at the model layer, and its published bill is where the caps become visible.
**Tension:** The saving is real and the caps are not on the invoice. A team can cancel a $5-per-1,000-load contract and run the same map on $0.01-per-gigabyte delivery, and the month the traffic spikes is the month it learns what the vendor's margin was for. Revolut's numbers say the same at 56 GPUs: 2,000 engineers is roughly 36 people per card, the fleet is sized for concurrent sessions, and throughput per engineer roughly quadruples after 5 p.m. — which is the queue becoming visible only when the box is busy. The reader who will forward this is the one who has been asked to sign a build case whose savings line is real and whose labour line is empty.
**Target reader:** eng_leader
**Single claim to defend:** A metered unit is a bound, not a price: Mapbox's map load absorbs a heavy client, a token bill does not, and the 100x self-hosted saving is the buyer taking back the variance — which is only collectible by writing the same caps the vendor had already written, in context length, latency and bandwidth, and staffing whoever holds them.
