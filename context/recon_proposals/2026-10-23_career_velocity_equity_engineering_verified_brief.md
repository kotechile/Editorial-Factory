**Gate rulings for the drafter**
- The $2.8 billion figure is a **plan ceiling**, not money spent: "$2.1 billion as of August 31" plus "approximately $700 million" of additional actions. Cash actually paid in the quarter was $324 million. Never write "Oracle spent $2.8 billion laying people off".
- $1,971 million of $2,103 million is the plan's **accrued cost** (severance, contract termination, other exit costs), not payroll alone; the filing says segment costs are "primarily related to employee severance costs". Keep "primarily".
- Oracle's fiscal year ends 31 May. Write "fiscal 2026, ended 31 May" — never "in 2026" for the 21,000 figure.
- The cancellation clause and the 26-week cap are press-reported from an internal FAQ. Attribute ("reported by Business Insider from an internal FAQ the company has not published"), never "Oracle's plan states" and never as a quote from the plan document.
- Do not claim the cancelled stock was "days from vesting", "worth millions" or "a specific employee's loss" — no such figure is verified here. The verified comparison is structural: weeks of base against years of granted equity.
- The $300 million is **not** a funding round and ElevenLabs did not "raise" it: it is a purchase of shares from employees by outside investors. Never write "raised $300 million". The $22 billion is the tender price; say so.
- Never imply the reader's shares are worth the tender price or that a $22 billion valuation is "their" valuation. A tender buys only what its terms allow; the terms of this one are not published.
- Reuters' "twice the valuation it secured in its February funding round" and the company's "double our valuation at our Series D in February" are the same claim from two sides; use one, cite one.
- Do not convert the $700 million into a per-employee figure: the number of people in this round has not been disclosed.
