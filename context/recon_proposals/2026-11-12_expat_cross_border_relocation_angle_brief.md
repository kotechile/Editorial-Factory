**Emergent Collision Point (stated in no single source):** The 5 November document prices two different answers to the same question — what may a family hand down before the federal government takes a cut? An American may leave an heir **$15,480,000** before the estate tax applies (§3.44). A former American who is a covered expatriate may hand the same heir **$20,000** a year before a 40% tax applies to the recipient (§3.46(3), §2801(a)-(c)). The two numbers sit six pages apart in one revenue procedure, and the ratio between them — **774 to 1** — is the whole argument. Nothing in either section says the other exists. The mechanism that finally makes the second number operative — Form 708, with its first returns due 15 June 2027 — appeared only in this administration's tax forms cycle, after 15 years in which §2801 had no way to be filed.

**Hook:** On 5 November the IRS published $15,480,000 and $20,000 in the same document. The first is what an American can leave a child free of federal tax in 2027. The second is what a former American can.

**Tension:** The exit tax is sold as a one-time toll: mark everything to market, take the $940,000 exclusion, pay, and you are done. §2801 is the part that does not end at the airport. It follows the money — a gift to a US-citizen child, a foreign house inherited by a US resident — and it charges the *recipient*, who may be a person with no idea that a parent's renunciation created a filing obligation in their name. The relief is $20,000 a year per recipient, indexed with the gift-tax annual exclusion, so it rises a thousand dollars at a time while the estate-tax exclusion above it moves in six figures a year. Two regimes, one document, a 774:1 gap.

**Target reader:** cross_border_expat — the reader weighing or already living with a renunciation, and the adult child who will receive from it.

**Single claim to defend:** For a covered expatriate, renunciation converts future gifts and bequests to American relatives into a 40% recipient-level tax above $20,000 a year, so the true price of the decision is not the exit tax but a permanent transfer tax on the family — and 2027 is the first year the machinery exists to collect it on a filed return.
