# Angle Brief: personal_microeconomics_tinkering_tax — 2026-10-31

**Angle Type:** Single-Signal
**Winner:** Two builds, one vault: the app you installed from the store is now the commercially licensed one, and the free path is a side-load.
**Scores:** N=8.9 A=9.5 S=8.7 → Composite=9.0

**Hook:** On 2026-10-09 Bitwarden told its community that "starting in the next release, the Bitwarden apps published to the various stores will be the commercially licensed builds." The notice adds that anyone who wants only the GPLv3 build "will need to download and side-load the Bitwarden application onto their devices" — and that those users "will not have access to any features that they pay for that is commercially licensed" (https://community.bitwarden.com/t/published-version-update-in-app-stores/102750). The licence those store builds now ship under grants use "for the sole purposes of internal development and internal testing, and only in a non-production environment" (https://github.com/bitwarden/server/blob/main/LICENSE_BITWARDEN.txt).

**Tension:** The self-hosting bargain is that the software is an artifact you hold, not a service someone can reprice. This month the artifact split in two, and the split is a business decision about which build a person is allowed to pay for. Bitwarden's framing is that nothing changed for users ("No action is needed, and the apps will work exactly as they do today") and that the move "only affects those who are repackaging and reselling" — while in the same thread staff write that "some future components will be published under the commercial license and will exist only in that build" and that new features "will be evaluated on a case-by-case basis for which license applies to them". That is a boundary, not a bug fix. The people it hurts are the ones who did the work the licence was supposed to reward: the self-hoster who side-loads to keep the free build, and every community project that mirrors the client — Vaultwarden's own maintainer, asked on 2026-10-15 whether the change reaches his users, could only answer "Where does it say that? … those paid features are not Bitwarden Licensed as far as i know" (https://github.com/dani-garcia/vaultwarden/discussions/7856). The people it helps are the enterprise buyers the commercial build is aimed at, and, honestly, a vendor that has to fund a password manager's audits somehow.

**Target reader:** systems_tinkerer_pro — "tinkering tax audits, self-hosting maintenance breakdown".

**Single claim to defend:** For a self-hosted stack, the licence boundary is now a maintenance line item — the free build is a distribution decision a vendor can move, and the cost of that move lands as an audit of every device you enrolled, not as a price rise you can see coming.

**Why this is a tinkering-tax piece and not a software-news piece:** the tax has always been priced in hours and watts on this beat. October added a third unit — the audit. A self-hoster now has to know which of two builds is on each phone, browser and desktop, what the licence on that build permits, and what happens to a self-hosted organisation's licence file on renewal (Bitwarden's own help page gives a self-hosted organisation 60 days from renewal to refresh the file "before your self-hosted organization is disabled", https://bitwarden.com/help/licensing-on-premise). That is maintenance work created by a licensing decision, and it is the thing the reader has to schedule.
