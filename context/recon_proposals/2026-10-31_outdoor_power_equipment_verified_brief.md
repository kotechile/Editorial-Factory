## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Never convert "extreme cold" into a degree value.** The notice does not say what temperature breaks the cable (row 11). A number there would be invented.
2. **The recall is a cable failure, not a battery failure.** Do not write or imply that a pack failed; the hazard is the power cable jacket and the shock that follows it (row 10).
3. **The -4°F rating belongs to a tool-plus-pack pair, and its basis is undisclosed** (rows 2, 3). Say "rated to" or "the company says", never "tested to" or "proven at".
4. **The recalled product is a corded 120-volt-class thrower sold in Canada through one retailer** (rows 9, 14, 15). It is not a cordless machine and the notice is not a US recall.
5. **The 65% capacity figure is laboratory vehicle-cell work** (rows 17, 18). Use it as the mechanism, say where it comes from, and do not attach it to a specific snow blower's runtime.
6. **State the Celsius conversion once, marked as a conversion** (row 19), and the five-day interval with both dates (row 20).
7. **No hands-on claims of any kind.** This desk did not run a snow thrower, a pack or a charger; the piece is a reading of two published records.
8. Everything the piece cites must trace to a row above. Anything else is `[NEEDS-SOURCE]` and comes back to the verifier.
