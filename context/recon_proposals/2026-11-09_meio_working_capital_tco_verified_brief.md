## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Purchase obligations are not cash spent and not inventory.** Row 3 is a schedule of amounts "payable within 12 months"; the money has not left. The article may say Apple *committed* $32.7 billion more, and must not say it *paid* or *prepaid* it. The only cash figure in the filing is row 2 ($8,905M absorbed by inventory).
2. **Apple attributes its FY2026 margin gain to mix and tariff refunds, "partially offset by higher costs, including memory" (row 5).** The article may not claim the inventory hedge caused the margin rise. It may claim margin rose in a year when memory costs rose, and that management credited carry-in inventory with part of the offset in the quarter (row 9).
3. **Q4 gross margin carried ~1 point of tariff refunds (row 8).** Any margin comparison across the quarter must say so or stay at the annual figure.
4. **The 45-46% guide is Reuters' report of the call, not a company release.** Attribute it to the call as Reuters reported it; do not present it as an Apple press-release figure.
5. **The 20% is a forecast** — TrendForce's read, itself citing Commercial Times — not a booked transaction price. Say "forecast", never "settled at". The 80/40/20 ladder is three separate quarterly estimates of the same series, not one measurement.
6. **Scope the arithmetic to memory.** The 20% rise applies to DRAM contract prices; the 20-30% carrying cost applies to all inventory. The comparison is legitimate only when stated as "on the memory portion", and the article must not apply 20% to Apple's whole $14.5B.
7. **20-30% is a vendor convention, not a measurement** (row 13). Say "the range most explainers use", never "the cost".
8. **Do not compare Apple's inventory turn to a retailer's.** No turn figure is in the brief; days-of-stock is not stated for Apple and must not be invented.
