## Rulings for the drafter (binding)

1. **60.7% is the applications figure, 74.9% is the all-hosts figure.** Never present 60.7% as "of all agent hosts". If the article names one number in the headline, use 60.7% and say "across the 25 applications studied" in the sentence that carries it; the 74.9% belongs beside it.
2. **The 5.4% counterfactual has a condition.** It applies to the *flagged* destructive tools only, and it assumes the host prompts on **anything not positively annotated read-only or non-destructive**. The 5.4% that remain are tools that declare `destructiveHint=false` or `readOnlyHint=true` — i.e. the residual is exactly the population that has self-declared safe, so the counterfactual does not make annotations trustworthy. Never write "5.4% of destructive tools would still run" (that is not what it measures: it is 5.4% of *pairs*).
3. **The flag the model can set is the sharpest single detail and must stay attached to its number.** Of 168 tools that elicit by default, 130 still run when the host cannot relay the question: 100 fall back to a model-settable flag, 30 fail open, 38 refuse.
4. **479 of 960 — say what a "run" is.** Live agent runs across four agent SDKs, four of five models attempting the injected deletion. Do not say "479 of 960 agents" or "in 50% of deployments".
5. **Use 1.3–1.8 times the odds, not 1.7–1.8, unless the sentence names exactly shell code, install packages and sudo.** The abstract's narrower range and the body's wider range cover different capability sets; the wider range is the safer claim.
6. **One in seventeen is about 88,000 skills out of 1.53 million — say the denominator once.** The 13.3%-versus-36.4% finding (most oversight language tightens) is the honest counterweight and belongs in the catch, because it stops the piece reading as "the skill ecosystem is hostile".
7. **NIST says the control belongs outside the model; it does not measure whether anyone built it.** Tenet 05/07/08 are a policy statement. Attribute them as such, never as evidence that approval is missing.
8. **Do not state that Google's control plane omits approval.** The page describes a gateway, a registry and SPIFFE identities; the article may say those controls sit at the traffic and identity layer and that the paper measured the tool-call path, and no more. #17 is FLAGGED for that reason.
9. **Meta's numbers are production anecdotes, not a controlled study.** "Under an hour", "two-thirds of changes land without significant human edits" and "1,300 lines" are the authors' own account of their system; never present them as a measured comparison against other architectures.
10. **The protocol's SHOULD is a quote.** Quote the spec exactly ("SHOULD always be a human in the loop with the ability to deny tool invocations") and never upgrade it to MUST. The same page's "MUST consider tool annotations to be untrusted" is a separate sentence about annotations, not about approval.
11. **No figure for "how many enterprises" anything.** Nothing in this window measures deployment rates, spend, or the number of firms running agent fabrics. Do not invent a market number and do not convert a study share into a population count.
