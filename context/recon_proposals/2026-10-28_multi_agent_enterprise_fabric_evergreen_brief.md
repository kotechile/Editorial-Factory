**Decision the reader is facing:** Whether the organization's *aggregate* limit — a customer compensation cap, a counterparty exposure, a cost-centre budget — is enforced by one owner that sees every agent's effect, or left to each agent's own approval limit plus the payment or tool layer's spend control and to each specialist's prompt; and whether a delegated call carries the requester's authenticated scope to the agent that answers it. It bites the first time three service agents each act inside their own limit against the same customer, and again the first time a specialist answers a delegate without asking what that delegate is allowed to see.
**Durability:** The mechanism is a property of the deployment, not of a release: when authority is local and the constraint is global, composition fails regardless of which model sits in the seat — the study's ten implementations from seven vendors produce the same failure class, and their safety orderings barely agree (mean Spearman ρ = 0.36), so no model upgrade or larger context window removes it. The measured rates are **as of** the EAIR study (arXiv:2610.14800v1, 10 October 2026; 1,158 analyzed runs); the aggregate-control precedent is quoted from 12 CFR Part 32 **as of** the eCFR's own 10/26/2026 currency banner; and the spend-control behaviour is Stripe's documented API behaviour **as of** 2026-10-28. Those three artefacts date the figures; the design rule — one owner of the sum, and the caller's scope carried on the delegation — does not expire.

**Thesis:** In an enterprise agent fabric the load-bearing failure is composition rather than error — each agent acts inside its own limit and the organization's cap still breaks (78.3% of episodes, and in all ten implementations), a delegation carries no requester scope so restricted fields reach an unauthorized caller (77.8%, with the scope-check channel used in none of 90 runs) — which makes the aggregate limit a thing one service must own at the ledger, and the caller's scope a field the delegation must carry, because the spend-control layer underneath computes its sum on a best-effort basis with up to a 30-second lag and no model choice fixes either.
