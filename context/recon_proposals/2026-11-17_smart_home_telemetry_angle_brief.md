**Signal A (Anchor 1):** SmartThings put its cloud API on paid plans launched 2026-11-04 and told Home Assistant users, in a post dated 2026-11-04, that the old integration stops working on **2026-12-15** — with a Developer Plan quota of **100,000 cloud API calls per month and 10 active app installs**, a flat monthly rate that the announcement never states, and an explicit clause that the December 15 date "applies to all Home Assistant users including users in countries where the Developer Plan is not available" (https://blog.smartthings.com/smartthings-updates/smartthings-api-additional-details-for-developers-and-business-partners/ and https://community.smartthings.com/t/changes-to-the-smartthings-integration-for-home-assistant-what-s-happening-and-what-you-need-to-do/311942).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Nabu Casa launched the **Home Assistant Connect AUX-2** on 2026-11-11 at **$69 / €59** — two RS-232 serial ports, a Bluetooth proxy, a combo analog/digital audio jack, a Grove port, PoE — pitched as "no device left behind": "pull it off some hellish proprietary cloud and back under your control". The Verge's same-day hands-on answers the obvious objection — "The Aux-2 doesn't do anything you couldn't do already with an ESP32 board… That's not a knock against the Aux-2. That's the whole point" (https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2026/11/11/no-device-left-behind-home-assistant-connect-aux-2/ and https://www.theverge.com/tech/1031601/home-assistant-connect-aux-2-hands-on).
**Emergent Collision Point:** Both events price the same thing — the right to read and command a device you already bought — and they price it at opposite ends. SmartThings bills at the cloud boundary, by the call, per account, with a hard date after which an unpaid integration is dead; Nabu Casa sells a one-time box at the physical boundary, into whichever port the device exposes. Neither document says the rule that follows from putting them side by side: the specification that now decides a household's recurring bill is not "works with my hub" or "is it Matter certified", it is **which of the device's ports answers without an account**. A device with a documented local protocol is free to control for as long as it runs; a device that only speaks its maker's cloud is a metered line item whose price its owner cannot see.
**Hook:** On 15 December the SmartThings integration stops working for every Home Assistant user, including the ones in countries where SmartThings will not sell them the replacement plan — and eleven days after telling them that, the local-first side put a $69 box on sale whose entire job is to reach the devices that still have a port.
**Tension:** The smart home's cost model is moving from "what the hardware costs" to "what the access costs", and it is being repriced in both directions within fourteen days. It hurts the homeowner who bought an appliance for its app, and it hurts hardest in the countries the plan will not reach. It benefits the vendor that owns the cloud path, because an installed base of appliances becomes recurring revenue with no new hardware sold. It benefits the local-first vendor too, but only where the device exposes a physical or documented interface — which is exactly the line the article has to draw honestly, because a $69 serial box does not rescue a washer.
**Target reader:** pro_homeowner
**Single claim to defend:** The purchase spec that decides a modern home's running cost is local access without an account, and the November window shows both sides of the meter — a vendor charging per call for the cloud path, and a vendor selling a $69 one-time port for the physical one.
