**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)

**Scores:** E=9.0 A=9.0 S=9.0 → Composite=9.0 (≥8, proceed; beats the best single-signal candidate by ≥0.3)

**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Moss Landing battery plant caught fire again on Sept 18, 2026 — https://calmatters.org/environment/2026/09/moss-landing-battery-fire-reignites-fears — roughly 1,500 still-charged, inaccessible modules spontaneously ignited during decommissioning; the site's fourth safety incident since 2019; the aging NMC-chemistry plant "keeps coming back to haunt us" (SJSU Prof. Dustin Mulvaney).

**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Gov. Newsom signed SB 905 (Electricity) and SB 913 (Resource adequacy: aggregated distributed energy resources) on Sept 30, 2026 — https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/30/governor-newsom-issues-legislative-update-9-30-2026/ — directing the CPUC to value and pay customer-owned batteries, EVs and smart thermostats for easing grid stress.

**Emergent Collision Point:** Centralized grid storage keeps failing physically (the flagship plant is on fire again, its sibling Elkhorn plant is dark until July 2027), so the state is formalizing the economic opposite — millions of distributed behind-the-meter batteries that the owner already paid for, compensated through the resource-adequacy market. The failure of the big battery is the argument for the small one.

**Hook:** Moss Landing's grid battery was burning for the fourth time in seven years on Sept 18; twelve days later, Gavin Newsom signed SB 905 and SB 913 to pay California homeowners for the batteries sitting in their garages.

**Tension:** The state bet on 300-megawatt centralized lithium plants to bank solar power; the flagship one is a recurring fire hazard built with a chemistry and enclosed design the industry has abandoned. The pivot hands the reliability problem — and a slice of the compensation — to homeowners, but it also hands them the chemistry risk question: the fire is nickel-manganese-cobalt, the home standard is lithium iron phosphate. Who the pivot empowers (battery owners, installers, aggregators) and who it leaves behind (renters, anyone without $15k for a battery, community-solar projects Newsom vetoed the same day).

**Target reader:** pro_homeowner

**Single claim to defend:** The fourth Moss Landing fire and Newsom's same-fortnight VPP signing are one story: California is giving up on a few giant grid batteries and paying homeowners to be the distributed battery instead — and the chemistry that burned is not the chemistry a homeowner should buy.
