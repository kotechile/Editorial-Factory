**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)
**Winner:** The local-first home split in two this fortnight — the hardware was sold, the software got more self-owned. Whose promise keeps your local API alive?
**Scores:** E=9.0 A=8.8 S=9.0 → Composite=8.9
**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Schneider Electric's intended takeover of Shelly Group SE, announced 2026-09-24 — EUR 70.00 a share in cash, roughly EUR 1.2 billion, a 27% premium to the unaffected reference price (EUR 55.2), a 95% minimum acceptance threshold, FSC clearance expected end-November/early-December 2026 and closing expected Q1 2027, supported by both founders who hold roughly 57%, with the headquarters, production, footprint and workforce preserved "for at least three years" (at least until 31 December 2029) (https://corporate.shelly.com/en/news/shelly-group-has-entered-into-an-investment-agreement-with-schneider-electric-on-the-intended-voluntary-public-takeover).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Home Assistant 2026.10, released 2026-10-07 — the platform's third-party map provider "started stamping an 'API key required' message across its map images, breaking every map in Home Assistant at once", and the fix moved map tiles to OpenStreetMap vectors fetched and cached through the homeowner's own instance; the same release added 15 new integrations, a Modbus connections panel, a local SolarEdge energy integration and a one-click way to connect an AI agent (https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2026/10/07/release-202610).
**Emergent Collision Point:** Signal A moves the *physical* layer of a local-first home — DIN-rail relays, energy meters, the CT-clamp monitor Home Assistant's own documentation tells buyers to choose because it speaks a local API — under an industrial energy-management group, and the protective language in the deal documents is a premises-and-headcount commitment that runs "at least until 31 December 2029" and never names the interface; meanwhile the seller describes its own future as "a software-led platform" whose "growth model combines interoperable hardware with cloud-enabled services" and which already "generates revenue from its cloud applications". Signal B moves the *software* layer the other way in the same fortnight: it dropped a third-party API dependency that had broken every map at once, cached the data path inside the house, and put an authorisation gate in front of the new agent endpoint. Neither document states the thesis they jointly produce: in a local-first home the durable asset is not the brand you bought but the interface you can verify — and the acquisition just moved the hardware out from under an interface the buyer was told to trust.
**Hook:** Shelly Group — the relay and energy-meter maker Home Assistant's own documentation tells buyers to pick "because it speaks a local API" — agreed to a €1.2 billion takeover on 24 September, and the protective clause that came with it is a three-year commitment about buildings and headcount, not about the interface.
**Tension:** The acquisition takes the residential entry point that an industrial energy-management group wants for "Energy Intelligence across residential, retrofit and small commercial buildings", and pays a 27% premium for it; the sellers' own release reframes the company as software-led with subscription hooks. The buyer of a Shelly relay or a CT-clamp meter made that purchase on a documented capability — a local API, MQTT, no account — and that capability is now backed by a corporate promise with a date on it rather than by a licence term. At the same moment the platform those devices feed proved the opposite discipline: it removed an outside dependency it did not control, and gated the new AI endpoint behind an administrator decision.
