## Rulings for the drafter

1. **Label the 26.1% as a scenario, not a universal constant.** Miller's figure comes from one setting (50% baseline, a test after every observation, a 150-observation cap) and he calls it "sort of a worst-case scenario". Write the setting into the same sentence. Never print "peeking always inflates 5% to 26.1%".
2. **The 57%–97% is the paper's own illustrative design, and the paper says so.** Its words: "illustrative 200-item, 10-epoch evaluation", "with the magnitude of savings depending on evaluation design". It is a single-author preprint from the UK AI Security Institute, not a peer-reviewed benchmark result. Print "illustrative", and print that savings depend on the design.
3. **Do not call the 2026 framework an always-valid confidence sequence.** It is a hierarchical Bayesian precision rule; the always-valid interval is the Johari line of work. The two are separate fixes to the same problem and must not be merged into one method.
4. **The 80% figure is a practitioner's simulation, not a published study.** Attribute it to Molas by name, say it is a simulation under a 0.95 threshold, and quote his own conclusion — the posterior stays interpretable while the fixed threshold does not control the error rate.
5. **Molas's numbers are peeks per 100 observations at three conversion rates (0.1%, 1%, 10%).** Do not say which rate produced the 80% — the post does not.
6. **Miller's n = 16σ²/δ² is a rule of thumb for a two-arm comparison**, not a universal eval-suite formula. If the article adapts it to an eval suite, say it is Miller's rule of thumb and say what δ and σ mean in the eval's own terms (the smallest score gap worth acting on; the variance of a pass/fail rate).
7. **No derived percentage may be invented in the draft.** If the piece wants "one run in four", compute it from the cited figure and write the arithmetic nowhere the reader has to do it.
8. **The mechanism sentence is the load-bearing claim and needs no citation beyond Miller/Molas:** a threshold carries a false-positive rate only when the number of looks is fixed in advance. Both sources state it; keep the wording plain and never present it as the desk's discovery.
