# Evergreen Brief: agentic_resilience_failure — 2026-10-28

**Archetype:** evergreen
**Vertical:** agentic_resilience_failure
**Persona:** infra_engineer
**Decision the reader is facing:** How many agent runs may be in flight against one shared key or endpoint at the same time — and whether the limit lives inside each agent (its own retry wrapper and local semaphore) or in one gate in front of the shared quota that owns the concurrency count, the rate-limit state and the retry queue — given that the fleet does not fail when a call is wrong, it fails when the pool is full.
**Durability:** The mechanism is queueing, not model behaviour: in-flight work equals arrival rate multiplied by latency, a shared quota is exhausted by the sum of the fleet's in-flight calls, and an admission gate at the shared resource is the only control that can see that sum — none of which changes with a model version or a provider's current quota. The figures are as of the sources cited in 2026 — the HiveMind evaluation (arXiv:2604.17111v1, submitted 18 April 2026, seven scenarios at 5–50 concurrent agents, plus a field run on 15 April 2026) and the AWS Builder Center queue-backlog and load-shedding articles and Google's SRE chapter 21, which are undated mechanisms whose worked examples are arithmetic rather than measurements — and the article recomputes the reader's own in-flight count from their arrival rate and latency instead of quoting any provider's quota, because a quota is the one number that re-indexes.
**De-dup:** `2026-10-14_gate-the-step-not-the-model` is this vertical's nearest prior evergreen (14 days, same vertical): it argues that a run's end-to-end reliability is set at the boundary between *steps* because errors compound 11–14x, and prices boundary controls (circuit breakers −89% cascade propagation, isolation −64% blast radius, output gates catching 73% of silent degradation). This brief is not about a wrong step or a fault that propagates — it is about contention on a *shared resource*, where nothing is wrong at all and every individual call is healthy while the pool is full. It also differs from `2026-10-12_agent-timeout-false-alarm-budget` (the deadline is derived from a stated false-timeout budget, a per-call control) and `2026-10-14_retry-permission-leaves-the-caller` (who may authorise a retry, and the 1.33x load a 10% budget leaves at a deep hop): both settle the controls *inside* a call, and neither can see the fleet. Today's news artifact `2026-10-28_your-90-restart-monitor-proves-nothing` prices a single restart decision against the branch it replaces, which is a per-run decision, not an admission decision. The nearest cross-vertical checks: `2026-10-21_agent-fleet-two-meters` (fleet cost accounting — which meter is instrumented, not how many runs may run) and `2026-10-06_rack-power-density-ceiling-build-before-you-buy` (facility capacity, physical). No prior artifact in any vertical argues in-flight count, admission control, or a shared retry queue.
**Thesis:** A growing agent fleet does not fail as errors, it fails as a queue: eleven agents sharing one key lost three runs and about 135,000 tokens on a day the API had enough aggregate capacity to serve all eleven one after another, uncoordinated fleets fail 72–100% of the time at 5–50 concurrent agents while a gate in front of the shared quota holds them to 0–18% — and the intuitive fix, capping concurrency inside each agent, still fails 81.8% of the time, so the number to size against is in-flight work (arrival rate × latency: 100 messages a second at 100 ms is ten threads, the same rate at ten seconds is a thousand) and the limit has to live at the one place that can see every agent's calls.
