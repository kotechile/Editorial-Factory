**Gate rulings for the drafter**
1. **The article's four costs, in the order it must argue them:** (i) holding-period restart on shares held a year or less (rows 8–10); (ii) loss deferral against the stock's own unrecognised gain (rows 7, 14); (iii) loss of qualified-dividend treatment (rows 11–12); (iv) interest and carrying charges capitalised, not deducted (row 13, **confined to financing cost** — do not claim the put premium is a carrying charge; the premium is the option's basis).
2. **The escape is narrow and must be stated as such:** a naked qualified covered call escapes the straddle rules (row 1) only while it is the *whole* straddle, so adding the put is what forfeits the exemption — and the call sells upside without buying protection. The benchmark ladder (rows 2–5, 15) is what decides whether the call you would write is even qualified.
3. **The 12-month term limit (row 16) means a two-year collar call is not a qualified covered call at all.** State it as Pub 550's rule for post-July 2002 options.
4. **No rate arithmetic.** The piece deliberately carries no marginal-rate table: the dividend leg is stated as a *bucket* change (qualified → ordinary) and the reader applies their own rate. No invented option premium, no invented dividend yield, no invented price — the only money figures are the statute's own thresholds, Pub 550's worked examples, and the position size the reader supplies.
5. **Derived arithmetic the drafter may perform, and only this:** the number of months a clock restarts by (purchase date + 12 months vs put close + 12 months, on a stated assumption), and the 17-point gap is **not** to be stated without a fetchable rate source — it is not available this run.
6. **§ 263(g) applies to stock only through § 1092(d)(3)** (row 6), so the article has to say the position is inside the straddle rules because the put is a position on the same stock — not assert that all stock is straddle property.
7. The August 17, 1954 carve-out in row 8 (put + identified shares acquired the same day) is a real exception; mention it once so the rule is not overstated to "any put, always".
