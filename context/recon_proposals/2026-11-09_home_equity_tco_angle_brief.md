**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)
**Winner:** Your Equity Locked Again at 7.18% — the reachable slice of the record $13.2 trillion grew a third because rates fell, and the market has already taken it back

**Signal A (Anchor 1):** First American Data & Analytics, inaugural Accessible Home Equity & Mortgage Rate Lock-In Trends Report, published 2026-10-28 — accessible home equity a record $13.2 trillion in Q2 2026, up 1.5% year over year, while $11.6 trillion (88%) was locked in, held by owners whose first-mortgage rate sat below the 6.49% prevailing 30-year average at 30 June 2026; locked-in equity fell 2.0% year over year; the extreme band (more than 150 basis points below the benchmark) held $9,642 billion, 83% of all locked-in equity, and fell 4%; the low band (no more than 50 basis points below) held $929 billion and grew 25%; the low-plus-moderate share rose from about 10% at the end of 2025 to 13% (https://www.firstam.com/news/2026/accessible-home-equity-record-13-2-trillion-q2-2026-20261028.html).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey, week ending 2026-11-05 — the 30-year fixed averaged 7.18%, down from 7.24% the week before and up from 6.22% a year earlier; the 15-year averaged 6.57%, from 6.60% the prior week and 5.50% a year earlier (https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms).
**Emergent Collision Point:** The equity report sorts every homeowner by the distance between their own mortgage rate and the prevailing rate, and it sorts them against a benchmark frozen at 6.49% on 30 June. The rate survey shows that benchmark has since moved to 7.18%. Because the "locked" definition is *below the prevailing rate*, a rising benchmark reclassifies loans upward without anyone borrowing a cent: every first mortgage between 6.49% and 7.18% — a band that did not exist as "locked" in June — is locked in now. The report's own direction of travel confirms the mechanism from the other side: locked-in equity fell 2.0% while the total rose 1.5% because rates eased between mid-2025 and mid-2026, and the reachable remainder grew to roughly $1.56 trillion. Neither document says that the improvement was a rate artifact, or that the same mechanism has reversed it since June.
**Hook:** The country's record pile of spendable home equity — $13.2 trillion — is a number that moves when the bond market moves, not when a homeowner decides anything: 88% of it is locked behind a mortgage cheaper than today's 7.18%, and the slice that was actually reachable grew by about a third in a year for one reason, which is that rates fell.
**Tension:** Every lender and home-improvement pitch built on "tap your equity" reads the total. The number that decides a roof replacement is the spread between the owner's own rate and the market, because that spread prices every dollar that would have to be repriced to reach the money — and the spread is at its widest in four quarters: 83% of locked equity sits more than 150 basis points under the benchmark, which is second-lien territory (a second lien preserves the first mortgage; a cash-out refinance reprices all of it). The 12% whose rates sit at or above the benchmark are the only cohort for whom a cash-out refinance is close to a lateral move, and that cohort is 13% of locked-in equity, up from 10%.
**Target reader:** pro_homeowner
**Single claim to defend:** The record accessible-equity number is measured against a mortgage rate the market has already left, so the amount a homeowner can reach without paying a rate penalty is a rate artifact — it grew while rates fell and is being re-locked now that the 30-year is back above 7% — and the decision it informs is not "do I have equity" but "how far is my own rate from today's, because that distance sets the price of reaching it."
