## Rulings for the drafter

1. **Do not attach a diesel or jet price to this story.** Diesel figures are already load-bearing in two
   desk pieces this month (`2026-10-02` used $6.53 a gallon, `2026-10-08` used $5.60), and the mechanism is
   the story, not the input. Say "the diesel and jet fuel prices the Energy Information Administration
   publishes" and no number.
2. **Do not state or imply that the 8 per cent charge ends on the same day the surcharge begins.** The filing
   notice gives an end at midnight Central on 17 January 2027; the FAQ gives only "January 2027" with no day.
   Write the month for the start and the exact date for the end, and say the agency has not published a day.
3. **The percentage is not capped, floored or scheduled in any document fetchable here.** Say the agency
   publishes a percentage weekly and that no ceiling appears in its own FAQ; do not invent a range.
4. **"Base postage" excludes extra services and nonstandard fees** (A4) — a shipper who adds signature,
   insurance or nonstandard handling pays the percentage on a smaller base than the invoice total.
5. **A quote from A13 must be verbatim**, including "14 out of 16 quarters" and "more price to take in the
   marketplace". No paraphrase inside quotation marks.
6. **USPS's June-quarter figures carry their dates** (quarter ended 30 June 2026, reported 7 August 2026) —
   they are background to a January 2027 mechanism, not evidence of its effect.
7. **The postage-due answer (A7) is the operational edge, use it once and exactly**: items mailed at the
   prior week's percentage are short-paid and become postage due.
8. **No hands-on or insider claim.** This piece has no access to a carrier's rate table or a shipper's
   invoice; every figure is a published one or labelled arithmetic.
