## Binding rulings for the drafter

1. **Only a covered expatriate creates §2801 exposure.** A renunciant below all three tests (§218,000 income / $2,000,000 net worth / five-year certification) transfers nothing that is taxable under §2801. Every sentence about the 40% must be conditioned on covered-expatriate status, in the same sentence or the one before it. No "everyone who renounces".
2. **Never say "the US taxes the gift twice."** §2801(e)(2) removes property that appeared on the expatriate's own timely filed gift or estate return; the article must not imply stacking. The honest structure is: what the estate tax would have caught, §2801 catches at the recipient once the transferor is outside the estate-tax system.
3. **The 40% is the top rate, borrowed, not a §2801 rate.** Write it as "the top estate-tax rate" and cite §2801(a)(1) with §2001(c).
4. **The $20,000 is per recipient per calendar year**, not per transferor and not per gift — §2801(c) aggregates all covered gifts and bequests received by one person in the year. Do not write "per gift".
5. **The form's own threshold exception is a filing exception only up to the same $20,000** — a recipient at or below it need not file. Do not upgrade it to "the first $20,000 is untaxed and the rest is a filing question".
6. **Do not call it an inheritance tax on the estate.** The liability is the recipient's (§2801(b)); an estate that distributes is not the taxpayer.
7. **Do not present the 774:1 ratio or the $192,000 as published findings.** Both are desk arithmetic on printed figures; the article must show the two numbers and then say what the division gives.
8. **The date to watch is 15 June 2027**, and it applies to 2025 receipts — not to 2027 receipts, whose form is due 15 June 2029. This is the single most error-prone date in the piece; if the draft cannot keep it straight, it must write "eighteen months after the year of receipt" instead of a date.
9. **Do not imply the IRS collects this automatically.** No source in the brief measures compliance, audit rates or collections under §2801; the article may say the liability arises by statute and the form now exists, and nothing about enforcement volume.
10. **The FEIE ($137,300 for 2027, §3.41) may appear once**, as the number that shows this document is the expat's document — never as a second thesis, and never as a comparison to the $20,000 (different populations: earned income excluded from income tax versus gratuitous transfers taxed at receipt).
