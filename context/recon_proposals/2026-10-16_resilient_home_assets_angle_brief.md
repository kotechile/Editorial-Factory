**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Terner Center for Housing Innovation (UC Berkeley), "Stabilizing Home Insurance and Enhancing Wildfire Resilience for Existing California Homes," published 2026-10-06 — the California Earthquake Authority's estimate of the total investment needed to harden all at-risk California homes is $30 billion or more; AB 888 is funded from 40 percent of property and casualty insurance premium tax revenues above a 2023 baseline, and its funding mechanism is "likely modest in size relative to the total investment needed" (https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/blog/stabilizing-home-insurance-and-enhancing-wildfire-resilience-for-existing-california-homes).

**Emergent Collision Point:** Signal A is a diagnosis that names the house — not the landscape — as the under-treated half of wildfire risk, and adds that a single parcel's hardening cannot decide whether a house survives. Signal B is the price tag and the payment mechanism for that exact gap. Neither document states the collision: the state now owns an official finding that the work is both under-funded and collective, and the funding channel is a share of a tax base plus a $3M setup account, while the federal mitigation money that reached counties has stopped. Hardening has become a private purchase of a public good, priced by owners, in the season the risk arrives.

**Hook:** On Oct. 2 the governor's own 723-page review said the state's efforts are "not consistently translating into reduced risk at the parcel, neighborhood, or landscape level"; four days later, Berkeley researchers priced the missing home-hardening work at $30 billion or more against a state grant whose $3 million covers setup.

**Tension:** The mitigation standard that insurers and fire agencies increasingly expect is a neighborhood-scale outcome, while the money is a per-household decision. Cost falls on owners who cannot capture the benefit; the programs that were meant to spread it are a $3M setup account (AB 888 / Safe Homes, portal still unopened) and a federal round (FEMA BRIC, closed 2026-07-23, selections pending) that a county agency has already judged unlikely to release implementation funding.
