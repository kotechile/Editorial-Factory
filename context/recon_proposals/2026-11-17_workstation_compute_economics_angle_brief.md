# Angle Brief: workstation_compute_economics — 2026-11-17
**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)
**Winner:** Would the Local Box Still Pay at 13.9 Tokens a Second? Rent Became the Transparent Side
**Scores:** E=9.5 A=9.0 S=9.0 → Composite=9.2
**Signal A (Anchor 1):** The same RTX Spark laptop delivered **65.4 tokens/s on Windows, 65.1 through WSL and 13.9 on native Linux** running GPT-OSS-120B, with the GPU pinned near **790 MHz** against ~2.2 GHz, 37 minutes against about 8 on a long benchmark, temperatures of 40–47 °C, and only **28 GB of the 128 GB** unified pool visible to Linux until a Windows-side reservation was dropped to 4 GB. The cause named is the *Nvidia Platform Controllers and Framework* power-table firmware, which only Windows can decrypt. Published 2026-11-04; the Hacker News submission drew 617 points and 293 comments that day (https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/linux-nvidia-rtx-spark-laptop). A second, independent hand-install on a different OEM's 128 GB RTX Spark laptop reached the opposite result — **116.0 GiB visible to the OS and CUDA, 30.9 tokens/s in vLLM** against a Windows review of the same machine at 29.4 and 31.4 (https://josie.lol/blog/linux-rtx-spark) — and an RTX Spark developer reports on NVIDIA's own forum that a 96 GiB GPU split leaves **30.49 GiB CPU-visible and a 60.25 GiB commit limit**, so a ~70 GiB weight set collides with Windows even with the reservation (https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/rtx-spark-on-windows-cuda-allocations-consume-system-commit-even-with-96-gib-dedicated-gpu-memory/386453).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** The rent side of the same decision is published, grouped and reproducible: H100 SXM 80 GB on-demand runs **$2.69 at RunPod Community, $3.99 at RunPod Secure, $6.88 on AWS p5.48xlarge and $12.29 on Azure ND96isr_H100_v5** — **4.57x for one chip**, $1,964 against $8,972 for a 730-hour month — with the collection script, raw price API returns and transcribed vendor rates in the repo (2026-11-09 — https://github.com/datareceipts/data-receipts/tree/main/109-gpu-pricing). And it is a moving number: the neo-cloud H100 index reads **$2.83/hr on 18 October and $3.08 on 16 November** (+8.83%), with B200 up **10.9%** and B300 **9.1%** in the last seven days (https://www.silicondata.com/products/silicon-index/h100).
**Emergent Collision Point:** The buy side's two load-bearing numbers — how many gigabytes you actually get and how many tokens a second it produces — are set by firmware and OS, not by the SKU, and nobody publishes them. The rent side's number is a public table with a reproducible script and a daily index. So the two halves of the build-vs-rent decision have swapped their information quality: renting is now the *auditable* option and buying is the *opaque* one, which is the reverse of the usual argument (cloud billing opaque, local fixed cost knowable). Neither the benchmark post nor the price dataset says that; each only supplies one side.
**Hook:** Start on the number, not the platform: the box a developer bought to stop renting ran GPT-OSS-120B at 65.4 tokens a second on Windows and **13.9 on the Linux it was bought for** — and the identical chip in a rival OEM's laptop ran at 30.9. Same silicon, 2.22x apart, nobody's spec sheet says which you get.
**Tension:** Who this empowers — the buyer who now has a checklist and a veto (demand a measured tokens/s on your own distro from the exact SKU, in writing, before a $4,699–$7,999 purchase). Who this threatens — every vendor whose launch slide is a petaflop and a gigabyte count, and every integrator selling "128 GB" as a number rather than a configuration. The harder implication: a payback model built on a spec-sheet throughput is not conservative, it is unfalsifiable, and a fixed asset whose throughput the vendor controls cannot be amortised against a rental rate that is publicly quoted to the hour.
**Target reader:** infra_engineer
**Single claim to defend:** In November 2026 the local-AI purchase became the un-auditable side of the build-versus-rent decision — capacity and throughput are firmware- and OS-set, two hands-on Linux runs on the same chip family measured 2.22x apart, and the alternative is now a public rate table plus a daily index — so a workstation should be bought only against a measured tokens/s on the buyer's own OS, and until that number exists the honest line item is the rental, not the box.
