## Handoff notes (drafting constraints)

- **Only claims 1–11 may be stated as fact.** Claim 12 stays out as a figure: no "average of X lines per order" for any industry, and the 4.9-line implication for the distributor may appear only if the writer labels it as their own arithmetic in the same sentence, or is dropped.
- **The exponent rule is a model, not a measurement.** Rows 1–2 are the source's own independence model (every line available independently). Row 7 is the same consultancy's measured experience, and it is *wider* than the model at their baskets. Present the model as a floor and the measured gap as the reason to measure the metric directly — never present 0.98^10 as an exact prediction for a named customer.
- **Arithmetic permitted, nothing else.** The article may state 0.98^10 = 81.7% and 0.98^20 = 66.8% (row 1), and may state the inverse rule — the per-line availability a target order fill needs is the target raised to one over the lines per order (e.g. 95% order fill at ten lines needs 99.5% per line) — because both inputs sit in the sentence. No dollar figure, no chargeback percentage, no inventory value, no annualised cost: the brief carries none and the figure audit forbids deriving one.
- **No retailer, no retailer programme.** Nothing here cites Walmart, Target, Kroger, OTIF targets or deduction rates. Do not name a retailer and do not say "your customer scores you at order level" as if a programme did it — the claim is about what the customer experiences (row 3, row 4).
- **The 92–98% band is a vendor's rule of thumb**, not a census (row 11). Write it as "the band the industry quotes", attributed.
- **Dates.** Inventoros explainer dated June 19, 2026; DCL Logistics page dated July 7, 2026; Benchmarking Success article published March 2018 (so the distributor's 82%/96% case is a decade-old illustration of a mechanic, never a current benchmark — say so); the F. Curtis Barry article prints no date on the page, so it is cited as an undated consultancy article retrieved 2026-10-26, with its client examples unnamed.
- **The client cases are one consultancy's unnamed engagements** (rows 5, 6), not a survey and not a sample of one industry. That limitation belongs in The catch.
- **Glosses at first use:** order fill rate, line fill rate, unit fill rate, SKU (stock-keeping unit), and OTIF (on-time, in-full) if used at all.
- **Observer voice.** The reading — that the metric an order desk optimises is the wrong one — is the writer's, and must be labelled as such.
