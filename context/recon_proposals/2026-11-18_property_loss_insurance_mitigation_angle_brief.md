**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Texas Department of Insurance publishes, by ZIP code and policy type, the reasons insurers gave for declining, cancelling or not renewing a home policy (https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/reasons-home-and-auto-insurance-policies-are-declined-canceled-not-renewed.html). Live record, observed 2026-11-18; reporting began 2026-04-01. Its codebook merges six separable homeowner defects — "insufficient defensible space; maintenance, occupancy, or vacancy issues; roof condition; or tree overhang" — into the single reason "Condition of property", while a company that refuses a roof *type* must code it "Other, Insurer's Action" instead, and a credit score can never be the only reason listed.
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** The Secretary of State's Texas Register carries TDI's own rulemaking on the reason-code machinery (https://www.sos.state.tx.us/texreg/archive/May222026/Proposed%20Rules/28.INSURANCE.html), 2026-05-22: "the reason codes are provided only for use in a statistical plan report submitted to TDI's statistical agents… the insurer should not rely on TDI's reason codes in its consumer notices or disclosures", and the new reason-source indicator must record whether a decision rested on "aerial imagery versus other types of third-party information".
**Emergent Collision Point:** Signal A is the first state file that explains, at ZIP level, why home policies end; Signal B is the regulator's own statement that the vocabulary inside that file was built for statistical agents and must not be used to explain a decision to a homeowner. Neither document says what the pair implies: the public record splits the causes a homeowner cannot control — a carrier that won't write the roof, an exposure appetite, a location — into separate lines, and bundles the six causes they can control into one, so the file can report that a neighbourhood is being dropped while being structurally unable to say which of six fixes would have kept one house. The one document that names a reason in words is the written notice the statute requires, and it is not the file.
**Hook:** Texas will now tell you why insurers dropped your ZIP. It will not tell you which of six reasons was yours, because the state's own codebook puts defensible space, roof condition, tree overhang, maintenance, occupancy and vacancy on one line — while a carrier that simply will not write your roof type gets a line to itself.
**Tension:** Mitigation spend is the only lever a homeowner holds at renewal, and the reason code that would credit it is the one code that cannot distinguish a hardened house from a neglected one. Add the clock: the newest notice month on the public file is 07/2026, four months behind the renewals it is meant to explain, so the tool is a neighbourhood weather report arriving after the storm.
**Target reader:** pro_homeowner
