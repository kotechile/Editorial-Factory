**Signal A (Anchor 1):** "Know the Shape, Find the Fault: Topology-Conditioned Diagnosis of Multi-Agent LLM Failures" — arXiv:2610.10126, published 2026-10-07. Communication topology is statistically associated with failure type (χ² = 409.9, p = 1.2×10⁻⁷⁰); topology context lifts gpt-mini Macro-F1 from 0.173 to 0.350 on 851 MAST-clean traces, and the topology-conditioned pipeline projects to ≈6% of the cost of repeated frontier diagnosis over 1,000 traces (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10126).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** "When Sub-Agents Work in Parallel: The Promises and Pitfalls of Dynamic Concurrency in Long-Horizon Coding Tasks" — arXiv:2610.10263, published 2026-10-07. Controlled comparison of matched Codex, Claude Code and Kimi Code executions with dynamic sub-agent spawning enabled vs disabled across 354 tasks and 2,124 executions; catalogs 13 concurrency-specific failure modes and 28 observable patterns, and finds model capability drives short-task outcomes while orchestration drives long-horizon completion (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10263).
**Emergent Collision Point:** Both papers relocate the load-bearing variable of a multi-agent system from the model to the *shape of the runtime*. Signal A shows the communication graph you wired predicts *how* the system fails and can be read back out of ordinary execution traces; Signal B shows that once tasks get long, the concurrency policy — not the model — decides whether the system finishes. Neither paper states the other's claim: A is a diagnosis method, B is an execution policy. Collided, they argue that topology and concurrency are the unit of architectural decision, and that this unit is now measurable at a fraction of the cost of the model-side fixes teams reach for first.
**Hook:** A study of 851 multi-agent traces finds the shape of the communication graph predicts the failure type at χ² = 409.9 (p ≈ 10⁻⁷⁰) — while a 2,124-execution trial of dynamic sub-agent spawning finds short-task results track the model and long-horizon results track the orchestration policy.
**Tension:** Framework adoption (CrewAI, LangGraph, AutoGen-style stacks) turns topology into a default rather than a decision, and most observability tooling still reads traces as flat text instead of structure. The reflex when an agent fails is to buy a bigger model; both papers suggest the failure was wired in. Who it helps: architects who own the orchestration layer. Who it threatens: the "swap the model" spending line and single-number benchmark claims that hold information access constant.
