**Gate rulings for the drafter**

1. **Do not describe the allegations.** Rows 4 and 6 are enough: an internal investigation had been retained and the
   board announced a resignation. The underlying personal allegations are not verified in any source listed here, are
   not load-bearing for the reader's decision, and must not appear in any form.
2. **Two intraday figures exist and they measure different moments.** NBC's 12% is the opening move (row 5); the WSJ's
   14% is the session's worst level (row 4). Use one of them, say which moment it describes, and never blend them into
   "fell 12-14%" or attribute the closing figure to the intraday low.
3. **The $75,000 is a ceiling on total annual additions, not on after-tax contributions.** Write it as the ceiling and
   say the after-tax room is what your own deferrals and the employer's match leave of it — a sentence that calls the
   remaining space "the $75,000 room" without saying what comes out of it is wrong by construction.
4. **The catch-up stacks and is not part of the ceiling** (row 11) — never add $8,500 to $75,000 in a "total
   contributions" figure without saying the catch-up sits outside section 415(c).
5. **The Roth catch-up wage threshold is a wage test on 2026 pay for 2027 catch-ups** (row 9); do not present it as a
   contribution-limit change.
6. **Never state or imply that a 401(k) contribution can absorb the share position.** The whole load-bearing argument
   is that it cannot: contributions are limited by compensation (row 10), and shares are not compensation.
7. **Derived arithmetic is limited to rows 18 and 19**, and each must be labelled in place as the desk's own sum with
   its inputs named. No other ratio, sum or percentage is authorised.
8. **The Florida-style "the stock recovered, so holding was right" trap.** Row 4 says the stock closed 4.3% lower after
   falling as much as 14%: the recovery is a fact and belongs in The catch, not a licence to imply the risk was small.
