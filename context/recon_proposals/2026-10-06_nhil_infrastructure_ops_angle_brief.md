**Hook:** On Sept. 16 the U.S. House voted 417-3 to make large data centers pay the full, incremental cost of the grid upgrades they trigger. On Sept. 30 the Senate killed the same bill, 57-43 — three votes short of the 60 it needed.
**Tension:** The political system is fighting over who pays for the wires at the exact moment American grid operators have told the largest new electricity users that the wires may not arrive on their schedule at all. ERCOT alone is tracking roughly 474 GW of large-load requests — about 90% of them data centers — and on Sept. 3 it stopped treating them as individual cases: it issued provisional "Batch Zero" classifications and opened a verification and audit before any project may advance. So the federal cost-allocation question failed on a procedural vote, but the question that actually sets data-center timelines — who is allowed to connect, and when — is now being answered by state commissions and by an audit, not by Congress. That empowers state utility commissions and grid operators, and it leaves the operators of the racks exposed: their power strategy is now a regulatory-timing strategy.
