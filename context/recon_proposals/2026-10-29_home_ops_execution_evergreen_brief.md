Notes the drafter must respect (read by eye out of the fetched pages, because the gate checks a figure, never its gloss):

- **The band has three different edges and the article says so.** HVAC School gives 14–23°F and calls the fixed 20-degree rule too simple; Resideo gives 16–22°F as a rule of thumb; the founder's pillar says 16°F–22°F. No source states a manufacturer's specification, and none may be implied: write "rule of thumb", never "spec", "target" or "required".
- **Location is the load-bearing claim, not the number.** The split is measured across the coil (before and after the evaporator, far enough out for the air to mix), not at a register and not with an infrared thermometer at a vent — HVAC School says both explicitly. A reading taken at the grilles is the number that lets a verdict be sold.
- **Direction of the fault:** high split → restricted airflow (filter, evaporator coil, blower, blocked return); low split → charge, metering device, compressor, or humidity masking the reading (more latent removal lowers the split). Resideo's own low-Delta-T cause list names the undercharge, the malfunctioning metering device *and* the malfunctioning compressor, which is exactly why the split alone cannot convict a compressor.
- **Airflow before charge** is the ordering claim, and InterNACHI's 350 cfm/ton line is the anchor: a charge verdict taken on restricted airflow is the standard misdiagnosis.
- **Duct leakage of 15-20%** (InterNACHI) is the reason a grille reading disagrees with a coil reading — it belongs in prose under row 3, not as its own claim row.
- **RMI's $719 / $72–$144 pair** is a 2024 summer cooling bill and a 10–20% efficiency-loss estimate, not an HVAC repair price: use it as the standing cost of running a degraded system, and never as the cost of a compressor. No national compressor-replacement price is cited anywhere in this brief; if the article wants a price, it comes from the reader's own quote, and the customer-truth anecdote's $7,500 figure is field data, attributed as one homeowner's quote rather than a market average.
- **No hands-on claim.** This desk did not test a system. The article's authority is the procedure and the citations, not experience in a mechanical closet.
