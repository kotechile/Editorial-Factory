**Signal A (Anchor 1):** HMRC, *UK resident individual members of LLCs and other reverse hybrids — Summary of responses*, published 2026-10-28 (https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/uk-residentindividualmembers-of-llcs-and-otherreversehybrids/outcome/uk-resident-individual-members-of-llcs-and-other-reverse-hybrids-summary-of-responses). The government abandons the transparency proposal it consulted on over the summer, keeps US LLCs "opaque for the UK tax purposes of an individual member", taxes distributions "at dividend rates of Income Tax", and offers "a new form of elective domestic credit relief" for foreign taxes the member has "paid by them personally". "Claims for the new form of credit will be possible from early 2028." 64 written responses. The companion consultation paper puts the damage at "potentially as high as 75%" and reports response evidence "commonly in the 55 to 75% range, with several respondents citing rates above 80% and certain examples exceeding 90%".

**Signal B (Anchor 2):** HM Revenue & Customs, *Budget 2026 — Overview of tax legislation and rates (OOTLAR)*, §1.1 and §1.2, published 2026-10-28 (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar), with *Annex A: rates and allowances* of the same date (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar/annex-a-rates-and-allowances). From the 2027-28 tax year the savings rates become 24% / 44% / 49% and the property rates become 24% / 44% / 49%, both from 6 April 2027 — a second two-point rise stacked on the two points already legislated in the 2025 Budget. The dividend table is not moved: 10.75% / 35.75% / 39.35% in both years.

**Emergent Collision Point:** A credit is bounded by the tax it is credited against. Because the new LLC relief is set against UK Income Tax on a distribution at **dividend** rates, the ceiling on the whole repair is 39.35% at the additional rate — and the Budget has just widened the gap between that table and everything around it: property income, charged today at the main 20/40/45 rates, becomes its own 24/44/49 table on 6 April 2027, and savings move up four points with it. So on the same day, in the same fiscal event, the Treasury chose the cheapest of its three income tables as the vehicle for the fix and raised the two tables a cross-border taxpayer is most likely to be standing in. A US member who also rents out the flat they left behind is relieved at 39.35% on one stream and charged 49% on another, and the relief on the first cannot start until 2028. Neither document says this; only the two read together do.

**Hook:** A UK-resident member of a US limited liability company can pay a 75% effective tax rate on the same profits — the government concedes the number in its own consultation paper — and the remedy it chose on 28 October is an optional credit that "will be possible" from early 2028.

**Tension:** The mismatch is structural, not a filing error. A US LLC is transparent in the United States, so the member is taxed there as the profits arise; HMRC has treated almost all LLCs as opaque since 1997, so the same member is taxed again in Britain when the money is distributed. Two charges on different bases are not "the same profits" under the UK-US treaty, so the treaty's relief machinery does not engage — which is why the effective rate can reach 75% and, in the consultation's own evidence, does. The government's answer keeps the second charge and refunds part of it, two tax years out. The political economy is the interesting part: the UK wants globally mobile capital to arrive, has built a four-year 100% relief regime for new arrivals to that end, and has now decided that the cheapest way to fix the 75% problem is to route the relief through its lowest rate table rather than to remove the double charge. Read against the same day's rate table, that is a decision about which table the money stands in.
