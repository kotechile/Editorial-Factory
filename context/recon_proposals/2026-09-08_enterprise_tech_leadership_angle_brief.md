# Angle Brief: enterprise_tech_leadership — 2026-09-08

**Winner:** McKinsey's new State of AI survey finds adoption at an all-time high while enterprise earnings impact stays frozen at 37% — and 32% of companies have already declined to buy software they can now build with agentic coding tools.

**Scores:** N=8.0 A=9.0 S=9.0 → Composite=8.6 (clears the ≥8 hard gate)

**Hook:** Forty percent of billion-dollar companies now say they are scaling AI agents, up from 27% a year ago. Yet the share that can point to any earnings impact is stuck at 37% — exactly where it was last year. And nearly one in three companies has already said no to buying software it can now build itself.

**Tension:** AI conviction is outrunning AI's financial returns. Companies are deploying agents and re-architecting how they build software — enough that 32% declined a vendor purchase — but the profit line hasn't moved. The victims are software vendors (a third of buyers now default to build), CFOs and boards who can't get a straight number, and the 6% "high performers" who prove the gap is a workflow problem, not a model problem.

**Target reader:** eng_leader (senior practitioner who ships; direct, skeptical of hype; wants concrete numbers and a Monday-morning decision framework).

**Single claim to defend:** McKinsey's Aug 25, 2026 survey (1,719 leaders, 97 nations) finds the share of organizations reporting enterprise-level EBIT impact from AI is flat year-over-year at 37%, even as 40% of billion-dollar companies now scale AI agents (up from 27%) — and 32% have declined to buy at least one software product because agentic coding tools let them build it in-house.
