## Gate rulings for the drafter
- **Yearly averages are a whole-year reconstruction, not a licence.** The general rule (row 1) is the rate on the day of receipt, payment or accrual. The article may compute a salary's dollar value at a published average **only** while saying which rate it used and that the rule for a specific payday is the rate that day. Do not write that the average is "required" for salary, and do not write that it is "forbidden" — neither is in a source.
- **Direction of the IRS table.** It is foreign-currency units per dollar; every conversion in this brief divides. The dollars-per-euro figures (1.1820 from 0.846) are the reciprocal and must be named as derived where used.
- **Do not total the ECB and Fed rates.** They are the same quantity published by two institutions, three-thousandths apart on overlapping days (1.1130 on 29 October vs 1.1209 on 19 October); the article may say they are close, never that one is "the" rate and never that their difference is a cost.
- **The cap is last year's, re-indexed.** $130,000 is the 2025 figure (rows 13–14). Say so; do not present it as the current year's amount, and do not guess this year's.
- **Removed during verification (do not use):** the year-end rate for account balances (FBAR / Form 8938) — `irs.gov/instructions/i114` and `fincen.gov` are unreachable to the fetcher, so no FBAR rate rule is stated in the article; and the social-security totalization five-year rule — `irs.gov/…/totalization-agreements` carries no figure and `ssa.gov` is unreachable.
