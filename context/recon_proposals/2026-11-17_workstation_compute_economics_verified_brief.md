## Binding rulings for the drafter
1. **The "35%" headline is wrong about speed.** 13.9 against 65.4 is **21.3%** — 4.71x slower. 35% is the clock ratio. Say which ratio is which, or use the token figures only.
2. **Never write "Linux is slower on RTX Spark."** One OEM's SKU lost 79% of its throughput on native Linux; another OEM's reached parity (30.9 against 29.4/31.4) and CPU parity. The claim is that the **SKU's firmware** decides, which is why the buyer cannot infer it.
3. **Do not present the 30.9 vs 29.4/31.4 comparison as an OS comparison.** The source itself says the quants, runtimes, context and timing differ. It is "two working configurations", nothing more.
4. **The 28 GB figure is Linux-after-a-Debian→Omarchy install on the MSI unit, not a universal law.** On the ASUS unit the same nominal pool read 116.0 GiB. Both are true; the difference is firmware.
5. **The Windows commit-limit row is one developer's report** (3 posts, 621 views), not NVIDIA's statement. Attribute it as a user report; never as "NVIDIA says".
6. **Rental prices are on-demand list prices, single region, and exclude spot, reserved and Lambda.** Do not call $2.69 "the price of an H100" — call it the cheapest listed H100 SXM 80 GB route in that dataset on 2026-11-09.
7. **Do not convert the box into a payback-months claim against a per-hour rental rate.** The two are not like-for-like (a 273 GB/s unified pool is not an H100 SXM 80 GB), and the vertical has already published `local-ai-payback-cloud-gpu-spread` (2026-10-06) on exactly that arithmetic. Keep the collision on information quality — measured versus published — and on throughput-per-workload, not on amortisation months.
8. **The $7,999 SKU was out of stock and the listings have since been taken down.** It is a posted MSRP, not a completed transaction; TweakTown and Notebookcheck reported the same listing.
9. **Silicon Data's $2.53 FAQ line is excluded.** Use $3.08 (16 Nov) and $2.83 (18 Oct) from the daily table.
10. **No hands-on claim.** This desk did not run these benchmarks; every number is quoted from the record that measured it.
