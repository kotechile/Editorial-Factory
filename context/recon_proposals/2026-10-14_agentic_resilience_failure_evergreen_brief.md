**Decision the reader is facing:** How many unverified steps one agent run may take before it must cross a hard verification boundary — and whether the next reliability sprint goes into the prompt/model or into the boundaries between steps (a gate, a hard stop, a quarantine path), given that a single wrong step makes the next one 11–14x likelier to be wrong.
**Durability:** The mechanism is structural, not a version artefact: failures compound along a chain of unverified steps, a boundary control acts on the propagation rather than on the fault, and the measured effect of removing enforcement is a collapse in safe completion while the run spends *more* compute. The figures are as of the sources cited — the trajectory study 2026-09, the fault-injection catalogue 2026-10, the ablation v2 2026-05, the production postmortem corpus 2026-06, the survey 2026 — and the worked-example arithmetic in the SRE chapter does not expire at all; only the percentages re-index as models improve, and the direction of every one of them has held across three independent study designs.

None of the three argues containment: this brief's load-bearing object is the *boundary between steps* — how chain length compounds a fault (11–14x, 30.5% recovery, 38.5% never detected), what boundary controls measurably buy (89 / 64 / 73 / 71), and what removing them costs (safe completion 14.4% → 3.1% while the run burns the most tokens). The 2026-10-07 evergreen brief dropped a containment candidate at 7.8 precisely because it had no distinct measured anchor; the fault-injection effectiveness numbers and the ablation ladder now exist, which is what makes the topic admissible today.
**Thesis:** An agent's end-to-end reliability is set at the boundaries between its steps, not inside the model — one wrong step makes the next 11–14x likelier to be wrong and detection fails in well over a third of runs, while the boundary controls with measured effect (circuit breakers cut cascade propagation 89%, isolation cuts blast radius 64%, output gates catch 73% of silent degradation, three or more patterns cut MTTR 71%) and the measured cost of removing them (safe completion 14.4% → 3.1%) make the containment set, not the model, the cheapest reliability purchase an infra team can make this quarter.
