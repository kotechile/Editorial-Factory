**Emergent Collision Point:** Both documents answer "how much of one company's future can a person hold at once?", and they answer in incompatible units. The statute limits an annual addition to the lesser of $75,000 or 100 percent of the participant's compensation, and an annual addition is the sum of employer contributions, employee contributions and forfeitures — so the ceiling is fed by a paycheck and cannot absorb a share position at all. The resignation shows the other side: the position is repriced by events (an investigation, a board decision, a Thursday morning) that no contribution calendar can see. The ceiling is therefore a savings rate that will never touch the position, and the position carries a risk the ceiling cannot price. Neither source says this; the IRS notice is silent on shares and the company statement is silent on retirement accounts.
