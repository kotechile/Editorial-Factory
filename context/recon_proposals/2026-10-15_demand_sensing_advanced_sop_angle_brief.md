**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Adobe Analytics 2026 holiday online forecast, published 2026-09-28 — U.S. online holiday sales to hit **$275.1B** (Nov 1–Dec 31, +6.7% YoY), with **$95.8B spent in October alone (+8% YoY)** and Cyber Week worth **$47.5B, 17.3% of the whole season**; Adobe's own line is that the pre-season spike "has changed the shape of the holiday season" (https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/09/adobe-us-holiday-shopping-season-to-hit-record)
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** AlixPartners 2026 U.S. Retail Holiday Outlook, published 2026-09-16 — **+4% to 7%** holiday sales growth forecast, while the *same* research finds consumers "pulling back on units" across sectors, **56%** unmoved by any promotion below **30% off**, store trips shifting to value players as full-price apparel trips fall **9%**, and credit-card balances at **$1.26T (+5.9% YoY)** with 90+ day delinquency at **12.8%**, the highest since 2011 (https://www.alixpartners.com/newsroom/press-release-us-holiday-sales-expected-to-grow-4-7-this-season-even-as-consumer-confidence-declines)
**Emergent Collision Point:** The two anchors measure one season in two different currencies and land on opposite answers. Adobe's number is a **dollar** series on a **transaction** basis and it is *early*: nearly a fifth of the season's dollars move in five days and the fastest growth month is October. AlixPartners' number is a **unit and behaviour** series and it is *down*: fewer units, a 30% discount bar, and a migration to cheaper channels funded by record credit. An S&OP plan reconciles one series into one number — and in retail that number is dollars at the top (merchandise financial planning, open-to-buy) feeding units at the bottom (allocation, replenishment). The collision is that the plan is being tuned for accuracy exactly where accuracy is not the error: the miss is in the *unit* and the *month*, and a single reconciled plan hides it. Neither source states this — Adobe never mentions planning systems or units, AlixPartners never mentions the calendar shift or the planning stack.
**Hook:** Open on the two figures arriving twelve days apart: a record $275.1B online holiday, and a consumer study that says the same shoppers are buying fewer things and need 30% off to move. Then the month: $95.8B of it lands in October.
**Tension:** What changed is not the shopper's willingness to spend but the *shape* of the demand the plan must serve — the peak moved earlier, the growth is price and promotion rather than volume, and the funding is credit (a 90+ day delinquency rate at its highest since 2011). The industry's in-window answer is still a planning-platform consolidation (RELEX winning a 335+ store multi-banner account by putting MFP, OTB, forecasting, replenishment and allocation on one data layer) and a forecasting-first AI roadmap (Gartner's Sept 16 research leads with demand forecasting, praising its "transparency and repeatability"; AlixPartners' own cross-cutting instruction is to "use AI for demand forecasting, not just discounting"). Capacity moves on a slower clock still: Tractor Supply's $200M, 865,000 sq ft automated DC — designed from December 2024, shipping October 2026 to 123 stores in nine states. The plan's unit basis, its peak week and its capex horizon no longer agree.
