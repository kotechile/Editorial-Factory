## Why no publish

This is the **first run** for `home_ops_execution`, and it landed on a weak window. The
vertical's sharpest acute content is a **July 1, 2026 contractor/permitting law wave** —
California SB 779 (unlicensed-contractor minimum penalty $200→$1,500), Georgia SB 553 (contractor
licensing rewrite + new oversight board), Florida HB 803 ($7,500 building-permit exemption), and
Washington 2SHB 1534 (Homeowner Recovery Program). All four are ~2.5 months old, **outside the
30-day window (Aug 25 – Sep 24)** and therefore dropped by the anchor-freshness rule
(`virality_judge.md` §3.5).

The single strongest in-window candidate is the **2027 I-Codes finalization**. But its load-bearing
dates are also out of window: the final Public Comment Hearing / Online Governmental Consensus Vote
results were released **July 13, 2026**, and the ICC news release on off-site construction provisions
is dated **August 24, 2026** (one day before the window). The September publication ("IBC, IFC and
IWUIC anticipated September 2026"; ICC Digital Codes "select 2027 I-Codes available now") is real but
forward-looking — no citable dated publication event exists to anchor a load-bearing claim. Even
granting the September publication, the substantive payload (ICC/MBI 1200/1205/1210 modular-construction
standards, 500-year floodplain expansion, three-strikes rule) skews toward builders/developers, not the
`pro_homeowner` persona executing renovations. Composite caps at **6.9**.
