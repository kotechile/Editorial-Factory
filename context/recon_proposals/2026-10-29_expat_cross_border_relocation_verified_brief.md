## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Do not say the UK "fixed" the double tax.** The outcome keeps the second charge and offers a credit; the words are "providing a new form of elective domestic credit relief". A headline or lead that implies the double tax is gone is a bait-and-switch on a document that says the opposite.
2. **75% is the government's ceiling language, not a typical outcome.** "Potentially as high as 75%" (consultation paper); the response evidence is "commonly in the 55 to 75% range", with some "above 80%" and "exceeding 90% after accounting for US state and local taxes". Never present 75% as what most members pay.
3. **Never say the relief is automatic.** It is elective, per member, and computed on an average foreign rate "as opposed to by tracing".
4. **Never say claims are open now.** "Possible from early 2028" — and legislation comes in "a future Finance Bill" after a further technical consultation. Do not call it law.
5. **The 39.35% ceiling is the writer's reading, not the paper's.** State it as a reading of how a credit works, with the government's own "broadly comparable" sentence beside it.
6. **Property rates are England, Wales and Northern Ireland.** Savings rates are UK-wide. Scotland and Wales can set their own property rates. Do not write "across Britain".
7. **Expiry check on the rate rise:** it takes effect 6 April 2027 and is legislated in Finance Bill 2026-27 — announced, not yet enacted. Say "from April 2027" and note it is a Budget announcement awaiting the Finance Bill.
8. **Do not claim non-resident landlords are unaffected or affected** — no source in this brief addresses the non-resident position. Keep the example a UK-resident member who also lets a UK property; do not assert anything about a British expat's UK rental.
9. **No FEIE / Form 2555 material this run** — the 2027 figure is still unpublished and belongs to a different vertical's beating heart; keep the piece on the LLC and the rate tables.
