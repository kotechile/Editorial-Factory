## 2. Authorised desk arithmetic (show the work, label it as arithmetic)

The drafter may compute these and **must present each as the desk's own arithmetic on the cited figures, never as a published measurement**:

- **Annual attrition years four to six.** 90% alive at four years, 65% at six → 25 percentage points lost across two years → about 12.5 points a year in that window. Allowed wording: "the curve between year four and year six costs about 12.5 points a year", with the two cited endpoints named in the same sentence.
- **The array multiplier.** If one drive's chance of dying in a year is p, a four-drive array's chance of at least one death is 1 − (1 − p)^4: at 12.5%, about 41%; at the fleet's 1.41% lifetime rate, about 5.5%. Allowed wording: "run the same arithmetic on your own array" — always naming which p it used. **Never** state an array probability without the p and the drive count in the same sentence.
- **The running-cost figure.** 16.31 W × 8,760 hours = 142.9 kWh; × $0.1730 = **$24.72 a year**. Allowed as "about $25 a year", with the wattage and the EIA rate named.
- **The cloud equivalent.** 8 TB × $6.95 = $55.60 a month, **$667.20 a year**, at B2's list rate. Allowed as "about $667 a year for the same 8TB".

## 3. Flags for the drafter
- **FLAGGED — population.** All survival and AFR figures come from Backblaze's own fleet: 200,000+ drives in its storage servers, spinning continuously, mostly 4–18TB. That is the best public telemetry available, but it is *not* a four-bay consumer NAS in a spare room with a light duty cycle. Say whose fleet it is in the same paragraph as the number, every time.
- **FLAGGED — the average hides the tail.** The 1.41% lifetime AFR is pulled down by young 20TB+ drives; in the same table, several models averaging five to eight years of age run 3.2% to 9.3%. Do not use 1.41% as "the failure rate of your old drive" — the article's whole point is the gap between that average and the aged cohorts.
- **FLAGGED — vendor specs and list prices.** The Synology wattage is the manufacturer's own measurement under a stated test setup; the B2 rate is a dated list price (as of 2026-10-17). Neither is an independent audit, and B2's price is not a like-for-like quote for a home NAS (egress, versioning and restore time all differ). Label both in prose.
- **FLAGGED — rebuild mechanics are inference, not a statistic.** The draft may say a rebuild reads every surviving drive end to end, and that the surviving drives are the same cohorts the fleet table shows failing faster; that is reasoning from claims 5 and 9. It may **not** assert a rebuild failure rate, a rebuild duration, or that a second failure is likely — no retrievable primary source for any of those is in this brief's set.
- **FLAGGED — no retail disk prices.** Every price host probed refused the verifier (403/404), so the article carries no retail drive price. The budget rule must be written in per-TB-year form with the reader's own purchase price, and the only dollar anchors are the EIA rate and B2's list price.
- **REMOVED —** SMART's predictive value, the share of homelabbers who replace on schedule, any vendor's claimed MTBF or workload rating, and any statement that a specific drive model is good or bad (Backblaze's own note says the low-count models' rates are noisy).
