## FLAGGED — scope rulings (carry each one; do not smooth them away)
1. **The two anchors are not contemporaneous and do not measure the same thing.** Adobe's lift is a
   two-day event measured against an average September day in 2026. PepsiCo's quarter is the 12 weeks
   ended 5 September 2026 — it closed a month *before* the event — and its figures are segment organic
   revenue and margin. The article must present the pair as two shapes of one lever, never as cause and
   effect, and must never imply PepsiCo's numbers were moved by the October event.
2. **Adobe's percentages are not incremental demand and cannot be netted.** +158% is event-day sales
   against a September average daily baseline from Adobe's own transaction panel; it includes any
   volume pulled forward from later weeks, and the source states no counterfactual. Do not write
   "incremental", "new demand", or a net-of-cannibalisation figure.
3. **The 280 bps is PepsiCo's own attribution, not a controlled result.** The same sentence credits the
   lap of a prior-year asset-sale gain and higher advertising, and credits productivity savings and higher
   organic volume as offsets. Write it as the company's stated cause ("reflecting the impact of
   affordability investments"), never as "price cuts cost 280 bps".
4. **The 2% North American volume figure is the trade report's, not the Q3 release's.** The release
   gives PBNA's organic volume direction without a number. Attribute the 2% to the report ("Supply Chain
   Dive reported…") or drop it; never present it as a Q3 segment figure.
5. **$178M of tariff refunds flatters the consolidated core operating profit by 4 points.** Do not add it
   to, or subtract it from, the segment margin figures; if the consolidated margin is cited, say the
   refunds helped.
