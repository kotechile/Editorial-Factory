## Derived arithmetic (no source needed — show the inputs in the body)
- **7 days:** 2026-09-08 (Proclamations 11064/11065 issued) → 2026-09-15 (collection ordered) = 7 calendar days. **7 days:** 2026-10-23 (signature) → 2026-10-30 (effective) = 7 calendar days.
- **30 days:** 2026-09-08 + 30 = 2026-10-08, which is exactly the closing boundary of the pleaded 30-Day Subclass. 2026-10-23 + 30 = **2026-11-22**, the first date on which the plaintiff's theory would allow collection on Part B goods newly enumerated on 23 October.
- **33 days:** 2026-07-20 (original proclamations) → 2026-08-22 (duties effective, after the three-day suspension) = 33 days, which is why the original action cleared the 30-day bar and the amendments are the contested ones.

## Drafter rulings (binding)
1. **Never state as settled law that the 23 October additions were unlawfully collected.** The 30-day question is the plaintiff's pleaded theory (C2, A2, A3); the government has not yet answered (X1). Attribute every legal conclusion — "the complaint argues", "on the plaintiff's reading" — and give the government's contrary reading its own sentence: the clock ran from the 20 July 2026 finding, and amendments do not restart it.
2. **Do not say the court has ruled, ordered refunds, or blocked collection.** Nothing has been decided. The described status is: complaint filed 2026-10-20, motions filed alongside it, answer due 2026-12-21.
3. **The 50 percent figure is a rate, not a dollar cost.** Report Gellert's dollars only as the complaint's own figures, tied to the entry window: "more than $381,000" on entries made 2026-09-15 through 2026-10-08; "at least $520,538" on 22 entries 2026-09-16 → 2026-10-11. Do not annualise them or multiply them by anything.
4. **"An estimated $40 million per year" is the complaint's estimate** of one importer's annual duty — attribute it, and do not present it as a measured run rate.
5. **Section 232 stacking** may be stated only as the proclamation states it ("shall apply in addition to duties imposed pursuant to section 232"). Do not repeat the "up to 100 percent" combined figure — that is the complaint's characterisation of a White House statement, and it is not in either anchor.
6. **Drop the $20 billion** figure for the covered Canadian imports. It appears only in trade press, not in either primary; the article does not need it.
7. **Do not imply the annex is final.** Both lists are HTSUS annexes that CBP implements, and Part A/Part B each carry a civil-aircraft carve-out (General Note 6) — say "unless the good meets the civil-aircraft criteria" if the piece names product lines at all.
8. **The enumeration date is the article's whole point.** Every section that discusses cost must answer "enumerated when?" — not "what is the rate?".
