**Emergent Collision Point:** A supply chain plan is a claim about capacity, and it is only as good as its model of whatever binds that capacity. The vendor's own mix says where the buyer's money has just moved: off the services line and onto the subscription. That matters because the same company's annual filing defines services revenue as the implementation, configuration and training work — "system planning, design, configuration, testing, and other software implementation support" — and says in the same sentence that those services "are typically optional and distinct from our software" (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056696/000119312526037138/manh-20251231.htm). It is also the line that bends first: services revenue fell $22.5M in fiscal 2025 to $503.0M, which the filing attributes to "customer budgetary constraints that shifted services work to future periods". Gartner's 5 November research says the constraint that decides whether the plan executes has itself moved — to electricity at a specific site, where "capacity shortages" and "interconnection delays" can hold a facility opening for years. Neither document says the two are related. Put together, they describe a loop: the subscription runs a standard model and is contractually insulated, the configuration work is optional and deferred when money tightens, and the physical constraint that decides feasibility is exactly the thing only the deferred work models. The crossover was $21,000 wide — a rounding error on a $306M quarter, which is the point: the milestones of a business model do not announce themselves, and this one is now permanent in direction because the cloud line compounds while the services line does not.

**Hook:** The week supply chain software started earning more from access than from the work of fitting a model to one network, Gartner said the thing that now decides whether a plan can execute is a power connection at one site.
**Tension:** The planning organization is measured on the accuracy of the forecast and the service level it delivers, while the input that decides whether the plan can be executed at all — a grid connection with a multi-year queue — is owned by a utility, a landlord and a capital committee. The vendor's economics reward the recurring line; the buyer's constraints live in the optional one.
