**Emergent Collision Point:** The US has charged an exit tax since 2008 and publishes a quarterly ledger of who left it; Italy has spent years selling residence — flat tax, investor visas — and by IMI's reading was, with Portugal, one of the last popular destinations that let a resident go without a deemed sale of their assets. On the same day — 30 October 2026 — one published a count of departures and the other put a price on departure. Neither document says the other's story: together they make the price of leaving the third column in a cross-border plan, and they show that the toll booth is now appearing at the destination end of the corridor, not only the American one.
**Hook:** On 30 October the US Treasury printed 1,331 names of people who left the US tax system in the third quarter, and Italy's government sent parliament a bill that would take 26% of the unrealised gains of anyone who leaves Italy after six of the last ten years.
**Tension:** Capital-importing countries have been in a bidding war for wealthy arrivals — flat taxes, golden visas, remittance holidays — and an exit tax is the opposite instrument: it raises the cost of the *next* move, which is the one thing an ageing economy trying to attract people cannot afford to make more expensive. The technical report's own numbers make the contradiction plain: households above the €800,000 threshold hold 73.1% of household financial wealth, and the measure is expected to raise €6.74 million a year — against refunds it expects to pay on half of the up-front payments. Meanwhile the American ledger shows the flow it prices is volatile rather than one-way: the quarter fell 16.4% year on year while the nine-month total rose 16.2%.
**Target reader:** cross_border_expat
**Single claim to defend:** An exit tax is becoming a standard feature of the countries people move *to*, not only the one they leave, so a relocation model that prices entry and residence but not departure is now incomplete — and the US quarterly list is the only public evidence of what that departure costs in practice.
