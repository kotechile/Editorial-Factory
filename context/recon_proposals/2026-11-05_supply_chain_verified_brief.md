## FLAGGED — carry the caveat, do not smooth it

1. **The Long Beach September figures rest on a trade write-up of the port's own briefing.** `polb.com` answers HTTP 403 to this run's fetcher, so the port's release page could not be read directly; Railway Age reports the figures from the port's 2026-10-15 Supply Chain Insight briefing and attributes them to chief executive Dr. Noel Hacegaba. The Los Angeles figures ARE read from the port's own statistics table, so the article may lead on Los Angeles and attribute Long Beach explicitly to the port briefing as reported. Do not present the Long Beach numbers as if read from a port page this run fetched.
2. **"Inland" is Cushman & Wakefield's own classification and the national report does not enumerate it.** The five markets named in B2 (Houston, New Jersey, Inland Empire, Savannah, Miami) are the report's **port** markets that improved, not its inland markets. The article may quote "80% of YTD absorption" and must not attach a market list to the word inland.
3. **No causal claim.** C&W does not say the inland shift caused the rail backlog, and FreightWaves does not say the backlog is caused by inland demand. The article may juxtapose the two and describe the mechanism (freight absorbed inland must leave the dock by rail; rail is the slow leg), and must not assert that one produced the other.
4. **A4 is a one-week snapshot** from a third-party congestion tracker, not a trend series. Say "one ship" for the week of Oct. 26, never "no ships" as a general condition.
5. **The rail inventory count is not an average wait** (A5). Do not convert 35,688 containers into "X days of cargo"; the age distribution (10,562 at nine days or more) is the load-bearing fact, not the total.
6. **Empty-container share is not anomalous.** Long Beach empties are 39.7% of September boxes and Los Angeles 35.6%; both ports' empties also grew (Los Angeles +20.4%, Long Beach +22.1%). If the article uses the empties, it must present the share as structural, not as a September spike — the desk has no prior-year share for Long Beach in this run and must not imply one rose.
