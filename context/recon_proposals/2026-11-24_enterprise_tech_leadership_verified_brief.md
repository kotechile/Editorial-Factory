## Binding rulings (write these into the copy; the gates cannot read them)

- **FLAG 1 — never present $6,860,836 as market spend.** The report's own sentence attributes it: one $2M NetSuite bill is 29% of the total. Write it as "shared bills", never as an aggregate market figure.
- **FLAG 2 — n=3.** ServiceTitan's $120,000 median rests on three shared bills. Always carry the n; never write it as an industry benchmark.
- **FLAG 3 — the 32% is McKinsey's figure as quoted by HissingKitty, and the survey was fielded outside the 30-day window.** Use it attributed ("as the report cites McKinsey's survey"), never as a HissingKitty measurement, and never as an in-window datapoint. The McKinsey page itself is listed in the signals file's dated background; do not cite it as a source of record.
- **The report is a first edition, two weeks of data, self-selected and self-reported.** Its own methodology section says so. The article's claims are about what the cohort reports, not what the market does. No "we tested" anywhere; the desk ran nothing.
- **Do not generalise the €300/month counter-case.** It is one solo founder's backoffice. It is a boundary condition on the thesis (savings exist at the low end of SaaS spend), not evidence that building pays in general.
- **Prohibited framings:** "building is a failure" (the report documents builds that succeeded functionally — the unproven part is the savings); "SaaS is overpriced" (the report is a pressure ranking, and says so); any claim that 26 builds is representative of enterprise procurement.
- **Corroborating clauses:** one2n's committed-spend line and the EmailEngine counter-case each appear at most once, attributed, with no argument resting on them.
