# Angle Brief: agentic_resilience_failure — 2026-11-04

**Angle Type:** Single-Signal
**Winner:** Your ten-minute spike ends; your engine does not — a brief surge can push an agent-serving engine past a boundary it cannot walk back from at the same request rate, because the sessions the burst left behind keep each other alive, and the only move that ended it in the published run was killing the burst's own sessions rather than adding capacity
**Scores:** N=9.5 A=9.5 S=9.5 → Composite=9.5 (0.40·9.5 + 0.30·9.5 + 0.30·9.5)
**Hook:** At a base load of 3.3 sessions a minute, an engine served a median first token in about 0.2 seconds. A ten-minute burst of twice that rate ended, the arrivals returned to 3.3, and the engine got worse: median first token reached about 60 seconds, the prefix hit rate fell from 0.95 to 0.27, and sessions arriving in the hour after the burst finished at 17 an hour instead of 138. Utilization, queue length and memory use showed headroom the whole way in. When the authors terminated the sessions the burst had created, service came back in two minutes; when they let those sessions finish on their own, the population kept growing for another hour at the load it used to hold.
**Tension:** Every capacity conversation on this beat is about the ceiling — how many sessions fit, what to cap, how much cache to buy — and the desk priced that ceiling two days ago. This measurement is about the floor beneath it: the same engine, at the same load, healthy at 09:00 and pathologically slow at 10:00, with no change to the code, the model or the traffic. The feedback is structural: a session ends only after its last turn, so slow calls keep sessions alive; live sessions' combined context is the working set; when it passes roughly twice the cache capacity, prefixes are evicted before their sessions return, and every turn becomes a full recompute. That recompute is what makes the next call slow. The party that gains is the team that can see its active-session population and working set as first-class metrics, because that is the quantity that decides whether the fix will take. The party exposed is the team whose recovery runbook is a capacity increase: in this paper more cache moves the cliff further out and leaves the engine just as vulnerable to a finite surge, and in the production incident that closes this piece the recovery backfill had to be paused because it caused delays in another part of the service.
**Target reader:** infra_engineer
**Single claim to defend:** A transient surge can leave an agent service persistently degraded at a load it previously sustained, because the accumulated sessions — not the surge — sustain the failure; so recovery has to be measured as a property of the state left behind (active-session population, working set against the cache cliff, and departures exceeding arrivals) rather than declared when the trigger clears, and the recovery action that worked was retiring the burst's sessions, not buying capacity.
