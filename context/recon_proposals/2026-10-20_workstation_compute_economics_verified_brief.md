## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Never say the RTX 3090 runs Bolt.** NVIDIA's table lists RTX discrete cards at "32GB+ / 48GB+"; a 24GB Ampere card is not in it. The 3090 figure is about the *price of 24GB on the secondhand market*, not about compatibility. If the piece mentions compatibility at all it says the vendor's table starts at 32GB.
2. **Do not claim unified memory is faster or better.** The claim is capacity per dollar, and the piece must state the bandwidth trade in the same breath (B5: capacity is not equivalent to dedicated VRAM; the GPU's share depends on configuration).
3. **"1 petaflop" only with its qualifier** — a theoretical peak with sparsity (B5). Best to leave it out; if used, qualify it.
4. **The benchmarks are NVIDIA's own** (A4). Say so. No independent reproduction exists in this window.
5. **Two currencies, no exchange-rate arithmetic.** The 5090 price is euros (C1); the laptops are dollars (B2, B4). Never convert, never subtract one from the other.
6. **The retail trackers are listing snapshots, not transaction averages.** Say "tracked", not "sold for", except for the used-GPU series, which is a sold-listing average (C2).
7. **Authorised desk arithmetic (any other derived number is forbidden):** 28GB recipe − 24GB card = 4GB short; $5,109 ÷ 128GB = $39.9/GB; $6,999.99 ÷ 128GB = $54.7/GB; $2,599 ÷ 24GB = $108/GB; €6,499.90 ÷ 32GB = €203/GB; $6,999.99 vs $5,109 = 37% more for the same capacity; €6,499.90 vs €4,499.90 = +€2,000, about +44%; $1,344 vs $701 = about +92%; 83.99 vs 96.63 and 100.56 TPS = slower than both. Label each as the desk's arithmetic.
8. **Do not state a launch MSRP for the RTX 5090.** The only launch reference in this run is dated background and secondary; the body uses the tracker's own ten-week series instead.
