## Notes for the drafter

- **The load-bearing claim is location, not the number.** Rows 3 and 7 are the article's spine: a split read at the grilles, or with an infrared gun at a vent, is not a split, and a charge verdict taken before airflow is proven (350 cfm/ton) is the standard misdiagnosis.
- **Use the eight-cause list (row 5) literally.** Its rhetorical force is ordinal: a malfunctioning compressor is item six of eight, sitting beside an undercharge, a stuck metering device and abnormally high humidity. Never write that a low split "means" a compressor.
- **Humidity is the honest caveat (row 6).** A low split on a humid afternoon can be the system doing its job on latent load, which is why one reading is not a diagnosis and trending matters more than a single number.
- **Cost math may only be built from rows 10 and 13.** A 10–20% efficiency loss on RMI's $719 summer bill is $72–$144 at RMI's own reading; if the article re-prices that loss at EIA's 17.30¢, it must show the arithmetic and label it as the desk's recomputation, not as either source's figure. Do not multiply a percentage by a bill and present the product as a quoted statistic.
- **Quote the statute's units exactly:** 13.4 SEER2 and 14.3 SEER2 / 7.5 HSPF2, and "manufactured on or after January 1, 2023" — the floor is on manufacture, not on install, and not on sale.
