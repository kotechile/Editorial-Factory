## Notes for the drafter
- The story is **state loss, not downtime**. Do not describe this as an outage with a long duration:
  AWS's words are "unable to restore access to the resources and data hosted exclusively in this
  Region", and it "exhausted every option". Never write that all customer data in the region is gone —
  the scope is data with no copy outside it, which AWS states twice.
- Do not name a state actor. AWS's attribution is "the conflict in the Middle East" and "drone
  strikes"; the Reuters headline says "after war". Keep it at that level.
- The counterfactual the piece turns on is claim #7: the design document enumerates the hazards the
  architecture was built against, and the event that exceeded the envelope is not on the list.
- "144" is the count of AWS services listed as disrupted for the Bahrain event, not a count of
  affected companies or of lost files. State it that way.
- Any figure not in this table — the number of affected customers, the value of the lost data, the
  cost of the rebuild — is **not** in a primary source and must not appear.
