## Gate rulings for the drafter (binding)

1. **The OIR ledger is a two-week snapshot, not a final closure rate.** The WUSF figures are "as of Tuesday" after an Oct. 9 landfall; Michael's $9.1 billion and ~159,000 claims are a settled multi-year tally. If the article contrasts the two payment ratios it must say so in the same breath — a young claim is not a denied claim. Never write "the denial rate" as though the storm were finished.
2. **"Closed without payment" ≠ "flood claim denied."** Those 8,500 are all lines — wind claims under a deductible, auto total losses, commercial — and flood claims from Isaias sit with NFIP (the $250 million Moody's RMS figure), not in this ledger. State the counts as measured; do not attribute them to flood, and do not add them to anything.
3. **NFIP is not the only water payer.** Private flood is a live market ($784 million net premiums written in 2025) and is unaffected by an NFIP lapse. The article may say the federal program is the mass-market one; it may not say it is the only one.
4. **Dates: keep Dec. 11.** Insurance Journal writes "midnight Dec. 12", which is the same instant as 11:59 p.m. Dec. 11. Use the FEMA phrasing and never print "Dec. 12" beside "Dec. 11" as if they were two dates.
5. **The Nov. 11 date is the desk's arithmetic.** Present it as computed from the 30-day wait plus the Dec. 11 authority end, and only as a date by which a purchase would have taken effect — not as a deadline FEMA has published.
6. **FEMA's English reauthorization page is stale** (it still says Sept. 30, 2026). Cite the Dec. 11 date to the updated record; do not cite the English page at all. Do not cite congress.gov's CRS URL for a figure the trade record already carries.
7. **B7 stays attributed.** "A GAO report" as reported in the trade press — no expansion of what "at-risk" means, no state figures, no reform proposals.
8. **Do not grade the storm.** Cotality's $2B–$3B (Oct. 12) and Moody's RMS's $2.5B–$4.5B and KCC's $3B are three modellers' estimates of different scopes on different dates; the OIR $582 million is Florida-only regulatory reporting and is not comparable to any of them. If the article uses one modeller it may use it alone, labelled as an estimate, with its scope.
9. **The Colorado material is out of scope for this artifact.** It belongs to a later run; do not import it.
10. **No figure for what flood cover costs.** No source in this run carries an NFIP premium, so the article must not imply a price or a payback. The decision it prices is a date, not a premium.
