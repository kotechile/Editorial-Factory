## Gate rulings for the drafter
- **The debt number is a growth rate, not a saving.** 89.76% smaller describes the *slope* of accumulated technical debt after migration. It does **not** mean the codebase's debt fell by 90%; the same paper records an initial spike caused by developing the new services. Never write it as a debt reduction.
- **The premium is "more than 20%", never a precise 20%**, and it is the company's own architects' and project manager's reported increase in overall development cost — a reported figure, not a modelled one.
- **Segment is first-party narrative, not a controlled experiment.** Say so once in the body: a company describing its own consolidation. The 32-versus-46 improvement comparison is the company's own count.
- **Arithmetic the article is authorised to do:** price three full-time engineers at the O*NET median ($135,980 each → $407,940 a year) and note that this equals about three times the annual premium a 20%-plus increase would add to a 280K-line codebase's development cost only if the reader supplies their own budget — do **not** invent a company budget to complete that sum. The only arithmetic the piece may state as its own is: 3 × $135,980 = $407,940; and a service ceiling of one service per 5-8 engineers means a 25-engineer org supports roughly 3 to 5 service scopes, not 48. Both are labelled as the desk's arithmetic on the cited figures.
- **No invented thresholds.** The piece may say the split pays when the annual carrying cost of the debt exceeds the measured premium; it may not put a percentage on "carrying cost" that no source states.
