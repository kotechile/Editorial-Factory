## Gate rulings for the drafter

- **F1 — FLAGGED, load-bearing.** 88,000 is the *forecast scope* of a planned event, not a count of homes that lost power. PG&E writes "may impact approximately 88,000 customers". Never write "88,000 homes lost power" or "88,000 were shut off". The measured de-energization figure in this window is 10,410 (row 4).
- **F2 — FLAGGED.** The CEC's three components (16,000 + 2,000 + 3,000) sum to 21,000 against a stated total of 21,112. The release is rounding each component. Use "about" on every component and never present the sum as a measured total.
- **F3 — FLAGGED.** EIA marks 2026 values as preliminary estimates "based on a cutoff model sample". Say the figure is EIA's estimate for August 2026, not an audited final.
- **F4 — FLAGGED.** "A grid battery cannot energize a de-energized distribution line" is an engineering inference, not a quoted sentence. Support the household-level consequence with SCE's own circuit explanation (row 15) and PG&E's own scope (row 1); do not put the inference in a source's mouth.
- **F5 — do not total the regimes.** The 29.75-cent retail rate, the export credit and the lost federal credit are three separate mechanisms read side by side. They are not additive: an export credit is not a discount on the retail rate, and the expired credit was a tax provision, not a bill line.
- **F6 — arithmetic is authorised only where stated.** Permitted derived figures: 63.8 percent above the U.S. average (29.75 ÷ 18.16), 85.3 / 14.2 percent fleet split, 3.8 percent decline in the California rate year over year (30.92 → 29.75), 3.4 percent rise in the U.S. rate (17.57 → 18.16). No other derived sum, ratio or interval may appear.
- **F7 — the honest counterweight.** The California rate *fell* 3.8 percent year over year in August (30.92 → 29.75) while the U.S. average rose 3.4 percent. Any claim that California rates are climbing is false on this data, and the piece must say the state's price eased even as its reliability exposure did not.
