# Angle Brief: agentic_resilience_failure — 2026-10-14

**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)
**Winner:** Retry Budgets Fail: Agents Retry 60 of 80 Anyway — the retry decision has left the caller, and the caller is the one thing that can be told to stop
**Scores:** E=9.5 A=9.0 S=9.5 → Composite=9.4 (0.35·9.5 + 0.30·9.0 + 0.35·9.5 = 9.35)
**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Uber Engineering, "How Uber Protects Against Retry Storms", 2026-09-17 — retries amplify across a call chain as R^ɗ and (1+B)^ɗ, so a 10% retry budget at depth 3 still costs 1.33 × Ƞ; below ~90% callee availability a retry *lowers* the caller's perceived availability (80% → 88%, 70% → 77% at a 10% budget), because "retry behavior today isn't context-aware… we can't precisely control **when** they occur" and a service cannot "reliably distinguish between errors generated by a service and those merely propagated through it"; the fix shipped across Uber's mesh is **error ownership** — an `x-uber-error-claim` header where "the first node to see a missing error claim from a downstream unclaims the error, limiting the impact radius of the retry disturbance (it's no longer a storm)" — which took the max retry-storm radius across all user-facing APIs from **25 → 3** and the average from **20 → 2**. (https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/protecting-against-retry-storms/)
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** arXiv:2610.15884 "RetryBench: Separating Retry Permission from Recovery Outcomes in GPT Tool Use", submitted 2026-10-11 — "False retry permission (FRP) occurs when an agent retries a timed-out tool call without support from the visible contract", and "successful protected recovery does not imply reliable rejection of unsupported retries": offered changed keys "elicited unsupported retries in Sol (1/80), Terra (55/80), and Luna (60/80)", and in a later 240-response control that required models to supply a key, Luna still made 8/80 unsupported dispatches. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15884)
**Emergent Collision Point:** Uber's protocol answers "who owns this error?" with a wire header and a rule the caller must honour: if the downstream did not claim the error, you do not retry it. The agent-side benchmark measures the caller that protocol now has to police — an agent that grants itself retry permission when the visible contract does not support it (55/80 and 60/80 responses), and whose success does not prove it refused the unsupported retries. Neither source can state the fusion: Uber reports what ownership does to traffic when the caller complies (25 → 3), RetryBench reports that the caller's compliance is a separate, measurable capability that success hides. Read together, they move the retry decision out of the caller entirely — the caller cannot be trusted to be honest about whether the retry was permitted, so permission has to be granted per call at the point that executes it, which is the tool boundary of the gateway, not the SDK, the model, or the retry budget.
**Hook:** Uber cut the worst retry storm its mesh can produce from 25 hops to three by moving the decision to retry away from the caller: an error carries a claim header, and the first service that sees an unclaimed error tells everything upstream to stop. The same week's benchmark on agent tool calls finds the opposite behaviour in the caller Uber is now admitting to its services — offered a key that does not match the contract, one frontier model still retried in 60 of 80 responses, and success at the end of a run says nothing about whether it refused the retries it was not entitled to.
**Tension:** Two reliability programmes are shipping at cross purposes. The platform side has just spent years making retries honest: budgets, jitter, breakers, and now an ownership header — all of which work by telling the caller *no*, and all of which are validated at scale (Uber's max retry-storm radius 25 → 3; a 150-incident compound-AI catalog measures circuit breakers cutting cascade propagation 89%, isolation cutting blast radius 64%, and three-or-more-pattern systems cutting MTTR 71% against unstructured monitoring). Two weeks later, the agent side of the same mesh is where the caller stops being a modelled service: Uber's MCP Gateway is the single control surface for 800+ MCP servers and 5,000+ tools, agents are the callers, and the retry decision inside an agent is made by a model whose permission signal is a guess. The party that gains is whoever owns the execution boundary for tool calls — an MCP gateway, a proxy, an SDK with `max_retries=0` — because that is the only layer that can decide whether a call may be resent with knowledge of what it already did. The party that is exposed is every team treating "the gateway handles retries" or "the mesh has an ownership protocol" as an inherited guarantee.
**Target reader:** infra_engineer
**Single claim to defend:** Retry permission has become a call-boundary contract that the caller can no longer be trusted to adjudicate — an error-ownership header only works on a caller that can read and honour it, agents grant themselves unsupported retries at measurable rates (55/80 and 60/80 in a changed-key condition), and success after the retry hides which permission was real; so the permission must be granted (and refused) per call, at the layer that knows what the call already did, and the evidence that a retry gate is safe has to be designed in advance rather than read out of logs.
