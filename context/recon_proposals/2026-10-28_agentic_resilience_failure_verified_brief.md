## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Rows 1–3 are one claim in two registers.** The −5/+35 swing is an *identity* under stated assumptions
   (an exact alarm-to-action relation, a clean restart, zero monitoring cost), and the paper says so twice.
   The article may open on it, but the sentence that follows must place it as what the arithmetic says, and
   the measured part (rows 5–6) is what carries the evidence. Do not write "a real 90% monitor loses five
   points"; write that a monitor at that accuracy can be either, and that what decides it is a quantity the
   classifier never reports.
2. **Keep the two RippleCP numbers apart.** 49.4 s per task is the *pooled* figure and it is dominated by the
   first checkpoint; the article must show the decomposition (100.0 s on 156.5 s, then −1.1 s on 41.8 s) in the
   same breath or the pooled number reads as "checkpointing pays everywhere", which is the opposite of the paper.
3. **Never attach "zero of 103" to monitors in general.** The 103 are *live task-time comparisons* in this
   study's traces across six decision times; the paper's own wording is that the certificate is unresolved, not
   that every restart is worthless ("They do not demonstrate that every alternative statistical method must abstain").
4. **The 42.29-point loss is a transfer result**, not a measured failure of restarting on the second model: a
   cutoff selected on the first model was applied to the second. Name the two models' behaviour (68.57% → 26.29%).
5. **CASEL (rows 14–15) is a simulator study.** If cited, say so in the same sentence. Its intervention step is
   assumed, so it can show what allocation rules rank under assumptions and cannot show deployment effectiveness.
6. **DeltaBox (row 18) is a design statement.** It is admissible as friction — what the industry ships — and must
   never be used to support a number. Do not describe its 203 stars as adoption evidence in a trend claim; a star
   count is a dated snapshot.
7. **No hands-on claim.** This run has no deployment of its own; the article reports other people's measurements.
8. **Prior-edition / adjacent-thesis bar:** the 2026-10-21 article for this vertical (`isolation-isnt-detection`)
   argued that silent wrong values are not noticed, and cited the Meta orchestrator and RITE. None of those
   sources or figures may be re-used here; the "Ten of Eighty-Four" field log (84 incidents, 10 caught
   automatically) is dated background only and no argument may rest on it.
9. **Acronyms to gloss at first use:** none is forced by the sources beyond the benchmark names. "SymPy" is a
   Python symbolic-math library; "SWE-bench Verified" is a set of real software-engineering issues with hidden
   tests — gloss both in passing, and never leave "SWE" bare.
