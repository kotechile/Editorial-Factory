**Decision the reader is facing:** Which object-storage meter to put a data-serving workload on — the headline storage price, or the egress meter that decides what it costs every time the data leaves — and how to compute the crossover before committing. It bites the week a nightly export, a customer download link or a cross-cloud sync starts moving more data than it stores, and someone notices the storage line is $40 while the transfer line is $360.
**Durability:** The four rate cards compared here are standing published prices with no expiry in their own pages — AWS internet egress $0.09/GB (Europe, Ireland) and inter-region $0.02/GB, Google Cloud Standard storage $0.020/GB-month ($0.000027397/GB-hour) with a 365-day Archive minimum, Cloudflare R2 storage $0.015/GB-month with $4.50-per-million Class A operations, Backblaze B2 $6.95/TB-month with $0.01/GB egress beyond 3x stored — all as of 2026-11-25 (retrieved and verified today). The mechanism — egress metered per GB out while storage is metered per GB-month, and the vendors who abolished the egress meter recovering it in a higher storage line — has been stable across the industry for years and is a business-model fact, not a quarter's promotion. If any single rate changes, the crossover arithmetic in the article recomputes from the same three published numbers.

This brief prices the *movement* of data across the network boundary — a recurring per-GB transfer meter — and the two vendors whose business model abolished that meter (R2 free egress, B2 free to 3x), which no published artifact in the corpus argues: `grep -ril "egress\|Cloudflare R2\|Backblaze" published/` returns only passing mentions in other verticals (agent-runtime and local-AI pieces), none of them pricing a transfer-out line. This is not the same argument with different numbers: one says opening the archive is expensive; this one says that where the data *goes* is the line that decides the bill, even when nothing is archived at all.
**Thesis:** Object storage is billed on the object's life — storage per GB-month, movement per GB out — so on AWS and Google Cloud one gigabyte leaving the cloud costs 3-6x what keeping it there costs per month, while the two vendors that abolished the egress meter sell you the storage instead: Backblaze B2 at $6.95/TB-month with egress free to 3x of stored data and Cloudflare R2 at $0.015/GB-month with egress always free — which means the right meter is chosen by the egress-to-storage ratio, not the storage list price: a 2 TB store moving 4 TB a month out costs about $360 in egress on AWS against about $14 total on B2, and the vendor that looks cheapest at rest is the most expensive one serving.
