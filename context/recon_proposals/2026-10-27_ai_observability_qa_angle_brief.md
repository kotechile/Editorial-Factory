# Angle Brief: ai_observability_qa — 2026-10-27
**Angle Type:** Single-Signal
**Winner:** Your agent's cache savings fail silently, at the invocation site — not in the trace, not in the bill
**Scores:** N=8.0 A=9.0 S=8.5 → Composite=8.5
**Hook:** A team studied 120 prompt-caching pull requests across 10 open-source agent systems and found that most of the mis-integrations fail silently — no exception, no latency spike, no alert — and that linting the invocation site against a dated provider capability oracle found 32 more across 571 projects, with 18 upstream fixes behind them including one in Anthropic's own SDK.
**Signal:** arXiv:2610.34817, "Silent and Costly: Characterizing and Detecting Prompt-Caching Mis-Integration in LLM Agents" (2026-10-26 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34817).
**Tension:** Caching is sold as a switch, but it is a contract with one provider's API at one point in time, and every agent framework, gateway and prompt-layout convention adds invocation sites that carry it. When a site drifts — a breakpoint that no longer covers the stable head, a read-side counter that measures the wrong tokens — the bill still looks like a bill: expensive, not wrong. The teams hurt are the ones that already did the layout work and stopped looking; the teams helped are the ones who can now check a site without reproducing a traffic pattern.
**Target reader:** evals_infra_eng (AI QA & Evals Infrastructure Engineer)
**Single claim to defend:** A prompt-cache defect is a correctness defect that lives at an invocation site and is decidable against the provider's capability on a date — so only a site-level precondition check plus the three usage counters can catch it, and neither the trace nor the bill ever will.
