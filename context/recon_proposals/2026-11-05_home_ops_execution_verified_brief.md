## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Do not state that a low lien index means contractors are being paid better, or worse.** Row 10 is the whole hinge: NCS says the index cannot distinguish the two causes, and the article's argument is that the spending data decides between them. Any sentence that presents the index's fall as proof of distress, without the caveat, is unsupported by the source.
2. **Attribute the 13.0% and 18.0% improvement figures to NAHB's reading of the Census release, not to the Census release itself.** The release PDF carries improvements only as a footnote inside private residential; the split is NAHB's.
3. **Never divide 2,442 by 73, or compare the CSLB action count to the bankruptcy count as a rate.** They are different populations counted by different bodies; row 15 is context only.
4. **Do not say liens are "filed at the end of a job" as a legal rule.** Write it as the source frames it — a lien is the step that follows unpaid work being furnished, and the index falls when fewer projects reach that stage (row 10). The statutory clocks live in the 2026-10-08 evergreen and are linked, not restated.
5. **"Improvement spending" is the Census category name for remodeling and repair; use it once with a gloss and then write "remodeling".**
6. **No claim about what a reader should do.** The tactical section reports what the data says about cash between draws, in the observer voice.
7. **The JCHS 1.5% is a projection, not a measurement.** Say "projects" or "expects", never "fell to".
8. **The 8.9%, 5.5% and 13.0% are three different bases** (private residential year-over-year, private residential month-over-month, improvement month-over-month). Never blend them in one sentence.
