The week of 15–16 October moved both halves of the build-vs-rent decision at once, and in opposite directions.
NVIDIA's new local agent model is labelled a **24GB** build, but the vendor's own deployment table asks for
28 GB and 32 GB recipes on discrete cards of **32GB+ / 48GB+**, and its headline speed (84 tokens a second) was
measured on a Surface Laptop Ultra with **128GB** of unified memory. The same day, those laptops went on sale:
24GB from $2,599, 128GB from $5,109 (HP's 1TB OmniBook X) to $6,999.99 (Asus's 2TB ProArt P16). Meanwhile the
32GB discrete card the recipes assume listed at **€6,499.90** in France on 16 October, up €500 in one week and
€2,000 in nine, on a tracker where nine of sixteen cards did not move at all and none fell — and the cheapest
route to 24GB on a card, a six-year-old used RTX 3090, was $1,361, up 47% on its twelve-month average.
