# Angle Brief: resilient_home_assets — 2026-10-23
**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)
**Winner:** Does a record 745 MW of home-battery dispatch prove the household is now a power plant — or that its aggregator is?
**Scores:** E=9.0 A=8.5 S=9.0 → Composite=8.9
**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Sunrun's 2026-10-14 release: home batteries delivered 745 MW on 2026-10-06 and 669 MW on 2026-10-05, against a prior residential record of 580 MW (2026-09-09) and 539 MW (2025-07-29), dispatched "at the request of the California Energy Commission and utilities" after CAISO's day-ahead locational marginal price "exceeded $200 per megawatt-hour", with "more than 120,000 residential batteries enrolled" at Sunrun alone (https://investors.sunrun.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/384/sunrun-leads-record-grid-dispatch-delivering-enough-energy).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** The CEC's DSGS program record: Option 3 pays storage VPP *aggregators* a performance-based capacity rate of $9.00 to $19.20 per kW-month by duration class — an "Annual Total ($/kW)" of $82.80 for the 4-hour class and $62.10 for the 2-hour class — and "Storage VPP aggregators shall allocate incentive payments between the storage VPP aggregator and its participants pursuant to the terms and conditions agreed to between the VPP aggregator and participant"; the season runs May 1 to October 31 and up to $42.7M funds Option 3 in the 2026 season (https://dsgs.olivineinc.com/faq).
**Emergent Collision Point:** The record is real and the money is real, but they belong to different parties. The dispatch record measures what the aggregator's portfolio can deliver; the program pays that portfolio a capacity rate and leaves the household's share to a private contract, while the appropriation behind the rate is a 2026-season pool with no new money allocated for 2027. Neither source says this: the release frames 745 MW as proof that "the energy infrastructure already installed in people's homes can deliver power at a scale that makes a real difference", and the program record frames the rate as an incentive to customers. Put together, the household is the fuel supply and the aggregator is the counterparty — and the season that pays for it closes on 2026-10-31, 25 days after the record was set.
**Hook:** 120,000 home batteries pushed 745 MW into California's grid on 6 October — a national record — 25 days before the program that pays for those dispatches ends its season with no money set aside for the next one.
**Tension:** California built the country's only statewide residential power plant on a device homeowners bought for outages, then paid for it through a program whose rate is set to the aggregator and whose household split is a contract term. That makes the enrollee a supplier with no published price, in a market where the buyer's funding is annual.
**Target reader:** pro_homeowner
**Single claim to defend:** The 745 MW record is evidence about the aggregator's portfolio, not about the homeowner's return: the program pays per kW of demonstrated capacity to the aggregator, the household's cut is whatever its contract says, and the 2026 pool is the only money currently appropriated.
