## Evidence rulings for the drafter
- **The three counts answer three different questions, and that is the article.** California's § 17014(a) defines a resident as someone in the state for other than a temporary or transitory purpose, or a domiciliary outside it for a temporary or transitory purpose; (c) says a resident stays a resident while temporarily absent; (d) is the only bright line and it is conditional. Oregon's 316.027(1)(a)(A) is a three-part *escape* for a domiciliary (no abode in state, an abode elsewhere, ≤30 days) and (B) a *trap* for a non-domiciliary (abode + >200 days). Virginia taxes on either domicile "at any time during the taxable year" or a place of abode for more than 183 days. Do not present 183 as a national rule or average the three.
- **California's shelter is written for "an employment-related contract" — that is the founder's catch, and it is the statute's own wording.** A self-employed reader has no such contract, so § 17014(d) does not apply to them at all and their absence is judged under (a) and (c), where the only question is purpose (temporary or transitory) and no day count settles it. State this as the statute's condition, never as an agency ruling.
- **The 546-day harbour has two more conditions that a plausible draft will drop:** returns must aggregate to ≤45 days in the taxable year (a single long visit is fine; a summer plus two holidays may not be), and the whole subdivision is unavailable where intangible income exceeds $200,000 in a year the contract is in effect — a landlord or a portfolio holder can be outside (d) on income alone.
- **Virginia's own definition is the article's cleanest sentence about domicile:** "the permanent place of residence of a taxpayer and the place to which he intends to return even though he may actually reside elsewhere". Use it verbatim for the intent limb; it is on row 6's page, quoted in the verified companion.
- **Oregon's 30 days is an allowance, not a residence test:** it is a condition of the domiciliary's escape, so a reader who keeps the Portland house cannot use it no matter how few days they spend there. The 200-day limb applies to someone who is *not* domiciled in Oregon but keeps an abode there — a different reader.
- **Do not quote a dollar saving.** These statutes set residency, not rates; the article carries no state tax rate and no computed bill, because no fetchable page in this brief states one and the desk will not invent a rate.
- **Units:** 546 days is 78 weeks, which is 18 months — say "78 weeks" in the body and "a year and a half" once, never "1.5 years" beside "546 days" as if they were two findings.
