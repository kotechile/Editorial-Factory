## Arithmetic the article performs (recorded so the rewrite cannot drift)
All conversions use HHV heat rates the article states: simple-cycle turbine at 35% = 9,750 Btu/kWh; reciprocating gas engine at 42% = 8,120 Btu/kWh; combined cycle at 52% = 6,560 Btu/kWh. CO2 factor 53.06 kg/mmBtu (row 2).

- Heat input per MW = heat rate ÷ 1,000 mmBtu per MWh. So 1 MW = 9.75 mmBtu/h (35%), 8.12 (42%), 6.56 (52%).
- 30 mmBtu/h ÷ heat input = 3.1 MW (35%), 3.7 MW (42%), 4.6 MW (52%).
- 25,000 t ÷ 53.06 kg/mmBtu = 471,165 mmBtu/yr. Flat (8,760 h) = 53.8 mmBtu/h → 5.5 MW (35%), 6.6 MW (42%), 8.2 MW (52%). At 50% duty cycle: 11.0 / 13.2 / 16.4 MW. At 20%: 27.6 / 33.1 / 41.0 MW.
- 75,000 t = 1,413,494 mmBtu/yr → flat 161.4 mmBtu/h → 16.5 MW (35%), 19.9 MW (42%), 24.6 MW (52%).
- NOx at 1.0 g/HP-hr, 1 MW = 1,341 hp: 1,341 g/h × 8,760 h = 11.75 t/yr per MW → 100 tpy at 8.5 MW flat. At 2.0 g/HP-hr: 23.49 t/yr per MW → 100 tpy at 4.3 MW flat.
- Turbine at 2.3 lb/MWh: 8,760 MWh × 2.3 lb = 20,148 lb = 9.14 t/yr per MW → 100 tpy at 10.9 MW flat.
- Emissions intensity: 53.06 kg/mmBtu × heat rate = 517 kg/MWh (35%) = 1.14 lb/kWh; 431 kg/MWh (42%) = 0.95 lb/kWh; 348 kg/MWh (52%) = 0.77 lb/kWh. Against 0.81 lb/kWh U.S. average and 0.96 lb/kWh for gas-fired grid generation (row 8).
- 20 MW reciprocating plant flat: 162.4 mmBtu/h × 8,760 = 1,422,624 mmBtu/yr × 53.06 = 75,484 t CO2e — the PSD line almost exactly.
- A 20 MW fleet at the 2.0 g/HP-hr emergency standard: 100 h/yr = 5.4 t NOx; flat = 470 t NOx (4.7× the Title V line); at the non-emergency 1.0 g/HP-hr standard, flat = 235 t.
