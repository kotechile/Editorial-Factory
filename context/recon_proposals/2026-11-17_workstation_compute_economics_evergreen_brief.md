**Decision the reader is facing:** Whether a multi-week training or evaluation job should run on spot GPU capacity instead of on-demand, and — if it does — how often the job must write a checkpoint so that the reclaim notice never costs more than the discount buys. The decision bites the week a run's wall clock outgrows the gap between interruptions, when the team has already committed to the cheaper lane and discovers that the thing it must build to survive there is a checkpoint pipeline nobody scoped.
**Durability:** The load-bearing content is a 1974/2004 result and a vendor mechanic, not a price: the optimum checkpoint interval is the square root of twice the failure interval times the checkpoint write cost, and the reclaim notice is a fixed number of seconds. Every figure is dated — the optimum-interval derivation and the 840-interruption worked example are **as of** arXiv 2410.18124v1, read 2026-11-17; the 419-unexpected-interruptions measurement and the above-90% effective-training-time claim are **as of** the Llama 3 herd paper (arXiv 2407.21783v1), a fixed historical snapshot of a 54-day pre-training period; the 91% ceiling on Spot discounts and the 24-hour preemptible cap are **as of** Google Cloud's Spot and preemptible documentation read 2026-11-17 and can be re-issued at the vendor's discretion; and the 30-second eviction notice is **as of** Microsoft's Azure Spot VM documentation read 2026-11-17. **Thesis:** Spot capacity is priced up to 91% below on-demand but is reclaimed on a 30-second notice that no large-model checkpoint can use, so the discount is not the economics — the economics are the checkpoint interval √(2 × failure interval × write cost), which at Llama 3's measured 3.09-hour failure interval and a one-minute write is about 19 minutes, and a rented job whose checkpoint takes longer than that interval is buying a cheaper clock, not a cheaper run.

**Lead:** The vertical's own first beat (`primary_angles`: "local workstation build vs cloud gpu compute matrix"), read as the half of the rent side the corpus has never priced. Every prior artifact in this vertical prices the *hour* — `2026-10-06_local-ai-payback-cloud-gpu-spread` the capex-to-rental spread, `2026-10-06_fine-tune-vs-rent-by-token` the per-token comparison, `2026-11-17_your-boxs-hidden-79-speed-cut` the local box's hidden speed cut against a published rental price table — and none prices the hours a rented job *loses*, which is the one line item where the workstation wins outright because its data is already on its own disk.
