**Durability:** The arithmetic is textbook-stable and does not expire: safety stock is Z × (standard deviation of *forecast error*) × √(exposure period ÷ forecast period), the z-ladder for a cycle service level is a fixed statistical constant (90% → 1.28, 95% → 1.65, 99% → 2.33), and the normality correction σ ≈ 1.25 × MAD is a distributional identity. The worked figures are quoted **as of the cited pages (retrieved 2026-10-08)** and are illustrative inputs (demand, σ, lead time), not market prices; the *method* stays true for years, and the article states each figure's source so a reader can re-run the math on its own SKUs. The only thing that moves is the reader's own error term, which is the point.

The 2026-10-01 piece is about AI autonomy adoption; neither touches the safety-stock formula.
**Thesis:** Safety stock is a working-capital line sized off *how wrong the forecast is*, not *how much demand moves* — and the two standard short-cuts push the number in opposite directions (sizing off demand volatility over-buffers, 26 units where the forecast error warrants 10; swapping in a raw forecast-error metric while keeping the textbook z-value under-sizes it, so a planned 95% service level arrives at 92.7%) — which means the right move is to feed the buffer the forecast's own error with the correct z↔MAD conversion and to price each service-level step as the working-capital decision it is.
