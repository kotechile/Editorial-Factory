**Emergent Collision Point:** The two load-bearing variables in any heat-pump TCO break-even — the annual savings that justify the switch and the upfront price those savings must pay back — are moving in opposite directions in the same 30-day window. RMI's number says the running-cost case for switching is stronger than ever; the manufacturers' September price list says the ticket price is climbing 5–15% with more to come. Neither source says the other's half of the sentence.

**Hook:** In the same week an independent study put the heat-pump savings for Maryland's oil-and-propane households at $1,600 a year, the HVAC manufacturers pushed through another round of 5–15% price increases — and Carrier's CEO told analysts he "can and will continue to raise price."

**Tension:** The homeowner doing the break-even math is told "switching saves $1,500 a year" while the quote in front of them keeps ticking up. For the ~330,000 Maryland delivered-fuel households (and the millions like them nationwide), the financing question shifts from "does it pay for itself?" to "does it pay for itself faster than the equipment price inflates?" Strong demand (Carrier's forecast was raised, not cut) means neither manufacturer feels pressure to hold price.
