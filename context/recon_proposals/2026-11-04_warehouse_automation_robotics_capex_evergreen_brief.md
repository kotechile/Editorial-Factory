**The specific material.** The code answers three questions the vendor's quote does not. *What class is the asset in* — nonresidential real property runs 39 years on the mid-month convention, while qualified improvement property sits in the 15-year class and drops out of it the moment the spend is attributable to an enlargement, an elevator or escalator, or the building's internal structural framework. *What allowance is available* — qualified property (a recovery period of 20 years or less, original use beginning with the taxpayer) takes a 100 percent additional first-year allowance unless the taxpayer elects out, and 39-year property takes none of it. *Which year the deduction lands in* — the year the asset is ready and available for its specific use, which for an un-integrated robot is not the year the crate arrived, and which for the personal property can be pushed into the mid-quarter convention when more than 40 percent of the year's bases are placed in service in the last three months.

**Authorised arithmetic (derived figures the article may state, each computed from the rows below; nothing else is authorised):**

- A $700,000 building-work line in the 39-year class recovers at **1/39 = 2.564 percent a year, about $17,950 a year** ($700,000 ÷ 39 = $17,948.72). The first year is less again because the mid-month convention allows only the months it was in service; state the annual figure and say the first year is pro-rated, never quote a pro-rated figure this brief has not fixed.
- The same $700,000 classified as qualified improvement property (15-year class, and therefore inside the 20-year test) takes the **100 percent allowance: $700,000 in year one**. Year-one difference on that one line: **about $682,050** ($700,000 − $17,948.72).
- **39.0×** is the ratio between the two schedules' first-year treatment ($700,000 ÷ $17,948.72 = 39.0) — write it as "thirty-nine times" and name the $700,000 line it belongs to, never as an industry average.
- A $2,400,000 project split $1,700,000 machines and $700,000 building work is **$1,717,950 of first-year deduction** if the building line is 39-year property and **$2,400,000** if the whole line clears the interior-improvement test — the gap is the same $682,050, and it exists on the same project with the same throughput.
- The Section 179 ceiling does not bind this project: **$2,500,000** is above the $1,700,000 equipment line, and the **$4,000,000** phase-out start is above the whole $2,400,000. It binds a programme, not a building — and it still cannot exceed the business's own taxable income, which is the limit a loss year hits.
