## Notes for the drafter and verifier

- **The $125 exam fee and the 3-year validity period ride in prose, not in a row.** The gate's matcher returns the window around the *first* occurrence of the row's first numeric token, and on `cloud.google.com` the token `125` first appears in the site's product menu ("transcription across 125 languages") while `3 years` first appears in a Cloud GPUs blurb. Both sentences are genuinely on the page and were read by eye: "Standard exam information Length: 2 hours Registration fee: $125 (plus tax where applicable)" and "Validity period: 3 years". Cite the page, quote the sentence, and do not give either figure a gate row of its own — the row that carries the page leads on `$75`, which lands inside the renewal block.
- **The 122 hours is a sum, not a page figure.** The page prints six per-course hours (19, 22, 28, 24, 23, 6) and a "3 months at 10 hours a week" design. The article must show the addition and say it is the sum of the six course cards; it must never write "Coursera says 122 hours". The same goes for "about three-to-six months", which is the page's completion estimate for a learner's pace.
- **O*NET's hourly figure is the annual median over 2,080 hours.** So "122 hours is 5.87% of a year's pay" is true by construction at every wage on the page ($3,628 at $29.74; $7,976 at $65.38 — both 5.87% of the annual median). Present it as arithmetic on the page's own two numbers, never as a survey of what anyone earned after a course.
- **A rung-step is not a certified premium.** $99,130 against $61,860 is the gap between two occupations' medians, not the return on a certificate. The article must say the certificate does not itself produce that step, that the reader's own offer decides it, and that the median gap is only the size of the prize if the move happens. This is the honest version of the claim the two earlier runs refused to fake.
- **The 75% is the vendor's own measure, and it is weak by design.** Quote it with its qualifier ("a positive career outcome (e.g., new job, promotion, or raise)", self-reported, United States 2022) and make the point that the number cannot distinguish a $500 raise from a promotion — which is precisely why the reader must run the arithmetic themself.
- **Renewal is the amortisation mechanic.** The Associate Cloud Engineer credential is valid 3 years and the renewal exam costs $75, so the cash cost is $25 a year — but the renewal exam is a further hour of the reader's time, which at $47.66 is worth more than the fee. Do not call the renewal free.
- **No chart.** The figures are list prices and two medians; no percentage series exists to plot.
- **Accessibility.** Expand O*NET, ACE and IT at first use; keep Flesch at or above 50 (target 60); prefer short sentences — the derived-ratio section is where long clauses creep in.
