## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Do not generalise the $885.** Apple's page prints the range as "Get $35–$885 for your trade-in" on the Duo's buy page without naming a model. The article may write "$885, the top of the range Apple quotes on the Duo's own buy page" and note that the top figure belongs to the previous flagship (X-row A6). It may not write "your iPhone is worth $885".
2. **The $999 is a configuration-specific service price for one device**, the iPhone Duo, from Apple's out-of-warranty list, and Apple says Authorized Service Providers set their own fees. Say both. Never present it as what a third-party shop will charge.
3. **No Apple price other than the ones in this table.** iFixit's 18 Pro cells are stale (X1) and PCMag's insured cell is wrong (X2). Google's $568 and Samsung's $579/$419/$139 stand — they come from iFixit's own reading of each vendor's estimator and are the only third-party figures in the piece.
4. **"Repairability" must not be scored.** iFixit published no X-out-of-10 for the Duo; its sentence is "the most repairable foldable smartphone we've ever taken apart". Quote that, never a number.
5. **The plan is a subscription, not a warranty extension.** Apple removed multi-year prepay in 2025 and sells monthly/annual "until cancelled"; the article must not imply a fixed two-year price. Cite the yearly figure with that word.
6. **Two batteries is iFixit's explanation and Apple's page never says it.** Attribute it.
7. **Retained but quiet:** the CNET $119→$129 battery figure belongs to the 18 Pro only and matches Apple's live page for the 17 ($119) and 18 Pro ($129); it is fine as one corroborating line, never as the piece's spine.
