## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **The 60.0% is not a real-world failure rate.** It is the share released by the unchecked policy under a fixed mutation mix in which 60% of mutations invalidate the decision *by construction*. Never write it as "60% of agent writes are wrong". State it as: the study built five mutations per scenario, two of them safe and three not, and the unchecked policy let all of the unsafe ones through.
2. **The 20% / 40% durable-cell numbers are exact-outcome agreement, not success rates of a deployed system.** Write "matched the exact operational outcome in 20% of the ten durable cells", never "worked 20% of the time".
3. **The study does not run an LLM inside its durable cells and does not extract the premises automatically.** Both caveats must survive into the article; they are the honest limit of the whole thesis.
4. **No derived arithmetic.** Do not compute a percentage saved, a failure reduction, a ratio of 6.0 to 0.6, or a sum of 8% and 1.6–2.3×. State the two measured endpoints side by side. Do not add 37.2% and 29.9% into a third number — quote 67.1% as the benchmark's own figure.
5. **Names and dates are load-bearing.** Yongjian Lyu and Yang Ren (Huawei), arXiv:2609.08015v1, 7 Sep 2026; Sajjad Khan, arXiv:2606.17182v1, 15 Jun 2026; Xin Yang, Letian Li and colleagues, arXiv:2608.18092v1, 6 Jun 2026. Documented AWS and Temporal limits as of 2026-10-21.
6. **Expand every acronym at first use** — Amazon SQS (Simple Queue Service), and do not carry the paper's runtime name "ATR" as a bare token: call it a selective-revalidation runtime. Do not write "MAS".
7. **The three clocks belong together in the transport paragraph**: 24 hours of retries, 4 days of retention (14 maximum), 2,000 pending signals per execution — each is a documented product default as of 2026-10-21, not a measurement.
