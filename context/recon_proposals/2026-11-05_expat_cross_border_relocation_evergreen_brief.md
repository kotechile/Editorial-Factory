**Decision the reader is facing:** Whether to buy the local index and mutual funds the host country's broker sells in the reader's own taxable US account — and what every one of those funds obliges them to file each year they hold it, sale or no sale.
**Durability:** The mechanism is statutory and has not changed in decades: the definition at 26 U.S.C. § 1297, the excess-distribution method at § 1291, the interest charge at § 1291(c) by reference to § 6621, and the filing rule at § 1298(f). The load-bearing thresholds — 75 percent income test, 50 percent asset test, 125 percent excess-distribution line, the $25,000 / $50,000 reporting exception — are in the Code and in the IRS instructions for Form 8621 as of the 2024 edition of the U.S. Code and the December 2025 revision of those instructions. The only figure that moves is the top marginal rate the regime applies: the instructions' own table prints 37% as the highest rate under § 1 for 2018 through 2025 as of that December 2025 revision, and the capital-gain comparison rate is 20% as of IRS Topic 409 for 2025.

**Thesis:** A fund registered in the reader's new country is a passive foreign investment company to the United States, and the default rule does not tax the gain once at capital-gain rates — it spreads the whole gain across every year of the holding period, charges each slice at that year's top ordinary rate, and adds interest for the deferral, which is why the most expensive part of the decision is made at the first purchase rather than at the sale.

## Evidence rulings for the drafter
- **The $25,000 exception is a filing exemption, not a tax exemption.** The operative sentence in the instructions is narrower than the heading: a shareholder need not complete Part I if the stock is worth $25,000 or less **and** they "do not receive an excess distribution from, or recognize gain on the sale or disposition of, the stock" — so the exemption disappears in the year of the sale, which is the year the tax is due. $50,000 is the joint-return figure; the instructions state it as a combined threshold.
- **The 125 percent line is the distribution test, not the sale test.** The instructions state that the entire gain on a disposition is treated as an excess distribution; the 125-percent-of-three-year-average test governs ordinary distributions from the fund.
- **The rate applied to each slice is the top rate for the year the slice is allocated to**, not the top rate in the year of sale — the instructions print a table of the highest § 1 rate by year (2018–2025: 37%).
- **Elections exist but each has a condition.** Mark-to-market is available only for "marketable stock" (§ 1296 via the instructions); the QEF election requires the fund to supply annual information, which a UCITS fund typically does not. Both are prose claims under the verified rows — do not turn either into a promise.
- **Form 8621 is filed per fund, per year** (§ 1298(f) and the instructions' "a separate Form 8621 must be filed for each PFIC"). Five local funds is five returns a year.
- The 37% and 20% figures are rate comparisons, not a computed saving: the article may state that the slice is taxed at the top ordinary rate instead of the 20% capital-gain rate, and must not present 37-minus-20 as the total cost, because the interest charge sits on top of it.
