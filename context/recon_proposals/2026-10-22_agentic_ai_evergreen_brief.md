**Decision the reader is facing:** How to configure the retrieval funnel that feeds an agent's L3 semantic memory tier — how many candidates to fetch, how to cut the chunks, whether to spend a rerank pass, and at what corpus size the tier stops earning its place — when every rung of that funnel costs latency and prompt tokens the runtime must budget per agent step.

**Thesis:** The agent's memory lookup is a funnel whose three rungs are priced differently — in the vendor's own benchmark, contextualising every chunk and then reranking cut the top-20-chunk retrieval failure rate from 5.7% to 1.9% (a 67% reduction) while the pipeline fetched 150 candidates to keep 20; in Chroma's independent token-level study a 400-token, no-overlap split reached 89.5% recall against 85.4% for the 800-token/400-overlap sizing OpenAI documents as its default, and strategies spread by up to 9% in recall; and in a third study the embedding model alone was worth 7.44% average F1 while semantic chunking paid extra compute for gains that did not hold — so the funnel order is model, boundary, rerank, and below roughly 200,000 tokens of corpus the honest answer is to skip retrieval and put the documents in the prompt.
