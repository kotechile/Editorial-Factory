**Rules read out of the same pages by eye and carried in prose rather than in a row** (each recorded here with its verbatim, because a digit-free rule cannot lead a gate row and a short-integer cell evidences on a page's own subsection numbering):

- § 4902(c), "Final termination": "in no case may such a requirement be imposed on residential mortgage transactions beyond the first day of the month immediately following the date that is the midpoint of the amortization period of the loan if the mortgagor is current on the payments required by the terms of the mortgage." On a 30-year note the midpoint is 180 months, which is the backstop: the premium cannot legally run past year 15 while the borrower is current.
- § 4902(a), "Borrower cancellation": the requirement "shall be canceled on the cancellation date … if the mortgagor" submits "a request in writing to the servicer", has "a good payment history", "is current on the payments", and satisfies the holder's requirement for "evidence … that the value of the property securing the mortgage has not declined below the original value" plus "certification that the equity of the mortgagor in the residence securing the mortgage is unencumbered by a subordinate lien". The request, not the calendar, is what starts this clock — and the evidence test is about *decline below the original value*, not about today's appreciation.
- § 4902(g), "Exception": a lender may keep the requirement past the termination date for a loan it classed as high-risk at origination, on terms disclosed to the borrower — the one carve-out that turns the automatic 78 percent date off.
- § 4903(a)(1)(A), "Disclosures for new mortgages": at consummation the servicer must give the borrower "a written initial amortization schedule" and written notice of the date on which cancellation may be requested "based solely on the initial amortization schedule", that cancellation may be requested "earlier than provided for in the initial amortization schedule, based on actual payments", and "what that termination date is with respect to that mortgage" — so the two dates are already in the borrower's own closing file.
