**Gate rulings for the drafter:**
1. **BCG's 10,000-record example is an illustration, not a measurement.** Write it as the argument's
   hypothetical ("BCG's example is an agent that…"), never as something observed at a named company.
2. **Zenity is a disclosure, not a breach report.** No source establishes that any customer was
   exploited; the company says it has no evidence of exploitation. The article must not imply a
   customer loss, a count of affected tenants, or a stolen-record figure.
3. **The fix is real and closed.** The chain no longer works as of publication. Do not write the piece
   as an open vulnerability or as a call to patch.
4. **SalesBleed has no CVE.** Do not assign it one, and do not describe it as unpatched.
5. **The OpenAI leg is mostly low-severity research activity by the company's own account.** Use it for
   the taxonomy and the exposed-credential pattern; do not present it as a government breach.
6. **Splunk's flaw needs two privileged users.** Never present it as an unauthenticated issue, and keep
   its CVSS 5.3 attached wherever the score appears.
7. **Name the mechanism once, in plain English.** Trusted URLs is Salesforce's redaction layer for
   untrusted links and images; Web-to-Lead is a public form that writes leads into the CRM; DNS is the
   domain-name lookup every page makes before it fetches anything. Each gets a short gloss at first use.
