## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Arithmetic the desk performs (authorised, label it as arithmetic):** 7 × 16 = 112 SMs of 132 (84.8%); 7 × 406 = 2,842 GiB/s of the card's 3,175 (89.5%); 4 × 26 = 104 SMs and 4 × 812 = 3,248 GiB/s for four 1g.24gb slices, with 4 × 23 = 92 GiB booked; 39.6 ÷ 276.3 = 14.3%. Recompute each in the final from these endpoints; never quote a derived ratio from the draft.
2. **Two bindings, one test.** SM-bound work (the paper's quantized Llama3) tracks the SM profile; bandwidth-bound work (long-context, batched decode) tracks the memory slice. The separating test is which profile change moves tokens a second — grow the SM-bearing profile alone, then grow the memory slice alone.
3. **The copy path is a Grace Hopper NVLink-C2C measurement.** Never call it PCIe and never transfer 39.6 GiB/s to a PCIe host. The paper also flags the size-independence as "likely a bug", so write it as a measured anomaly, not a design property.
4. **Every performance figure comes from the two papers.** The two vendor pages carry partition shapes and the slice definition only — they publish no throughput.
5. **Write the SMs as measured (16 per 1g on this 96 GB part), and say the vendor's "roughly one seventh" is approximate.** Do not print "18.9 SMs".
6. **Do not present MPS as the villain or MIG as the answer.** The co-execution study gives MPS up to 30% in favourable conditions and MIG the isolation that removes a ~30% contention loss, while noting MIG's rigid granularity can underutilise.
7. **No price anywhere.** No source here prices a card, a slice or an instance; the decision is a resource allocation, not a purchase.
8. Expand at first use: MIG (Multi-Instance GPU), SM (streaming multiprocessor), HBM (high bandwidth memory), KV, DMA if used, GiB/GB only as the sources print them.
