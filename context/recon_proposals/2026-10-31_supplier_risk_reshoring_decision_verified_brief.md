## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **The deferral is not a zero and not a relief.** Write "no tariff was imposed" and, in the same breath, "the rate is undecided" — never "robots are tariff-free", never "exempt". The proclamation sets no rate; the fallback list explicitly includes tariffs, duties, quotas and fees.
2. **Keep the two hedges that carry meaning:** "could reach 400,000" is the association's projection and must not be reported as a 2026 result; "approximately 309,000" is a projection from first-half announcements.
3. **The 14-of-64 conversion figure is the honest counterweight** to the record announcements and belongs in the catch: announcements are not projects.
4. **Do not import the Section 338 Canada story.** This vertical published it on 2026-10-24 (`7-days-notice-rewrites-your-duty`); the same rule change cannot be re-argued inside seven days. If a duty contrast is needed, use the machinery deferral against the pre-existing Section 232 duties already in force on steel, aluminum, copper, automobiles and trucks (proclamation, fact sheet) — a contrast between categories, not a second Canadian leg.
5. **Attribute the "third time in three months" count to the trade press.** The White House documents do not count their own deferrals.
6. **Do not describe the review as retroactive, a refund window, or a deadline for importers.** Nothing in the proclamation sets an entry-date rule; it sets a reporting date. The distinction between a reporting date and an action date is the piece's whole point, so it must not be blurred.
7. **TCO is not a synonym for landed cost.** The report measures "total cost of ownership analysis" as a named method; keep the phrase and do not rewrite it as "landed cost modelling" or "TCO feel" in later mentions — one method, one name.
