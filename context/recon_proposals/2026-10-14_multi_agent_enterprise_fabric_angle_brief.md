**Signal A (Anchor 1):** "CreepScope: Measuring Post-Use Authorization Exposure in Tool-Using Agents" — measured 2026-10-11, published as arXiv:2610.15988 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15988). Load-bearing figure: a 96-cell deterministic replay over 12 storage, communication and cloud-operation workflows in which all three persistent-grant regimes share the same stale-after-use exposure of 13 capability-steps, an oracle-controlled step lease holds zero by construction, and Full Access alone adds 91.33 never-required capability-steps — then an executed 24-episode mail workflow in which an injected stale send runs in all nine persistent-policy episodes and in none of the three step-lease episodes.
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** "When Agents Talk, Secrets Travel: Topology-Driven Leakage and Recipient-Conditioned Sanitization in Multi-Agent LLM Systems" — measured 2026-10-12, published as arXiv:2610.16039 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16039). Load-bearing figure: across 485 scenarios over three topologies and seven instrumented channels, disclosure to topology-introduced recipients rises from 0% to 52.2% while surface leakage stays nearly flat at 60.6–62.8%, framework choice alone drives a 150x leakage spread, and the recipient-conditioned sanitizer cuts residual disclosure from 96.7% to 29.25% while keeping 94.1% of authorized disclosures.
**Emergent Collision Point:** Neither paper states the product. CreepScope fixes the *time* axis — the per-call authorization check says nothing about how long an earlier grant stays reachable — and Secrets Travel fixes the *path* axis — the same disclosure is legitimate to one agent and illegitimate to the agent the coordination graph introduced, with no sensitivity label able to tell them apart. Multiply them and the fabric's exposure surface is (how long every grant stays live) × (how many hops the graph can carry it along), and **both factors grow when you add an agent**, while every control shipping this quarter governs a single request: a registry that inventories nodes and issues identifiers (Google Cloud Agent Registry page, last updated 2026-10-08 — https://docs.cloud.google.com/agent-registry/overview), an identity service that decides "who makes the request", and a compiled per-call policy gate. A node-level artifact cannot bound a lease or an edge — which is why the risk register written in requests understates what the fabric leaks.
**Hook:** Open on the executed case, not the framing: three standing-grant policies, the same 13 stale capability-steps, and a stale send that fires in nine of nine persistent-policy episodes and zero of three step-lease episodes (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15988) — then change only the shape of the graph, and watch 52.2% of protected facts reach an agent the topology introduced, up from 0% (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16039).
**Tension:** Adding an agent reads as adding capability and is sold as adding oversight; measured, it also adds grant lifetime and relay paths, and the two controls that ship do not measure either. It threatens the platform team that has already bought the inventory and signed off the least-privilege matrix; it empowers the architect who asks for a *lease* and a *recipient* — but only if the detector behind the lease holds up, which is where both papers put their own residual (the sanitizer's detection coverage accounts for 95.5% of what it still lets through; the containment case is explicitly one workflow, one small model).
**Target reader:** ai_architect (AI Systems & Distributed Architect — rigorous, protocol-literate; MCP contracts, state recovery, RPC boundaries)
**Single claim to defend:** In an agent fabric, authority and information outlive the call that justified them, so the unit of exposure is the lease and the path — and a per-request permission model, however well compiled, cannot bound what the fabric leaks.
