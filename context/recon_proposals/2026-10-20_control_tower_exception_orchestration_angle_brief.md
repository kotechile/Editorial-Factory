**Emergent Collision Point:** The industry's most mature digital standard is a single document, and twenty days before that survey landed, DCSA widened the visibility standard, while the survey itself measures that the document still stalls at the seam between platforms rather than at the schema or the law: 51.1% adoption and 46.2% of respondents naming interoperability as the barrier. Widening the container standard from three event categories (shipment, transport, equipment) to five, with condition data (a geofence crossing, a reefer alarm, a setpoint change, each emitted by a different party on a different clock), multiplies the number of readings of the same shipment at exactly the layer the survey says is unsolved. Neither publisher says this: the standard's benefit is stated as fewer disputes, and the survey's finding is that disputes have moved from definitions to transfer.
**Hook:** On 30 September 2026 the Digital Container Shipping Association published the version of its container-visibility standard that finally carries sensor data — and walled it off for members until the second half of 2027. Twenty days later, the industry's own bill-of-lading survey reported that the document standard it has promoted since 2022 still fails where platforms meet: 46.2% of open-ended answers named interoperability, not law, as the barrier.
**Tension:** The control tower's integration work was never field mapping for its own sake; it was deciding whose timestamp to believe. Standards now publish the field names, and the largest visibility vendor has started selling completed work rather than the visibility product ("AI models are becoming a commodity", FourKites, 2026-10-15), which prices the mapping layer down from both directions. What none of that settles is authority: when the carrier's discharge event, the terminal's gate-out and a reefer alarm disagree, a tower still has to rank them, and no standard in this window defines that ranking. The architect who treats 3.0.0 as a 2027 upgrade waits three quarters while the exception queue keeps growing; the one who treats the mapping work as the moat keeps funding a layer the industry is giving away.
