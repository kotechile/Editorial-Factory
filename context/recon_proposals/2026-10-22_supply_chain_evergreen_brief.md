**Decision the reader is facing:** How many days of stock a forward pick face should hold, and which SKUs get the golden zone — because that single sizing parameter sets both the distance the pickers walk and the number of replenishment trips the lift drivers make, and a slotting list built on velocity alone quietly pays for one crew's saving out of the other crew's labour.

**Durability:** The trade-off is structural, not a market level: a pick face's days of supply is its capacity divided by its pick rate, and moving that number moves walking against replenishment in a ratio that holds whatever software or rate card a warehouse runs. The figures are dated where they are dated — the wage anchor is O*NET's 2025 median for stockers and order fillers (median wages (2025) $17.95 hourly, $37,330 annual, as of the 2025 O*NET release), the pick/replenishment comparison and the walk-time share come from trade-press articles dated 2015 and 2025 whose mechanics are unchanged, and the travel reduction is a 2025 modelled estimate for one facility. **Thesis:** A pick face is a capacity decision before it is a location decision: a face holding a week of stock spares the replenishment crew to one pallet a week but costs the pickers an extra 10 miles of walking in that same week, so a slotting list ranked on velocity alone moves work between two labour pools rather than removing it — and the number that settles the trade is the face's days of supply, not its rank.
