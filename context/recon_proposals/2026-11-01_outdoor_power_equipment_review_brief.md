## Derived arithmetic (computed from printed endpoints, never estimated)

- **Flow at working pressure is flat, and falls at the top.** 1.2 GPM (2,300) → 1.2 GPM (2,700) → 1.1 GPM (3,000): the top rung moves **8.3% less water** at its own peak pressure than the two below it (1.1 ÷ 1.2 = 0.917).
- **PSI climbs 30.4% while flow does not.** 3,000 ÷ 2,300 = 1.304 across the ladder, against a flow change of −8.3% at the top and 0% in the middle.
- **Sale price, not spec, sets the order.** $312.99 → $349.99 → $379.99: the 3,000 is **$30.00 under** the 2,700 and **$37.00 over** the 2,300.
- **Dollars per PSI:** $312.99 ÷ 2,300 = **$0.136**; $379.99 ÷ 2,700 = **$0.141**; $349.99 ÷ 3,000 = **$0.117**. The most powerful unit is the cheapest per pound of rated pressure — because it is the most heavily discounted (30% against 15%).

- **The accessory ceiling:** 2,300 PSI (surface cleaner) against 3,000 PSI (top rung) = the machine is **30.4%** above the cleaner's rating; 700 PSI.
- **Same SKU claim, two prices:** $424.99 − $349.99 = **$75.00** for the same "3000 PSI / 2.0 GPM" headline on the same store.

## What the article must carry

- **The two box numbers are measured at two different nozzles, and the vendor says so.** The Greenworks sentence is explicit — "3000 Max PSI at 1.1 GPM water flow (2.0 GPM flow at 100 PSI)" (row 10); DEWALT publishes the same qualification as footnotes, max PSI at the 15-degree nozzle and max GPM at the soap nozzle (row 17). So any "cleaning units" product of the two headline numbers multiplies a pressure and a flow that never occur together. Say that plainly; do not compute the product.
- **100 PSI is rinse pressure.** The max-flow figure describes the widest, lowest-pressure nozzle, which is the soap/rinse setting — a garden hose is in that neighbourhood. Label it as the vendor's own condition, not a measurement.
- **What actually changes across the ladder is pressure, not pace.** Rows 2, 6, 10 and 12: the same 14-amp brushless motor and the same 25-foot hose on all three, so the difference a buyer feels is what the water strips, not how fast it covers. The 2,700 and the 2,300 move the same 1.2 GPM at pressure; the 3,000 gives up flow for its extra 700 PSI.
- **The purchase today is a markdown, not a lineup.** 30% (row 11) against 15% (row 7) and 21% (row 3) is why the top rung sits $30.00 under the middle one. A reader buying on the PSI ladder's logic pays more for less flow; a reader buying the markdown gets the most pressure for the least money per rated PSI — and still the least water at pressure. Present both, then the verdict by situation.
- **The trap is the accessory, and it is priced.** The brand's own universal 12-inch rotating surface cleaner is $39.99 and rated to a maximum of 2,300 PSI (rows 14, 15) — the rating of the cheapest machine and 700 PSI below the top rung. The head that makes a driveway fast is the part not specced to the machine that would drive it fastest. The 2,700 also ships as a combo with a 15-inch cleaner and wand extension for $479.98, which is the accessory priced inside the box rather than after it; mention it as the alternative to buying the head separately, and label the combo price as the manufacturer's own bundle.
- **A second SKU carries the same headline $75.00 higher** (row 13): shop the SKU (5128802), not the series name — the documented buy-window failure of the last review in this vertical, repeated here with a different pair of numbers.
