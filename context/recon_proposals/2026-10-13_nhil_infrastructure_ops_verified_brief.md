## Gate notes for the drafter

1. **Dual-anchor gate: PASS.** Leg A carries 9 primary-verified claims from the OCP specification itself (A1–A9) plus the FERC order as reported (A10–A12); leg B carries 4 primary-verified claims from two arXiv records (B1–B4). Nothing is REMOVED; A15 and A16 are FLAGGED and must be attributed, not quoted as record.
2. **Freshness.** The two load-bearing anchors are in-window: the OCP spec is effective **2026-09-17** and the FERC order is dated **2026-10-09**. The July 22, 2026 event (A13) is the *illustration*, not an anchor — attribute it and date it every time it appears, and note that the spec's own introduction names it, which is what makes it citable at all.
3. **Audit the trigger, not just the number.** The ride-through seconds in A3 are a *minimum time before a site may transfer to backup*, measured from the moment the point-of-interconnection voltage leaves the normal band; PFAPR's clock starts when that voltage returns to 0.90–1.10 p.u. Do not write either as "the site must recover within 2 seconds of the fault" — the 2 seconds is the ride-through floor at a shallow dip and, separately, the recovery window after the voltage is back.
4. **Never invent an acronym expansion.** OCP is the Open Compute Project; PUCT is the Public Utility Commission of Texas; TCEQ is the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality; NERC is the North American Electric Reliability Corporation; FERC is the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission; MISO is the Midcontinent Independent System Operator; ERCOT is the Electric Reliability Council of Texas; LVRT is low-voltage ride-through; HVRT is high-voltage ride-through; PFAPR is post-fault active power recovery; VFD is variable frequency drive; MTTR is mean time to repair. Write each expansion in parenthesis at first use in the body.
5. **Only VERIFIED figures may appear.** The 3.8 GW / 2,970 MW + 1,099 MW figures come from A13 (out of window — background, attributed). The 3.9 GW figure used by RTO Insider is the same event at a different rounding; use one figure and one source, not both. Do not convert GW to MW or vice versa except as A13 already states.
6. **Do not overstate the corporate positions.** Google, Microsoft, Meta and NVIDIA authored a specification. There is no evidence any of them has deployed to it, and the spec itself grandfathers existing sites; say "published" and "committed in the specification", never "now operate".
