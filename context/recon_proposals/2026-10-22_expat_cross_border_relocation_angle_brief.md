**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Argentina's Economy Ministry announced the country's citizenship-by-investment programme on 2 October 2026 at Argentina Week in Paris — USD 350,000 non-refundable to the National Treasury, or an USD 800,000 public security created for the programme, USD 100,000 for a spouse or a child aged 18–25, USD 25,000 per minor, USD 500,000 for a family of four, applications open in the fourth quarter of 2026 (https://www.argentina.gob.ar/noticias/luis-caputo-anuncio-la-puesta-en-marcha-del-programa-de-ciudadania-por-inversion-de). Reuters carries the same two routes and the same Q4 timing (https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/argentina-announces-citizenship-by-investment-program-2026-10-02).

**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Portugal's Agency for Integration, Migration and Asylum published its 2025 Migration and Asylum Report on 22 September 2026: 1.585.854 foreign residents at 31 December 2025, 337.880 residence titles granted in the year (up 55% on 2024, growth the report attributes largely to completing pending files), 2.685 residence permits for investment granted all year, and only 1.324.411 of those 1.585.854 residents holding a valid title — the remainder being temporary protection, pending regularisations, or expired titles already paid for and awaiting renewal (https://aima.gov.pt/docs/Relatorio_de_Migracoes_e_Asilo_2025.pdf). The residence route ends at a naturalisation clock that Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026, in force on 19 May 2026, set at seven years for EU and Portuguese-speaking nationals and ten years for everyone else (https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/lei-organica/1-2026-1123539996).

**Emergent Collision Point:** Both states are selling the same object — the passport — and the two documents price it in different currencies. Argentina takes cash, up front, in a single quarter, and hands over citizenship; Portugal takes years of residence, paid in work, and then makes you wait out a statutory clock that was just doubled for most applicants. Neither document says the obvious thing that appears when they are read together: the investment channel that brokers sell as "buying your way into Europe" is a rounding error in Portugal's own numbers — 2.685 permits of 337.880, eight-tenths of one percent — while the country's actual intake is a labour queue of 1.59 million people, of whom 261,443 — one in six — do not hold a valid residence card. The market that just got a public price tag is not the European one.

**Hook:** Argentina will sell you a passport for $350,000 starting this quarter. Portugal's own migration report, published three weeks earlier, shows what the other route costs: 337,880 residence permits in a year, most of them for work, a stock of 1.59 million residents — and a naturalisation statute that now asks most applicants for ten years of it.

**Tension:** The buyer's mistake is treating the two prices as comparable. $350,000 buys an Argentine passport: a travel document, a second nationality, consular protection, and a route out of a citizenship-based tax system that does not follow you home. It does not buy a European residence right, a Schengen work permit, or the free movement that the Portuguese route eventually confers. Conversely, ten years of Portuguese residence is not a $350,000 product with a discount — it is a different asset, priced in years and payroll, and it is now ten years rather than five for most nationalities. The two markets are converging on the same buyer from opposite directions: the cash market needs the buyer to accept a weaker document, and the residence market needs the buyer to accept a longer wait.

**Target reader:** cross_border_expat

**Single claim to defend:** Argentina has put a public cash price on citizenship while Portugal's own data shows the European route is a labour queue ending in a ten-year clock — so the two are different assets, not two prices for the same thing, and only one of them buys the right to live and work in Europe.
