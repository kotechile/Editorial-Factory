**Decision the reader is facing:** Whether it is safe to release the final (or the next progress) payment to a general contractor on a residential remodel — and what the owner must file or collect, and by which deadline, so that a subcontractor or materials supplier the owner never hired cannot record a mechanics lien against the title after the money is gone.
**Durability:** The mechanics-lien clock is set by each state's statute, not by the homeowner's contract, and these figures are quoted as of 2026-10-08: the numbers below are the deadlines in force in the cited codes and contractor-board guidance. **Thesis:** The money a homeowner has already paid a contractor is not protection: a subcontractor's or supplier's mechanics lien runs on a statutory clock rather than on the contract, the owner holds only two real controls over it — recording a Notice of Completion to shorten the clock and collecting a signed unconditional lien release before releasing each payment — and the state recovery fund that backstops a contractor who vanishes caps out well below a typical remodel.
