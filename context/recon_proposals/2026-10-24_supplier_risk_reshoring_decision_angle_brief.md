**Emergent Collision Point:** Signal A is a complaint about one cohort; Signal B re-executes the same structural act three days later on a fresh cohort, and neither document says so. The collision is not "tariffs are litigated" — it is that **the list and the waiting period have come apart**. § 1338(d) ties the 30-day bar to the proclamation "that enumerates" an article, so the operative date for any HTS line is the date that line was *first enumerated*, not the date the country was found to discriminate. Read the plaintiff's way, every future amendment that adds a product on a short fuse manufactures a new class of entries whose duty is refundable with interest, which makes the 50% rate a *recoverable receivable* for the first 30 days of a newly listed line rather than a cost. Read the government's way, the clock ran once from the 20 July 2026 finding and amendments never restart it, which makes the rate a permanent cost. The same seven days now separates a cost from a claim. And because Part A removes products at the same moment Part B adds them, a buyer who re-sourced on a 7-day-old list may be paying relocation cost to escape a duty that ceased to cover the good before the new line was qualified. The decision engine's input is not the rate; it is the enumeration date and the re-source lead time.

**Hook:** One food importer has paid more than $381,000 in Section 338 duty on shipments that the statute arguably did not permit collecting yet — and on Friday the same administration added cocoa powder, carpets and air conditioners to the same 50% list with the same seven-day fuse.

**Tension:** The people who administer this are customs and finance teams, not trade lawyers. Their total-landed-cost model carries tariff as a rate per HTS line, reviewed annually at contract renewal. The instrument they are modelling now changes composition on days' notice, stacks on Section 232, and carries its own refund claim that is decided by an entry date rather than a rate. Re-sourcing is a 12-to-24-month, largely irreversible commitment; the list and the litigation both move faster than that. Whoever capitalises the 50% into a relocation business case is pricing a number that is contested, revisable in both directions, and — for the first 30 days of a newly enumerated line — possibly repayable with interest.

**Target reader:** `ops_leader` (the executive who signs the sourcing decision and the duty bill; needs working-capital and landed-cost math, not a legal opinion).

**Single claim to defend:** On a Section 338 line, the date your HTS code was first enumerated determines whether the 50% you paid is a cost or a refund claim, and that date is now moving faster than any re-sourcing decision can follow.
