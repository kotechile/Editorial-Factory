**Emergent Collision Point:** The flat subscription was never just a billing convention — it was a hedge. The vendor bought the inputs (compute, storage, memory, egress), carried the variance, and sold the buyer one predictable number. In the fortnight of 1–13 October 2026 both halves of that hedge were renegotiated in public. On 13 October the base price of the bought tooling went up 3–10%, and by 3 December the AI part of it becomes a meter. On 1 October the inputs the alternative depends on went up faster and without grandfathering: an already-running OVHcloud dedicated server renews +28% to +41.9% on OVHcloud's own tables, RAM options on new orders are up 68% to 652% on the Hacker News reading of the same price list, and TrendForce still forecasts DRAM +10–15% and NAND +15–20% quarter-on-quarter into 4Q26. The two legs point the same way and neither states it: the cost of owning the inputs rose several times faster than the cost of renting a finished tool. What the buyer lost in October was not a discount — it was the ability to price the decision with one number per year.

**Hook:** On 13 October Atlassian raised its Cloud list prices 3–10% and confirmed that its AI features become meters on 3 December. Twelve days earlier, on 1 October, OVHcloud applied its own increase to dedicated servers that were already running — +41.2% at renewal on one documented configuration — and put the reason in the first paragraph: DDR5 costs six times what it did a year ago. One of those increases is the price of buying; the other is the price of the alternative.

**Tension:** Every build-vs-buy spreadsheet of the last three years rested on a comparison between a subscription (fixed, forecastable, someone else's problem when capacity costs move) and a build (variable, owned, exposed). AI collapsed the build's *labour* line and everyone drew the obvious conclusion — build is cheap now. The window's evidence inverts the conclusion without touching the labour line: what got cheap was typing code, and what got expensive is everything the code runs on. The vendor reprices annually and passes through a slice; the operator reprices at renewal, with no slice to hide behind. So the vendor's purchasing power became the product. This hurts the team that read "AI made building cheap" as a mandate, and it helps whoever still has a seat contract signed before October.

**Target reader:** eng_leader

**Single claim to defend:** The build-vs-buy decision in the last 30 days is no longer a choice between two prices but between two cost curves: the bought option rose 3–10% with a meter attached from December, while the inputs to the built option repriced harder and sooner — DDR5 at six times a year ago on OVHcloud's own account, live dedicated servers +28% to +41.9% at renewal from 1 October, and DRAM still forecast up 10–15% quarter-on-quarter into 4Q26. Buying stopped being the predictable option and started being the one with the better memory contract.
