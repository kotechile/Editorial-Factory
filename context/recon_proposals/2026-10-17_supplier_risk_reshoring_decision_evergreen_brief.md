**De-dup:** `2026-10-10_why-importers-forfeit-duty-refunds` is this vertical's nearest prior evergreen artifact and the closest thesis on the duty line — but it is about money flowing *back* to the importer (drawback's 99% refund on exported or destroyed inputs, 19 U.S.C. 1313). Neither rule set appears in the other, and `2026-10-10_nearshoring-doesnt-move-the-trade-case` (incidence attach to a good vs. a file) and `2026-10-17_your-mexico-hedge-renewal-risk` (USMCA term and ownership) are about where risk sits, not what a wrong entry costs.
**Thesis:** The expensive part of a misclassified entry is not the duty you underpaid — 19 U.S.C. 1592(d) makes you pay that back either way — it is the penalty layered on top, which reaches four times the duties for gross negligence and the merchandise's domestic value for fraud, unless a prior disclosure lands before CBP records a formal investigation and you tender CBP's calculated loss; so the exposure is decided by three dated windows that open the day you learn of the error (ten days to put an oral disclosure in writing, thirty days to supply missing data, thirty days to tender), not by how wrong the classification was.
