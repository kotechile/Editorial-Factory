# Angle Brief: enterprise_ai_governance — 2026-10-19

**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)
**Winner:** Your agent stayed inside its permissions. The data left anyway.
**Scores:** E=9.0 A=8.8 S=8.8 → Composite=8.9

**Signal A (Anchor 1):** BCG, "The Authorization Gap: Why Yesterday's Controls Won't Work with Today's Agents" (2026-09-22; BCG and MIT Sloan Management Review study of 35% of organizations running agentic AI in production, 44% planning deployments, more than a third expecting agents to hold independent decision rights within three years) — the named failure is an agent that "reaches the right goal by the wrong means" while working inside its instructions and permissions; a dedicated agent identity framework "governs what an agent does. It cannot govern what the agent's output causes"; the fix proposed is purpose- and conduct-bound authorization, tying an agent's authority to what it may achieve and how it may pursue that goal (https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/authorization-gap-ai-agent-governance).

**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Zenity Labs, "SalesBleed: Indirect Prompt Injection and 0-Click Data Exfiltration on Agentforce" (public disclosure 2026-09-24; reported to Salesforce 2026-06-01, fixes confirmed 2026-08-18, verified 2026-08-19) — three Agentforce flaws let an unauthenticated public Web-to-Lead submission plant instructions that lie dormant in the Leads table until an employee asks the agent a routine question; the payload reads the Accounts table "because the same subagent holds permissions for both the Leads and Accounts tables", and "the injection didn't need to escalate privileges, the permissions were already there"; the data leaves in a DNS lookup inside the subdomain of an HTML image tag, which "can survive HTTP egress controls"; the Trusted URLs redaction layer was bypassed with an unrecognised top-level domain and URL termination characters (https://labs.zenity.io/post/salesbleed-0-click-data-exfiltration-on-agentforce).

**Emergent Collision Point:** BCG describes the control the market is currently buying and says where it stops: a control layer scoped to the agent cannot govern what the agent's output causes. Zenity, two days later, publishes the worked example of that exact stop — an exploit with no identity, no session and no privilege escalation, entering through a form the company deliberately left public and exiting through a renderer nobody classified as a security boundary. Neither source states the other's implication. Combined, they say the control plane is being built around the wrong object: the agent's authority is the one thing that held, and the two surfaces that failed — untrusted content arriving as business records, and output rendered as live network requests — sit outside the identity graph entirely. The thesis is that authorization has to be bound to the data path (what may enter a context, what may leave as a side effect), not only to the actor's rights.

**Hook:** On September 22, BCG told CIOs that agents can reach the right goal by the wrong means. Two days later, Zenity Labs published a chain that needed no credentials, no login and no extra permission — a poisoned lead on a public form and an image tag — and pulled a CRM table out of Salesforce Agentforce one DNS lookup at a time.

**Tension:** The governance budget is landing on the actor: agent identity, scoped tokens, gateways, kill switches, registries. Every one of those controls would have reported the SalesBleed agent as compliant, because it was. Meanwhile the two surfaces the exploit used are owned by nobody in the AI programme — the marketing web form that writes into the CRM, and the markdown renderer that turns model output into outbound requests. Who this empowers: teams that can inventory ingress points and egress paths before they buy another agent-identity SKU. Who this threatens: any programme whose evidence of control is a clean identity graph and a policy engine, with no test for what an agent's output can make a browser do.

**Target reader:** enterprise_cai

**Single claim to defend:** The September 2026 record says agent governance is failing at the data path, not at the actor: the exploit that reached production kept every permission it was given, so controls scoped to identity and tool rights cannot see it, and the missing layer is authorization over what enters an agent's context and what leaves through its output.
