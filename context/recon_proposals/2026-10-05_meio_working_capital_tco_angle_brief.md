**Emergent Collision Point:** A rate hike raises the cost of every dollar tied up in inventory — the "carrying cost" that MEIO and working-capital math exist to minimize. But the ISM report released two weeks later shows manufacturers are already drawing their own raw materials down into contraction while their customers' shelves read "too low" and supplier deliveries keep slowing. Neither source says it, but together they describe a trap the standard playbook can't solve: you can no longer "cut inventory to save working capital" because the inventory is already gone, and the stock you'd re-buy now costs more to finance and more to ship.

**Hook:** On September 16 the Fed raised rates for the first time in three years; on October 1, the ISM reported manufacturers were contracting their own inventories while their customers' inventories read "too low." Two weeks apart, one squeeze.

**Tension:** The classic working-capital playbook says higher rates → hold less inventory. That move is now closed off. Cutting further invites stockouts and the expedited freight that just got more expensive on the same rate hike. Operators are squeezed between a cost of capital that punishes holding and a physical system that has no slack to give.
