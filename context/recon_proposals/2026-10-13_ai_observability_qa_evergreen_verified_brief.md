## Notes for the drafter

- **Fictional-source discipline.** Claims 8 and 9 come from a paper whose model names and scores are
  explicitly its own fictional illustration ("two fictional models", "Table 1: Hypothetical data").
  The arithmetic and the reporting rule are the paper's real contribution; the *scores* are not
  measurements of any system. Present them as the paper's worked example, never as a finding about a
  named model.
- **Do not merge the two intervals into one story of disagreement.** Bowyer et al. measure
  *coverage of a nominal interval at small N* (too narrow, under-covering). NIST compares
  *estimators for a named benchmark size* (the plain average is under-confident for benchmark
  accuracy against a regression-free method, and generalized-accuracy intervals are wider still).
  Both say the plain interval is not the right tool by default; neither is offered as a refutation of
  the other.
- **No figure may be re-derived.** Do not compute a new sample size, a new interval width, or a
  ratio (e.g. "1 in 13 runs") that no source states.
- **Accessibility:** expand or avoid every acronym (CLT → "central limit theorem"; CI → "confidence
  interval"; GLMM → "generalized linear mixed model"; NIST → "NIST (the U.S. National Institute of
  Standards and Technology)"; benchmark names GPQA-Diamond / BIG-Bench Hard / Global-MMLU Lite may be
  dropped in favour of a plain description if the checker flags them).
