Notes the drafter must respect (read by eye out of the fetched pages, because the gate checks a figure and never its gloss):

- **The exemption is not a licence and the article must not say the owner "needs" one.** Every one of these sections *removes* the owner from a licensing statute. The claim is narrower and stronger: the exemption has conditions, and the role it confers is the general contractor's role.
- **California's labour condition is the one that breaks in practice.** § 7044(a)(1)(B) exempts the owner only where "the property owner personally performs all of the work or any work not performed by the owner is performed by the owner's employees with wages as their sole compensation". A sole-proprietor handyman or an unlicensed framer is neither, so the exemption is gone — in prose under row 2, no row (the sentence carries no figure the matcher can anchor).
- **Florida's own disclosure statement names the kickback pattern the article is about.** § 489.103(7)(c) requires the owner to "personally appear and sign the building permit application" and to be handed a disclosure statement reading, in item 7: "it is a frequent practice of unlicensed persons to have the property owner obtain an owner-builder permit that erroneously implies that the property owner is providing his or her own labor and materials." Item 8 adds that any unlicensed person working on the building "must work under my direct supervision and must be employed by me", which means FICA withholding and workers' compensation; item 7 also says the owner's homeowner insurance "may not provide coverage for those injuries" — quote that as the statute's own disclosure about **worker injuries**, never as a policy exclusion for unpermitted construction (no fetchable primary states that exclusion; the 2026-10-15 run reached the same verdict).
- **The resale rules are presumptions, not automatic forfeiture.** California's one-year rule is rebuttable as to burden of proof and only becomes conclusive at five or more structures; Florida's is a presumption; Washington's is a bar on the exemption itself for property owned under twelve months. Keep the legal weight of each in the sentence, and never write "you lose your licence" — there is no licence to lose.
- **Oregon's permitted-work clause is the sharpest single line in the set** and it contradicts the usual advice: contracting with licensed trades is not enough when a building permit is required — the work must be performed by, or under the direction of, a **residential general contractor**. Carry it in prose under row 6.
- **Washington's five-hundred-dollar floor is not a DIY threshold.** RCW 18.27.090(9) excludes work that is part of a larger operation and excludes anyone who advertises as a contractor; a kitchen that is "one small job" is not casual, minor or inconsequential.
- **No hands-on claim and no invented price.** This desk has not pulled a permit in any of these states, and no permit fee or GC markup is cited anywhere in this brief: the article's arithmetic is the count and the date, not dollars. If the body wants to size the saving, it must say the permit fee is on the reader's own invoice.
