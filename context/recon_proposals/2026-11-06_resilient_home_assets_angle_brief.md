**Emergent Collision Point:** Neither document says what happens at the service panel. Read together, the liability failure that is being priced into the utility's cost of capital is being *paid* in queue position rather than in rates — and the household's own resilience work (a new service drop for a heat pump, an EV charger, a battery, an ADU, a rebuilt house) needs exactly the approval the utility has just deprioritized. So the binding constraint on a household hardening plan has moved off the roof and off the battery and onto a date the household does not control; a homeowner can buy the panel, the battery and the hardened vent, and still not be able to energize any of it.
**Hook:** In October, PG&E sent letters to about 25,000 people who were waiting for power and told them the date could slip six months. The reason given was not a shortage of copper or crews. It was a wildfire-liability bill that died in Sacramento at the end of the legislative session.
**Tension:** Who this empowers: the household that treats the connection as a long-lead procurement item and orders it early, and the backup design that does not require a service upgrade to work. Who this threatens: the resilience purchase that assumes the meter will be ready — the battery, the heat-pump conversion, the ADU, the rebuild — and any advice that says hardening is a checklist of equipment. The disputed part, which the piece must carry honestly: ratepayer advocates call the delay leverage ("pure, pure blackmail", Consumer Watchdog's Jamie Court) and say the deferred work is already in rates, while PG&E says the deferred projects are not yet in rates; the piece presents both and takes neither as fact.
