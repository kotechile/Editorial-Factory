**Thesis:** The employee stock purchase plan's "qualifying disposition" is not a discount you unlock by waiting — it is the later of two statutory clocks whose start date a plan detail decides (18 months after purchase with a share-capped offering, 24 months without one), and what the wait buys at the top bracket is about 13 cents for every dollar the stock rises above its grant-date price, capped by a $25,000-a-year purchase limit, in exchange for a year and a half of everything the stock can fall.

- 26 U.S.C. § 423(a)(1) — "no disposition of such share is made by him within 2 years after the date of the granting of the option nor within 1 year after the transfer of such share to him": the two clocks, both of which must clear because the statute joins them with "nor".
- IRS Publication 525 (2025), "Holding period requirement": "You satisfy the holding period requirement if you don't sell the stock until the end of the later of the 1-year period after the stock was transferred to you or the 2-year period after the option was granted."
- IRS Publication 525 (2025), "Holding period requirement not satisfied": "your ordinary income is the amount by which the stock's FMV when you exercised the option exceeded the option price. This ordinary income isn't limited to your gain from the sale of the stock." — the phantom-income rule, and the single most expensive thing in the piece for a reader who sells after a drawdown.
- IRS Publication 525 (2025), "Option granted at a discount": compensation on a qualifying disposition is the lesser of the grant-date spread or the actual gain, and "If you have a loss from the sale, it's a capital loss, and you don't have any ordinary income" — the asymmetry the article turns on.
- IRS Publication 525 (2025), Examples 10 and 11 — identical facts (100 shares, $20 option price, $22 grant value, $23 at exercise, sold at $30): sold 14 months after exercise, $200 of wages and $800 of capital gain; sold 6 months after exercise, $300 of wages and $700 of capital gain. The whole rate arbitrage is that $100 of reclassification plus whatever long-term treatment the $800 gets that the $700 does not.
- 26 CFR § 1.423-2(i)(1) — the annual limitation's accrual rule, including that a right which accrues under one option "may not be carried over to any other option", which is why a missed purchase year is lost rather than banked.
