**Thesis:** The safety-stock radical is a variance budget, not a forecast problem: because the lead-time term scales with the square of the lead time while the demand term scales with the lead time itself, the buffer behaves as if the supplier's coefficient of variation were multiplied by √L before it competes with demand variance — so on a two-week lane a standard deviation of one day (7.1% of the lead time) already carries 64% of the buffer's variance and halving it cuts the buffer 41.5% while halving forecast error cuts only 14.5%, shortening the quoted lead time without tightening its spread buys just 9.4%, and the peer-reviewed result says the whole ordering inverts below a service-level threshold above 50% — so the lever a team is told to pull depends on the service level that SKU class is actually held to, and "cut lead-time variability" is wrong advice in a band most companies operate in.

**Lead:** The vertical's own `primary_angles` in `context/verticals.json` — "the working capital trap carrying cost of stagnant inventory against volatile supplier lead times" — plus the founder's standing position that a single-node safety-stock formula is what amplifies buffers (`founder-voice.md` §3, meio pillar: "Holding safety buffers at every warehouse node compounds capital lockup"). The evidence that turned it into a decision rather than a restatement: the buffering literature's own dispute over whether supply or demand variance dominates, which resolves into a single measurable ratio, and the peer-reviewed inversion of the standard prescription.

## Desk arithmetic (reproduced, not quoted)
Base case: 100 units/day, σd 20, L 14 days, σLT 1 day, Z 1.65 (95% CSL).

- Buffer with both terms: 206.1 units; demand-only (σLT 0): 123.5 units → the lead-time term carries 40.1% of the buffer and 64.1% of the squared term.
- Halve σLT (2 → 1 day): 352.3 → 206.1 units, a 41.5% cut.
- Halve σd (20 → 10): 206.1 → 176.2 units, a 14.5% cut.
- Halve the mean lead time (14 → 7 days) holding σLT at 1 day: 186.7 units, a 9.4% cut — less than the forecast-accuracy lever, because halving L doubles CV_L.
- Crossover (lead-time term = demand term) at CV_d 0.20: σLT 0.75 days on a 14-day lane, 1.10 days on a 30-day lane.
