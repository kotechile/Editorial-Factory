**Gate rulings for the drafter**
1. The article's spine is D1/R1. R1 is arithmetic on the release's own definition and may be stated as arithmetic ("by the report's own definition…"), never as the publisher's finding. The publisher's finding is the *direction*: locked-in equity fell 2.0% while the total rose 1.5% (A3) and the low band grew 25% (A4).
2. Do not print "88%" and "87%" as if they were the same measure: 88% is the locked share of *all* accessible equity; 87% is the severe-plus-extreme share of *locked-in* equity. The article uses 88% and 83%.
3. The June 30 benchmark (6.49%) and the November 5 survey (7.18%) are different instruments on different dates: one is First American's prevailing national average 30-year rate at quarter end, the other Freddie Mac's weekly survey average for 20%-down, excellent-credit conforming purchase loans. Say so once, in the catch, and never present the 69 basis points as a single measured series.
4. D3 is arithmetic over rounded figures. Write "about $1.6 trillion" and "about a third", and mark it as this desk's arithmetic on the report's own rows. Never print a precise $1.564 trillion.
5. Never say the equity is "unavailable" or "frozen": the report measures an economic incentive to keep a cheap loan, not a legal bar. The word the sources use is "locked in", a classification, and the report's own FAQ calls the whole measure an estimate.
6. Do not use the LendingTree survey's figures (34% / 32% / 23% / 3%) — this vertical published them on 2026-11-02.
7. Avoid framing the piece as advice. The decision belongs to the reader; the article reports what the spread does and what the two cohorts are.
