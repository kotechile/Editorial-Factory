# Angle Brief: last_mile_routing_fleet_carbon — 2026-09-25
**Winner:** The largest electric truck order in U.S. history — 2,500 Class 8 trucks that nearly double the electric fleet — wasn't unlocked by battery tech. It was unlocked by a lease that moves residual-value risk off carriers' books, plus shippers pooling demand so OEMs could price at scale.

**Scores:** N=9.0 A=9.0 S=8.0 → Composite=8.7

**Hook:** On Sept 22, a coalition of the world's largest cargo-owning shippers — Microsoft, PepsiCo, Ikea, Red Bull — placed a single order for 2,500 battery-electric Class 8 trucks. It's the largest electric truck order in U.S. history, and it nearly doubles the entire U.S. electric Class 8 fleet in one stroke.

**Tension:** Electric trucks have been stuck in pilot purgatory for a decade — only 875 zero-emission heavy trucks sold in the U.S. in all of 2025 (0.3% of the market), while the median battery-electric Class 8 tractor cost $411,200 (27% more expensive than two model years ago, and 2.4× the $172,500 median diesel). The blocker was never the battery: it was residual-value risk (no resale market for a truck with a battery whose replacement cost is unknown) and fragmented demand that kept OEMs from pricing at scale. ZET SCALE attacks both at once — a fair-market-value lease ("ZET Lease") that shifts residual risk off the fleet operator, and pooled freight demand that forced competitive pricing through an independent RFP.

**Single claim to defend:** The EV fleet transition's bottleneck was never the battery — it was residual-value risk and fragmented demand; the moment a lease structure removed the former and demand aggregation removed the latter, the largest electric truck order in U.S. history closed.
