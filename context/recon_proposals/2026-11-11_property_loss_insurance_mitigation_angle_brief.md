**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)

**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Colorado Division of Insurance news release, 12 October 2026 (https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/CODORA/bulletins/42e8cde). HB25-1182 — signed 28 May 2025, effective 1 July 2026 — now forces every insurer that prices, underwrites, surcharges or non-renews on a wildfire risk score to put property-level and community-level mitigation into its model, or to discount a policyholder who demonstrates the work. From 1 October 2026 the written notice carries the score, the features behind it, the actions that move it and the right to appeal; a new applicant gets the score within 15 days, an appeal is acknowledged in 10 calendar days and decided in writing within 30, and coverage continues at least 10 days past expiry if the decision is late. Insurers must post their mitigation discounts publicly or state that none exist. The release names the carrier terms: CSAA's "15% discount on the wildfire portion" for the IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home Enhanced or Essential designation plus a guarantee of wildfire eligibility for the Enhanced level with a companion auto policy, and State Farm considering properties "even in high wildfire risk areas" that attain the designation.
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** The Florida Office of Insurance Regulation's catastrophe record for Hurricane Isaias, landfall at Destin on 9 October 2026, as reported on 9 November 2026 (https://floridaphoenix.com/2026/11/09/hurricane-isaias-claims-keep-climbing): 68,071 claims filed, total estimated insured losses above $735.5 million, 58.2% of claims closed, **25,211 closed with some form of payment** and **14,402 closed with no payment** — 6,835 of those because the claimed damage fell under the deductible, 5,374 for administrative reasons including duplicates, wrong loss dates and policyholders who never made contact. Another 20,482 claims were open with nothing paid. More than 81% of all claims were residential property; the OIR data carry no average payment.
**Emergent Collision Point:** The two records describe the same homeowner's two gates and never touch each other. Colorado governs the gate in front of the policy — the score, the credit and the appeal — and it puts the burden of showing the math on the insurer, on a 10-day and 30-day clock. Florida's record is the gate behind the policy, and it is decided on what the homeowner produced: a third of the closed claims that paid nothing failed on the deductible or on the file, not on a coverage exclusion. Neither document says the thing the pair says: **the mitigation credit is the rehearsal, and the claim is the performance.** The documentation discipline a score appeal rewards — pictures, receipts, a certificate, a deadline met — is the same discipline that separates a paid claim from a closed one, and only one of the two has a state standing behind it with a timetable.
**Hook:** Colorado now makes an insurer show you the arithmetic behind your wildfire price, and gives you ten days to answer it. Four weeks after Isaias came ashore, 14,402 of the 68,071 claims it left behind had been closed with nothing paid. The state can time the first conversation. It cannot time the second.
**Tension:** The mitigation economy is being rebuilt around evidence, and the evidence is the homeowner's to keep or lose. That empowers the owner with a phone full of dated photographs and a folder of receipts; it hurts the owner who did the same work and cannot show it, because a credit that requires proof and a claim that requires proof fail the same way — quietly, and after the deadline.
**Target reader:** pro_homeowner
**Single claim to defend:** The wildfire credit and the claim payment are decided by the same thing — a documented file the homeowner can produce on demand — so a mitigation spend that is not papered is only half-bought, and the state's new appeal clock is the cheapest rehearsal available for the claim that follows.
