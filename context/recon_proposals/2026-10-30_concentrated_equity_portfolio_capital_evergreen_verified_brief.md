## Flags the drafter must carry

1. **A 0.5 percent floor sits in the same subsection.** § 170(b)(1)(I) reads "Any charitable contribution otherwise allowable … shall be allowed only to the extent that the aggregate of such contributions exceeds 0.5 percent of the taxpayer's contribution base for the taxable year." It is cited from the code text current at 2026-10-30 and is **not computed anywhere in this article**; the copy must not promise that the full 30 percent arrives as a deduction. One sentence, in The catch, is enough.
2. **The regulation's own cross-reference is stale.** 26 CFR § 1.170A-8(a)(1) still describes § 170(b)(1)(C) as the "unlimited charitable contributions deduction" — a pre-1986 usage. Cite the regulation only for the 20-/30-/50-percent ladder and Example 5's arithmetic, never for what § 170(b)(1)(C) now says; the statute is the source for that.
3. **The 20 percent rate is the top long-term rate, not a universal one.** IRS Topic 409 states 0% and 15% bands below the thresholds. Never write that a gain "is taxed at 20%" without the band condition.
4. **The 3.8 percent is a surtax on net investment income, not a capital-gains rate.** Write it as an additional charge on the gain, and keep the $250,000 married-filing-jointly threshold attached to it.
5. **§ 170(e)(1)(A) reduces the *contribution*, not the *gain*.** The mechanism is: the deduction falls back to basis because the appreciation is stripped out. Do not describe it as "the gain becomes taxable" — nothing is realized.
6. **The 30 percent is taken against the contribution base, which the code defines as adjusted gross income without a net-operating-loss carryback.** Say "adjusted gross income" and keep the parenthetical short; do not call it "income".

## Authorised arithmetic (label each as the desk's own)
- Illustrative position: 2,000 shares at $75 = **$150,000** of value, vest-date basis **$40,000**, so **$110,000** of embedded gain. 150,000 − 40,000 = 110,000.
- 30 percent ceiling on a $400,000 contribution base = **$120,000** deductible this year; **$30,000** carried into the five-year queue. 400,000 × 0.30 = 120,000; 150,000 − 120,000 = 30,000.
- Tax the gift avoids on the gain: 110,000 × 0.238 = **$26,180**, where 0.238 is the desk's own sum of the cited 20 percent rate and the cited 3.8 percent surtax. Name both parts in the sentence.
- The year-one discount: a lot held a year or less deducts at basis, so the same gift would be **$40,000** of deduction rather than $120,000 — **$80,000** less this year (and the $110,000 of appreciation never becomes deductible at all).
- The election's arithmetic: 50 percent ceiling on the same base = $200,000, but the base of the deduction falls to $40,000, so the election buys room the gift cannot use and gives up $110,000 of deduction. Do not present this as a rule of thumb; present the two ceilings and the two deduction bases side by side.
- **No other arithmetic.** No tax-savings figure built on an assumed marginal bracket, no "you save $X" total, no comparison to a donor-advised fund (no source in this brief).
