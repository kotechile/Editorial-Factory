**Emergent Collision Point:** Anchor A measures the vessel; anchor B measures the container after it has been unloaded and is waiting for a train. Neither publisher states what the two readings say together: the delay that now dominates a shipment's variance is not on either leg the control tower instruments best, it is in the seam between them — and when half the vessels miss schedule and the inbound gateway's rail outbound dwell is at a 12-month high, the two latencies compound rather than cancel. A control tower sold on predicting and preventing deviations is being pointed at a network where deviation is the modal state, so its alert volume rises while its actionable lever (prevention) shrinks; what remains is re-pricing the miss (expediting, re-booking, absorbing demurrage) rather than removing it.
**Hook:** Open on the two records set in the same month at opposite ends of one container's trip: 49.9% global schedule reliability — the lowest since September 2022 — and 6.75 days of rail dwell at Los Angeles and Long Beach, the longest in a year, on a port complex that had just posted its busiest three-month stretch ever.
**Tension:** The control tower's commercial premise is that exceptions are deviations to be detected and resolved. Sea-Intelligence's own capacity note (2026-09-21) shows what the network does with a delayed ship: it "bunches" delayed vessels alongside regular sailings, generating 12 double-sailings in two weeks and a 27% year-on-year capacity increase that is a congestion artifact, not a strategy. The exception has become the input. Who it hurts: the supply chain architect who just bought per-leg visibility and now owns a rising queue of alerts nobody can prevent. Who it helps: whoever already holds buffer capacity at the gateway seam (on-dock rail slots, near-port transload, dual-gateway routing), because scarcity there is what the next four weeks will be priced on.
**Target reader:** supply_chain_architect (Supply Chain & Logistics Systems Architect)
**Single claim to defend:** A control tower built to triage per-leg exceptions cannot manage a network whose reliability has fallen to ~50%, because the compounding happens in the handoff between legs and the only levers left are cost-shifting — so the architect's decision is where to hold buffer capacity at the seam, not which leg to instrument more.
