**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Constellation Brands Q2 FY2027 earnings recap, published 2026-10-06 (https://www.cbrands.com/blogs/stories/earnings-recap-constellation-brands-second-quarter-fiscal-2027-earnings-results) — beer shipments 123.9M case equivalents against 117.4M a year earlier, **+5.5%**, while beer depletions (distributor shipments to retailers, third-party data) were **-0.6%**; the company's own gloss is that it "shipped slightly more cases than were depleted during the quarter as distributors ordered to rebuild inventory days on hand to healthier levels."
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Netstock 2026 Benchmark Report: The State of Supply Chain Planning, released 2026-09-23 (https://www.netstock.com/research/supply-chain-planning-report) — platform data from more than 2,500 customers plus a survey of over 150 SMB users; 53% of SMBs are "ordering earlier or in larger quantities," and "among businesses ordering earlier, only 44% report service above 90%, compared with 62% among everyone else," while typical inventory value stayed at $3.91M inside a $3.8M–$4.0M band for three years and the share reporting more than 10% dead stock doubled since 2024.
**Emergent Collision Point:** Both anchors are the same measurement error seen from opposite ends of one chain. Constellation's shipment line rose ~6 points faster than the consumer line in a single quarter because distributors were refilling — the refill is real volume, but it is a change in *channel inventory*, not in demand. Netstock's survey shows the mirror image one level downstream: the cohort that moved its orders earlier to protect itself against lead-time swings is the cohort with *worse* service (44% above 90% against 62% for everyone else), and the act of ordering earlier did not change what the typical business holds ($3.91M, flat for three years). Neither document says what the pair implies: moving an order re-times when the same volume arrives, so an order-based plan records buffer motion as demand on both sides of the same transaction, and a plan that sums the two counts the same case twice.
**Hook:** Constellation's beer shipments grew 5.5% in the quarter while the volume distributors actually sold to retailers fell 0.6% — a 6.1-point gap management explains, in its own release, as distributors rebuilding inventory days.
**Tension:** Constellation then guided full-year shipments and depletions to track "within 99% of each other" on the 2026-10-07 call, which is a statement about the quarters still to come: if the full year closes at parity after a quarter where shipments outran depletions by 6.1 points, the remaining quarters have to give that gap back. Meanwhile the buyer-side evidence says hedging the order date does not buy service — so the buffer is not just unhelpful, it is legible as demand exactly once, and then it is a subtraction waiting to happen.
**Target reader:** ops_leader
**Single claim to defend:** A shipment line and an order line both record the movement of a buffer as well as the movement of demand, so a plan is only honest when it nets the channel's inventory change out of the order number — Constellation's quarter is that subtraction made visible, and Netstock's earlier-ordering cohort is what happens to service when the subtraction is never done.
