**Signal A (Anchor 1):** U.S. Postal Service, "USPS Fuel Surcharge Implementation — Frequently Asked Questions", PostalPro, dated 2026-10-26 (https://postalpro.usps.com/fuel-surcharge-faqs) — the agency is "creating a Fuel Surcharge, which will be charged on all competitively priced packages and calculated as a percentage of the base postage", updated weekly off diesel and jet fuel prices as described on eia.gov; the trade report of the same day adds that it starts in January 2027, applies to USPS Ground Advantage, Priority Mail, Priority Mail Express and Parcel Select, is domestic only, that the percentage "will be published three to five days prior to implementation" for diesel-applicable shipments and three to four days for jet, and that negotiated contracts "may have customized language" (https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/usps-to-debut-weekly-fuel-fee-in-2027-matching-fedex-ups-practices/872778/).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** UPS, "UPS Releases 3Q 2026 Earnings", 2026-10-27 (https://about.ups.com/us/en/newsroom/press-releases/financials/ups-releases-3q-2026-earnings.html) — U.S. Domestic revenue $14,865 M against $14,220 M, "increased 4.5%, driven by a 6.1% increase in revenue per piece"; and the same day's call, as reported, "Revenue per piece increased 6.1% despite a 3.3% drop in average daily volume", with "about half of revenue per piece growth" from base rates and customer mix and "higher fuel rates made up the remainder" (https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/ups-gears-up-for-peak-season-with-less-amazon-volume-in-tow/872845/).
**Emergent Collision Point:** Neither document compares itself to the other, and neither draws the conclusion. UPS's own attribution splits its price growth into a negotiated half (base rates, mix) and an automatic half (fuel) in the same quarter that its daily volume fell 3.3% — so the automatic half is what let revenue rise while the network shrank. Twenty-four hours earlier, the universal-service carrier that had "steadfastly avoided surcharges" announced it is building exactly that automatic half into permanence, as a percentage of base postage republished every week, on the four products that carry its e-commerce volume. The 8% flat "bridge" the agency installed in April expires at midnight Central on 17 January 2027 and the weekly percentage starts in January, which leaves a shipper's 2027 parcel line resting on a number that is announced three to five days before it is charged. The emergent thesis: price no longer follows volume in parcel, and the part of price that does the work is the part nobody negotiates.
**Hook:** Open on the handoff date and the two numbers side by side — 17 January 2027, when the Postal Service stops levying a flat 8% bridge and starts levying a weekly percentage of base postage, in the same week UPS reports it grew U.S. revenue 4.5% on a 6.1% per-piece lift while moving 3.3% fewer packages a day.
**Tension:** Who this empowers and who it threatens. It empowers the carrier: a percentage of postage is self-indexing, needs no filing, and arrives with an API update days ahead. It threatens the shipper's budgeting process: a contract that fixes a base rate fixes the part of the bill the carrier had to defend, and leaves the part it did not. The secondary tension is inside the shipper's own organisation — procurement owns the base rate, finance owns the fuel line, and only one of them is in the room when the percentage is set.
**Target reader:** ops_leader
**Single claim to defend:** The fuel percentage — not the base rate — is now the line that moves a parcel budget, because it is the only part of the bill that reprices without a negotiation, and from January 2027 it reprices weekly on the largest flat-priced network in the country.
