**Archetype:** evergreen

**Persona:** infra_engineer
**Decision the reader is facing:** You own one 96 GB accelerator and three to seven things to put on it — three teams' small models, or one base model plus its evaluation sweeps. Do you cut it into the smallest MIG profiles for maximum tenant count, take two or three larger slices, or leave it whole and share it with MPS — and which resource does the extra tenant actually take away from the others?
**Durability:** The mechanism is the card's own allocation rule, not a part number: a GPU memory slice is roughly one eighth of the memory *including its bandwidth*, an SM slice is roughly one seventh of the SMs, and the copy engines are divided with the memory — so capacity, bandwidth and DMA engines fall in eighths while compute falls in sevenths. That asymmetry is architectural (it is stated in the vendor's own concepts page and independently measured on a Grace Hopper H100), and the Blackwell/Rubin pages still show the same seven-instance shape, so the rule survives a generation change; what changes is the constant. Figures are as of the 96 GB H100 measurement as of its 9 April 2026 preprint, the GB200 partition shapes as of the vendor MIG page read 2026-11-04, and the slice definitions as of the vendor user guide read 2026-11-04.
**De-dup:** `2026-10-07_kv-cache-is-the-concurrency-ceiling` is this vertical's nearest prior evergreen artifact on "how much of one card you get", and it argued the KV-cache budget that caps *sessions on an undivided card*; this piece starts after that decision, assumes the card is already the right size, and settles how its bandwidth, SMs and copy engines are divided between tenants. Also differentiated: `2026-10-14_your-chip-stopped-deciding` (the market event — the vendor moving its scheduler and quota layer into open source — a claim about who owns the allocation layer, not about what a partition measures), and today's news artifact `2026-11-04_18x-lpddr-isnt-18tbs` (measurement bases for bandwidth claims). Cross-vertical greps of `published/` for "multi-instance gpu", "MIG", "time-slic", "gpu sharing" and "multi-tenant gpu" return only those two artifacts, neither of which measures a partition.
**Thesis:** A MIG profile is not a proportional discount: the memory, its bandwidth and the copy engines are cut in eighths while the SMs are cut in sevenths, so seven 1g slices hand out roughly nine-tenths of one card's bandwidth and 85% of its SMs across seven tenants — and any workload fed from host memory is stuck near 14% of the undivided copy path no matter which profile you buy. Size the profile from the resource your workload saturates, not from how many models fit.

## Notes the drafting stage must carry
- **The copy-path figure is an NVLink-C2C number, not a PCIe number.** The measurement platform is an H100 96 GB inside a Grace Hopper system, whose CPU-GPU link is NVLink-C2C at "up to 450 GB/s in each direction". Write it as that link; do not transfer the 39.6 GiB/s figure to a PCIe Gen5 host, and do not call it PCIe.
- **Table II's "wasted" column is GPU-wide best case, not per instance.** Seven 1g.12gb instances waste 17.5 GiB across the whole card; one instance does not waste 17.5 GiB.
- **The 1g.12gb profile's usable SM count beats the documentation's fraction in the wrong direction, and the article should say so.** The docs say an SM slice is "roughly one seventh" (18.9 of 132), the measured usable count is 16 (12.1%). That is the caveat the persona will check.
- **The paper's own LLM result says Llama3 scales nearly perfectly with SM count**, so its Q8 8B benchmark is SM-bound, not bandwidth-bound. The article must therefore present both bindings and the test that separates them (does tokens/s rise when only the SM-bearing profile grows? does it rise when only the memory slice grows?), and must never claim decode is always bandwidth-bound.
- **Do not cite the 3g.48gb row's "6/9%" cell** — the table prints two values in one cell; the safe comparison is 1g.24gb / 2g.24gb (26 versus 32 SMs at the same 812 GiB/s) and 3g.48gb / 4g.48gb (60 versus 64 SMs at 1,611 and 1,635 GiB/s).
- **Recompute every aggregate from the table after the rewrite:** 7 x 16 = 112 SMs of 132 (84.8% booked); 7 x 406 = 2,842 GiB/s against the card's 3,175 (89.5%); 4 x 26 = 104 SMs and 4 x 812 = 3,248 GiB/s for four 1g.24gb slices (which is why the smallest profile is not the most efficient split); 39.6 / 276.3 = 14.3%.
- The vendor pages state the partition *shapes*; they state no performance figure. Every performance number in the article comes from the two papers.
