## Handoff notes (drafting constraints)

- **Only claims 1–13 may be stated as fact.** Claim 14 stays out of the copy entirely: no "most states", no state count, no national average.
- **Dated figures.** California's section is cited as the 2025 code edition; 16 CFR Part 429 as of the eCFR snapshot updated 10/20/2026 (unchanged since 1/03/2017); the Maryland and Massachusetts pages as retrieved 2026-10-22.
- **Keep the mixed ladder intact.** California's cap is a dollar figure OR a percentage, whichever is less — never render it as "10 percent" alone, and never as "$1,000" alone. Massachusetts' rule is the *greater* of one third or special-order material cost — do not flip "greater" to "less".
- **Keep the noun attached.** California's section bars payment above the value of *work performed or material delivered*; it is not a cap on the deposit (that is subsection 3) and not a retainage rule (the 2026-10-15 article owns retainage).
- **The federal window is about WHERE, not WHAT.** It attaches to a sale solicited and agreed away from the seller's fixed place of business. Do not say it covers "home improvement contracts" generally.
- **No claim that a deposit is protected.** A cap limits exposure; it does not make the money recoverable, and no cited page says otherwise.
- **Acronyms to expand or avoid:** Federal Trade Commission (never "FTC" bare), no state abbreviations.
