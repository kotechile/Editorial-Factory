## Notes for the drafter

- The load-bearing sentence is Berkeley Lab's own: "Frankly, there are more productive ways to save energy with an investment of an hour." Quote it verbatim and attribute it to the lab. It is the desk's thesis handed over by the source.
- The article's own arithmetic must be shown as division, not asserted: 10,791 × 5% ÷ 8.76 hours-per-watt-year = 61.6 W. Never print 62-123 W without the two inputs in the same section.
- The 2025 rate is 17.30¢; the lab's example is 13¢. Say which is which, and re-price the rule at today's rate. A reader who checks the lab's page will see $1.20 and must find the explanation in the article.
- The federal ceiling row is 0.5 W for adapters up to 250 W nameplate. Do not extend it to devices with internal supplies.
- No vendor prices are cited anywhere in this piece: there is no smart plug, no meter brand, no power strip in the evidence set. Do not put one in the prose — the $50 is the lab's own figure for a meter, and it is a ceiling ("less than $50"), not a list price.
- Do not claim the audit is worthless. The evidence says it pays from year two if the reader skips the per-adapter hunt and goes after the loads a whole-home baseline exposes. That is the balanced verdict.
