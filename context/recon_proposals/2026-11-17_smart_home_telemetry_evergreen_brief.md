**Arithmetic the article must state (recompute, never copy from the draft):**

- Orifice flow scales with the square root of pressure. Going from a 60 psi fixture rating to a measured 100 psi raises flow by √(100/60) = **1.29×**, about **29%** more water through the same opening; at 120 psi it is √(120/60) = **1.41×**, about **41%**. State it as derived from the square-root relation, and say plainly that pressure-compensating aerators and flow regulators blunt it — the multiplier is the physics, not a promise about a specific faucet.
- 2.5 gpm standard showerhead at its 80 psi rating versus 2.0 gpm WaterSense: a **0.5 gpm** difference, and a 10-minute shower is **25 gallons** against **20 gallons**.
- 82 gallons per person per day × 4 people = **328 gallons** a day at home; 180 gallons a week of leak loss is **9,400 gallons** a year on the EPA's own arithmetic (180 × 52 = 9,360, and the agency prints 9,400 — quote the agency's figure, do not "correct" it).
- A reducing valve's setpoint is not the same as the house's pressure: the code caps *static* pressure, so the gauge has to be read with no water moving, ideally overnight, not while a tap is running.
