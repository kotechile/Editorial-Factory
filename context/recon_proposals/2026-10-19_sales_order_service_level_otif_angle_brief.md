**Winner:** Would a falling OTIF rate be the buffer running out rather than trucks running late? — *"The in-full half of the service score is where a price spike meets a depleted shelf, and it is failing before the on-time half."*

**Signal A (Anchor 1):** SourceDay, Fall 2026 Supplier Reliability Index, published 2026-10-08, built on purchase-order lifecycle data from Q1 2023 through Q3 2026 (https://sourceday.com/blog/supplier-reliability-trends-2026/). Load-bearing figures: on-time-in-full fell to **63.6%** of purchase order lines in Q3 2026 from **66.1%** in Q2; on-time delivery fell to **66.5%** from **68.4%**; the report says the widening gap "suggests quantity shortfalls are becoming more prevalent even when shipments arrive on schedule"; supplier-initiated PO changes hit a record **10.9%**, up from 8.8%; **25.4%** of comparable PO lines carried a price increase, the highest quarterly share in the dataset, with large increases at a record **11.9%**; average supplier response time rose to **52.2 hours** from 48.1, the slowest since Q3 2024; **36.4%** of closed PO lines changed at least once; the composite index fell from **114.0** to **111.0**, the largest quarterly drop recorded.

**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Institute for Supply Management, September 2026 Manufacturing PMI Report, released 2026-10-01 (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/manufacturing-pmi-at-54-5-september-2026-ism-manufacturing-pmi-report-302894520.html). Load-bearing figures: the Prices Index registered **77.9%**, up **6.8 points** from 71.1% and back to "its level at the start of the Iran War"; the Customers' Inventories Index registered **41.6%**, in "too low" territory for **24 straight months**; the Inventories Index registered **48.6%**, down 2.0 points and back in contraction; the Supplier Deliveries Index registered **59.0%**, indicating slowing deliveries for the **10th month in a row**; **24 commodities** were reported up in price and **none down**, with memory, dynamic random access memory and electronic components listed in short supply.

**Emergent Collision Point:** A service-level score has two gates — the date and the quantity — and they are protected by different things. The date is protected by freight; the quantity is protected by stock. In Q3 the freight side was broadly intact and the stock side was not: on-time delivery lost 1.9 points, the combined score lost 2.5, and SourceDay reads the widened gap as quantity shortfalls. In the same quarter, purchasing managers reported the buffer that covers a short shipment is gone — customers' inventories "too low" for 24 straight months, own inventories flipping back into contraction at 48.6 — while the Prices Index jumped to 77.9 with 24 commodities up and none down. Neither document states the connection. Put together: the in-full gate is the shock absorber for a price spike, and with the buffer depleted there is nothing behind it. A supplier facing a 77.9 prices index cannot hold the whole price, so it holds a smaller promise — the record 10.9% supplier-initiated change rate is that decision showing up in the data before the delivery does.

**Hook:** Open on the split inside the score — quantity fell twice as fast as timing — and on the second number nobody puts beside it: customers' inventories have been "too low" for 24 months while prices returned to their war-start level.

**Tension:** The buyer's instinct on a bad score is to buy speed; the constraint has moved to the quantity, which speed cannot fix once the stock is gone. The penalty for a miss is fixed as a share of the goods value, so it does not track the input cost that caused it. Who this helps: buyers who read the change log and the buffer together before they renew a freight contract. Who this hurts: anyone whose scorecard records an in-full miss as a carrier failure.

**Target reader:** ops_leader

**Single claim to defend:** In Q3 2026 the OTIF score fell mostly on quantity rather than timing because the buyer's buffer had been depleted for two years while input prices returned to their war-start level — so the record supplier-initiated purchase order change rate, not carrier performance, is the leading indicator of the next miss.
