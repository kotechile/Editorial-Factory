**Signal B (Anchor 2):** The Atlanta Fed's Survey of Business Uncertainty finds firms are sitting on their IEEPA tariff refunds. 70% of firms plan to retain at least some of the refund as cash/cash equivalents; more than half plan to route some to R&D or capital projects; 17.2% customer rebates, 14.8% lower prices, 12.7% staff bonuses. The refund pool is nearly $170B, with roughly $100B refunded by late July. Source: Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta, "How Are Firms Using Their Tariff Refunds?" (Sept. 21) https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/publications/policy-hub-macroblog/2026/09/21/how-are-firms-using-tariff-refunds

**Emergent Collision Point:** Neither story says it alone. The truce alone is a calendar event — a deadline pushed two months down the road. The refund survey alone is balance-sheet news — firms prefer cash over reinvestment. Combined, they are one thesis: the refund-hoarding is the market's *revealed expectation* that the tariff calm is temporary. CFOs are treating a two-month policy pause as a reason to build a cash buffer, not to spend. Tariff policy has become a quarterly balance-sheet event, and the smart money is already positioned defensively ahead of Jan. 10.

**Hook:** The US and China bought two months of tariff calm on Sept. 23 — and a day later the Atlanta Fed showed American firms don't believe it, with 70% holding their tariff refunds as cash rather than reinvesting.

**Tension:** Who it hurts: companies that reinvest the windfall aggressively and get caught if tariffs snap back on Jan. 10; suppliers and customers downstream of firms that hoard cash instead of passing refunds through. Who it helps: the cash-rich firms and the "financially constrained" third of refund recipients who need the buffer most. The systemic friction: two months of "stability" is too short to re-plan sourcing, but long enough to lull a team into inaction.
