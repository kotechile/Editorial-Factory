## Notes for the verifier
- **Do not "fix" Lokad's 98.5% by recomputing it as M/(M+H).** For the milk example that simple form returns 96.5% (0.45 ÷ (0.45 + 4/365 × 1.50) = 96.48%), because Lokad's model also lets the stocked quantity move with the target; the 98.5% is that page's output, not this article's arithmetic. The article quotes it as Lokad's result and keeps its own worked examples in the marginal single-period form, whose sources are rows 1–2 and row 4's own definitions.
- **Cycle service level is not fill rate.** The ratio returns the probability a SKU is in stock at the end of its lead time. The fill rate a customer experiences is a different, generally higher number for multi-unit orders, and the article must say so rather than equate them. Do not import the order-fill exponent from the 2026-10-26 sibling; that mechanism is already published.
- **Every derived figure in the final must be recomputable from the two inputs printed beside it.** An earlier run of this vertical shipped a ratio that was internally consistent and wrong (977 → 227 fps reported as 83% instead of 77%), so recompute each percentage from its own endpoints after the rewrite, not from the draft.
- The worked SKUs are the article's own illustrations, not sourced benchmarks: unit cost, margin, cover days and carrying rate are stated as inputs in the body, and the only cited constant is the 25% carrying rate (row 3) and the critical values (row 1). The 5%-of-invoice chargeback used in one sensitivity is a labelled hypothetical the reader is told to replace with their own contract's number — no retailer is named and no programme percentage is asserted.
- Row 2's "40%" and row 1's ratio are the same fact stated two ways (2.326 ÷ 1.645 = 41.4%); the article should present the ladder once, and the fact that two independent publishers state it is the point of having both rows.
- The strongest counter-argument to the piece — that a contractual minimum target binds regardless of the arithmetic — must appear in the body, not be hidden: where a customer's OTIF clause fixes a floor, the ratio tells the reader which SKUs that floor over- and under-serves, and the answer is a supply-mode change on the under-served ones, not a uniform extra buffer.
