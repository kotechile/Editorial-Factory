# Angle Brief: career_velocity_equity_engineering — 2026-09-25

**Winner:** Seven in ten vested startup options are never exercised — and a 401(k), not a bigger grant, is the lever that decides who cashes in.

**Scores:** N=9.0 A=9.0 S=9.0 → Composite=9.0

**Hook:** Over 70% of vested startup stock options are never exercised, per Carta's State of Employee Equity and 401(k) Plans report (Sept 10, 2026). The option grant at the center of most offer letters is, for the majority of employees, paper wealth that never converts to a dollar.

**Tension:** Equity is sold as the startup's headline benefit, but Carta's data shows it mostly goes unclaimed — and the unexpected variable that separates exercisers from non-exercisers is a retirement account. Employees with a 401(k) exercise at 26.1% vs 22.8% without. Close to half of US companies on Carta offer no 401(k) at all, so most startup employees are betting their long-term security on a single illiquid, concentrated asset that may never pay out.

**Target reader:** equity_career_strategist (High-Growth Tech Careerist & Equity Negotiator — senior IC to VP, skeptical of paper wealth, negotiation-oriented).

**Single claim to defend:** A 401(k) is one of the strongest predictors of whether an employee ever converts vested equity into cash — so offer negotiation should weight the retirement match as heavily as the option grant.
