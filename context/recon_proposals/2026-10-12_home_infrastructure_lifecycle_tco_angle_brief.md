# Angle Brief: home_infrastructure_lifecycle_tco — 2026-10-12

**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)
**Winner:** The Outage Is National; the Battery Payback Is a Zip Code
**Scores:** E=9.0 A=8.5 S=8.5 → Composite=8.7
**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Sunrun / Talker Research homeowner outage survey, released 2026-09-17 — 2,000 U.S. homeowners surveyed Aug 18–24: two-thirds reported a power outage in 2026, those affected averaged three outages with the most recent lasting 5.5 hours, 47% name a backup power source as the single upgrade that would make them feel most prepared, and 28% have someone at home who depends on electricity for a health need (https://investors.sunrun.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/380/two-thirds-of-american-homeowners-have-experienced-a-power).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** ACP / Wood Mackenzie *U.S. Energy Storage Monitor* Q3 2026 edition, released 2026-09-22 — residential storage installed 676 MW in Q2 2026 (fourth-largest quarter, −15% q-o-q), the solar-plus-storage attachment rate hit 46% from 41%, the national residential market is forecast to **contract 4% in 2026** after the tax credit died, and the top five markets took **88%** of residential capacity (https://cleanpower.org/news/usesm-q3-2026).
**Emergent Collision Point:** The two halves of the home-battery case have come apart. Outage exposure is a *national* condition — the same two-thirds share of homeowners lose power whether they live in a VPP state or not — while the money that makes a battery pencil out is federal-credit-free and now concentrated in five markets, and even there it is being cut (SMUD dropped its per-kWh buy-down 40% at the margin on 2026-09-23; a 13.5 kWh Powerwall's incentive fell from $5,400 to $3,240). Signal B is what the homeowner is being asked to buy; Signal A is the demand that keeps arriving with no subsidy attached to it. Neither source states the fusion: **the battery has stopped being a bill-savings asset and become an insurance asset whose premium is set by your utility, not your meter.**
**Hook:** Lead on the collision — two-thirds of U.S. homeowners lost power in 2026, an average of three times each, and in the same month the industry's own data said the residential storage market will shrink 4% this year with the tax credit gone and 88% of what still sells concentrated in five states.
**Tension:** The resilience case is universal; the payback machinery is local and shrinking. A homeowner weighing a battery is now making a utility-territory bet, not an energy-efficiency bet — and the two numbers they are usually quoted (bill savings, payback period) describe neither side of it.
**Target reader:** pro_homeowner
**Single claim to defend:** A home battery's payback now runs on grid-outage exposure plus whatever your specific utility pays for the battery, because the federal credit is gone, the buy-downs are being cut, and the programs that remain are concentrated in five states.
