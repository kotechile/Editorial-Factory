# Angle Brief: agentic_resilience_failure — 2026-10-28

**Angle Type:** Single-Signal
**Winner:** Your 90% Restart Monitor Proves Nothing: restarting an agent is a purchase, and the thing it is priced on — how accurately a monitor spots a failing run — does not determine whether the restart pays; the quantity that does is what the replacement attempt can still win with the budget the abandoned run gives back, and in a 194-execution study not one of 103 live restart decisions could be certified either way
**Scores:** N=9.0 A=9.0 S=9.0 → Composite=9.0 (0.40·9.0 + 0.30·9.0 + 0.30·9.0)
**Hook:** A restart monitor that classifies failing runs with 90% accuracy can be worth a five-point gain or a five-point loss in task success, and the same monitor, unchanged, swings all the way to a 35-point gain — because the number that decides it is not in the classifier at all. It is the success probability of the replacement attempt, an Intuit pair measured on 194 executed runs, and when they audited the live decisions in their own traces, zero of 103 could be certified as a restart worth taking.
**Tension:** The industry is buying recovery machinery faster than it can price it. DeltaBox Lite open-sourced an abort/commit sandbox for coding agents on 16 October, shipped Windows support on 21 October, and describes it as "Git for agent sessions" — every tool call a reviewable, committable, abortable unit. That is a per-call checkpoint policy, which is exactly the policy the only counterfactual measurement of checkpoint placement prices at a *negative* return one step later (−1.1 s on 41.8 s of protected work, a conversion of −0.03). Both halves of the recovery stack — when to cut, when to fire — are being validated on proxies that do not predict the decision. The party that benefits is whoever can measure the branch they did not take: the harness owner, the eval owner, the platform team that already logs full traces and can compute a counterfactual from them at no extra model cost. The party exposed is every team that bought a restart feature against a vendor's detector-accuracy chart, and every team whose reliability metric is "the monitor caught 94% of failures" when the useful number is "of the restarts it triggered, how many beat waiting".
**Target reader:** infra_engineer
**Single claim to defend:** A restart is only worth taking if the replacement attempt's own success probability, on the budget the abandoned run hands back, beats continuing — a quantity monitor accuracy never reports — so reliability engineering has to move from detection metrics to per-decision counterfactuals, which means (a) evaluating a monitor only where an alternative action can plausibly recover value, (b) treating a checkpoint as a purchase priced by the re-derivation it saves rather than the work it preserves, and (c) accepting that with the traces most teams actually hold, the honest answer to "restart or continue" is often "the evidence does not resolve it".
