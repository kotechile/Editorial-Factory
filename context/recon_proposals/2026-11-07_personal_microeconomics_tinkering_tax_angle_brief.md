**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Spotify's own support documentation for the Premium Family plan, in its current form (observed 2026-11-07): when a household check fails, a member has **7 days** to re-verify from the email, and if they fail or miss the window they "lose access to the plan and switch to the free version", and "can't join any other Family or Duo plan for **12 months** from the date verification failed" (https://support.spotify.com/us/article/family-address-verification). The QR variant requires the plan manager and the member to be in the same place, in the mobile app, because the code "changes every few seconds". The enforcement is new this month, not the policy: Ars Technica reported on 2026-10-23 that Spotify "appears to be stepping up enforcement of existing policies" after users began posting the QR prompts, and quoted Goldman Sachs' 2026-10-15 "Music in the Air: 10 Years On" report — "a meaningful share of family plan users are not actual household members, but rather friends sharing a plan to reduce costs" and "we see additional monetization upside from tightening household verification on multi-user plans. Most family plans require members to reside at the same address, but enforcement has been minimal in practice" (https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/10/spotify-ramps-up-policy-enforcement-on-family-plans). Spotify's own US list prices, checked 2026-11-07: Individual **$12.99/month**, Duo **$18.99**, Family **$21.99 for up to six accounts** (https://www.spotify.com/us/premium/).

**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Raspberry Pi's announcement of 2026-11-02, by Eben Upton: "we are raising the price of the 16GB variant of Raspberry Pi 5 by $80, to **$385**", the 16GB Compute Module 5 variants rise "$40", and the cause is stated plainly — "a lack of alternative supply for 16GB DRAM, compounded by other ongoing component shortages, means we now need to make further price increases on some of our highest-density products" — while "we are holding the price of Raspberry Pi 500+ ... which also features 16GB of RAM" (https://www.raspberrypi.com/news/price-rises-for-some-16gb-raspberry-pi-products/). The same board launched at **$120** on 2025-01-09 (https://www.raspberrypi.com/news/16gb-raspberry-pi-5-on-sale-now-at-120), so this is a 221% move on one SKU. The post's own advice to buyers is a capacity audit, not a discount: "make sure you're not paying for more memory than you need".

**Emergent Collision Point:** Neither document says the thing the reader needs, and neither company is talking about the other. Spotify is converting an unenforced household rule into a 12-month exclusion; Raspberry Pi is converting an unmeasurable memory shortage into an $80 line on one SKU. In both cases the *informal* part of the deal — the friend on the family plan, the headroom you bought "just in case" — is the part that just acquired a price. That is the tinkering tax this month: not your weekend hours, and not the sticker on the box, but the two informalities the whole cheap-stack argument quietly rested on. The person who priced neither now pays both.

**Hook:** A music subscription and a bare circuit board have nothing to do with each other, except that in the same fortnight both stopped giving away the loose part of the deal: the friend who was never quite in the household has 7 days to prove an address or lose the rate for 12 months, and the 16 GB board that launched at $120 costs $385 from 2 November.

**Tension:** Every cheap-stack argument on this desk — self-host it, share the plan, buy the bigger board so you never touch it again — has been an argument about the *sticker*, and the sticker was never the whole price. What changed this fortnight is that the two subsidies a household actually lives on became explicit and priced: the shared account is now a compliance exposure with a dated penalty, and the spare capacity is now an $80 invoice. The people it hurts are the ones who did the arithmetic the old way — the six-way family split that penciled out at $3.67 a head, the 16 GB buy made to avoid a second upgrade. The people it helps are the ones selling the honest version: an individual plan priced at $12.99, a 2 GB board at $77.50, and a used mini PC that does more than either.

**Target reader:** `systems_tinkerer_pro` — "tinkering tax audits, self-hosting maintenance breakdown".

**Single claim to defend:** The discount a technical household lives on is not a price, it is an informality — an unenforced rule and an unused capacity — and once a vendor prices that informality, the audit has to carry two new lines: what verification failure costs, and what headroom costs when you never use it.
