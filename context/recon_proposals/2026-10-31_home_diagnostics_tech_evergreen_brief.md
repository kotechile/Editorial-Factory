## Notes on the rows

- Rows 5 and the Lung Association's kit guidance disagree on the precondition and the article must keep them apart: New York's own page says the house is sealed **24 hours before and during** the test, the Lung Association's kit instructions say closed-house conditions begin **at least 12 hours prior**, and New York's *mitigation* page (row 7) uses 12 hours for a post-install test. The honest reading is that the start time is set by the laboratory whose device you bought — so the protocol that satisfies every one of them is 24 hours. Do not average the two, and do not print 12 hours as "the rule".
- Row 7 is the mechanism, and it is the one figure in this set that a reader can check against his own house: ventilation dilutes the sample, and the concentration returns to its previous value within about 12 hours of closing up. That is why an open-window test is not a low reading but a *different measurement*, and the article may say so.
- Row 1's 21,000 and 2,900 are agency estimates with a published uncertainty range (the same EPA page carries "8,000 to 45,000" for the 21,000); quote 2,900 as the never-smoker share of the agency's estimate, never as a death count measured in a year.
- Row 2 is the one that kills "4 is safe": the agency's own risk table puts a never-smoker's lifetime risk at 1.3 pCi/L — a third of the action level — at 2 in 1,000. Use it to explain what "consider fixing between 2 and 4" is for; do not convert it into a "safe level" claim in either direction.
- Row 8's ordinal floor (under $1,000 is a red flag) is a consumer-protection heuristic from a state health department, not a market price. The article must not print $1,000 as "the cost of mitigation" — the Lung Association's own cost band on row 10's page prints a garbled "$1,00 - $2,500", so no dollar range for an install is citable; the piece carries the quote floor and sends the reader for three bids.
- Row 4 is a state program price, not a retail price. It is the honest anchor for "the cheap test is the accurate one": a laboratory-analyzed device posted to a lab, at $12.25, against monitors whose reading is instantaneous. Do not generalise the figure outside New York; say what it is.
- Every row is a government or health-charity page; none is a device vendor, so no row in this brief depends on a manufacturer's claim about its own instrument. That is deliberate — the article's whole argument is that the protocol, not the device brand, decides the number.
