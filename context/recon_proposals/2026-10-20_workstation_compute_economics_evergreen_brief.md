**Thesis:** Cloud transcoding is sold per *output* minute scaled by a multiplier the price list does not lead with — a six-rung delivery ladder bills eleven normalized minutes for every source minute on MediaConvert's Basic tier, and AWS's own worked example normalizes an HD 60 fps VP9 multi-pass output at 22.97 times its duration — so one hour of source costs $4.95 to $9.90 to deliver, while one Blackwell-class GeForce card encodes 1080p at 977 frames per second on its fast preset for under a cent of electricity per output hour: the local box therefore does not compete with the transcode fee at all, it competes with your own volume, and at $4.95 per source hour a $2,500 box has to be fed about 506 source hours (12.6 months at 40 hours a month, 50 months at 10) before it is the cheaper line — which is why the honest build-versus-cloud rule for a render pipeline is "count renditions per source minute, not minutes of footage", and why a workstation that encodes six rungs of a 45-minute show is a volume decision rather than a technology one.
