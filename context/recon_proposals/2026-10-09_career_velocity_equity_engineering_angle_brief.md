**Hook:** SpaceX's next lockup tranche — up to 328.4 million shares, 7% of the restricted block — becomes eligible to sell on October 9, 2026, the day this run fires. It is the fourth of 15 primary unlock dates, and the point of the piece is that there is no single "lockup expiration" to plan around anymore: the standard 180-day cliff has been cut into a schedule of six selling windows, each with its own tax clock, trading window and behavioral trap.

**Tension:** A single 180-day cliff forced one decision — everyone free on the same day. The staggered release converts that into a sequence, which sounds like a gift (flexibility, less concentrated pressure) and mostly is, but it quietly relocates the hard part from "decide once" to "decide correctly six times." Most of the tax was already fixed before the first sale: RSUs were taxed as wages at delivery and withheld at the 22% supplemental rate, so the gap between withholding and a senior earner's real rate is due in April, not at the sale. It helps employees who built a plan before the first window; it punishes anyone who treats each window as a fresh market call, because that is how you end up eleven months later, still concentrated, having "decided" six times to do nothing.

**Single claim to defend:** A staged lockup is a schedule, not an event, and the employees who keep the most of that equity are the ones who engineer the schedule — tranche by tranche, tax year by tax year — rather than reacting to the headline unlock date.
