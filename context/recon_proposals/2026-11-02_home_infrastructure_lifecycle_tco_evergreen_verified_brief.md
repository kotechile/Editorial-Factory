## Notes for the drafter
1. **The thesis is a disagreement between three official sources, not a claim that any is wrong.** EPA (3–5 years), Penn State (2–3 years, and 18 months for four people on 1,000 gallons) and South Carolina (3 years for the same household, 12 years for one person) are all defensible because each fixes a different per-person solids load and a different allowed fill. Write the arithmetic, name each source's measure, and let the reader see why the calendars differ.
2. **Ratio to quote:** the repair band is 10 to 60 times the fee band at the named ends — $5,000 ÷ $500 = 10 and $15,000 ÷ $250 = 60. Name both ends; do not present a midpoint.
3. **No drainfield-life claim.** No fetchable source states a service life for a drainfield or a tank, and the desk has already established that service-life integers cannot carry a gate row. The piece may say a clogged field is a construction job, not a service call — it may not price or date one beyond the agency's $5,000–$15,000 band.
4. **No additive or "septic-safe" product claims** — not verified this run.
5. **Do not attribute the 30 percent to a regulator.** Penn State writes "both the regulatory and pumping industry recommend", which is the extension service describing a convention; attribute it to the extension service.
6. **Cite the depth trigger with its own nouns** — "12 inches of the outlet" and "six inches of the bottom of the outlet" are depths measured by the pumper, not volumes; the article's method depends on that distinction.
