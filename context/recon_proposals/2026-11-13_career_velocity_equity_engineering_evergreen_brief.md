## Notes for the drafter and verifier

- **The multiplier ladder is the article, and every rung is arithmetic the reader can re-run.** 1.18× = payroll-tax wedge × the 2,080/1,840 paid-versus-billable ratio; 1.35× adds the employer's $20,143 health share; 1.39× adds a 4% match; 1.52× is what you get if you also price one unpaid month between contracts. Print the arithmetic, not just the answers: $10,402 of employee payroll tax against $19,213 of self-employment tax, of which half is deductible above the line, leaving a $5,737 wedge — 4.2% of the salary.
- **Recompute every derived percentage from the two figures it divides after the rewrite.** The wedge is 4.22% of $135,980, the gross that restores it is $141,717 (×1.0422), the billable year is 1,840 hours (2,080 minus 30 days), and $141,717 ÷ 1,840 = $77.02, which is 1.178× of $65.38. A rewrite that rounds these into different ancestors (for example "$78 an hour, about 20% more") must still be arithmetically true.
- **Lead the payroll-tax penalty with its ceiling, not its rate.** The extra social security a contractor pays stops at 6.2% of the wage base — $11,439 at $184,500 — plus 1.45% of everything, so the "double tax" is a fixed dollar penalty that is 4.2% of the median developer's pay and about 6.0% of a $250,000 package. That inversion is the piece's contrarian spine: the higher the rate, the weaker the payroll-tax argument for a contractor premium.
- **The draft must say what it is not pricing.** A contract has no equity line, no paid leave entitlement, and no employer 401(k) match; the piece prices the benefits it can source and states the equity absence in one sentence without inventing a value for it.
- **Accessibility floor.** Do not write `O*NET` in prose — name "the Labor Department's Occupational Information Network" and keep the acronym for `## Sources`. Expand `KFF` as "KFF, formerly the Kaiser Family Foundation" at first use, spell out "self-employment tax" before any `SE`, and never write `FICA`. All-caps tokens the glossary does not know fail the gate hard.
- **Shape gates.** One inline, site-relative link in the body (probe it with `curl -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}'` first) and no figure repeated across more than two body sections — the worked example belongs in one place.
- **No vendor claims and no survey of "what contractors earn".** Every figure here is statute or agency publication; if a sentence needs a market number (day rates, bench time, utilisation), cut the sentence or state it as an assumption the reader supplies.
- **Headline shape.** A figure from the piece plus a verb of consequence, six words or fewer in the core — the honest contrast is the naive multiplication against the real one, e.g. "Your $100 Hour Is Not $208,000" or "$100 Hourly Buys a $154,000 Salary".
