## Discrepancies and exclusions recorded for the drafter

- **Two NVIDIA documents give different concurrency ceilings.** The Video Encode and Decode Support Matrix lists "12" maximum concurrent sessions for the GeForce RTX 5090, while the NVENC Application Note's licensing policy states eight per system for non-qualified GPUs. Both are NVIDIA pages and both are live (matrix: `developer.nvidia.com/video-encode-and-decode-gpu-support-matrix-new`). The article states the licensing-policy figure as the conservative published limit and names the discrepancy rather than picking the friendlier number.
- **The 22.97x in row 5 is a Professional-tier VP9 60 fps multi-pass figure, not a default.** It must never be presented as what a plain AVC ladder costs; the plain Basic-tier AVC ladder figure is the 11x in row 16.
- **No street price for a GPU is cited.** The break-even is expressed per $1,000 and per stated capex so no unpriced hardware claim enters the article; the founders' internal anecdote rig costs are not citable and are not used.
- **Egress, storage and job spin-up are outside this evidence set** and the article says so rather than pricing them.
- **Not claimed:** that NVENC matches software-encoder quality at equal bitrate (row 15 says the project found the opposite), or that a GeForce card scales concurrency with more cards (row 11 says it does not).
