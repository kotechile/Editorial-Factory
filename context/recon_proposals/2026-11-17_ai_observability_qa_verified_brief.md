## Rulings for the drafter

1. **Never print an enforcement number without its coverage number.** Pair "1.7% wrong" with "correct answers fall from 16.4% to 2.4% under faults" (A) and "violations 67.5% → 16.2% at a normal-success cost under 7%" (B). The trade is the story; a bare 1.7% is a coverage regression dressed as an accuracy win.
2. **Attribute every "up to" to one model.** 96.8% is the top of a 23.5%–96.8% range across four models; 67.5%→16.2% is Luna; 1.7% is a pooled figure whose per-model spread is 0.0–3.7. Write "across four models" or "on Luna", never "all models".
3. **Advice is measured, not asserted.** The −8.33 pp is GPT-4o mini's; the other two models' intervals include zero (0.00 and +1.06, the latter with missing-data bounds "inconclusive"). The honest sentence is "advice moved success by −8.33, 0.00 and +1.06 points across three models — two of the three indistinguishable from no advice at all".
4. **691 is one API's arithmetic.** ChEMBL: 2,921,148 returned where 4,225 was correct. Do not average it, do not call it "the average inflation".
5. **Do not re-argue the 2026-11-03 grader thesis.** The renderer/parser and instrument-error material is deliberately absent; nothing here should claim "the grading code is part of the measurement".
6. **No number from the Datadog post in the argument.** It may appear once, as friction: an engineering team runs spec-derived monitors over 1,300+ pods at about 2% CPU — i.e. the deny path is affordable. Say the page is undated if it is cited at all.
7. **The abstention lever is its own row.** "Warning + may abstain" (18.2%) is a different intervention from enforcement and must not be merged into the enforcement claim — it is the finding that permission beats evidence.
