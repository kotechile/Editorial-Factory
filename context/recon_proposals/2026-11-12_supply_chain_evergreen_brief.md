- **§ 1.1455(a)(1)** — records may be "original paper or electronic records or true copies", which is the honest correction to the vendor claim that paper is non-compliant.

## Figures the article may state, and from what

- **The clock:** 24 hours to produce the records on request (rows 1 and 5), in any format for a routine request; as an electronic, sortable spreadsheet when FDA determines an outbreak or emergency exists (§ 1.1455(c)(3), quoted in prose).
- **The retention:** two years from the date the record was created or obtained (row 2). The article may say plainly: a lot shipped 20 months ago is still in scope, and one shipped 25 months ago is not.
- **The scoping threshold:** $250,000 average annual food sales over the previous three years for the small-retail exemption (row 3) — a retailer's exemption, not a distributor's.
- **The date:** January 20, 2026 was the compliance date in the 2022 final rule; the 7 August 2025 proposal would move it 30 months to July 20, 2028 and states it does not amend the requirements (row 4). The body must write "proposed", not "extended".
- **One labelled worked example (the only derived arithmetic):** a distributor receiving 40 lots a day on Food Traceability List items holds 40 × 730 = 29,200 lot records inside the two-year window, and the drill is to produce any one of them — every key data element, joined to the shipment that carried it — inside 24 hours. The article must label the 40-lot volume as the reader's own input, and must not convert it into a cost figure: no labour rate, no software price and no penalty amount is cited anywhere in this brief.
