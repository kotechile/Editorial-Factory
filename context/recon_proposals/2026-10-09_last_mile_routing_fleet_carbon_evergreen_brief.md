**Decision the reader is facing:** Which emission-factor version, factor vintage and system boundary to write into the last-mile carrier scorecard and the Scope 3 disclosure this year — and whether the "X% carbon cut" a carrier or a route vendor just reported is a real route improvement or a factor swap on a published rate table.
**Durability:** The arithmetic does not expire. The diesel factor (10,180 grams of CO2 per gallon) has been the federal conversion factor since the 2010 EPA/DOT rulemaking and is still what EPA publishes; the grid factor is re-published by EIA (0.81 pounds of CO2 per kWh is the figure for the most recent published year, 2023 data) and the tariff classes are annual averages (13.41 cents commercial vs 8.62 cents industrial per kWh, 2025 annual averages). The article therefore states every rate *as of* its own release — the mechanism (activity × a published factor, on a chosen boundary, priced on a chosen tariff class) stays true for years, while the rates that move are dated rather than presented as permanent.

Neither touches emission factors, boundaries or the tariff class; this topic is the durable accounting-arithmetic counterpart to the news run's cost-gap thesis.
**Thesis:** A last-mile carbon number is arithmetic on published rates — 10,180 grams of CO2 per gallon of diesel, 0.81 pounds per kilowatt-hour of grid power — not a measurement of your route, so a carrier's reported cut is only comparable once the factor version, the vintage and the boundary are pinned, and the electric route is priced on a second rate table (the depot's tariff class) that no route plan negotiates.
