**Gate rulings written for the drafter (all binding):**

1. **The household share is never a program fact.** The program sets the aggregator's capacity rate and explicitly leaves the split "pursuant to the terms and conditions agreed to between the VPP aggregator and participant" (row 7). Do not write "$82.80 per kW for you" or any per-home dollar figure as if the state pays it. The rate is the aggregator's, before whatever the contract passes through.
2. **$82.80 and $62.10 are classes, not one rate.** $82.80/kW is the full-season total for a 4-hour resource, $74.52 for 3-hour, $62.10 for 2-hour (row 6). Never merge them, never round to "$83", and never present a 2-hour battery's capacity as earning the 4-hour rate.
3. **"No money for 2027" is appropriation, not death.** The statute authorises DSGS through 2032 (row 13); the 2027 season has no new appropriation per reporting (rows 11–12). Write "no money was appropriated for 2027", never "the program ends" or "the program was cancelled".
4. **The 745 MW is power, not energy.** It is a peak-power record for two evenings (rows 1–2). Never convert it to megawatt-hours, and never claim it ran for a stated number of hours.
5. **Sunrun's 21,000-device San Jose plant is a plan under a non-binding letter of intent** (row 5). Never state that it is operating, or that its dispatching has begun.
6. **The 97% failure-rate statistic is commercial and grid-scale, not residential.** EPRI's database covers BESS failures at commercial and grid installations (rows 15–16). Home batteries are behind-the-meter units and are not in that denominator. May be used only as the industry's trust case, always with EPRI's own small-sample caveat, and never as a statement about a garage battery.
7. **PG&E's 70,000 is a notice, not an outage.** The release says the event "could affect" those customers (row 14). Never write that 70,000 homes lost power.
8. **One aggregator's reserve floor is not the program's rule** (row 19). Attribute the two-hour cap, the 20% floor and the outage carve-out to that contract, and say the program leaves those terms to the contract.
9. **Recorded inconsistency, do not resolve silently:** the FAQ says "up to $26.9 million of DEBA Program funds may be used to support the DSGS Program in the 2026 program season" while also stating "up to $42.7 million is available to fund Option 3" (row 9). The article reports the $42.7M Option 3 pool as the program's own figure for Option 3 and does not present $26.9M as the total.
10. **Arithmetic permitted:** 2026-10-06 to 2026-10-31 is 25 days. Nothing else is to be derived; no per-home, per-kW or fleet totals are computed from the rate table.
11. **Dual-anchor check:** Leg A rows 1, 3 and 4 are VERIFIED primary; Leg B rows 6, 7 and 9 are VERIFIED primary. Both legs clear the §5 gate. Rows 11–13 are the funding context, carried as attribution.
