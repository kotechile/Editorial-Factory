**Emergent Collision Point:** Nineteen days apart, two employers priced the same asset in opposite directions and neither price had anything to do with the individual holder. Oracle's filing converts a cut into a number the company books — a severance reserve, sized in dollars and reconciled quarter by quarter — while the equity that vests on the same four-year service clock is cancelled at the door and never enters that reserve at all; its cost to the company is the unbooked reversal of stock-based compensation the company had already granted "over the service period of the award, which is generally four years" (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341439/000119312526277521/orcl-20260531.htm). ElevenLabs does the mirror image: it manufactures the liquidity event itself, at a price it and its chosen investors set, and frames it as a recurring obligation ("We're committed to giving our team regular opportunities for liquidity"). Read together, an employee's equity is not valued by the company's valuation at all — it is valued at whichever event arrives, a tender that clears it or a restructuring that cancels it — and both events are scheduled by the employer. Neither document says that, because each is written from the company's side of its own calendar.

**Hook:** On 11 September 2026 Oracle told the SEC it had added $700 million to a restructuring plan whose costs are "primarily… employee severance", and on 30 September ElevenLabs announced it had spent $300 million buying employees' stock at twice its February valuation. Two ways to settle an equity promise inside one month — and only one of them reaches the person holding the grant.

**Tension:** The severance formula prices tenure in weeks; vesting prices equity in years. A 26-week cap is roughly half a year of base pay, while a single four-year grant can hold years of granted compensation that has not yet vested — so the formula that decides what a departing employee receives is structurally blind to the largest line in the same employee's pay. That blindness is not an accounting accident: forfeited awards return to the plan pool, and Oracle states plainly that it accounts "for forfeitures of stock-based awards as they occur". Meanwhile the tender side shows the opposite arrangement working: a company that wants its staff to hold the paper will build a market for it, on a cadence, at a price it picks. The employee sits inside both regimes at once and controls only one variable — how much of the next year's pay is riding on a vest date.

**Target reader:** equity_career_strategist (senior IC to VP-level tech professional)

**Single claim to defend:** The value of an employee's equity is set by which corporate event arrives — a tender that prices it or a restructuring that cancels it — so the only number worth engineering at offer time is how much granted-but-unvested pay is exposed to the next event, not the valuation the offer letter quotes.
