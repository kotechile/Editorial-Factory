## Verification notes and caveats the drafter must carry

1. **FLAGGED — the wage is a category median, not a picker's wage.** C4 is SOC 53-7065.00, "stockers and order fillers", a broad labour category. It is a reference price, not the rate on any one site: quote it as the category median and never as "what your pickers earn". Wage rows go stale annually, so it carries the release vintage in the sentence.
2. **FLAGGED — C5 and C6 are one capstone study at one facility, not an industry benchmark.** "Estimated" and "modelled" stay in the sentence, and the piece must not imply the 24% was measured after a live re-slot.
3. **C1 and C7–C10 come from a trade-press article dated 1 October 2015.** Cite it for a mechanic; never present its numbers as current benchmarks, and say plainly that the comparison is a decade old.
4. **C9's "30%" must not be a gate-style headline figure.** On that page the string "30%" first matches "after 30 years", so if the number appears in prose it is attributed to the consultant's estimation, not stated as a measured result.
5. **The 10 miles never converts into dollars or hours in the body.** That conversion needs a walking pace no source supplies; the article gives the reader the shape of the formula and names what it lacks.
6. **Authorised arithmetic only:** $17.95 ÷ 60 = $0.299 per picker-minute (about 30 cents); at 80/20, 1,000 active SKUs means about 200 SKUs carry 80% of pick activity; 40% walk share × 24% travel cut = 9.6 percentage points of picking time, stated conditionally. Nothing else is derived — no aisle widths, no pick rates, no payback periods, no walking speeds.
7. Source dates: Modern Materials Handling 2015-10-01 (C1, C7–C10) and 2025-09-01 (C2, C3); O*NET 2025 wage release (C4); MIT CTL capstone publication date 2025-05-01 (C5, C6).
