**Emergent Collision Point:** Forrester prices the next twelve months of AI as a routing decision — half the tokens for a twentieth of the spend — while the buyers Open Future Forum surveyed have set their payback test at six months and are measuring it in whole tokens. Neither document can state the consequence, because Forrester only forecasts the composition of the bill and the CFO report only records the deadline and the instrument. Put together: the deadline is about to be met or missed on a number that is losing its relationship to the money, so a workload can clear its six-month ROI gate by being routed to a cheaper model without any business process changing — and Forrester's own explanation for why more than half of CIOs will still miss is that the process *didn't* change. The meter cannot see the difference.
**Hook:** Forrester expects open-weight models to carry half of enterprise AI tokens next year while taking five percent of the spend — and 45 percent of the buyers running those tokens require payback inside twelve months.
**Tension:** The spend is not in dispute and the standard is not moving: AI takes a fifth of business technology budgets in 2027 and 45 percent of decision-makers want their money back inside a year. What is missing is an instrument that can tell a routed token from a changed process. The FinOps Foundation's own planning guidance puts token and API cost at 10 to 25 percent of total AI spend once energy, capital, infrastructure, software, labor and process are counted — so the number under the ROI test is the smallest line of the bill, and the line that decides payback has no owner in 13 percent of organisations and no budget in 31 percent of them.

**Single claim to defend:** The six-month AI payback test is being run on token spend at the exact moment token volume stops tracking token spend, so the test will increasingly pass on routing and fail on the business outcome — and the fix is to put the payback clock on a named process change with an owner, not on the model bill.
