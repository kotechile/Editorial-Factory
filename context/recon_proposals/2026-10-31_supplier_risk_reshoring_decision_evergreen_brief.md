**Thesis:** A foreign-trade zone does not reduce your duty — it changes three things about it: the value it is charged on (the parts' admission price, not the finished good's transaction price), the tariff line it is charged at (the parts' rate if you elect privileged status, the finished good's rate if you do not), and the date you pay. The date is the lever everyone sells and the smallest one: at bank prime and a 60-day cycle a deferral is worth 1.15 percent of the duty, about a tenth of a percentage point of the goods. The money is in the base and the rate, and both are decided by the spread the reader's own bill of materials carries between its component lines and its finished-good line.

## Gate rulings and hand checks (for the audit trail)

- **The two rate rows are one publisher and one page, and they earn their place.** Prime and the effective funds rate bracket the answer: a deferral is worth *something between* them no matter how the reader funds the buffer, so the article's arithmetic holds for a company on a revolver and for one sitting on cash. Both cells were probed with `citation_hub_dossier.figure_evidence` and land on the correct table rows ("Bank prime loan … 7.00" and "Federal funds (effective) … 3.88").
- **The election itself cannot carry a gate row and is carried in prose.** 19 CFR 146.65(a)(1) classifies privileged foreign merchandise "at the rate of duty and tax in force on the date of filing, in complete and proper form, the application for privileged status" and (a)(2) classifies nonprivileged merchandise "as constructively transferred to Customs territory at the time the entry or entry summary is filed"; (b)(2) sets the dutiable value at "the price actually paid or payable for the merchandise in the transaction that caused the merchandise to be admitted into the zone". None of those sentences contains a numeric token a row could lead on without matching a subsection marker, so the mechanism rides in prose under citations [3] and [5], and the verbatim quotes above are recorded here for the fact-check pass.
- **A real trap worth one watch item:** § 146.65(a)(1) also says classification of merchandise "subject to a tariff-rate import quota will be made only at the higher non-quota duty rate" when privileged status is granted — electing the parts' rate on quota goods forfeits the in-quota rate. Quoted verbatim here for the draft.
- **Do not present the $25 / $485 figures in 19 CFR 24.23(b)(1)(i)(B) as today's floor and ceiling.** The regulation prints the unadjusted amounts and says they are "adjusted in accordance with the terms of § 24.22(k)"; the article says the fee is 0.3464 percent of value inside a floor and ceiling CBP adjusts each year, and gives no dollar figure for them.
- **The 61st-day provision is narrow and must be written narrowly.** It applies to "merchandise that consists of goods subject to USMCA drawback, as defined in section 4534(a)", manufactured or changed in condition in a zone and exported to a USMCA country. It does not make every zone-to-Mexico shipment dutiable, and the article must not generalise it to "exports are taxed".
- **Arithmetic to recompute in the final, not copy from this brief:** duty × rate × days ÷ 365. At 7.00 percent over 60 days the factor is 1.1507 percent of the duty; at 3.88 percent it is 0.6378 percent. On $1,000,000 of duty that is $11,507 and $6,378. Expressed against goods value at an illustrative 10 percent duty rate, the deferral is 0.115 percent of the goods — one tenth of one percent — and that illustrative rate must be labelled as an illustration, never as a claim about the reader's line.
