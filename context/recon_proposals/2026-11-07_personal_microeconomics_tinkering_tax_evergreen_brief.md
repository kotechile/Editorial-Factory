**Arithmetic the article must carry (recomputed here, not in the draft).** Break-even month = annual price ÷ monthly price. Fastmail: $60 ÷ $6 = **10 of 12 months** (83%); $114 ÷ $6 = **19 of 24** (79%); $168 ÷ $6 = **28 of 36** (78%). Backblaze: $99 ÷ $9 = **11 of 12** (92%); $189 ÷ $9 = **21 of 24** (87.5%). The discounts on the same rungs: Fastmail 16.7% / 20.8% / 22.2%; Backblaze 8.3% / 12.5%; Google One's printed ceiling 16%. So the deeper the rung the earlier the break-even month proportionally — but the money at risk rises in absolute terms: cancel Fastmail's 36-month term at month 12 and $168 has bought $72 of service, a $96 loss; cancel the 24-month rung at month 12 and $114 has bought $72, a $42 loss; cancel the 12-month rung at month 6 and $60 has bought $36, a $24 loss. On the survey anchor, a $219 monthly itemized bill is $2,628 a year, so a 16.7% prepay across a stack of that size is worth about $439 a year and would put about $2,190 on the vendor's side of the ledger at once.
