## Claim rulings for the drafter

1. **Two bases in one paper — quote the 224, never the 468, for the word "comparisons."** The abstract sums the sweep differently ("HBF reduces the minimum GPU count … in 98 of 468 comparisons"), while the results sentence gives the base that makes the number mean something: 98 of the **224** comparisons where both layouts pass and conventional serving needs more than one GPU. Write "98 of 224" and say what the 224 counts (the comparisons where the conventional layout also met the target). A sentence pairing 98 with 468 reads as a 21% success rate; the honest reading is 44% of the eligible set.
2. **The paper's price is an assumption, not a quote.** "Half a GPU price per stack" is the premium the authors test against; they do not use an HBM or flash market price. Never convert it to dollars, and never say an HBF stack costs half a GPU — say the saving survives *when* a stack costs half a GPU.
3. **This is a simulation.** Say so before the first number: results come from a discrete-event simulator over public component data, and the paper's own sensitivity audit leaves 2 of 14 reference wins standing. The paper's headline is not "flash wins"; it is "flash wins under these conditions, and the conditions are fragile".
4. **Samsung disclosed no volume, no price level and no customers.** "Sold out at an agreed target price" is the whole of it. Do not attach a percentage, a dollar figure, a customer name or a 2027 capacity number to that sentence.
5. **"Agreed target price" is not "higher price".** Samsung says the price reflects product competitiveness and that negotiations completed at target levels. Any statement that the 2027 price is up must be attributed to TrendForce's forecast (row 7 is DRAM contract prices; the 121% HBM forecast is out of window and was already used on 2026-10-07 — do not reuse it).
6. **8-Hi is a forecast about a configuration, not a contract.** TrendForce expects the premium; it does not report a signed 8-Hi price. Write "is expected to cost" and name TrendForce.
7. **The KV-cache claim belongs to Samsung, and it is about SSDs.** Row 4 supports "the vendor says KV cache growth is driving TLC SSD demand". It does not support "KV cache is moving off HBM" as a general industry fact, and it does not support any claim about flash reading KV cache — the paper models flash holding **weights**, with active KV kept near the GPU.
8. **No hands-on claim.** This desk has run none of these configurations. Every number is attributed to the paper or to the company; the article's own contribution is the connection between them.
9. **Do not name a GPU price.** The break-even is expressed in GPU-equivalents for a reason; a dollar GPU price would be invented and would also date the piece.
