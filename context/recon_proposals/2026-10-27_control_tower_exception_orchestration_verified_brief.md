Two honesty constraints follow and are binding on the drafter: (1) the $100 billion figure is a **vendor's scenario model**, and the piece must say so in the body, not in a footnote — a company that sells chargeback-prevention software produced the estimate of chargeback cost; (2) Signal B's sample is 107 analysed responses with 11 C-suite respondents, labelled directional by its own authors, so the function scores are quoted as a survey reading and never as a measurement of capability.

**Bars on the drafter.**
- Do not describe the OTIF programme as new or tightening this month: Walmart's programme began in July 2017 and the 98% / 3% standard was tightened in 2020, both stated in the same FreightWaves piece.
- Do not present the $100 billion as an audited or documented loss; the trade account calls the recovery band "modeled estimates, rather than documented savings".
- Do not restate purchase-order fill-rate arithmetic (line fill raised to the power of the lines, 63.6% quarterly OTIF) — that is another vertical's published article and shares no claim with this one.
- Do not say a control tower "sees" chargebacks: it sees delivery events; the chargeback is a finance artefact produced from those events. The precise claim is that the exception stream carries the delivery event and the deduction, and neither carries the shelf-space consequence.
- No figure in the body may appear without one of A1–A10, B1–B7, C1, C2, or a labelled arithmetic step from C3–C5.
