## Gate rulings for the drafter (from `skills/fact_check.md` §5)

1. **The rule is a clarification, not a new right.** TDI's own filing says the amendments "do not change the rule's meaning or application". Never write that Texas has just banned roof-age declinations, that a new protection started, or that anything changed for a policyholder on 9 October. The news is that the component-age reading is being written into the text and that the comment window is open.
2. **Condition survives, in the agency's own words.** Any sentence about age must carry the exception in the same breath: physical condition of the property or its components remains a lawful ground to decline. Do not imply that a worn roof cannot cost someone a policy.
3. **The credit has no published amount.** Bulletin B-0008-26 is dated 16 September 2026 and is a standing record, not an in-window event; cite it as background, and never attach a percentage to it. Its absence of a number is the claim, and it is a negative fact established by reading the bulletin.
4. **Attribute the weighting finding and every State Farm figure to 13 Investigates.** R1, R2 and R3 are a TV station's read of TDI filing data; the release itself says only "averaged". Write "TDI's spokesperson told 13 Investigates…" and "13 Investigates' review of the filings found…". Never put those numbers in TDI's mouth.
5. **Do not convert the average into a reader's own bill.** $150 is what 4.3% of the *state average* premium comes to; a specific renewal can be going up or down by any amount. Say what the figure is measured against in the same sentence (`skills/claude_humanizer.md` §3.5).
6. **No hands-on claim and no advice voice.** This desk has no inspection of any roof. Report what the filings and the rule say; do not tell the reader to buy, file, appeal or switch.
7. **Do not generalise Texas to the country.** Other states run mandatory schedules (Louisiana's benchmark, 29/43/49% by zone, effective 1 January 2027) and a cross-link to the desk's own 2026-10-21 piece is the honest way to say so, not a claim about "the market".
