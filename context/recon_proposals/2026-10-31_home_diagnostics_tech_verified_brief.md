## Gate rulings for the drafter (binding)

1. **The alarms' documented failure is smoke and fire, not carbon monoxide.** Both warnings read "fail to alarm in the presence of hazardous smoke and fire". These are combination smoke/CO units, but no CPSC document in this brief says they failed a CO test. Write the alarm failure as a failure to alert to smoke and fire and never as "failed to detect CO".
2. **Never total the two warnings.** 51,825 belongs to model JZA-540COM alone. Model YHW-542COM-R has no published unit count; describe it as "a second warning the same day, with no unit count published".
3. **"Working" is the regulator's word.** The stopgap sentence is Health Canada's, in the joint recall record; attribute it there rather than to CPSC.
4. **The boiler does not explode.** The front cover detaches after unburned gas ignites inside a sealed cabinet; CO poisoning is one of three listed hazards, alongside fire and impact.
5. **The jaw belongs to HPAC Magazine.** CPSC and Health Canada confirm "one report of injury in Canada" and never describe it. Attribute the technician's broken jaw to the trade report.
6. **Prices are sold-price ranges for a model, not list prices for a unit.** Keep "between $11 and $107" attached to model JZA-540COM and "between $18 and $168" to YHW-542COM-R, with their sale windows.
7. **The standards are not interchangeable.** UL 2034 is the CO alarm standard; UL 217 is the smoke alarm standard. CPSC's sentence names both because the products in question are combination units.
8. **Two manufacturers publish two different lives.** First Alert says five to seven years; Kidde says seven to ten. Carry the disagreement — never average it into "about seven years" presented as consensus.
9. **Every dated-background figure keeps its year.** The 36.4%-to-50.0% installation estimate rests on 2011 and 2009 sources inside a report published in March 2014; the death counts are 2009 and a 2007–2009 average. None of them may be written as a current figure.
10. **A technician's instrument is not in this brief.** Combustion analysis (flue CO, draft) appears only in trade explainers that carry no publication date, so it is background at best: if the draft needs it, it is one attributed clause, never a figure.

## Relational arithmetic the draft may use (authorised)

- 4,726 US units against 2,261 in Canada is 6,987 units in the joint recall — a sum of two published counts, allowed.
- 34 occurrences against 6,987 unit-reads is not a failure rate and must not be written as one: the occurrences are reports of one part breaking, and the recall covers units, not years of service.
- The 51,825 units at $11 apiece is an order-of-magnitude floor on what a household spent on a device that may never sound; the draft may say "a $11 unit" and "51,825 of them" but must not present a product of the two as a market figure.
