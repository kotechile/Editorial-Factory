# Evergreen Brief: gpu_hardware — 2026-10-28

**Archetype:** evergreen
**Vertical:** gpu_hardware
**Persona:** infra_engineer
**Decision the reader is facing:** You have 32 accelerators and a model that does not fit inside one node's memory budget. Do you widen tensor parallelism across the node boundary, or keep TP inside the NVLink domain and spend the extra width on replicas or on expert parallelism — and how do you tell, from the collective rather than the spec sheet, which one your stack is actually doing?
**Durability:** The mechanism is the shape of a ring all-reduce, not a part number: it needs 2(N−1) serial reduce-scatter/all-gather phases, so per-rank *volume* approaches 2M while the number of synchronisation points keeps growing, and in the decode phase the message it carries is only batch × hidden dimension — about 128 KB for a 70B model at batch 8 — which is a latency problem, not a bandwidth one. That is a property of transformer inference on any interconnect less capable than HBM, so the rule survives the next silicon generation; what changes is the constant. Figures are as of: the multi-node measurement as of its CAIS '26 publication (20 May 2026), the collective-overhead measurement as of version 5 of the TokenWeave preprint, the topology simulation as of 29 Sep 2026, and the NVLink bandwidths as of NVIDIA's product pages read 2026-10-28.
**De-dup:** `2026-10-14_prefill-decode-two-pool-gap` is this vertical's nearest prior evergreen artifact, and it argued *which phase runs where* — prefill and decode split into pools, with the KV-cache transfer between the pools as the price of the split. This piece does not re-argue that split; it takes a multi-node deployment as given and settles where the parallelism topology stops paying — the all-reduce inside every layer, which is a different object from the cache that moves between phases. Also differentiated: `2026-10-07_kv-cache-is-the-concurrency-ceiling` (how many sessions one fixed card holds), `2026-10-21_cap-your-card-at-80` (the power cap as an operating point) and `2026-10-21_your-68k-tokens-cost-every-step` (energy per token set by a request's own KV history). Cross-vertical greps of `published/` for "tensor parallel", "all-reduce", "NVLink" and "interconnect" returned no published artifact at all — the only hits are this vertical's own briefs — so nothing in the corpus argues the scale-up-domain thesis. Today's news artifact `2026-10-28_your-480gb-card-wont-skip-the-memory-queue` prices memory *capacity against memory bandwidth* inside one card; this is about the wire between cards.
**Thesis:** Tensor parallelism stops paying at the node edge: past the NVLink domain each extra rank adds a serial all-reduce phase to a latency-bound ~128 KB message, so the card that was supposed to double throughput mostly doubles synchronisation — and the measured lever is the collective and the sharding layout (hierarchical all-reduce, fused/overlapped collectives, low TP with high EP), not another GPU.

## Notes the drafting stage must carry
- **Perlmutter's node is four GPUs, not eight.** The paper says "four GPUs (single node) to eight GPUs (two nodes)", and that TP latency for the 70B model then "remains almost constant or increases with GPU count" after 16 GPUs. Write the boundary as *the node*, and say Perlmutter's node width explicitly — an article that calls 8 GPUs "one node" is wrong about a cited detail the persona will check.
- **The 3.68–4.35× is TP-vs-EP at a fixed 32-rank budget, not "doubling the GPUs makes it slower".** Keep the comparison inside one budget, and label it as simulation.
- **NVIDIA's "900GB/s versus 128GB/s for PCIe Gen5" is bidirectional aggregate per GPU, not per-direction, and it is not a throughput claim.** Give the ratio (about 7×, which the page itself states) and do not convert it into a tokens-per-second promise.
- **The paper's 128 KB decode message is arithmetic, not a benchmark**: batch 8 × hidden dimension 8,192 × 2 bytes. Show the multiplication so the reader can redo it for their own batch.
- Recompute every ratio in the final from its two cited endpoints after the rewrite (§ the derived-percentage rule in `skills/evergreen_topics.md`).
