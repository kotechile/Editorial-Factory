# Angle Brief: workstation_compute_economics — 2026-11-24
**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)
**Winner:** The Local Box Doesn't Need New Memory — It Needs the Memory the Market Already Wrote Off
**Scores:** E=9.0 A=9.0 S=8.5 → Composite=8.8
**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Sam Altman on the Cheeky Pint podcast (episode dated 2026-10-29; TechPowerUp's report published 2026-10-31 — https://www.techpowerup.com/354346/openai-ceo-argues-home-pcs-waste-the-worlds-limited-dram-supply) called local inference a global misallocation: "quite terrible to have everybody have a bunch of the world's limited supply of DRAM sitting in their own computer" and "being used 5% of the time." The same report places OpenAI's own Stargate memory demand at up to 900,000 DRAM wafers a month — around 40% of global output — with Dell's CEO expecting the shortage to worsen in 2027. The argument assumes local inference consumes NEW memory: wafers that would otherwise serve the data center.
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Three decommissioned Nvidia V100-16GB cards in a $1,042 used-server rig hit 100 tokens per second on Qwen 3.6 35B (Tom's Hardware, 2026-11-19 — https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/three-e-waste-nvidia-v100s-hit-100-tokens-per-second-on-qwen-3-6-35b-in-a-usd1-042-homelab-rig-decommissioned-gpus-outpace-a-usd5-399-m5-max-mac-studio-at-long-contexts), outpacing a $5,399 M5 Max Mac Studio at long contexts and Nvidia's own DGX Spark at 45.8 tok/s, across 130+ llama.cpp tests on nine builds of K80/P100/V100 parts. The builder daily-drives Qwen 3.6 35B on it. The VRAM in that rig was manufactured years ago and was headed for scrap — it allocates no new wafer.
**Emergent Collision Point:** The window's loudest argument against local inference (Altman's: home machines hoard scarce DRAM at 5% utilization) and the window's loudest price signal (the RTX 5090 at $6,919–$9,400, the RTX 5080 pulled from Nvidia's own store — https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/nvidia-store-quietly-pulls-the-rtx-5080-following-rtx-5090-removal-street-prices-climb-to-2-2x-over-msrp) both price local compute in NEW memory. But the measured escape route is OLD memory: the $1,042 rig of already-written-off V100s beats the new $5,399 flagship on the same model. Neither source accounts for the third allocation state — memory that is bought once, scrapped once, and computes for a decade. If throughput is what a workstation sells, the allocation argument's denominator (new DRAM) is the wrong stock: the correct unit of allocation is a token-second of already-existing VRAM, and decommissioned silicon is the only inventory in this market whose price did not move.
**Hook:** OpenAI's CEO says the memory in your computer is a waste — 5% utilized, better off in his servers — at the very moment a $1,042 rig of scrapped V100s out-runs the $5,399 Mac Studio he never has to bill you against.
**Tension:** Who this empowers — the infra engineer who stops treating the DRAM shortage as a forecast and starts treating secondhand VRAM as an allocation arbitrage: a measured 100 tok/s of Qwen 3.6 35B for $1,042 of capital that no wafer shortage can reprice. Who this threatens — the argument itself: if the freed-up inventory is decommissioned datacenter silicon rather than newly squeezed consumer cards, then "everybody hoarding DRAM" describes an allocation failure of inventory that was already allocated, not demand that competes with Stargate; and the workstation tier of this market (RTX 5090 at $216/GB of VRAM versus $62 at launch) becomes the worst entry point, not the default.
