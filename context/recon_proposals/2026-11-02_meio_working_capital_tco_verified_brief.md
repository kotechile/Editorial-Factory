## Gate rulings for the drafter (read before writing a word)

- **FLAGGED — not a like-for-like comparison.** An equipment maker books customer advances because it sells years ahead of the build; a retailer sells at the counter and cannot take a deposit on a bag of feed. The 24% is a property of *that* business model, not a benchmark any firm can hit. Say what it is and do not hold it up as a target.
- **FLAGGED — Caterpillar's build is proportional, Tractor Supply's is not.** Caterpillar's inventory grew 22.5% against sales growth of 21%; Tractor Supply's grew 11.2% against 3.6%. Do not write that Caterpillar "loaded up" or that its build was unusual.
- **FLAGGED — the two advances numbers do not tie.** The balance-sheet change is +$2,015M; the cash-flow line is +$4,002M against $1,649M. The release does not reconcile them. Use the balance-sheet figures for levels and shares, and refer to the cash-flow line only as money the line "contributed" — never add, subtract or merge the two.
- **FLAGGED — the tariff credit is context, not thesis.** The $128M of IEEPA recoveries is a one-time credit; this desk published a tariff-refund article on 2026-10-26 and this piece must not re-argue it. At most one sentence, attributed.
- **FLAGGED — days of stock is a rate, not an average.** D3 and D4 divide closing inventory by nine-month cost of sales. Tractor Supply's own annualized turns (3.08) imply about 118 days on average inventory, a different base. Present 162 and 129 as the desk's own calculation on one basis; never compare the 129 to the company's turns figure as though they were the same measure.
- **FLAGGED — 4% is the top of a range, not a company's cost.** The Fed's range is 3-3/4 to 4 percent. A company's own borrowing cost differs by credit. Write the range, and label any interest figure as the desk's arithmetic on it.
- **Not supported anywhere — do not write it:** that tariffs caused either build; that Caterpillar asked for the advances this quarter; that Tractor Supply's build was a mistake; that either company's stock will be written down. No source says any of these.
- **Required attribution:** the dealer-inventory sentence belongs to Resource Industries (mining, heavy construction, quarry and aggregates), not to Caterpillar as a whole — Construction Industries grew on end-user sales.
- **Voice:** the article reports what the filings show and labels its reading as a reading. No advice to the reader, no "operators should", no second-person instruction.
