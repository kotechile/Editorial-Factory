## Binding rulings for the drafter

1. **"21%" is a share of all claims filed, not of the closed ones, and the piece must say which.** 14,402 of 68,071 is 21.2%. Of the 39,613 that had closed by 6 November, 36.4% closed with nothing paid. Use the 21% for the headline (it is the conservative one) and be explicit that it is of all claims filed; use the closed-claims share only if the sentence names the denominator in the same breath.
2. **Nothing here says why a claim paid nothing, and the article must not imply it.** The OIR table prints counts by status and by line, and it prints the *categories* of common reasons on the page. The 6,835 under-deductible and 5,374 administrative counts come from a newsroom's reading of that data (row 12) — attribute them to the reporting, and never convert "closed without payment" into "denied".
3. **Open claims are not failures.** 28,457 claims were still open on 6 November, a month after landfall; 7,975 of those had already received a payment. Do not add open and closed-without-payment together to produce a larger "paid nothing" number.
4. **Colorado's 15% is conditional, not an entitlement.** The release says a homeowner "may be eligible for a 15% discount … if they meet underwriting requirements with CSAA", and the eligibility guarantee is CSAA's, for the Enhanced designation plus a companion auto policy. Do not upgrade either into a state guarantee — the law requires the notice, the score, the appeal and the pricing of mitigation; it requires no carrier to insure any home.
5. **The law's dates are three, and each is different.** Signed 28 May 2025; in force 1 July 2026; notices from 1 October 2026. The 12 October document is a consumer release, not the effective date. State the timeline once, in that order, and never call 12 October the day the law took effect.
6. **No hands-on claim and no invented cost.** This run has no installation receipts, contractor quotes or measured payback. The article may not say what a designation costs, how long it takes to earn, or what a homeowner will save in dollars. It may say what the documents say the credit is and what the clock is.
7. **The reason split and the IBHS research position are attributions.** IBHS's view that the first five feet matter is IBHS's position, which its own release states; the live burn is a demonstration, not a measurement, and the article must not present it as a tested result.
8. **Florida's rate decreases are context, not a leg.** They exist to show that repricing and claim outcomes are different conversations in the same quarter. Do not average them with anything and do not build the thesis on them.
9. **Do not re-argue Louisiana, Texas or the California FAIR Plan.** Louisiana's filed-discount table and the Legislative Auditor's 22% payback are outside the window and were this vertical's 2026-10-21 thesis; Texas was 2026-11-04; the FAIR Plan's 29.1% increase and 16.4% credit ceiling were published on wellroost.com on 2026-10-03 (`/california-fair-plan-premiums-jump-291-oct-15-your-exit-strategy/`). None of the three appears in the copy — a second piece on the same front anchored to the same two numbers is cannibalization, not coverage.
