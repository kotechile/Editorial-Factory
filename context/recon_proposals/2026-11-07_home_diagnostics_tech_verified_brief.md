## Drafter rulings (read before drafting)

1. **The article's claim is about a number, not about a product being bad.** The P3 Lite's page discloses the upscale in its own overview copy and specification table. Do not write that the camera lies, that the resolution is "fake", or that it cannot see what it claims to show. Write that the headline figure counts rendered pixels while the detector row counts sampled ones, and that both appear on the same page.
2. **Do not imply the upscale has no value.** Super-resolution sharpens the picture a reader looks at, and the vendor says so. The defect is in the buying comparison — a spec-sheet reader ranking 512 × 384 against 160 × 120 — not in the algorithm.
3. **No app is dead and no vendor is accused of abandonment.** The strongest available statement is the design principle, quoted from the builder, plus Hackaday's line. Do not cite app-store reviews, do not say a specific app stopped working, and do not generalise from one hobbyist's motive to a brand's policy.
4. **The $1,399 camera is not a rip-off in this piece.** Its page sells calibration, MSX, a touchscreen, a 12-month cloud subscription, a warranty and training. Say what the premium buys, and say plainly that a buyer who needs a report another party will accept is buying exactly those things.
5. **Use $269 as the list price and $239 as the sale price**, both stated, and say "prices read 2026-11-07". Never build the argument on the discount.
6. **Terms to gloss in place, at first use:** microbolometer (the detector chip that measures heat), native resolution (the pixels the detector samples), upscaling (software adding pixels between sampled ones), noise-equivalent temperature difference (NETD — the smallest temperature step the camera can distinguish). "MSX" expands to Multi-Spectral Dynamic Imaging.
7. **No sensor price, no bill of materials.** Row 16 is binding.
8. **Do not generalise beyond imager resolution.** Nothing here says anything about moisture meters, borescopes or gas detectors, and no claim may be extended to "all cheap tools".
