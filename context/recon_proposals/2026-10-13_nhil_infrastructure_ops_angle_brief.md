**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Open Compute Project, *Interface Specification Between Hyperscalers and Utility Grid for Disturbance Performance, Version 1.0.0* — OCP Base Specification **effective 2026-09-17**, authored by engineers from Google, Microsoft, Meta and NVIDIA. The hyperscalers "commit to this LVRT/HVRT and PFAPR performance solely for the computational load component of the data center, which is typically ~70-90% of total data center load": **2.0 s** of ride-through between 0.80 and 0.90 per-unit voltage, **0.5 s** between 0.50 and 0.80, **0.25 s** between 0.35 and 0.50, **0.15 s** below 0.35, and a post-fault active power recovery commitment to "restore to **90% of pre-fault load within 2 seconds**". Frequency ride-through, rate of change of frequency, phase jump, counter-based protection schemes, ramp rate, intra-minute variation and forced oscillations are pushed to §4.6.1 *Future requirements* with **no numeric values in 1.0.0**, and cooling loads are excluded entirely. ("Data Center Facility Project: Interface Specification Between Hyperscalers and Utility Grid for Disturbance Performance Version 1.0.0" — https://www.opencompute.org/documents/ocp-base-spec-interface-specification-between-hyperscalers-and-utility-grid-for-disturbance-performance-pdf)
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** *Self-Healing Harness for Runtime Oversight of Agent Self-Modification* (arXiv:2609.24130, **2026-09-21**) — an external runtime gate on an agent's own behavioral changes. Across 16 matched baseline/harness runs (AppWorld, Terminal-Bench, τ²-Bench) the gate **rejected 383 replay-decided proposals**, and of those **211 (55%) improved their triggering failure while degrading a case that previously worked** — "locally beneficial self-modifications can introduce collateral regressions often enough to materially affect gate decisions" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24130). Corroborating: AegisFlow (arXiv:2610.06971, 2026-10-03) — 98.1% MTTR improvement (170 min → 3.2 min) at a 92% patch success rate, worst case 85% on Shadow DOM; ResonAct (arXiv:2609.34701, 2026-09-28) — a runtime self-healing control plane with detection precision 70.59%–82.91% and recovery rates of only 10.48%–46.67%.

**Emergent Collision Point:** Both halves of this vertical now have the same failure, at two different layers, and each side has published the evidence without naming the other. On July 22, 2026 a 230 kV line in Northern Virginia failed mechanically and "the fault was properly cleared" — and roughly **2,970 MW** of data center load transferred to onsite generation anyway, followed by another **1,099 MW** as system voltage rose (≈3.8 GW, "more than twice the size" of the ~1,500 MW July 2024 and February 2025 events), forcing PJM to cut generation and deploy reactive power before restoring its area control error inside nine minutes. Every one of those transfers was a correctly functioning protective action: the facility did the right thing for its own equipment, and the aggregate of three dozen right local decisions removed a block of demand large enough to move voltage across the Eastern Interconnection. The self-healing-harness paper measures the identical shape in software — 211 of 383 locally beneficial self-modifications broke a case that used to work — which is why its authors' answer is an *external* gate rather than a better local repair rule. FERC's order of October 9, 2026 closes the loop on why this is now the operator's problem rather than the utility's: the commission **approved PJM's large-load registry** (location, ramp schedule, capacity supply, backup generation, fuel sources) and **rejected the curtailment-first IRAS framework** as an intrusion on state jurisdiction and potentially discriminatory — so the grid gets the data and not the switch, and the only remaining lever on 3.8 GW of behaviour is the setting inside the facility. The ride-through spec is the industry writing that lever down for itself: a 2-second duty where the chillers have no duty at all.

**Hook:** On July 22, 2026 the grid did everything right — a 230 kV line in Northern Virginia failed, its fault cleared normally, and the system held. Then roughly 3.8 gigawatts of data center load left anyway, in two waves, because a few dozen facilities each correctly decided that the disturbance was a threat to their own equipment.
**Tension:** The reliability obligation has migrated from the interconnection tariff to the protection and control settings inside the fence, and nobody has priced that migration. FERC refused to give a regional grid operator the right to curtail large loads on October 9; PJM's operating committee chair said the data centers involved "should not disconnect from the grid"; Texas responded to the same problem on September 21 by suspending data-center permit issuance pending ERCOT and water audits. That empowers whoever wrote the ride-through standard — Google, Microsoft, Meta and NVIDIA, whose v1.0.0 leaves frequency ride-through, phase jumps and rate of change of frequency unnumbered and exempts cooling — and it exposes the operators who inherit a grandfathered site, because the spec's own implementation plan exempts facilities "already operational or that have executed interconnection agreements as of the effective date" for their lifetime while making incremental growth at those sites subject to the new duty.
