## Gate rulings for the drafter

- **Both anchor papers are unreviewed preprints (FLAGGED, not blocking).** Write "a preprint posted on 30 October", never "a peer-reviewed study", and keep the two groups' affiliations out of the claim (the brief does not establish them beyond author names).
- **PrivacyTrace measures synthetic profiles (FLAGGED).** 1,000 profiles are synthetic and 4,000 trajectories were generated with real MCP tools; the 26.25% is a benchmark rate, not a field measurement of any deployed fleet. Never write "26% of users are identified" — write that the benchmark's attackers reached 26.25% against 17.97% for guessing.
- **The 2% attack rate is the authors' scenario, not a measured base rate (FLAGGED).** Report it as the rate the audit used to convert AUROC into precision; do not claim 2% is what production fleets see. The audit's own finding is that the eleven evaluations used 16%–86%, which is the point.
- **`mcp.session.id` is Recommended, not Required, and the conventions are marked Development (FLAGGED).** Write that the specification recommends a session identifier on MCP spans; do not write that every span carries one. Do not claim the standard ships it universally.
- **The MCP protocol revision 2026-07-28 removes the protocol-level session.** This belongs in the catch as the counter-argument: the join key the conventions recommend is being retired at the protocol level, so cross-session linking is a choice an operator makes by retaining traces, not a property of the protocol.
- **Do not total or net any two figures.** The 26.25% and the 7.6% are different quantities measured on different populations (profile inference by an attacker; alarm precision of a detector); they may sit side by side, never be added, subtracted, or averaged.
- **No hands-on claim.** The desk ran no benchmark, no red team and no deployment. Nothing in the piece may say "we tested", "our tests", or "in our environment".
- Arithmetic allowed: none beyond the papers' own numbers. The relation "0.076 precision at a 2% attack rate" is the paper's computed number, not the desk's.
