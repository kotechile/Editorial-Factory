## Verification notes and gate rulings the drafter must carry

1. **RULING — never write "the deduction is capped at $3,000" without the gains-first half.** § 1211(b) allows losses against capital gains *in full and with no ceiling*; $3,000 is only the slice that can reach other income once losses exceed gains. A sentence that compresses this into "losses are capped at $3,000 a year" misstates the statute and would make a reader with realised gains believe a harvest cannot offset them. The correct coupling: dollar-for-dollar against realised gains, then up to $3,000 against salary, then carry forward.
2. **RULING — the loss is deferred, not destroyed (C6).** The disallowed loss moves into the basis of the replacement shares and is recognised when those shares are sold. "You lose the deduction" is wrong; "the deduction follows the new lot" is right. Anything about *which* lot (specific identification) is outside the sources and stays out.
3. **RULING — do not assert what an RSU vest is.** § 1.1091-1(a) covers an acquisition "by purchase or by an exchange upon which the entire amount of gain or loss was recognized by law". Neither the regulation nor § 1091 says whether shares delivered at vest count as either, and no source in this brief addresses it. The article states the rule's own words and says plainly that the vest question is not settled on the face of the rule — that honesty is a feature of the piece, not a hedge to be tidied away.
4. **RULING — the band reaches backwards, and that is the whole point.** Half the rule sits *before* the sale date: a purchase 20 days before a harvest can void it. Do not let the rewrite reduce the window to "wait 31 days". The correct statement is a band of 30 days either side, 61 days end to end, with the first clean re-buy falling on the 31st day after the sale — and that last figure is the desk's reading of the regulation's own "ending 30 days after", labelled as arithmetic, not a quote.
5. **RULING — no tax rate, and no dollar-of-tax figure.** No source in this brief states a marginal rate, a capital-gain rate or the net investment income tax, so the article carries no rate and never converts a loss into tax saved. The only authorised arithmetic is division of a loss by the $3,000 annual slice, stated as the desk's own sum with both numbers named (a $60,000 loss against $3,000 a year is twenty years of deduction, and that is the piece's punchline). No payback periods, no break-even rates, no "worth X in tax".
6. **RULING — "substantially identical" is a facts-and-circumstances test with one stated default.** C5 gives the default (one corporation's stock is ordinarily not identical to another's) and the two listed exceptions in the same paragraph are reorganisations and convertible bonds/preferred — neither is the reader's case. The article may say the test is not a definition the rule settles; it may not say the Internal Revenue Service has never ruled on index funds or on a sister exchange-traded fund, because no source here establishes that negative.
7. **Acronyms, expanded once at first use:** restricted stock units (RSUs); employee stock purchase plan (ESPP); Internal Revenue Service (IRS). No other all-caps token may appear — the regulation and the code are cited by section in prose, with the URLs in `## Sources`.
8. **Field note is provenance, not evidence.** The customer-truth file carries no anecdote for this vertical, so the piece opens on the rule's own arithmetic and the reader's own situation. Do not invent a client story, a fund name or a broker's margin rate.
