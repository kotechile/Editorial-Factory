## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Never write that Postman shared or exfiltrated API data.** The report says the opposite about its own scope; the remote MCP failed to authenticate. Say the client registered the connection and installed the plugins.
2. **Attribute the self-approval precisely**: the *source* attempts to approve its own hook; the trust record alone does not prove who created it.
3. **The vendor reply is a thread reply from an account listing no company.** Attribute it as a reply that speaks for Postman; do not print "Postman said" as an official statement, and do not say the vendor "admitted wrongdoing" — it conceded the opt-in should be clearer.
4. **Do not generalise the versions.** Two users reported 12.33.4 and 12.35.1; the piece says "on the two builds described", never "every install".
5. **Do not claim the hook executed in every host.** Whether a host runs an unrecorded hook is its own gate; the installer's self-approval attempt is the claim.
6. **The licensing line has no published enterprise rate** ("contact sales"). Quote the unit, not a price.
7. **Do not use the Free tier's credit count** — the page contradicts itself (row 17).
8. **Out-of-window figures are barred**: Temporal's 92.3% rebuild figure (2026-08-25) and Octopus's 57.9% / 62.2% / 27.5% (2026-10-01) may not appear.
9. **The 866 figure is Postman's own internal inventory**, not a market measure; label it as the vendor's own count.
10. **Expand API and MCP at first use** ("application programming interface (API)", "Model Context Protocol (MCP)"), and avoid other all-caps tokens.
