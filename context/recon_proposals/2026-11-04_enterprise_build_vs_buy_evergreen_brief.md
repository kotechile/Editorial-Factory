**Decision the reader is facing:** Whether the "production" support plan on a cloud bill is a fixed cost the team can budget or a variable one that grows with spend — and, at what monthly spend the support line is worth more than the engineer the team would have to hire instead to run the same stack itself.
**Durability:** Still true in twelve months because the mechanism is a billing architecture, not a price level: every hyperscaler sells support as a percentage ladder with a minimum (`9%` of monthly charges to $10K, then `7%`, `5%`, `3%`), while the self-serve clouds sell it as a flat monthly fee that does not move with the bill at all. **Thesis:** Support is sold two ways — as a share of the bill (AWS `9%`→`3%`, Google Cloud `10%`→`3%`, both with a minimum) or as a flat monthly fee that ignores the bill (DigitalOcean `$24`/`$99`/`$999`, Azure `$29`/`$100`/`$1,000`) — and the two buy different things: the percentage ladder buys a severity clock that gets more expensive as the company grows, reaching one full-time engineer's monthly wage at roughly `$190K` a month of cloud spend, while the flat plan buys a fixed queue position at any spend.
