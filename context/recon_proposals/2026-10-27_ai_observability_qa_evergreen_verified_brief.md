## FLAGGED (carry the caveat into the draft)
- **F1 — the 31.08% base.** The paper's abstract calls it "31.08% of the passed patches"; its Figure 1 caption calls it "31.08% of the resolved instances". Both refer to the same 251 hand-labelled patches (78 ÷ 251 = 31.08%). The draft must say *of the 251 patches that passed every test*, never "31% of all agent patches" and never apply it to a different benchmark.
- **F2 — the 76% is one model, not a field average.** The sentence names o3-mini explicitly; the ten-LLM comparison is the paper's wider study, not the 76% figure. Write "o3-mini identified the buggy file with up to 76% accuracy".
- **F3 — the 4% is an observation about the suites in one study,** not an industry rate. Never generalise it to "your suite kills 4% of faults"; the honest form is "a suite can reach 100% coverage and still kill 4% of injected faults".
- **F4 — Stryker's 80/60 is a tool's default, not a standard.** Label it as the default an off-the-shelf mutation runner ships, and do not present 60 as an industry-agreed floor.
- **F5 — both primary datasets are coding benchmarks.** The generalisation to a team's internal suite is an argument, not a measurement; the draft states it as the reason to check, never as a measured finding about the reader's own harness.
- **F6 — no hands-on claim.** This desk ran no mutation tool on any suite; nothing in the article may say "we ran", "we tested" or "our results".
