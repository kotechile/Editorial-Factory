**Gate rulings for the drafter** (each one is a way this piece can be wrong while every figure is right):

1. **"Entry" is the customs declaration, not the warehouse receipt.** Gloss it at first use — the filing that tells Customs what the goods are, what they are worth and what duty applies. A tower's "received" event is not an entry.
2. **"Landing" is the regulation's word, not "arrival".** The 15-day clock in 19 CFR 142.2 starts at landing from the vessel, aircraft or vehicle (or arrival at the destination port for in-bond freight), which is not the same moment as a carrier's arrival milestone or the container's availability date. Do not write "arrival" where the rule says landing.
3. **The 24-hour clock runs backwards from a foreign port.** It is measured from lading aboard the vessel abroad, so it has already expired by the time a container first appears in a domestic visibility feed. Nothing in the tower can "detect" a late filing; the exception is a missing acknowledgement, not a late event.
4. **"Deemed liquidated" is neither a refund nor a penalty.** It fixes the duty at the rate the importer asserted, by operation of law, when Customs does not act in a year. State it as a fixed number with a dispute window, never as money coming back.
5. **The one-year clock is extendable.** Say so, with the two ceilings (3 years of extensions, a four-year outer wall) in the same breath, or the article asserts a deadline that Customs can lawfully move.
6. **Carrier demurrage is a different rulebook.** 46 CFR Part 541's billing invoices (the vertical's 2026-10-06 piece) is a private carrier charge; general order is Customs custody. Never present one as the other.
7. **Do not print the wage as the reader's own hour.** $25.13 prices a cargo and freight agent's paid hour, which is how the manual re-check is costed. Frame it as a third party's cost, not as "your time is worth".
8. **The 10 data elements and the 180-day duplicate spend no gate row.** Both verify on their pages but the matcher would evidence them on a navigation digit or a duplicate clause, so they are carried in prose under their citations and their verbatims are recorded above.
