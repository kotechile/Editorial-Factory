**Emergent Collision Point:** The two biggest consumer ecosystems answered the same question — what happens at the door when your own network is gone — in opposite directions, six days apart, and neither release mentions the other's answer. Apple's route is the standard: Matter + Thread + Aliro, unlocked on the device, with a hub you buy and Wi-Fi you keep. Amazon's route is the network: no hub, no home Wi-Fi, an encrypted hop to a Bridge up to half a mile away, and the cloud on the far side. Both call the result local. Neither is lying — they are local to different things, and the word now hides the only decision a homeowner actually has to make.
**Hook:** Ring's first smart lock, announced 2026-10-07, keeps working when your Wi-Fi dies because it talks to a neighbour's Echo instead. Six days later Apple announced a lock that works when the internet dies because the bolt decides on the device itself. Both are sold as local. One of them has to be alive for you to get in.
**Tension:** The Matter badge has been treated as the interop floor since 2022, and Apple is now shipping first-party hardware that earns it while Amazon ships a door lock that routes around it entirely — not because Amazon cannot certify (its ecosystem has Matter devices) but because the network it already owns reaches 90% of the country at no charge and needs no hub on the customer's side. The cost lands on the buyer in two places a spec sheet never shows: the hub-and-Wi-Fi dependency on Apple's side, and the coverage-and-operator dependency on Amazon's. A third case in the same fortnight shows the floor is not even stable — Home Assistant removed its SolarEdge Local integration because the vendor switched its local web API off in firmware updates, and replaced it with Modbus over TCP. "Local" is a property of whoever can turn it off.
