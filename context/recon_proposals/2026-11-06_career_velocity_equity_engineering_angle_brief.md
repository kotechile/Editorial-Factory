**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Carta, "State of Startup Compensation: H1 2026", published 2026-10-28 (https://carta.com/data/startup-compensation-h1-2026). Since the start of 2024 the median salary benchmark for a new hire "one step below senior" is up **18.5%** while "median initial grant size has risen by **25.4%**"; at senior director the same measures are **+6.3%** salary and **+3.9%** equity; "For employees at the VP level and above … the median size of initial grants has shrunk over the past two and a half years." **Signal B (Anchor 2):** Microsoft's Voluntary Exit Offer, announced **2026-10-26** in a memo from chief people officer Amy Coleman (https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/26/introducing-a-voluntary-exit-offer), reported by GeekWire the same day as **about 60,000** people in participating teams in the U.S., Canada, the U.K. and India, "about a quarter of its workforce, which numbered **223,000** people at last count", open at "level 70 and below" (https://www.geekwire.com/2026/microsoft-makes-new-voluntary-exit-offer-to-more-than-a-quarter-of-its-workforce-including-some-execs). The company describes the package as its standard severance — "base pay based on tenure and level, continued stock vesting, healthcare coverage for a fixed period and career transition services" — with **no age or tenure requirement**. Prior-year reporting on the same severance formula puts the cash at "a minimum of 60 days of base pay … up to a maximum of **39 weeks** of base pay", with levels 64 and below at one week per six months of service and levels 65 to 67 at two weeks (https://www.businessinsider.com/microsoft-is-offering-buyouts-read-the-memo-2026-10).

**Emergent Collision Point:** Neither document says what the pair says. Microsoft has aimed a voluntary exit at the layer whose equity grants have been growing fastest, and it has priced that exit in base pay — the component that, for the same layer, grew slower than the grant. Carta's data shows where the market put its money over the last two and a half years: 25.4% more initial equity for the IC one step below senior, 3.9% at senior director, a shrinking grant at VP and above. Microsoft's offer email follows the older shape, a tenure formula capped at 39 weeks of base with an equity term the company has not published. So the largest line in the package of the person being offered the buyout is the one the formula cannot see, and the smaller line — base pay — is the one the offer quantifies. That inversion is the story: the pay market moved toward paper at exactly the level where the exit is priced in cash.

**Hook:** Microsoft is offering about 60,000 people — roughly a quarter of its 223,000 staff — a voluntary exit with a cash package capped at 39 weeks of base pay, in the same week Carta reported that the median initial grant for an IC one step below senior is up 25.4% since the start of 2024 while the senior director's grant is up 3.9% and the VP's has shrunk.

**Tension:** Who this empowers: the senior IC who reads the grant-growth data and negotiates at the level where the market is actually bidding — initial grant size, refresh cadence and level, rather than a title with a flat grant. Who this threatens: the manager who assumed seniority is where the equity is, and the recruiter who sells a director title on a grant that has not grown in two and a half years. The systemic friction: a company that flattens its org chart to buy AI infrastructure is simultaneously creating the IC premium it then has to pay for, and its exit instruments are still built for a pay mix in which salary was the majority of the package.

**Target reader:** equity_career_strategist (senior IC to VP-level tech professional)

**Single claim to defend:** The 2026 pay market has moved equity down the ladder — grants grew 25.4% for the IC one step below senior, 3.9% for senior directors, and shrank at VP and above — so a package is now mostly paper at exactly the level where exit offers are still priced in weeks of base pay, and the metric that decides both an offer and a buyout is grant size relative to base, which no offer letter states.
