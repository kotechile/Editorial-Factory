## Rulings for the drafter

1. **Battery service dollars are iFixit's November-2024 estimates of Apple's prices.** Write them as such ("iFixit's table of Apple's out-of-warranty prices, dated November 2024") — never as "Apple charges $99 today". The $89 / $99 cells agree with the live iPhone service ladder this desk read on 2026-10-30 ($89 on an iPhone 12, $99 on the 15), so the figures are usable with their date attached.
2. **The 500 → 1000 change is a change in the published rating.** No source says the cell got more durable. Write "the rating doubled", never "the battery lasts twice as long".
3. **"18-24 months" is iFixit's gloss**, attributed in the sentence that uses it. Apple's cycle count is the primary number; the months convert it.
4. **Apple's own service page cannot be a citation for a price** — it renders "Pricing failed to load" to a non-browser fetch. Cite it only for the warranty and AppleCare terms in rows 3 and 4.
5. **The EU row is a comparator.** It binds products placed on the EU market from 20 June 2025. Never imply a US-sold device meets, misses or must meet it.
6. **No handset price exists in any verified row.** Do not print a phone's sticker price; compare in per-cycle and per-month terms.
7. **Derived arithmetic to recompute, never copy:** 500 cycles at one full cycle a day = 1.37 years; 1000 = 2.74 years; 800 = 2.19 years. $46.99 ÷ 500 = $0.094 per rated cycle; $49.99 ÷ 1000 = $0.050; $89 ÷ 500 = $0.178; $99 ÷ 1000 = $0.099. $46.99 over the 18-24 month range = $1.96-$2.61 a month of extra life; $89 over the same range = $3.71-$4.94 a month. The two published ratings differ by 2x; the EU floor is 1.6x the older rating and 80% of the newer.
