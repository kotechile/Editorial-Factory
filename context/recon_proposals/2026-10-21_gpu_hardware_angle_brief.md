# Angle Brief: gpu_hardware — 2026-10-21
**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)
**Winner:** "Your Agent's 30th Call Costs More Joules" — the same KV history that makes an agent session fast is the thing that makes each later token cost more energy, and the only lever the production data actually measures is a clock on retention, not a bigger card.
**Scores:** E=9 A=9 S=9 → Composite=9.0

**Signal A (Anchor 1):** *The Token-Energy Law of LLM Decode: Active Bytes and Realized Time Account for Joules per Token* — Niu, Sencer, Zhang, Chen, submitted 2026-10-20 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28576). Board-level energy per output token is an affine function of `active bytes` and `realized decode time`; `active bytes` explicitly separates dense weights shared inside one decode iteration from the private KV history read and updated for each request. Fixed coefficients plus observed decode time reproduce held-out condition-mean energy within 3.73% across three models under BF16 on Hopper and Blackwell, including two-GPU tensor parallelism. The authors state the mechanism plainly: "longer attended histories increase private KV demand".

**Signal B (Anchor 2):** *Understanding KV Residency in Production-Scale Agentic LLM Workloads* — Arya, Bari, Parayil, Simmhan, St. Amant, Rühle, Rajmohan (Microsoft), submitted 2026-10-16 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22863). Four production M365 traces over more than 121 million model calls. In Deep Research the median context grows from about 10K to 68K tokens by the 30th call and about 80% of context tokens come from the prefix cache; one 99th-percentile session exceeds the 80 GB HBM of an H100 and holding all live sessions at peak needs an estimated 501.8 TB of HBM, while inference occupies only 5.6% of that retention time. A one-hour idle cap cuts the ceiling 3.6x and lifts recompute only from 4.07% to 4.53%; at a tenth of peak demand LRU eviction recomputes 63% of calls against 13.6% for multi-resolution retention (int4 demotion); reuse falls from 89% to 29% when the next call reaches a different deployment.

**Emergent Collision Point:** The two papers price the same object from opposite ends and neither states the other's conclusion. Signal B measures *how long* an agent's history must be held — and finds it is held roughly eighteen times longer than it is used (5.6% of retention time) — while Signal A measures *what that history costs every time a new token is produced*: private KV bytes are read and updated on each decode step, and the model's own structural note is that longer attended histories raise that byte demand per token. Put together, prefix caching does not remove the cost, it relocates it: the cache that spares the prefill recompute keeps the history resident so every subsequent decode step re-reads it. Neither paper says this; A has no retention policy and B has no energy term. The consequence is that a per-token price list is the wrong unit for an agent, because a token's energy bill is a function of how old the session that produced it is.

**Hook:** One Deep Research session in Microsoft's production traces outgrows the 80 GB of an H100 by itself, and by the 30th call the median session is carrying about 68K tokens of history. Four days later, a separate team published an energy law that says the joules behind each new token are set by exactly that number — the bytes of private history the request carries.

**Tension:** Every cost model in this market is built on two constants: a price per token and a price per card-hour. Both are averages. The production data says the thing an operator buys grows inside the request — median context nearly seven-fold from first call to 30th — and the energy measurement says the growth is charged per token, at every step, not once. So the buyers holding the largest agent fleets are the ones whose unit economics drift worst, and the drift is invisible in every invoice they receive. It also inverts the usual reflex: batching more requests per GPU amortises weights (Signal A's shared term) but does nothing to the private KV term, so the lever that fixes a chat service does not fix an agent service.

**Target reader:** `infra_engineer` (deep technical, benchmark-literate; wants real numbers, caveats, reproduction details).

**Single claim to defend:** For agentic serving, energy per output token is set by the bytes of private KV history a request carries, so a session's cost per token rises with its own age — and the only measured levers are a time cap on retention and a cheaper representation of the retained state, not a larger card or a bigger batch.
