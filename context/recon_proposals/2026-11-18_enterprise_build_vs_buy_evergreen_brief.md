## How the figures convert (desk arithmetic, to be recomputed in the final)

- Same workload, two clouds: 10,000 secrets × 0.40 = **4,000 USD a month** of secrets on AWS, plus 12M calls at 0.05/10,000 = **60 USD**, for the vendor's own **4,060.00 USD** total. On Google, 10,000 active versions less the 6 free = 9,994 × 0.06 = **599.64 USD**, plus 12M access operations at 0.03/10,000 = **36 USD**, for **635.64 USD** — a **6.4× spread** on an identical inventory.
- The crossover is a practice, not a vendor: 0.40 ÷ 0.06 = **6.67**, so the seventh live version of a secret is the one that makes Google dearer than AWS for that secret. Rotation is free on the per-secret meter and is a multiplier on the per-version meter; a destroyed version is the free action.
- Annualised: 4,060 × 12 = **48,720 USD** a year = **745 engineer-hours** at 65.38; 635.64 × 12 = **7,627.68 USD** = **117 engineer-hours**; the gap between the two buy options is **41,092 USD a year, about 629 engineer-hours** — larger than the operations work of running the thing, which is why the meter is the decision and the vendor name is not.
- The bill being avoided is small: the vendor's own 15-secret example is **6.02 USD a month**, **72.24 USD a year**, or **1.1 engineer-hours** — while the build side's floor is five nodes, three of them quorate.
- Never present 4,060 as the price of "a secrets manager"; it is the price of 10,000 secrets fetched 40 times a day each, on the meter that charges for the inventory.

## Prose claims read by eye on the same pages (no row; each rides under the citation above)

- **AWS:** secrets are prorated by the hour, which is why the vendor's ephemeral example prices 5M one-hour secrets at 0.00056 USD per secret-hour; a secret that lives for an hour costs a fraction of a secret-month. API calls are billed separately at 0.05 per 10,000 in every plan; there is no free tier for secrets themselves (the "free" offer is a 30-day trial, not a permanent allowance).
- **Google:** the free tier is 6 active secret versions and 10,000 access operations per month per account; management operations are free; destroyed versions are free; rotation notifications beyond 3 cost 0.05 each. Versions are what the meter counts, so an old version left live for rollback is a line item and deleting it is the fix.
- **Vault:** integrated storage uses Raft and quorum is ceil((N+1)/2) of the peer set; with an even number of members the cluster is no better off than one fewer, so a five-member set tolerates two failures and a three-member set tolerates one. Deleting the leader does not lose the write if quorum holds — which is exactly the operational property a buyer pays the cloud for.
