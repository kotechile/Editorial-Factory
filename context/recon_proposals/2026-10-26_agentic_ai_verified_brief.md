## Gate rulings for the drafter

- **Both legs are primary.** Anchor 1 is an empirical study with a validated dataset; Anchor 2 is the vendor's own general-availability announcement plus its public repository. Use Microsoft's and NVIDIA's own words for what the boundary enforces; use the study's own numbers for the gap. Do not let either leg's claim migrate to the other: the study measures open-source code, not MXC containers, and MXC publishes no outcome measurement.
- **The 73% is "of the decision-relevant outputs".** Write it as "73% of the decision-relevant outputs never reach a person". Never "73% of all outputs".
- **"Unmet" is the study's word**, for 63% of expected constraints. Never write "63% of checks are missing" — many exist and are inadequate.
- **The 5.3% is the whole-contract figure**, not a success rate: "only 5.3% of call sites satisfy their whole contract".
- **The Gemini CLI case is the paper's own reading of its data.** Attribute it to the study. It is a good example; do not generalise it to "every vendor's guardrail allows the call".
- **Intervals and derivations:** the 17.73 s to 1.50 ms contrast is the paper's own paired latency figure; do not compute a ratio and present it as a measured speedup of a specific product.
- **Do not claim MXC or OpenShell fails.** The claim is that containment is a different boundary from the per-call contract, and that the window contains no measurement of containment's effect on this gap. Write "no published measurement" if needed, never "containment does not work".
- **No hands-on claim.** The desk ran no benchmarks, built no sandbox and read no source code; it read a paper, a launch post and a repository.
- **Acronyms to expand at first use:** large language model (LLM), Microsoft Execution Containers (MXC), application programming interface (API), operating system (if used), and the paper's own term "call site" needs one plain gloss ("one place in a program where it calls a model and acts on the reply").
