**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Anthropic's own research post, "The components of agent memory", published 2026-11-06 (https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory), reporting per-model memory runs to about step 10,000 of Pokémon Red: Claude Sonnet 3.7 wrote 413 memory files across 225 folders and earned one badge, while Claude Fable 5 wrote nine files in two folders plus a global.md and earned four badges; on the CL-Bench 1.0 database task a fixed retention rule ("record only failures") lost much of the boost memory gave Opus 5 and Opus 4.7; Sonnet 5 read its memory before answering 9 of 10 times while Sonnet 4.6 did so less than half the time.
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** "Agent Memory as Rate-Limited Filtering", arXiv:2611.02361, submitted 2026-11-02 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02361), measuring when explicit state beats reading the log on an executable access-control benchmark: open 7–32B models fall from 72–86% after 8 or 16 events to 48–55% after 256, reasoning-trained 32B models reading state reach 96% and 98%, and a 120B reasoning reader trails the state by 4 points at 256 events, 7 at 512 and about 10 at 1,024 — an operational horizon of about 1,024 events, versus a lead of +0.12 at about one twentieth of the tokens.
**Emergent Collision Point:** The benchmark measures *when* a store starts paying and finds the break-even moves with the reader; the lab's own logs measure *what* a store should hold and find the answer is salience judgment, not capacity — with a fixed rule subtracting the gain. Neither document states what follows for an architect: the memory tier is a contract with one model version. Upgrade the reader and the same folder becomes overhead — the nine files that carried a frontier model four badges are the 413 files that buried a weaker one, and the log-reading shortcut the stronger model can still afford is the accuracy cliff the cheaper model fell off at 256 events. Sizing the store against tokens, or against a policy written for the last model, prices the wrong axis.
**Hook:** Anthropic's own run logs have one Claude writing 413 memory files to win a single badge and another writing nine to win four, while a benchmark submitted four days earlier puts the point where explicit state beats reading the log at about 1,024 events for a 120B reader — and roughly a quarter as far for a small one. The store did not change. The reader did.
**Tension:** Memory has become a product category — four incompatible formats on one developer's machine, a standards push modelled on the 2019 observability merger, vendors selling stores by capacity — and the two measurements say the variable those products compete on is not the one that sets the return. The architect who buys a bigger store to fix a weak reader pays twice: once for the tokens on every turn and once for the accuracy the reader loses in the log. The architect who keeps a fixed write rule after a model upgrade keeps a policy that has started subtracting value. Who this threatens: anyone who has standardised on a memory format and called the model swap a configuration change. Who it empowers: the team that measures its own horizon before it buys a tier.
**Target reader:** ai_architect
**Single claim to defend:** A memory store is priced per model version — the log length past which explicit state wins is a measured property of the reader (about 1,024 events at 120B, far shorter at 32B), and the write policy that produced a small, useful store on a frontier model is neither portable upward nor downward; size and re-curate the store when the reader changes, or budget for a store that buys a horizon your reader never reaches.
