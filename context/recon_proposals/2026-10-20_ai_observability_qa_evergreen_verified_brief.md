## Notes for the drafter

- **Two different failure classes, do not merge them.** Claims 12–14 measure the *harness* changing its
  own answer between executions (semantic query engines re-run through their own benchmarks). Claims
  1–11 measure the *serving stack* changing the answer for an identical request. They support one
  sentence — a score is a property of the run — and nothing stronger: 2610.13823 is a
  database/semantic-query study, not an LLM eval-suite study, so do not present it as one.
- **Do not convert the 992/8 split into a reliability figure.** It is one prompt, one model, one
  serving build, on 2026-09-10. "992 of 1000" is not "99.2% reliable" and not a general pass rate.
- **No re-derived arithmetic.** Do not compute a new noise band, a per-case cost, or an
  accuracy-per-dollar figure from these numbers. The only arithmetic the sources state themselves is
  the 0.95^4 = 81.4% compounding in claim 3.
- **The fix has two prices, both stated.** Batch-invariant serving costs throughput (about 20% versus
  cuBLAS; 26 s → 42–55 s on the 1000-sequence test) and FP32 greedy costs speed and memory in a way
  the paper does not quantify — say "not quantified here" rather than inventing a number.
- **Version discipline.** The vLLM behaviour is documented for the V1 engine as of 2026-10-20; do not
  claim it for older builds or for closed API vendors, whose sampling is not documented at this level.
- **Accessibility:** expand every acronym — BF16 → "bfloat16 (a 16-bit number format)", FP32 → "32-bit
  floating point", cuBLAS → "the standard matrix-multiplication library NVIDIA ships", GPU → "graphics
  processor". Keep the model name Qwen3-235B out if the checker flags it; "a 235-billion-parameter
  open model" carries the point.
