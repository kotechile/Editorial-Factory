**Authorised arithmetic (derived figures the article may state, each computed from the rows below; nothing else is authorised):**

- One maintenance and repair worker costs **1.33 pickers** — $23.84 ÷ $17.95 = 1.328. One industrial machinery mechanic costs **1.73 pickers** — $31.02 ÷ $17.95 = 1.728.
- On an annual basis the general maintenance role is **$12,260 a year** more than the picker ($49,590 − $37,330), which is **683 picker-hours** at $17.95; the industrial mechanic's premium is **$27,190 a year**, or **1,515 picker-hours**.
- The break-even a station must clear: with **N** stations per technician, the pickers a station removes must exceed **1 + 1/N**. At one station per technician that is **2.33** pickers; at four, **1.25**; at eight, **1.13**. One operator is staffed per station at the picker's own median, and the technician is staffed once per fleet, which is why the ratio is gentle at scale and brutal at the pilot.
- At a 50-hour week both roles cost **1.1 times** their base rate (55 ÷ 50), because both are hourly and both collect the premium — so premium hours scale the money in the case and leave the **1.33** ratio untouched. Do not present the technician premium as something a peak-heavy schedule rescues.
- Do not compute a per-pick cost figure, a payback month or a capex number: this brief carries no machine price and no station throughput, and the 2026-10-21 and 2026-10-28 briefs own those denominators.
