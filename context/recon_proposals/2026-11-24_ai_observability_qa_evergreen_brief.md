**Decision the reader is facing:** Whether a benchmark score or an in-house eval pass rate may gate a release when the eval set itself may sit in the model's training data — and what the release gate has to print instead of the contaminated number: a re-run on a fresh mirror or held-out slice the model provably has not seen, not the word of a contamination detector. The call bites the first time a green score is used to ship a model or a prompt change this quarter.
**Durability:** Train/test leakage is structural, not a model-generation artifact: as long as eval suites are public and pre-training corpora are scraped wholesale, benchmark text lands in training data, and the fix — measure on data you can prove is new — does not expire. **Thesis:** A green score on a benchmark the model could have memorized is not a gate: a lexical scan found 13.8% of MMLU test questions contaminated (18.1% in STEM, up to 66.7% in Philosophy), the same six-model study lost an average of 7.0 percentage points when question wording was paraphrased into indirect reference, a freshly built mirror of GSM8K showed overfitting of up to 8% in the peer-reviewed version (13% in the first-release abstract), 21 of 25 models scored the original GSM8K questions more than a standard deviation above their own paraphrased distribution, and the tools that claim to certify integrity solved only 201 of 335 detection challenges (60%) — so the release gate runs on a fresh mirror or held-out slice, uses paraphrase as the cheap diagnostic, and never treats a membership-inference detector as proof.
