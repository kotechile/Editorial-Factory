## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **The Italian measure is a bill, not a law.** Every sentence about it carries "would", "draft", "proposed", or names it as the bill presented on 30 October. Italy's parliament can amend it, and the article says so in one clause.
2. **26% is the technical report's own word, not the article's arithmetic.** The rate is quoted, and the 48.08% base is quoted; the effective 12.5% on government bonds may be written only as desk arithmetic (0.26 × 0.4808 = 0.125), labelled as such, or left out.
3. **1,331 and 1,592 are counts, not published totals.** Say "the notice lists 1,331 names" or "a count of the notice's rows". Never "the IRS reported 1,331" — the IRS reports no total.
4. **The nine-month comparison is desk arithmetic** on four published notices: 4,573 against 3,934, up 639, or 16.2%. Label it as a sum of the four notices, and do not call it a rate of renunciation per capita (no denominator exists here).
5. **The 16.4% quarterly fall is arithmetic too**: 261 fewer than 1,592. Both directions belong in the piece; a piece that reports only the fall (or only the nine-month rise) is selective.
6. **The $910,000 and $211,000 are 2026 figures.** The 2027 amounts were not published when this ran (only the HSA-only Rev. Proc. 2026-24 is out), so the text dates them to 2026 and does not project.
7. **The $2 million net-worth test is a flat statutory number.** The IRS page lists the income test as inflation-adjusted and gives no such adjustment for net worth; the drafter may say "not indexed" only while pointing at the statute's flat figure, never as a claim about future policy.
8. **The 73.1% is a share of *financial* wealth, not of all wealth.** The technical report measures it "al netto dei depositi bancari". Do not write "73.1% of Italian wealth".
9. **The €6.74 million is the expected *annual* yield including the deferred part**, and the table's later column is net of refunds (3.37 from 2032). Quote it as an annual estimate, and quote the refund assumption beside it.
10. **No claim about double taxation.** The bill does not say how the Italian deemed sale interacts with a treaty partner's tax on the real one, and no source establishes the answer. The piece may report that the question is open; it may not resolve it.
11. **France is IMI's reading.** Attribute the art. 167 bis comparison to IMI rather than stating French law as verified.
12. **No "the wave is over" and no "record exodus" framing.** The series is volatile (954, 1,285, 1,057, 1,592, 1,462, 1,780, 1,331) and the notices are published on a lag. The only defensible claims are the two directions of movement and the fact that the list exists.
