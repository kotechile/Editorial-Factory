**Gate rulings for the drafter (binding):**
1. The 6% and 13% are reductions *relative to what residential rates would otherwise be* in summer-peaking states. They are never a promised cut to a reader's bill, and they are never a household's payback.
2. $86 and $200 are per customer per year, averaged across summer-peaking states. Never attach them to a specific utility, state or house.
3. Never add, subtract or divide these figures with the J.D. Power bill values. The $188 bill and the 13% rate effect are different quantities on different bases, and neither source computes a household's net position.
4. Say plainly that the RMI numbers are model output, not measured outcomes, and that RMI itself flags them as directional.
5. The 109 million / 86 million / 23 million counts describe homes inside today's grid; they are not a limit on future deployment, and the report says so.
6. Do not recompute the "three out of four" ratio from 109 million; print the report's own proportion or omit it.
7. The J.D. Power figures are averages from its own customer studies, not a census; the water bill is water only.
8. No hands-on claim of any kind is available in this story — no testing, no installation experience, no measured household bills.
