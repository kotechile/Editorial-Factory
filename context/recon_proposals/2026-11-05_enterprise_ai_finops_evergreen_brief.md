## Gate rulings and traps
- **The units are not comparable as printed, and the article must say so first.** $0.99 (Intercom outcome), $1.50/$2.00 (Zendesk resolution), $2.00 (Salesforce conversation) and $0.20 (Salesforce credit-metered request) are four quantities; the comparison is only legal once each unit's *contents* are on the table, which is what the three-event audit does.
- **A cell leading on `2` collides with Salesforce navigation.** `$ 2 USD/Per conversation` evidences on a marketing menu (“B2B Marketing Platform”); the conversation price therefore rides in prose under row 4, and row 5 leads on the printed worked example instead (`24,000 Flex Credits` → the `US$120` line).
- **Intercom's `50 outcomes` cannot hold a row** — the token matches “50 free Lite seats” in the same page; the minimum commitment rides in prose.
- **Human baseline is arithmetic from one cited wage, not a benchmark.** $21.53 an hour ÷ 60 = $0.3588 a minute, so a six-minute contact costs $2.15 in wages alone ($1.44 at four minutes, $3.59 at ten). The article prints wage cost, never a loaded cost, and says the reader's own handle time and overheads decide the comparison.
- **No load-bearing figure for AI resolution rates.** Zendesk's page advertises “up to 80%+” and Intercom's carries a customer reporting a 50% resolution rate; both are unverified marketing, so neither becomes a row — the arithmetic uses the reader's own resolution share, stated as an input.
- **Arithmetic to recompute before the push:** $500 ÷ 100,000 = $0.005 a credit; 40 credits = $0.20; $0.99 ÷ $2.15 = 46%; $2.00 ÷ $2.15 = 93%. Every derived percentage in the final is recomputed from these two endpoints, never copied from the draft.
