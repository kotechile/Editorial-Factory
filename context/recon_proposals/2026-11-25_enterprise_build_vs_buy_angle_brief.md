**Signal B (Anchor 2):** The renewal clauses of 14 SaaS vendors, read line by line and checked 2026-11-13 — https://licenseware.io/saas-renewal-terms-notice-periods-price-increases — all 14 auto-renew by default; Notion demands 60 days' written notice just to reduce seats; leaving early costs Adobe 50% of the remaining obligation, Google the full commitment, Atlassian the rest of the term at once; Microsoft allows seat cuts only within 7 days; most renewals reset to then-current list price and promotional discounts expire; price-change notice runs 0–30 days (Microsoft 365, Atlassian, Adobe: not stated).
**Emergent Collision Point:** Both documents price the same thing — the exit — and neither says so. The renewal clause prices the *chosen* exit (narrow windows, written notice, percentage-of-remaining penalties, list-price resets), while the incident proves what the *unchosen* exit feels like: a vendor rotating credentials detonates the customer's integrations instantly and silently. Signal B shows the contract narrows the door; Signal A shows the vendor can close it anyway from the other side, and that what your SaaS really holds is a live map of your credentials against every other system you run. The renewal decision is therefore a credential-escrow decision, and no one is invoiced for the escrow.
**Hook:** On 2 November at 19:00 CET, a SaaS docs vendor invalidated every one of its customers' API tokens at once; four days of reading of 14 vendors' renewal clauses shows none of them promise more than 30 days' notice — and several promise none — before doing the same thing at a price of their choosing.
**Tension:** Whoever holds the tokens sets the timetable: the vendor's rotation schedule (attack response) and the renewal clause (60-day written notice to even cut seats) both outrun the customer's. This empowers buyers who inventory the credential map *before* the renewal, and threatens the enterprise ops posture that treats SaaS credentials as plumbing. The AI-agent vector of the attack raises the stakes on both sides.
**Target reader:** eng_leader (persona per `run_pipeline start`; enterprise engineering leader deciding renewals and self-host floor)
**Single claim to defend:** The real price of a SaaS subscription is set by two documents that never meet on one page — the renewal clause (the exit you may not take cheaply: 60-day written notice, 50%-of-remaining penalties, list-price resets) and the credential inventory (the exit the vendor can take for you: every token invalidated at once) — and a buyer who has not priced both is negotiating over the smaller number.
