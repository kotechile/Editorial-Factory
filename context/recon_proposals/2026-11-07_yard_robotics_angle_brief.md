# Angle Brief: yard_robotics — 2026-11-07
**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)
**Winner:** The duty is arriving after the price war was already lost — so next season's mower price will follow the factory, not the brand
**Scores:** E=8.5 A=8.5 S=8.5 → Composite=8.5
**Signal A (Anchor 1):** The European Commission's anti-dumping disclosure in case AD745, published 2026-10-21 and quantified by the German trade title Motorist on 2026-10-29, sets proposed definitive duties of 53.3% on Positec/Kress machines, 45.6% on Mammotion, 47% on other cooperating exporters and 53.3% on all other Chinese-origin robot mowers, with no retroactive collection and a statutory decision deadline of 2027-01-19 (https://www.motorist-online.de/antidumping-zoelle-so-gut-wie-sicher-kress-reagiert-bereits-29102026), confirmed as a received disclosure with that deadline by Husqvarna's own release of 2026-10-21 (https://www.husqvarnagroup.com/en/press/husqvarna-group-notes-eu-commissions-disclosure-robotic-mower-anti-dumping-investigation).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Reuters, 2026-10-21 — CEO Glen Instone says Husqvarna would likely have to lower the roughly €1,000 ($1,122) starting price of its robotic mowers, rules out fighting machines under €500, and confirms some robot mower production has already moved from Newton Aycliffe in Britain to Czechia, affecting around 50 roles, with Asian production under consideration; the company lost more European share in units than in value (https://www.reuters.com/world/china/husqvarna-looks-east-chinese-rivals-chip-away-robot-lawnmower-lead-2026-10-21); the same day's Q3 report carries the quarter behind that decision — operating income SEK -334m, organic sales -5%, and robot mowing "a difficult quarter" with "elevated channel partner inventories in Europe" (https://www.husqvarnagroup.com/en/press/interim-report-january-september-2026-2490517).
**Emergent Collision Point:** The company that filed the anti-dumping complaint is the same one telling investors it must cut its starting price and has already shifted the factory. A 45.6% to 53.3% duty does not restore a price the incumbent has conceded; it restores a margin on machines whose origin has been changed to escape it — Kress has moved most of its portfolio out of China and expects most models to sit outside the duty, while the two models it still builds there get a price adjustment from 1 January 2027. Neither source says the thing that follows: in the 2027 season the number on the sticker tracks the customs origin of the individual unit, so two machines from one brand, one showroom and one spec sheet can carry price lists tens of percent apart, and nothing on the carton says which one you are looking at.
**Hook:** A 53.3% duty lands on Chinese-built mowers while the market leader cuts its own starting price below €1,000 — and one brand is already holding 2026 prices only until 31 December for the models it still builds in China.
**Tension:** The buyer's leverage is a calendar, not a negotiation: order a China-built unit before the year closes and the 2026 price holds, wait for the spring and the assessment is in the sticker. The brands that moved production are protected from the duty and inherit the pricing room it creates; the ones that did not are forced to pass it on, and the customer cannot tell which is which from the model name.
