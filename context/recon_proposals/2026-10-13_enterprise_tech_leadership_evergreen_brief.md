## Angle and the rules the article will carry (all figures from the rows above)
The article is a procurement instrument, not a productivity sermon: it gives the reader a trial to run before signing and the three numbers that decide the seat count.
- **Measure with a trial of your own, randomized per task, on issues ≤ 2 hours from your own repositories.** The METR design (16 developers, 246 issues, randomize each issue to AI-allowed or AI-disallowed, outcomes read from the repository rather than from the developer) is the floor to copy; any team member who forecasts their own speedup cannot be the measurement, because forecast (24% faster) and after-the-fact estimate (20% faster) both survived a measured 19% slowdown.
- **Expect the gain to live on code the team does not already know.** The published positive result (55.8% faster) is a one-hour greenfield task; the published negative result (19% slower) is maintainers on repos they had years of context in, with the follow-up still unable to separate a real speedup from selection (intervals of -38% to +9%). Buy the seats for onboarding, migrations and unfamiliar services; re-test the claims on your own flagship repository.
- **Budget the maintenance line as a separate cost.** The vendor dataset shows cloned-line share rising (8.3% to 12.3%) while the refactoring share of changed lines falls (25% to under 10%) — read as a rework liability the article tells the reader to price, with the vendor-claim caveat attached in the body, never as an established causal finding.
- **Do not buy the agentic tier on the assistant's business case.** The best available operational benchmark puts autonomous-agent pull requests at fewer than 5% of the top-decile volume and a 79% merge rate against 92% for human-only work.
- **Treat distributed trust as a review-cost input, not a training problem.** 46% of surveyed developers actively distrust the accuracy of the tooling and the most experienced are the most sceptical (20% highly distrust), so the model is a merge-gate question, not a rollout-nudge question.
Compliance with AGENTS.md rule 9 and `skills/evergreen_topics.md` §3: no novelty score, no 30-day window, no synthesis seed; the artefact is a decision framework whose figures are dated and whose value is not tied to an in-window event (the one in-window item, the Stack Overflow 2026 survey, is deliberately not used as a row). If the piece starts drifting into "AI changed software development" commentary, it has failed the archetype and should be pulled.
