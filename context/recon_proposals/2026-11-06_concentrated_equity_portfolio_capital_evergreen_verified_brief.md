## Rulings written for the drafter
1. **The "12 months" folklore is wrong, and the article must say so with the mechanism.** The one-year period after transfer only binds when the grant predates the purchase by more than a year. With a standard six-month offering and a share-capped plan the binding date is 18 months after purchase; where the plan does not fix a share count, the regulation makes the exercise date the grant date and the binding date is 24 months. Never write "hold 12 months and you're qualified".
2. **Two different "discount" quantities are at stake and must not be merged.** The disqualifying path books ordinary income on the spread between value at *exercise* and the price paid; the qualifying path caps ordinary income at the spread between value at *grant* and the price paid. A lookback plan with a rising stock therefore has a bigger ordinary-income slice to move than the 15 percent headline suggests — that is the whole reason qualifying can be worth anything.
3. **The 15 percent is a return, not a tax rate.** Say "17.6 percent on the money you paid" only with the division shown; do not call it a 15 percent return.
4. **Do not claim the qualifying path always wins.** On a loss, Publication 525 says the qualifying sale produces a capital loss and no ordinary income, while the disqualifying path still books the exercise-date spread — so in a drawdown the clock the folklore tells you to avoid is the one that protects you. State both directions.
5. **The rate spread is bracket-dependent and the article must carry the rule, not one number.** The 13.2 points is the top-bracket case (37 percent against 23.8). At a 24 percent marginal rate the spread is 0.2 points, i.e. the hold is worth nothing on rate. State the subtraction each time.
6. **$25,000 is measured at grant, not at purchase**, and it is a cap on rights accruing in a calendar year — it is not "you can buy $25,000 of stock", which is what most copy says.
7. **Every derived figure in the copy must be recomputed from its endpoints** (17.65 percent, $3,750, 18 and 24 months, 13.2 points, and any dollar saving built on them) after the frontier pass, not copied from the draft.
8. **Cite the paper trail as a document, not as advice**: Form 3922 is the corporation's statement carrying the grant-date value, due January 31 — it is what makes the qualifying computation checkable, and it is the only place a reader sees the grant-date price if the plan's grant date is the offering's first day.
