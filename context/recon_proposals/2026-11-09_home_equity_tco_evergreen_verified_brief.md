**Drafter rulings (carry these, they are load-bearing):**
- The 78 percent date is *schedule-only*. The statute's words are "irrespective of the outstanding
  balance" — do not soften that to "roughly" or "usually", and do not claim it moves with extra principal.
- The 80 percent date is schedule-or-actual-payments, and it is not self-executing: it needs a written
  request plus good payment history, being current, evidence the value has not fallen below the original,
  and no second lien. Never write "it cancels automatically at 80 percent" — that is the 78 percent date.
- Falling prices cut both ways: the request route can be blocked by the no-decline test, while the
  automatic date never looks at value at all. State that asymmetry; do not present appreciation as a lever.
- The midpoint backstop applies "if the mortgagor is current"; it is not a promise for a delinquent loan.
- The high-risk exception exists and must be named once in prose; never tell a reader their loan cannot
  carry it without checking.
- The price range ($30 to $70 per $100,000) is a secondary compilation attributing the range to Freddie
  Mac, dated December 23, 2025. Label it as a range, not a quote, and never convert it into a promise
  about a specific lender's premium.
- No advice framing and no tax content. The piece reads published rules and tells the reader which
  document to pull and which number to compare.
