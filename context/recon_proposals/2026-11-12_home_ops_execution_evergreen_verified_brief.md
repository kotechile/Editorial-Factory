## Rulings the drafter must honour

1. **The ceiling, not "everything".** "Full measure of the bond" in § 7071.6(b) means $25,000 in California. The article must state the cap and must never promise recovery of the loss.
2. **Bands are bands.** Arizona's "$5,000" is the floor of a $5,000–$15,000 band (§ 32-1152(B)(5)), and the specialty floor is $1,000 of a $1,000–$7,500 band. Write "may be as little as", never "is".
3. **Two clocks, two different triggers.** Washington's two years run from substantial completion or abandonment of the work; Oregon's 14 months run from a licence or bond event. Neither runs from the homeowner noticing the defect — say that explicitly, because it is the piece's most useful sentence.
4. **The bond does not refill** (ORS 701.068(3)) and the state's reaction when it is exhausted is a licence suspension plus a bond of up to five times the ordinary amount (ORS 701.068(5)–(6)). That is the evidence for the thesis: the instrument exists to make licensing enforceable.
5. **Derived figures must be labelled as arithmetic.** $50,000 (twice $25,000), $125,000 (five times $25,000) and the 5 percent bond-to-insurance ratio are the desk's arithmetic on cited figures, never quoted as a source's own sentence.
6. **Insurance is not the bond and the bond is not insurance.** ORS 701.081 puts them side by side; the article may compare their sizes and may not claim either covers the other's risk. No claim that the general liability certificate covers defective work, and no claim that the bond covers water damage.
7. **No price, no premium, no hands-on.** The arithmetic is the bond ceiling against the reader's own contract number.
8. **The field cases stay anecdote.** One homeowner's case, labelled as the desk's, and no inference about policy language.
