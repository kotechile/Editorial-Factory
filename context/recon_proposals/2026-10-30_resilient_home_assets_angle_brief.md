**Signal A (Anchor 1):** PG&E's own shutoff notice of 2026-10-14 put approximately 88,000 customers across 29 counties in the scope of a wildfire-safety shutoff forecast to run from the evening of Wednesday 2026-10-14 through Saturday afternoon 2026-10-17 — including about 5,500 Medical Baseline customers and six Tribal governments — after the event's scope was revised the same morning by adding about 25,000 customers and removing 7,300, with more than 40 Community Resource Centers opened across 23 counties; the utility's own update trail for the preceding 2026-10-09/12 event records 10,410 customers de-energized on 2026-10-11 inside a 12,200-customer, 17-county scope and warns that "customers who have been restored could experience another outage" (https://investor.pgecorp.com/news-events/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/With-Change-Statewide-Fire-Weather-Forecast-Planned-PGE-Public-Safety-Power-Shutoff-Event-Now-Includes-88000-Customers-in-Portions-of-Twenty-Nine-Counties/default.aspx and https://www.pge.com/en/newsroom/currents/safety/potential-public-safety-power-shutoff-could-impact-small-portion.html).

**Signal B (Anchor 2):** The U.S. Energy Information Administration's Electric Power Monthly, released 2026-10-23 with August 2026 data, puts California's average residential price at 29.75 cents per kilowatthour against 30.92 cents in August 2025, and the U.S. average at 18.16 cents against 17.57 — making California the third-highest residential rate in the country behind Hawaii (47.18) and Maine (32.11), and 64 percent above the national average (https://www.eia.gov/electricity/monthly/epm_table_grapher.php?t=epmt_5_6_a).

**Emergent Collision Point:** California's own fleet accounting, published by the California Energy Commission on 2026-08-07, shows where the state spent its reliability money: of 21,112 megawatts of battery storage, nearly 16,000 megawatts come from 310 utility-scale systems inside the state and another 2,000 from utility-scale plants in Nevada and Arizona, while about 3,000 megawatts are spread across more than 300,000 smaller systems — which the release says "can provide backup power during outages". Neither the utility's notice nor the federal price table states the consequence: a battery behind a substation cannot energize a de-energized distribution line, so about 85 percent of the fleet that carries most of the capacity does nothing for the household whose circuit the utility cuts — and the 14 percent that does is the part that lost its federal credit on 2025-12-31, exports under a tariff the state regulator itself describes as "usually lower than the retail rate", and now sits behind a fixed monthly charge solar cannot offset.

**Hook:** On the morning of 14 October, PG&E added 25,000 homes to a wildfire shutoff list and took 7,300 off it. By that afternoon 88,000 customers across 29 counties were in scope for a four-day outage, in a state whose residential power already costs 29.75 cents a kilowatt-hour — the third-highest rate in the country.

**Tension:** The same wildfire risk drives both halves of the bill: the utility cuts the circuit to avoid igniting a fire, and the state pays for grid-scale batteries, transmission and fire-hardening through the rate that is already 64 percent above the national average. The household is therefore paying the highest-in-a-generation price for a grid that still cannot keep its lights on, while the only asset that would — a battery on its own wall — is priced at full retail with no federal credit and a tariff that pays roughly a tenth of retail for whatever it exports.

**Target reader:** pro_homeowner

**Single claim to defend:** The resilience gap a California homeowner faces in a shutoff is no longer a technology problem or a safety problem — the state has 21,112 megawatts of batteries and grid-scale failures fell more than 97 percent — it is a siting and subsidy problem: about 85 percent of the fleet sits on the grid's side of the meter, where it earns capacity payments and cannot reach a de-energized house, and the 14 percent on the household's side lost its 30 percent federal credit on 2025-12-31, so the only asset that covers the outage is now the one the owner pays full price for.
