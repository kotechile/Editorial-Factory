**Thesis:** A home battery is repaid by the peak-to-off-peak spread on the household's own retail rate, and that spread is a contracting and ratemaking artifact rather than a property of the hardware — in one utility's territory on one effective date it runs from zero (25.6¢ at every hour on the TOU-ELEC plan for a community-choice customer who buys delivery only) to 67.1¢ (EV-TOU-5 for a bundled customer) — so the honest threshold is arithmetic: a 5 kWh battery cycled once a day needs about 6¢ of spread per kWh to recover each $1,000 of installed cost inside ten years, and a quote that assumes more than the reader's own schedule publishes is a backup-power purchase wearing a payback model.
