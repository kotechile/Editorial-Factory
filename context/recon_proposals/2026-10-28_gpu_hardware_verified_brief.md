## Gate rulings for the drafter (binding)

1. **480 GB belongs to partner cards, not to Intel's card.** Intel's reference design is 160 GB (twenty 8 GB LPDDR5X devices). Every mention of 480 GB must say whose configuration it is. Do not write "Intel's 480GB card".
2. **The card is not for sale.** It is sampling; there is no price and no announced general-availability date, and NAND Research's judgement is that volume availability appears to be a 2027 event. Never write "ships", "launches this quarter", "costs", or a price per gigabyte.
3. **Attribute the spec split correctly.** Capacity, watts, form factor and the "no HBM" line are Intel's own page. 1.3 PFLOPS, 1.5 TB/s, the 160/480 split, the 870 ops-per-byte ratio, the 2.9–4.9 tokens-per-pass figure and the "no pricing" line are NAND Research's reading of Intel's Hot Chips 2026 material — write them as the analyst's figures, not as Intel's marketing copy.
4. **No HBM price figure.** Do not use "121%", any ASP forecast, or any per-stack price. The 2027 blended-ASP forecast was anchored by this vertical's 2026-10-07 article; the in-window price evidence is TrendForce's category direction only (10–15% QoQ conventional DRAM, 15–20% NAND), and it is supporting, not load-bearing.
5. **Do not say LPDDR5X is cheaper than HBM.** Nothing retrieved states a price for either. The defensible claim is structural: the card's economics "depend heavily on DRAM pricing" (NAND's words), every DRAM category TrendForce tracks is rising, and the supplier lists LPDDR5 for AI servers as high-value product. Write "no published price", never "cheaper".
6. **Do not write "sold out through 2029".** That phrasing comes from secondary summaries of the call. The release says LTAs cover "more than 50% of total production capacity for 2027 and 2028".
7. **The 80% is an operating margin, on the quarter, under K-IFRS, and the release calls the figures preliminary.** Do not present it as a full-year margin, and do not compare it to another company's margin (no NVIDIA comparison — nothing in the primaries supports one).
8. **gpu-unlock is a consumer-card result.** GA102/GA103/GA104/GA106 GeForce parts, the repository owner's own benchmark on an RTX 3090 Ti at 450 W, with user-reported gains ranging +25% to +81% depending on the card and its power cap. Use it for the *phase split* (prefill moves, decode does not), never as a data-centre performance claim.
9. **Authorised derived arithmetic (the desk's own, to be shown with inputs):** 480 GB at 1.5 TB/s against 96 GB at 1.6–1.8 TB/s (RTX PRO 6000) is about five times the capacity at similar bandwidth; 1.5 TB/s against MI350P's 3.6–4 TB/s is about 2.4 to 2.7 times less bandwidth; 480 GB ÷ 1.5 TB/s versus 96 GB ÷ 1.8 TB/s. Any other derived figure must be shown with both inputs.
10. **Do not describe the D270 as Intel's return "after five years"** unless attributed — NAND Research says it is "Intel's most credible entry into data center AI acceleration in five years" and that is an analyst's judgement, quotable as such.
11. **Do not assert a causal chain the sources do not state** — e.g. "HBM is cannibalising DRAM wafers, which is why LPDDR is scarce". TrendForce says suppliers shift capacity toward server products; SK hynix says it is ramping HBM4 while expanding 1c DDR5/LPDDR5. Report both, draw the inference as the writer's reading, labelled as such.
