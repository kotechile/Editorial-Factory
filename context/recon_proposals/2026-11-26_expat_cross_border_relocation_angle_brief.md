**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Türkiye's new investor residence permit — Presidential Decision No. 11994, signed 2026-11-17, published in Official Gazette No. 33434 2026-11-18, in force the same day; US$150,000 in qualifying Turkish financial assets buys a 3-year permit (18-month minimum hold), US$200,000 buys 5 years (30-month hold); four instruments, none of them property; Article 4 bars the citizenship step; no work rights (IMI Daily, 2026-11-18 — https://www.imidaily.com/turkey-investor-residence-permit).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** The 183-day rule is not a universal safe harbor — the day-count number people plan around is not the test in any of the four countries examined; US 183 *weighted* days over three years plus ≥31 current-year days, UK SRT decisive only at 183 with sufficient-ties below, Ireland 183/tax-year or 280 across two tax years with a ≤30-day year ignored; "182 days is not a globally recognised 'safe' number" (Flamingo Compliance, published 2026-11-05, tracing to HMRC RDR3 and Irish Revenue guidance — https://flamingo.tax/blog/183-day-rule-for-tax-residency-367).
**Emergent Collision Point:** Within nine days the window priced both halves of the relocation decision and only one of them is for sale. Türkiye now sells legal *presence* as a liquid financial product — deposit instruments, fund units, pension contributions, releasable after 18 months, deliberately decoupled from both property and citizenship. But the *tax* half — where the annual bills land — cannot be purchased at any price: it is decided by weighted multi-year day counts, sufficient-ties tests and per-country domestic law that the permit never touches. A buyer who treats the permit as a tax plan is mis-buying the product; the permit settles Article 31 presence, and no line item settles residence. Neither source states this: the launch copy sells the permit, the compliance piece debunks a day count, and only their collision reveals that the market now offers a liquid market in the thing it does not price — the tax position.
**Hook:** On 18 November Türkiye published a price for living in the country: $150,000 in Turkish deposits, bonds, funds or pension contributions, held 18 months, buys a three-year residence permit — no property, no job, and explicitly no step toward a passport. What the six-article decision does not price, and cannot, is where the holder becomes a tax resident — because in the UK, the US and Ireland, even 182 perfectly counted days settles nothing.
**Tension:** The residency market is commoditizing presence — entry thresholds are falling into ETF-shaped products — while the determination that actually bills you (tax residency) stays anchored in multi-year day-count arithmetic no invoice can settle. It empowers the genuinely mobile who read both documents; it threatens the Plan-B buyer who assumes a permit purchase settles the tax question, and the advisory layer selling permits without the residency analysis.
**Target reader:** cross_border_expat — the reader pricing a Plan-B residency and assuming the permit is the hard part.
**Single claim to defend:** The true price of relocation has split in two: presence is now a priced, liquid product ($150,000 / 18-month hold in Türkiye as of 18 November 2026) while tax residency remains an unpriceable, multi-year, per-country determination — so the permit fee is the *cheapest* number in the decision, and treating it as a tax plan is the expensive mistake.
