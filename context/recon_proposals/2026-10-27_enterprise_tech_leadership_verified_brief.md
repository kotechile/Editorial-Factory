## Gate rulings for the drafter (binding)

1. **The "price, not volume" reading is the desk's inference, not Gartner's sentence.** Gartner says memory prices more than doubled and that affordability pressure *is expected to* suppress end-user demand. It publishes no unit or capacity figure. Write the inference as the desk's read ("my read", "the way I read the table") and never attribute the volume claim to Gartner.
2. **"Memflation" is Gartner's coinage** — attribute it, gloss it in plain words on first use ("memory prices inflating the spending number"), and never present it as a term the reader should already know.
3. **No sum of growth rates, no annualising, no "real spend" figure.** The only arithmetic authorised: $7,352bn − $6,391bn = $961bn of added spend between the two years, and $1,366bn − $879bn = $487bn added by data center systems. State both as arithmetic on the table's own values if used at all.
4. **44% and 75% are shares of 203 surveyed CEOs**, not of enterprises. No projection beyond the sample, no "most companies", no "nearly half of all CEOs".
5. **No demand-decline figure is authorised.** The PC-shipment release (2026-10-12) is excluded from this run as a desk retread, so the article carries no measured units number in either direction. Gartner's demand statement stays in its future tense.
6. **Do not present the two releases as one study.** They are different research programmes (a 203-CEO survey and a vendor-sales-based market forecast); the fusion is the desk's, and the piece should say so once.
7. **The corroboration rows are corroboration.** TrendForce prices and the I&O token-cost prediction may each appear once, attributed, with no argument resting on them — the memory-contract figures have already anchored two other verticals this month.
8. **$7.35 trillion has a basis the reader must be told:** worldwide end-user spending on IT, forecast, in current dollars — not AI spending, not cloud spending, and not a measurement of anything that has happened yet.
