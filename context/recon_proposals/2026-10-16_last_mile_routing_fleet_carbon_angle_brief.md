**Signal A (Anchor 1):** NYC DOT's Off-Hour Deliveries (OHD) incentive program, announced 2026-09-24 — $5 million of MTA Central Business District Tolling Program (congestion pricing) money to move commercial deliveries into the 7 p.m.–6 a.m. window; up to 14 businesses receive more than half a million dollars in the first phase; 27 businesses already run off-hour deliveries across over 1,120 locations; the Delivering Green plan targets 5,000 OHD locations and an estimated 62,000 trucks off peak by 2040; roughly 90% of goods move through New York City by truck; OHD was made permanent in 2010 after a 2009–2010 pilot with Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2026/nyc-dot-to-provide-incentives-to-reduce-truck-deliveries-during-busiest-hours.shtml). The program's own record of the gain is that carriers shifting a route to the off-hours cut operational costs 35–45% and save $30,000–$50,000 per year on every regular-hour route replaced (https://www.informs.org/Impact/O.R.-Analytics-Success-Stories/The-New-York-City-Off-Hours-Deliveries-Project-A-Business-and-Community-Friendly-Sustainability-Program).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** EIA on-highway diesel, week of 10/05/26 — U.S. average $6.199/gal, down from $6.529 (09/21) but $3.711 above the $2.488 of a year ago; West Coast (PADD5) $7.229; California $8.082, the highest state reading in the table (https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/gasdiesel/). Corroborated 2026-10-15: California's statewide average $8.09/gal on Monday with September weeks peaking near $8.25, and California Trucking Association president Tim Pollock saying small carriers cannot float fuel before surcharges land and that surcharges cover only about 70% to 75% of the cost (https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/california-diesel-prices-remain-above-8-per-gallon-as-truckers-call-for-re/833092/); UNFI is attacking the same bill with hedging and Samsara route optimization across close to 50 distribution centers and 2,000 delivery vehicles, against $6.18/gal and +$2.51 year over year on Oct. 12 data (https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/how-unfi-tackles-higher-fuel-prices/832977/).
**Emergent Collision Point:** The city has found a lever it can afford and a fleet cannot: the hour. Every dollar of the OHD program buys a *schedule* change, and the saving it buys is real and documented — 35–45% off carrier operating cost, $30,000–$50,000 a year per converted route — because the work is time and miles (fewer hours in traffic, more stops per shift). The cost line that is closing small carriers is priced in a different unit: the gallon. New York can shift 62,000 trucks to the night by 2040; it cannot re-price the diesel those trucks burn, and in California that gallon has been above $8 while the national average sits $3.711 above a year ago. Neither side of the collision states it: the release prices a clock, the pump prices a gallon, and the two ledgers never meet on the same page.
**Hook:** New York will pay businesses, out of the toll charged to the trucks themselves, to move their deliveries to the night — while the diesel those trucks burn at 11 p.m. costs $8.09 a gallon in California and $3.711 a gallon more than it did a year ago nationally.
**Tension:** Carriers reading the OHD incentive as a cost fix are being handed the right answer to a shrinking question. The program's saving is time; the squeeze is fuel, and the surcharge recovers only 70–75% of it for the smallest operators. Meanwhile the money is self-referential — a congestion charge on entering the Manhattan zone funds payments to keep trucks out of the daytime traffic that generated the charge — and the plan's unit is trucks-per-day over fifteen years, against a price that moved $3.711 in twelve months and that an energy engineer expects to stay elevated well into 2027. Whoever reads the incentive as the remedy is optimizing the smaller number.
