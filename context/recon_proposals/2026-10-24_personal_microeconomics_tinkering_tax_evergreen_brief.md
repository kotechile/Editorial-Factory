**Arithmetic the article must carry (recomputed here, not in the draft).** 87.93 W × 8,760 h = 770.3 kWh a year; at 17.30¢ that is $133.26. That electricity becomes 770.3 kWh of room heat (3,412 BTU per kWh, and Synology's own 300.23 BTU/hr confirms the 1:1 conversion). A split-system heat pump at the 7.5 HSPF2 minimum delivers 7.5 BTU per watt-hour, i.e. a coefficient of 2.198, so the same 770.3 kWh of heat costs 350.4 kWh = **$60.62 — a 45.5% credit** on the box's bill. A single-package unit at 6.7 HSPF2 (coefficient 1.964) costs 392.3 kWh = $67.86, a 50.9% credit; a resistive coil (coefficient 1.0) is a 100% credit, which is the only case where the folklore is exactly true. Cooling inverts it: an air conditioner at the 14.3 SEER2 minimum removes 14.3 BTU per watt-hour, so pumping the same 770.3 kWh of heat back out takes 183.8 kWh = **$31.80, a 23.9% penalty** — the box is never "free" and only a resistive-heated home gets a dollar-for-dollar rebate. Per 10 watts of continuous draw: 87.6 kWh and $15.15 a year.
