## Derived arithmetic the drafter MAY use (labelled as arithmetic, never as a source's figure)

- Loaded rate: $65.38/hr (row 1) × 1.3 ≈ **$85/hr** — state the multiple in the sentence; it is the reader's own assumption, not a source's.
- Payback threshold: hours a year = capex ÷ loaded rate; minutes a day = that ÷ 240 working days × 60.
  - $3,900 step (row 2: $8,000 − $4,100) at $85/hr = **11.5 minutes a day** every working day for a year.
  - $4,100 part = 12.1 min/day; $8,000 part = 23.5 min/day.
- The measured case: a 90-second saving on a once-a-day full rebuild = 1.5 min × 240 days = **6 hours a year** = **about $510** at $85/hr → **7.6 years** to return $3,900.
- The vendor's case, checked: 30 minutes a day × 240 days = 120 hours = **$10,200 a year** at $85/hr (or **$7,845** at the unloaded median $65.38) → an $8,000 CPU returns in **about 9-10 months** on the loaded rate and **about a year** on the unloaded one. So the vendor's "fairly quickly" holds only at its own 30-minutes-a-day figure, which is the whole caveat.
- Running cost: 1 kW of sustained load = 1 × 24 × 30.4 = **729.6 kWh a month** → **$99.4/month** at the commercial-class 13.41¢ (or $97.6 at the 13.63¢ average) ≈ **$1,190 a year** — more than twice the $510 the 90-second saving is worth.
- Do NOT invent: a payback period the tables above do not yield, a "minutes lost" figure, a machine total price (the brief carries CPU list prices only, not a configured system), or a warranty/failure number.
