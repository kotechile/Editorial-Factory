## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Signal A's headline is a null, not a cure.** The paper's central result is "no benefit under a fixed simulated user". Do not write that the team *always* loses, and do not write that the study proves the 45% rule wrong — the authors explicitly say the decline question is unresolved ("slope -0.41, 95% CI [-1.51, +0.68]"), so cap the claim at the 13 valid cells and the weighted mean. Carry A's two stated limits: one validity bar unmet (the planner never saw the tool schemas), and the single gain appears only under a second simulator.
2. **Signal A is about user-facing work only.** The comparison is a conversation with a user under a written policy on the dual-control benchmark's airline, retail and telecom domains. It is not evidence about autonomous coding or research tasks; Signal B supplies those, and B's own scope statement must be carried ("one benchmark of full-stack web apps … may not carry over to other kinds of work").
3. **Signal B's one win is real and must not be hidden.** Sol's medium-effort team gained 7.3 points at p = 0.005, and the page explains where it came from (B6). A piece that only lists the losses is dishonest and would be quotable against the desk. The honest structure is: one significant win, three nulls, and a cost line that ran 1.8 to 5.1 times.
4. **Never merge B's two cost comparisons.** Sol's max-effort *single* agent cost $4.82 against $3.07 for its *medium-effort team*: that is an effort comparison, not a team comparison. The team comparisons are 2.5× (Sol medium), 1.8× (Sol max), 2.2× (Opus medium) and 5.1× (Opus max). The 1.8-to-5.1 range is the team range only.
5. **Signal C compares two shipped settings, not parallelism.** The `high` and `max` recipes differ in more than agent count, the max arm forced Sonnet 5.5 on every subagent under a $30 cap, and one configuration of 20 PRs was run once. Say "the fan-out setting", never "parallelism alone". Also carry that the author built a competing reviewer, and that 188× is the page's own subtotal ratio ($398.03 / $2.12), not a per-review figure ($20.95 / $0.11 is about 190× on finished reviews — use the page's recorded subtotal and its own 188× statement).
6. **No derived arithmetic beyond the sources' own.** Authorised: the 188× ratio, the "thirty times the cost" for Opus's max-effort team (the page's own wording), 2.3× runtime, 5.5× tokens, 1.8-5.1× team cost, the 224 / 17 / 55 million cached-token comparison, 12 of 13 cells, -28 points with its interval. Forbidden: any per-agent average, any "dollars per point" figure, any percentage of budget.
7. **D is a next-signal, not an anchor.** The self-report finding (D1, D2) belongs in the forward-looking section as the mechanism that would make a team's claims checkable. Do not imply the papers in A, B or C measured self-report contamination; B6 reports agents claiming tests passed, which is B's own finding and must be cited to B.
8. **Task-type honesty.** A is a simulated-conversation benchmark; B is a first-party benchmark run by an evaluation vendor on its own bench; C is a vendor measuring a competitor's shipped command on 20 PRs. All three are primary measurements of their own setting, and the article must say so in one clause each rather than presenting a consensus.
