**Decision the reader is facing:** Whether to elect the foreign earned income exclusion or take the foreign tax credit for a year of foreign earnings — and whether that choice can still be changed on a return already filed — because the two reliefs have different ceilings, different forfeiture rules and different clocks.
**Durability:** The load-bearing figures are statutory, not annual: the $80,000 base exclusion and its cost-of-living adjustment (26 U.S.C. § 911(b)(2)(D), unchanged since 2005), the one-year-back / ten-years-forward carryover of unused foreign tax (26 U.S.C. § 904(c)), and the ten-year window in which the credit-versus-deduction choice may still be made or changed on an amended return. Exactly one figure set is re-indexed every year — the maximum exclusion and the housing limit derived from it (30 percent of the exclusion) — so those are stated as of the 2025 return, and the piece says so: $130,000 maximum exclusion and $39,000 housing limit for 2025 as of the 2025 Instructions for Form 2555. Every figure below is as of 2026-10-22.

**Thesis:** The exclusion is a trade rather than a relief: it caps how much income escapes, forfeits the foreign tax credit on everything that escapes, and then locks the choice for years — while the credit has no dollar ceiling, carries unused amounts forward ten years and can still be claimed on a return filed a decade ago.

## Notes on the evidence
- **Hosts and independence.** Two hosts (`govinfo.gov`, `irs.gov`) and five distinct documents; the two govinfo rows are different sections of the Code (§ 904 limit and carryover, § 911 exclusion), and the three irs.gov rows are three different publications — the Form 2555 instructions, the Form 1116 instructions and Publication 514. No vendor appears at all.
- **Window checks done by eye, not just by the gate.** Row 4's leading token (`10`) first appears in § 904(a)'s "10-percent owned foreign corporation", so the gate's evidence excerpt points at the wrong sentence although the quoted phrase — "in any of the first 10 succeeding taxable years" — is verbatim in § 904(c); verified by reading the fetched body. Rows 1, 2, 3 and 5 return the operative sentence themselves.
- **The de minimis row is the sharpest gotcha** and it is stated in the Form 1116 instructions in one place: the $300/$600 election avoids the form and, in the same paragraph, bars carrying that year's foreign tax to or from any other year (carryovers to and from other years are unaffected, and the article must say so).
- **Pub. 54 states the coexistence rule the piece must not get wrong** (fetched live, cited in prose and in `## Sources`): "Once you've made a foreign earned income exclusion election or foreign housing exclusion election, you can't take a foreign tax credit or deduction for foreign taxes on income you choose to exclude", and then, in the same note, "If you're a high wage earner, it is possible to take the foreign earned income exclusion up to the limitation for the year. Then, you can take a foreign tax credit for any foreign taxes paid on the portion of the wage that wasn't excluded". So the exclusion does not kill the credit outright: it kills the credit on the excluded slice, which is why the decision turns on how much of the foreign wage the cap covers, not on a blanket verdict. The same page carries the revocation clock — "If you revoked a choice and within 5 years again wish to choose the same exclusion, you must apply for IRS approval" — which is cited in prose rather than carried as a gate row because the page opens with a stack of disaster-extension notices whose dates collide with the gate's numeric matcher.
- **The 2025 figures are labelled as 2025 throughout.** The maximum exclusion and the housing limit re-index annually; the article may state $130,000 and $39,000 only with the year attached, and must not present them as the current year's numbers.
- **What the piece must NOT do:** claim the exclusion is always wrong (in a low-tax or no-income-tax host country the credit is worth little and the exclusion wins — the decision rule turns on the comparison, not on a verdict); re-argue the residency clocks of `2026-10-08_split-year-two-clock-seam`; re-argue the disclosure penalties of `2026-10-15_three-forms-cost-more-than-tax`; or state a tax saving the regime does not produce.
