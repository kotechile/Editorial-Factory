## Notes for the fact-check pass
- A hedge is not a retry. The A6 spec is explicit that hedging sends copies *before* a failure is observed ("hedging can be seen as retrying the original RPC before a failure is even received"), and that it is "only enabled for methods that are safe to execute multiple times without adverse affect". Do not write "retry" where the piece means a second copy in flight.
- The deadline does not stretch. A6 states that the call deadline applies across all attempts and across the whole hedging chain: after the deadline the operation fails "regardless of in-flight RPCs". A hedge buys a better draw inside the budget, not a longer budget.
- The throttle arithmetic is derived, not quoted: threshold = maxTokens/2 = 5, each failure −1 token, each success +tokenRatio (0.1), so one failure costs ten successes and the retry layer goes quiet at a failure ratio of about 10% — the failure rate at which a brownout is most likely. Recompute it from 10 and 0.1, and say it is our arithmetic.
- The SRE numbers are worked examples from a published chapter, not measurements of a named production system. Say "worked example", keep the chapter attribution.
- nginx's next_upstream is about an upstream that *fails* (error/timeout), not one that is *slow*; `proxy_next_upstream_tries 0` removes the try cap. Do not claim nginx hedges — it does not; it is the row that shows the duplication cap the reader's own top layer probably left open, and the row that shows the idempotency guard the proxy applies and the agent wrapper usually does not.
- The turn-tail arithmetic is ours: 1 − (1 − p)^k. At p = 1% and k = 12 that is 11.4% (one turn in nine); at k = 20, 18.2%. Expected slowest step of k draws with mean 800ms is H_k × 0.8s = 2.48s at k = 12 (H_12 = 3.103). Label it derived, never quoted.
- Keep hedging illegal for writes in the body even where a vendor's config makes it technically possible: the constraint is the tool's semantics, not the client library's flag.
