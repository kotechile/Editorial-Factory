**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Gartner, "44% of CEOs Struggle to Demonstrate AI Financial Returns as Technology Becomes Central to Growth" (2026-10-20, Orlando; 2027 Gartner CEO Survey of 203 global CEOs and senior business executives fielded April–May 2026) — "Forty-four percent of CEOs struggle to demonstrate AI-driven financial returns to stakeholders", while "75% plan to increase technology investment" (https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-20-gartner-says-44-percent-of-ceos-struggle-to-demonstrate-ai-financial-returns-as-technology-becomes-central-to-growth).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Gartner, "Worldwide IT Spending to Grow 15% in 2027, Totaling $7.35 Trillion" (2026-10-21, Orlando) — 2027 spending of $7.35 trillion against $6.391 trillion in 2026; Data Center Systems from $879 billion (+73.8%) to $1,366 billion (+55.5%); "demand from data center systems has driven memory prices to more than double" in twelve months, a "memflation" effect that persists through 2027 and whose "pressure on affordability is expected to suppress end-user demand throughout the forecast horizon" (https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-21-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-it-spending-to-grow-15-percent-in-2027-totaling-7-point-35-trillion).
**Emergent Collision Point:** The forecast's own fastest-growing line is priced, not built. Data center systems add $487 billion of 2027 spending while the same release says memory prices more than doubled and that the resulting affordability pressure is expected to suppress demand — so the $1 trillion of new IT spend is substantially the same estate at a higher unit price. Signal A is what makes that fatal rather than interesting: three-quarters of the CEOs funding that number are raising technology investment in the same week that 44% of them admit they cannot demonstrate a financial return on it. Neither release states the joint consequence — that a rising budget and a rising return stop being the same signal, and that the only defensible position left is a price per unit of work, held at renewal.
**Hook:** The world is forecast to spend $7.35 trillion on technology in 2027, 15% more than this year, and the fastest-growing line in that budget is memory that got more expensive rather than capacity anyone added.
**Tension:** Memory suppliers are repricing contracts quarter by quarter (TrendForce has conventional DRAM contract prices up another 10–15% in the fourth quarter and server DRAM undersupplied), while the buyers are being told by their own analysts to raise spend to redesign the operating model. The vendors capture the price; the buyer carries the return risk; and the CFO's question lands on the same renewal the engineer signed. Who this helps: teams that can price a workload per unit and put a ceiling on the variable line. Who this hurts: anyone budgeting in headlines — "we grew IT spend 15%" now describes inflation, not capability.
**Target reader:** eng_leader
**Single claim to defend:** In 2027 a bigger IT budget is not evidence of more capacity or more AI value — the growth this forecast describes is an input-price effect — so the defensible move is to price what you already run per unit of work and hold the contract to that number, because the return question and the renewal arrive together.
