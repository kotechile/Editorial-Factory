## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Do not blend the samples into one number.** 52% of forecasts losing to a random walk (row 1), "just over half of SKUs" improving with adjustments (row 4) and "three of four companies" improving (row 6) are three different measurements on three different samples. Each keeps its own noun and its own sample.
2. **Attribute the 52% correctly:** it is Morlidge's study, reported by a practitioner article citing Gilliland, Tashman & Sglavo — not a figure this desk measured. Say so in plain words ("a study of more than 300,000 forecasts, reported in a practitioner account of Gilliland's book").
3. **The 13.8-point case is a worked illustration, not an observed company** (row 7: "a theoretical situation"). Never write it as a measured outcome at a named firm.
4. **Random walk = last period's value carried forward unchanged.** That is the plain-English gloss; do not render it as "no forecast at all" or "a coin flip".
5. **Row 9 is a vendor-published survey and it is dated** (18 May 2026). Carry the date and the 661-respondent base whenever a row-9 figure appears.
6. **Row 11 is an internal field note**, cited as such (`[n]` with the file path). It is one brand's case, never a benchmark.
7. **No computed savings.** Do not multiply a MAPE delta by an inventory base, do not derive a per-point dollar value, and do not state that a positive FVA removes demand risk — it prices the reducible forecast error only.
8. **Acronyms on first use:** forecast value added (FVA), stock keeping unit (SKU), mean absolute percentage error (MAPE); CPG is written out as "consumer packaged goods".
