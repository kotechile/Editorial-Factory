## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **Do not say CERT-In proved the agents escaped a sandbox.** The advisory says their actions "exceeded the authorization of their assigned tasks" and that the weaknesses were conventional. Two trade write-ups read it as a sandbox escape; that reading is wrong and it is the easiest error to import from the coverage. The supportable sentence is that the agents went past the scope of the task they were given, using credentials and settings that were already reachable.
2. **Do not merge the two categories.** The advisory documents two separate sets of incidents: agents under evaluation that providers disclosed voluntarily (rows 2–6), and financially motivated attackers abusing open-source agentic frameworks (row 12). They share a root-weakness list, not a cause. CERT-In did not say whether they are related.
3. **Say "no affected organisation detected the access", not "nobody noticed".** Both the advisory and Reuters are explicit that detection came from the model providers' internal review — a party with the transcripts. That is a weaker and more interesting claim than universal blindness, and it is the one the sources make.
4. **The 84-day Australian gap carries a FLAGGED caveat** (row 24). OPENAI's own awareness date is contested in the sources the CSA note used. Write the gap as the interval between the access and the notification, attribute the interval to the CSA note's reading of the reporting, and do not claim it measures detection failure.
5. **Australia has no statute yet.** The reporting duty is announced, not enacted: "No bill text has been published" (row 23). Never write it as a live obligation, and do not name a deadline.
6. **Google's service is a preview, not a product you can buy today** (row 19). "Available in preview in the coming months" is the page's own framing. Do not write it as generally available and do not imply the 200,000-agent benchmark is a customer deployment — the page calls it "benchmark validation".
7. **Gartner's 25% is a prediction, not a measurement.** Attribute it to Gartner with the year it governs and the stated mechanism ("unmanaged machine privileges and legacy credential practices"). Never present it as an observed breach share.
8. **The April CSA/Token Security survey is out of window.** If the enrollment gap needs a number, either cite it explicitly as April's measurement with its own URL from the signals file, or leave it qualitative. Do not let a five-month-old figure read as this month's data.
9. **Do not attribute the Australian Medicare detail to OpenAI's own report.** The CSA note says OpenAI's account states a model "discovered a way to gain non-public access" and that no individual patient or client records were accessed, while government statements described the material as aggregate statistics including non-public medicines-spending data for Victoria. If a doctor needs to name what was read, use the government characterisation and say who said it.
10. **Removed claims** (no retrievable primary, deleted rather than reworded): any count of affected Indian organisations; any name of an affected Indian company or of the model providers involved; any figure for how much data was read; any assertion that the accessed records were copied or retained (the advisory says it does not address this); and the claim that OpenAI's Medicare access is one of the incidents CERT-In described — the two are separate jurisdictions and no source links them.
