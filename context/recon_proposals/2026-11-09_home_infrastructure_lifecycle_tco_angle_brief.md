In the same month the materials an install consumes went up **3% to 15%** across thirteen manufacturers, effective 2026-11-01 to 2026-11-23 (https://www.achrnews.com/articles/166947-hvac-price-increase-list-november-2026).
**Emergent Collision Point:** The fuel a household burns hit a record while every escape from it — the duct, the gas piping, the controls, the water heater swapped in the same visit — got more expensive on the same calendar, and the federal supply response is smaller than one day of the country's heating-oil consumption. Neither the price series nor the solicitation says this: a 75% fuel shock normally argues for switching, and this month's numbers argue for staying, because the cost of the switch rose by the same mechanism (materials and freight) that drove the fuel up. The break-even a homeowner is quoted this month is the only number in the decision that no agency publishes.
**Hook:** At $6.19 a gallon, EIA's own 402-gallon season costs $2,488 — $373 more than the government's October forecast, and $1,071 more than the same tank cost at last October's price — while the 315,000 barrels Washington is lending the Northeast are four-tenths of one percent of the 78 million barrels U.S. homes burn in a year.
**Tension:** The households with the least room to switch are the ones the price hits hardest (about 4.65 million oil-heated homes, half of Maine), and the replacement mix data shows the market is not switching away from oil furnaces at all: oil warm-air furnace shipments rose 27.1% in August, a six-month streak, while gas furnaces fell 4.3%. The fuel is being priced like a crisis and bought like a habit — because the alternative needs an electrical panel, a cold-climate unit and a contractor, and that bill went up this month too.
