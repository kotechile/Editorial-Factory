**Decision the reader is facing:** Whether to size, price and commit a Kubernetes node pool by the VM's advertised memory or by the allocatable the scheduler will actually promise — and how much of every node to hold back for the kubelet's eviction signal before a QoS class starts losing pods. It bites at the next capacity commitment, node-shape change or SLO renewal, not at an incident.
**Durability:** The arithmetic is the durable part: allocatable = capacity − kube-reserved − eviction headroom, the reserve is regressive (it takes a larger share of a small node than of a large one), and the kernel throttles at memory.high = requests + memoryThrottlingFactor × (limits − requests) before it OOM-kills at memory.max. ## The rules the article will carry (every figure derived from the rows above, recomputed here)
- **Compare allocatable, not the spec sheet.** Under the GKE ladder plus its 100 MiB eviction buffer, a 4 GiB node advertises 2.90 GiB (72.6%), an 8 GiB node 6.10 GiB (76.3%), a 32 GiB node 28.34 GiB (88.6%) and a 128 GiB node 118.58 GiB (92.6%). The reserve is regressive, so the small node is the expensive one per usable GiB and "scale out with more small nodes" buys system overhead, not capacity.
- **The provider's formula is part of the price.** On an 8 GiB-class node the GKE ladder reserves 1.80 GiB before the eviction buffer, while the AKS lesser-of formula on the same 8 GB node reserves 650 MB for 30 pods — the same spec sheet, about a third of the loss. A cross-provider capacity comparison made on VM specs is comparing two different products.
- **Requests are the promise; limits are the throttle.** memory.high is computed from both (0.9 factor, 256 MiB request against a 1 GiB limit → roughly 947 MiB), a Burstable pod with no limit inherits node allocatable as its limit, and a Guaranteed pod is not throttled at all. So an unbounded Burstable pod is not "safe because it has a request": it is throttled against the whole node's allocatable.
- **Eviction is a signal, not an OOM.** The kubelet starts evicting at memory.available<100Mi (and nodefs.available<10%, imagefs.available<15%, inodesFree<5%) with a 0s grace period on hard thresholds, and only pods exceeding their requests are candidates — BestEffort first, then Burstable, then Guaranteed.
- **The disk reserve is the one teams forget.** A 10% nodefs threshold on a 100 GiB node means 10 GiB is never usable for images, logs or emptyDir, which is the reserve that arrives as an eviction wave after a noisy log deployment.
- **Set the reservation where the provider lets you.** GKE versions from 1.37.2-gke.1325000 make the system reservation configurable, and the kubelet enforces kube-reserved/system-reserved only when the cgroup and enforceNodeAllocatable are set — so on modern clusters the reserve is a decision the team makes, not a fact to discover.
