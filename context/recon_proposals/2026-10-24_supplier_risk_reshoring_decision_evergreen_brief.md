**Decision the reader is facing:** You are placing tooling with a supplier — moving a part offshore or into a nearshore plant — and building the landed-cost model: is the mold a capital cost that sits outside the duty base, or a dutiable assist that rides on every unit, over what base do you apportion it, and can one company-wide tooling factor cover all your imports.
**Durability:** The rule is statutory and has not moved since the Trade Agreements Act of 1979 amended section 402 of the Tariff Act of 1930: 19 U.S.C. 1401a(b)(1)(C) adds "the value, apportioned as appropriate, of any assist" to the price actually paid or payable, 1401a(h)(1)(A)(ii) puts tools, dies and molds inside that definition, and 19 CFR 152.103(d)-(e) fixes how the value is computed and apportioned. The regulation text fetched for this brief is the eCFR edition "up to date as of 10/22/2026" (title 19 last amended 8/26/2026), the interpretative examples are those published at T.D. 73-175 (38 FR 17477, July 2, 1973) and unchanged, and the enforcement ruling cited (CBP HQ H311213) is dated February 10, 2021. None of it depends on a tariff rate, an election or a trade action, so a reader in 12 months re-reads the same three provisions; what changes is only the duty rate they multiply, which this piece leaves to the reader's own HTS line.
**De-dup:** `2026-10-17_prior-disclosure-clock-cuts-penalty` is this vertical's nearest prior customs-mechanics artifact — the penalty ladder under 19 U.S.C. 1592 and the dated disclosure windows in 19 CFR 162.74 — and today's news run owns `2026-10-24_7-days-notice-rewrites-your-duty` (the enumeration-date clock under 19 U.S.C. 1338(d)). **Thesis:** The tool you hand a supplier is not capex outside the duty base — CBP treats it as an assist worth its acquisition cost plus the freight that puts it in the plant, apportions that value onto the imported units, and offers exactly three sanctioned bases (the first shipment, the units produced by then, or the whole anticipated run), so the loading per unit can differ by a factor of the run length while the duty you actually owe is fixed by which product lines the tool was used for and what rate each line carries — which is why a single company-wide tooling factor was refused in HQ H311213 after the importer spread assists across 233 subheadings and 42 countries of origin.
