## Gate rulings carried to the drafter

1. **The strike is a threat, not an event.** No walkout had been called as of the 4 November extension. Every
   sentence that describes it uses "could", "would" or "threat", and the caveat sits in The catch as well.
2. **The 85% and 33% cargo shares belong to the employer association.** Attribute them to the Pacific Maritime
   Association in the sentence, every time. Do not present them as port statistics.
3. **The 35,688 figure is a snapshot of containers awaiting on-dock rail, not a dwell average.** The piece may
   say that 10,562 had been on the terminal nine days or longer; it may not say the average box had waited.
4. **The $170,000 wage is the employer's stated position in a dispute, offered as context for why the union's
   demand is contested — not as a fact about the workforce.** Prefer to leave it out; if used, attribute it.
5. **No hands-on claim and no prescription.** This desk has no terminal, no drayage fleet and no visibility
   platform; the tactical section reports what operators are doing and what to watch, never what the reader
   should do.
6. **The nine-day coincidence is arithmetic, and it must be shown as arithmetic** — both dates named, both
   counts named, so a reader can check it.
7. **Do not claim a stopped gate emits no events as a sourced fact.** It is the desk's analysis of how
   event-driven towers work and must read as the writer's observation, not as a cited finding; if the sentence
   cannot be written that way, cut it.
