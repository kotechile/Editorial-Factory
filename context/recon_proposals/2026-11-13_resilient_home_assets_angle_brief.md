Neither document says that; together they change what a homeowner should do with the year.
**Hook:** The Dove Fire burned 226 acres outside Sonora, destroyed 12 structures, and its alleged arsonist was arrested the same afternoon — and on 10 November that fire bought about 50,000 policyholders across three counties a year of insurance protection whose clock had been running since 15 October.
**Tension:** A protection counted from an administrative declaration is easier to spend than to notice. The households inside it mostly did not lose anything, the ones outside it by one ZIP code get nothing, and the residual market behind it is raising rates while writing fewer new policies — so "you have a year" is true and thinner than it sounds. It empowers the household that reads its own bulletin date; it threatens the assumption that a moratorium means the market is healing.
