The window closed on a cluster this vertical has not argued before: **the price of intervening**, not the detection of failure. One day apart, three independent groups published measurements of the three decisions a recovering agent harness actually makes. When to abandon a trajectory, and whether the replacement attempt can do better with the budget that is left (2610.36544). Where to cut a checkpoint, and whether the cut saves more re-derivation than it costs to take (2610.09088, in window since 6 October). Whether an error even needs a human, given that roughly half of them resolve themselves a few steps later (2610.36471). Each paper measures its decision against the branch it replaces; each finds the industry's usual proxy — classifier accuracy, preserved work, error count — pointing somewhere other than the value.

The friction is shipping. DeltaBox Lite open-sourced an abort/commit sandbox for coding agents on 16 October and shipped Windows support on 21 October, i.e. the rollback primitive is arriving as a feature in exactly the "just take a checkpoint" shape the placement measurement prices at a negative return one step later.
