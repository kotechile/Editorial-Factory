**Durability:** The figures are as of 27 October 2026 — the Reolink RLC-810A spec sheet, the Frigate 0.18 camera-setup and recording docs, and the Synology DS224+ spec sheet as each page printed them on that date — and the arithmetic outlives all three: kilobytes per second divided by eight, times 86,400 seconds, is the archive a camera writes per day, and a recorder's decode ceiling is published as total frames per second at a stated resolution rather than as a camera count. **Arithmetic note (for the drafter and the fact-check pass — every derived figure must be recomputed, never copied):** 6,144 Kbps ÷ 8 = 768 kB/s; × 86,400 s = 66.36 GB per camera per day (decimal GB); 4 cameras × 30 days = 7.96 TB; the 4,096 Kbps floor of the same code-rate range gives 44.24 GB/day, so the range spans 44.2-88.5 GB/day per camera. The vendor's own capacity table is the cross-check: 512 GB ÷ 194.2 h = 2.636 GB/h = 63.3 GB/day, which implies ~5,859 Kbps — the same order as the 6,144 Kbps default it publishes. The substream default (256 Kbps) is 2.76 GB/day, exactly one twenty-fourth of the main stream. The recorder ceiling: 170 FPS ÷ 25 fps = 6.8 cameras at 4K H.264 on a DS224+, and four cameras at 25 fps sit at 100 of the 170 frames.
