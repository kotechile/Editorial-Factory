**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Revolut's self-hosted inference deployment, reported 2026-11-03 (https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/03/british-fintech-revolut-slashes-ai-coding-costs-10x-by-cutting-out-claude-and-going-self-hosted/5307345). Blended cost about $0.30 per million input/output tokens, "about a tenth of using OpenAI's Codex or Anthropic's Claude Code"; roughly 90% cut in AI coding inference cost in under a year; 56 Nvidia B200 GPUs serving GLM-5.3 to about 2,000 engineers; before the switch, "engineers spend $1,000 a month, $10,000 a month with not so much ROI" so the company "launched it with strict budget limits". The engineering decision and its caveats are recorded first-hand in the conference organiser's own talk page from 2026-10-13 (https://ai.engineer/nyc/2026/talks/revolut): "When tokens feel scarce, engineers save them for a difficult coding task instead of using them across the surrounding work."

**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Apple's per-employee AI allowances, reported 2026-11-04 (https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/04/apple-reducing-employee-ai-spending/). "In October, Apple limited some engineers to $200 per day for AI spending, down from $300"; further cuts "could bring their daily allowances down to as little as $100"; "some teams could have their spending capped at $25"; teams are asked to use "less expensive models or switch to on-device AI"; and "few were using their full $300 daily limit". The Reuters wire copy on 2026-11-05 adds that some non-engineering groups "already operate under a $100 daily cap, which could be reduced to as little as $25" and that employees needing more "would be able to seek exemptions from senior executives" (https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/apple-cuts-employee-ai-budgets-in-cost-control-push-bloomberg-news-reports-6525411).

**Emergent Collision Point:** A daily dollar cap is denominated in the one quantity that is collapsing. Anthropic's price per million tokens fell 77% in under five months, to $0.62 in October 2026 from $2.65 in May (https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/firm-that-lets-you-rent-out-your-idle-mac-for-ai-inference-workloads-warns-of-plummeting-token-costs-darkbloom-network-sees-earnings-drop-to-usd1-23-a-day-as-anthropic-tokens-fall-77-percent). So Apple's one-third cut in the allowance still raises the tokens each engineer can buy: $300 at May's price bought about 113 million tokens, and $200 at October's price buys about 323 million, which is 2.85 times as many. Neither source states this. The Register reports the price collapse as a supplier problem and the Apple reporting reports the cap as cost discipline; together they say the control is not controlling spend at all, it is re-indexing a shrinking budget onto a price that keeps falling. Revolut is the other side of the same arithmetic: it spent a year discovering that a token cap changes engineer behaviour — tokens get hoarded for the hardest task and the surrounding work (debugging, Jira, Confluence) goes undone — and then removed the cap by owning the silicon at a tenth of the price rather than by lowering the ceiling.

**Hook:** Two companies moved in opposite directions inside 48 hours. Revolut took the cap off 2,000 engineers by making a token cost 30 cents a million; Apple lowered the daily allowance for some of its engineers from $300 to $200. The same month, the price of the tokens those allowances buy fell 77%.

**Tension:** A dollar cap is legible to finance and meaningless to engineering. It caps a unit that is repricing quarterly, so it neither holds spend flat nor says what work to stop doing; and because it is per seat, it prices the same task differently in every function (a $25 team cap and a $200 engineering cap buy wildly different amounts of the same model). The firms that keep the cap get a budget that silently loosens; the firms that re-base the supply take on GPUs, tuning and compliance work that a mid-size firm cannot staff — which is why the cap survives in the org chart even where it has stopped working.

**Target reader:** enterprise_cai

**Single claim to defend:** A per-seat dollar cap is a proxy control that leaks as unit price falls, so the only durable AI cost control is the marginal cost of the work you decline to delegate — which means a CFO has to choose between re-indexing the cap every quarter and re-basing the supply.
