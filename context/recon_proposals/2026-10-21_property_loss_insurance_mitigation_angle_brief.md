**Emergent Collision Point:** Both prices are set on 1 January 2027 and neither document acknowledges the other. Louisiana fixes a floor under what a FORTIFIED roof is worth in dollars per hurricane-premium dollar, on the same date the reinsurance market is expected to hand carriers a double-digit cut in the cost of the hurricane risk that discount is drawn from. Two forces therefore hit one line item at once: the state's mandatory credit (a percentage of the hurricane slice) and the reinsurance cycle (a percentage of the carrier's cost of that same risk). A homeowner cannot attribute their renewal to their roof, and a state that has just spent $100M to buy discounts has bought them against a price that is already falling.
**Hook:** Louisiana will require every admitted insurer to take 29% to 49% off the hurricane portion of a premium for a FORTIFIED roof by January 1 — the same date reinsurance brokers expect property catastrophe rates to fall at least 10% because capital is abundant and the 2026 wind season has been quiet.
**Tension:** The discount is real, but it is measured against a base the carrier no longer sets alone. Louisiana has made the roof's value a regulated number while the underlying cost of hurricane risk is dropping for reasons that have nothing to do with any roof. That is good news for a homeowner who was going to re-roof anyway and a trap for one borrowing against a discount that may be partly a cycle. Carriers, meanwhile, must fund a mandatory credit out of a softening reinsurance market — and the credit applies only to the hurricane slice, leaving the rest of the bill to carry whatever the carrier needs.
**Target reader:** pro_homeowner
**Single claim to defend:** A mandatory mitigation discount is a percentage of one peril slice set on a date that also reprices the reinsurance behind that slice — so the dollars a FORTIFIED roof saves in 2027 depend as much on the reinsurance cycle as on the roof, and the certain value in the Louisiana program is the capped grant, not the headline percentage.
