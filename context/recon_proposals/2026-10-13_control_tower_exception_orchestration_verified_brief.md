## Notes for the drafter
- Report the August readings as August readings: Sea-Intelligence's GLP issue 181 covers figures "up to
  and including August 2026" and the PMSA dwell data is for August too. Do not present either as a
  September number.
- Reliability (claim 1) and dwell (claim 5) are different metrics from different publishers; the article
  must not imply one causes the other. The defensible bridge is temporal and directional: both are
  August readings, both moved the wrong way, and they sit at either end of the same container's trip.
- 6.75 days against 6.34 days is a rise of 0.41 days (FreightWaves states it) — if the article names
  the change, use 0.41 days, not "half a day".
- The truck-versus-rail divergence is the load-bearing detail: local truck dwell fell while rail dwell
  rose, which is exactly the seam argument. Keep both figures.
- Do not convert 49.9% into "half the fleet is late" more strongly than the source supports: the source
  says 49.9% schedule reliability, meaning a little over half of vessel arrivals missed schedule. A
  headline built on "half" is defensible only if the body states the 49.9% figure in the same piece.
- The vendor claim that agents now resolve these exceptions automatically (project44's public product
  page) is NOT a verified claim: it is an undated marketing page with no testable figure. The article
  may describe the category generically but must not attribute a number to it.
