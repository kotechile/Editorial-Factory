## Arithmetic to be written as the desk's own (inputs cited above)

1. Gas: $15.34 ÷ 1.037 = **$14.79 per million Btu of gas input**; ÷ 0.5633 UEF = **$26.26 per million Btu of delivered hot water** (rows 1, 3, 7).
2. Electricity: 17.30¢ × 293.1 kWh per million Btu = **$50.70 per million Btu of electricity input** (rows 2, 6); ÷ 0.9207 = **$55.07** at the resistance floor, and ÷ 2.30 = **$22.04** at the 2029 heat-pump floor (rows 4, 5).
3. Crossover: the heat-pump tank at the 2.30 floor overtakes the gas tank when residential electricity is above about **20.6 cents** a kilowatthour at $15.34 gas ($26.26 × 2.30 = $60.39 per million Btu of input ÷ 293.1 kWh); the gas tank overtakes it when gas falls to about **$12.88** a thousand cubic feet at 17.30¢ electricity ($22.04 × 0.5633 × 1.037).
4. Ratios: 26.26 ÷ 22.04 = **1.19**, i.e. the gas tank costs about 19 percent more per delivered million Btu and the heat-pump tank about 16 percent less; resistance versus gas tank = 55.07 ÷ 26.26 = **2.10**, more than double.

## Audit notes for the drafter (carried from the gate fetch, not the gate marker)

1. **Every row above was fetched and probed for its own phrase, not just its status.** Two matcher windows need a human eye-check and are recorded here: row 6's `3,412` tokenises to `3` and `412`, so the verifier's excerpt lands on the page's dry-measure table ("3.29122 bushels") rather than the electricity line — the phrase "1 kilowatthour=3,412 Btu" is on that page and was read by eye; row 1's evidence window prints the whole 2020s row of the annual table, so the year mapping (2020 10.78, 2021 12.18, 2022 14.75, 2023 15.40, 2024 14.50, 2025 15.34) was read off the page's own column order and confirmed against its 2026-09-30 release date.
2. **The floors are floors, not the units for sale.** 0.5633 and 0.9207 are computed from the statute's own coefficient at 50 rated gallons for the *medium* draw pattern; a certified unit on a shelf will be at or above them, and the draw pattern matters (very-small-draw floors are far lower: 0.3456 − 0.0020 × V). Say "the federal floor for a medium-draw 50-gallon tank", never "a typical tank's efficiency".
3. **`V r` is rated storage volume in gallons** (the page's own footnote), so the coefficient must be read with the volume the reader is quoting — a 40-gallon tank gives 0.5803 gas and 0.9227 electric, and the article must not silently imply the 50-gallon numbers apply to every tank.
4. **The two efficiency regimes are a date, not a trend.** 430.32(d)(1) covers units manufactured before May 6, 2029 and (d)(2) covers units manufactured on or after that date; the 2.30 figure is a *manufacturing* floor for new electric storage tanks, so it says nothing about the tank a reader already owns and nothing about installations. Never write "heat pumps are required in 2029" without "for new electric storage water heaters manufactured on or after".
5. **No product price, installed cost, rebate or tax credit may appear.** The federal 25C credit expired 2025-12-31 (recorded in the 2026-10-12 home_equity_tco verified brief) and no tank price is in this table; the article's payback talk must stay in cost-per-million-Btu terms with its two inputs named in the same sentence.
6. **Every arithmetic line must show both inputs.** The four lines above are the only derived figures permitted; each must be written as the desk's own arithmetic with the two cited numbers visible beside it. No annualised savings in dollars, because no household hot-water load is cited.
7. **Not a money verdict for everyone:** at 20.6 cents the two classes cross, so a reader in a high-price state gets the opposite answer, and that is the point of printing the crossover rather than a national ranking.
