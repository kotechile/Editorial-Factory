## Notes for the drafting stage (not gate fields)
- **Two states, not a national rule.** Florida's clocks and its statutory interest are Florida's; Washington's conduct rules are Washington's. Say who each binds and tell the reader the ask is "what is my state's prompt-pay rule" — do not imply the 60 days or the interest is a national standard. Every other state has a version, and the article's job is to teach the shape of the ask.
- **Do not print an interest rate.** § 55.03 is a construction (averaged New York Fed discount rate plus 400 basis points, reset quarterly), and any number written into the piece is wrong within a quarter. Give the reader the formula and where the current quarterly rate is published.
- **The interest is not a lawsuit.** § 627.70131 says failure to comply is a violation of the code but "does not form the sole basis for a private cause of action" — so the honest lever is the interest line on the payment plus a complaint to the state insurance regulator, not a threat to sue. Do not overstate it into litigation leverage.
- **The estimate copy and the license number are the paper the piece is about.** Florida puts a copy of the adjuster's own detailed estimate in the homeowner's hands within 7 days of it being generated, and puts the adjuster's name and license number on the inspection; Washington, from 10/18/2026, requires an insurer using a database, survey or estimating software to disclose — on the claimant's request — the date the data was collected, where it came from and which businesses supplied it (WAC 284-30-380(8)), and says a reasonable investigation "may not rely solely on the use of a database" (WAC 284-30-330(4)). Carry those two in prose under row 6's citation; neither sentence contains a digit the gate can verify, so they cannot carry their own rows.
- **Washington's 30-calendar-day accept-or-deny rule** (WAC 284-30-380(1)) and its 30-day written extension are prose, not rows: the `30` token on that page first appears inside the section number "284-30-380" in the page's own title bar, so a row leading on it would evidence on navigation.
