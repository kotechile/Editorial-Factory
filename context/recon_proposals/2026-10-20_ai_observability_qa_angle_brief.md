**Signal A (Anchor 1):** arXiv:2610.25947, "Claim Laundering: When Agent Decision Models Trust Records Over Reality" (cs.AI, submitted 2026-10-19 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25947). Across Android screen traces, ALFWorld and WebShop the authors build paired states that vary the observation, the logged action and an explicit success claim independently. Their headline result: "In all 12 configurations of four decision models and three domains, records have a larger average effect on commitment decisions than observations; after claimed-but-failed steps, the models still stop, advance, or purchase in 53-100% of cases." The evidence that the failure is visible to the model but not acted on is internal — "linear probes on intermediate hidden states distinguish successful from failed steps on held-out apps, tasks, and goals (AUROC 0.77-1.00)" — and their fix, EVIGATE, is "an intermediate-layer veto that requires no additional forward pass and adds 0.04-2.0% serving latency", lifting task success "by 18.7 points in live ALFWorld agents, 5.0 in WebShop, and 5.1 in a phone simulator". The limit they state: "gates calibrated on offline counterfactual states do not transfer reliably to live agent trajectories."

**Signal B (Anchor 2):** arXiv:2610.26632, "When the harness decides success: state that cannot be re-observed" (cs.AI/cs.CL/cs.LG, submitted 2026-10-19 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26632). The harness — the layer that drops old turns, summarizes them, or replays the model's own reasoning — is treated as a cost knob; the paper asks when it changes whether the agent succeeds. In a pre-registered probe, truncation "costs a low-cost reasoning model nothing in success when a needed fact can be looked up again (it looks the fact up instead), but drops its success to chance when the fact cannot be re-observed: the two penalties differ by 92.5 percentage points (90% CI [89.5, 95.5])", with "a pre-registered replication on one model from a second provider" showing "the same contrast". On ARC-AGI-3, "summarizing old turns instead of dropping them, while keeping the Standard harness's invitation to take notes, recovers 86% of a 63-point gap without replaying the model's reasoning. The same harness without the invitation recovers 43%, and adding the provider's native replay of the model's reasoning recovers the remaining 57%." The paper is explicit that these are "point estimates from one counted run per configuration and game".

**Emergent Collision Point:** Observability is built on one unexamined premise: that a trace *observes* the system. Signal A removes the observer's neutrality from the agent's side — when an action fails silently, the record says success and the model follows the record (53-100% of the time), even though its own hidden states carry the contradiction (AUROC 0.77-1.00). Signal B removes it from the harness's side — the same record is *authored* before the model ever reads it, by a context policy that decides which facts survive, and that choice is worth 92.5 points of success when nothing in the environment can restore what was dropped. Put together: the trace is a jointly written claim about state, and both parties with an interest in it read it as fact. That is a different failure than "the suite is blind." A blind suite reports nothing; a laundered trace reports *success*. It is also a different failure than a flaky run: the state did not change between runs, it was never in the record. So a green trace certifies that the record is internally consistent — that the harness kept what it kept and the tool wrote what it wrote — and not that the environment reached the state the record names. The gate that follows from it is not a better reader of the trace; it is a check on re-observability (can the agent re-derive the fact if it is dropped?) and on the decision point itself (does anything compare the record against the state before the next action commits?).

**Hook:** A step reports success. It did not take effect. In the claim-laundering experiments the agent keeps going anyway — 53% to 100% of the time, across 12 configurations of four models and three domains. In the harness experiments, when the fact behind a step can no longer be looked up again, success falls to chance: 92.5 points of it, one context policy apart.

**Tension:** The eval and observability stack was sold as the answer to agents that fail silently, and both papers say the stack's unit of evidence is the wrong object. The record is written by the tool, edited by the harness and read by the model, and every one of those three has a reason to make it read clean. Vendors are now pricing the read side aggressively — Laminar's flow-1 claims GPT-6-sol-level trace-error detection 23x cheaper on its own 523-trace benchmark — which makes "analyse every trace" affordable exactly when the traces themselves have stopped being neutral. The people who own release quality are being asked to certify a run from an artifact that the run's own participants compose, under a context policy nobody versioned.

**Target reader:** evals_infra_eng

**Single claim to defend:** A passing trace certifies that the record is consistent with itself, not that the state it names was reached — because the harness chooses what survives into the record and the model acts on the record over the environment — so the gate has to test re-observability and compare record against state at the decision point, rather than read the log harder.
