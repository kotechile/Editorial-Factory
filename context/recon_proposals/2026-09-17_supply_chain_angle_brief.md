# Angle Brief: supply_chain — 2026-09-17

**Winner:** Samsung is suing ocean carrier CMA CGM for at least $186 million at the Federal Maritime Commission — the biggest test yet of whether a shipper can claw back pandemic-era detention and demurrage fees it was forced to pay on delays outside its control.

**Scores:** N=8 · A=9 · S=8 → Composite=8.3 (≥8 → proceed)

**Hook:** Samsung Electronics America filed a complaint before the Federal Maritime Commission (FMC) against CMA CGM, served September 1 as Docket No. 26-12, seeking at least $186 million in reparations — $148 million in allegedly improper demurrage, detention and rail-storage charges, $8.1 million in mitigation costs, and at least $30 million in prejudgment interest. Samsung says it ultimately paid more than 121,000 demurrage, detention and associated charges on "store-door" shipments where the carrier was responsible for inland delivery but repeatedly failed to complete the moves.

**Tension:** The Ocean Shipping Reform Act of 2022 (OSRA-22) and the FMC's 2024 detention-and-demurrage billing rule gave shippers a real legal standard to dispute charges — an invoice must be issued within 30 days, name the right party, and trace to a delay the shipper actually caused. For four years most shippers paid these fees as a cost of doing business. Samsung's nine-figure complaint is the proof that the FMC complaint process has become a cost-recovery weapon, not just a grievance channel. Who it hurts: carriers and forwarders whose billing practices don't survive the FMC's invoice test. Who it helps: beneficial cargo owners (BCOs) who keep clean records and dispute systematically — the same money is sitting in the invoices of every mid-market importer.

**Single claim to defend:** A shipper is now suing a carrier for $186 million in detention and demurrage charges it says were unlawful — 121,000 of them — which proves OSRA-22 turned the FMC complaint process into a real cost-recovery lever, so an ops leader's edge is to audit and automate dispute of their own detention/demurrage invoices rather than write the fees off.
