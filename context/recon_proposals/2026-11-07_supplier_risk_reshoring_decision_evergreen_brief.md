## Gate rulings and hand checks (for the audit trail)

- **The differential is arithmetic and the brief shows the arithmetic, not a forecast.** (1 + 0.0625) / (1 + 0.0388) − 1 = 2.2815 percent a year. Recomputed in the final from the two cited cells, never copied: at 42 days of exposure it is 26 basis points of the exposed amount (2.2815 × 42 ÷ 365), at 4 days it is 2.5 basis points, and on a $10,000,000 annual peso spend locked for twelve months it is $228,150.
- **Prime is deliberately not a row.** The 2026-10-31 brief used bank prime (7.00 percent) because it priced *borrowed* money carrying an unpaid duty bill. A forward is priced off deposit/policy rates, not the prime lending rate, so using prime here would misstate the mechanism; prime is not cited in this piece at all.
- **The exchange-rate conversion rule cannot carry a "5 percent" row, and is carried in prose.** `figure_evidence("5 percent …")` on 19 CFR 159.34 lands in § (a)(1), the proclaimed-rate exception, not § (a)(2), the certified-daily-rate exception the article turns on — the nav-digit class. The rule (quarterly rate governs every export date in the quarter; the certified daily rate takes over only when it differs by 5 percent or more, published in the Customs Bulletin) rides in prose under citation [4], and row 4 leads instead on `1981`, which lands squarely on the section's own amendment note.
- **19 CFR 159.35 is quoted in prose only.** It is the section that says the daily rate is the Federal Reserve Bank of New York's buying rate certified to the Treasury, and that a New York bank holiday rolls the rate back to the last business day — no numeric token, no row.
- **The 42-day and 4-day cycle lengths come from `customer-truth.md` Anecdote 1 and must be labelled as our own field anecdote**, never as a measured industry transit figure; the article uses them for exposure-days arithmetic only.
- **What the article must not claim:** that a forward removes the expected cost of a peso move (it fixes the rate at a premium that contains the market's expectation), that a dollar-denominated Mexican contract removes currency exposure (the supplier prices or carries the same differential, and re-prices at renewal), or that the 5 percent band means duty can never move within a quarter (the daily rate replaces the quarterly rate beyond the band, and a proclaimed rate is a separate branch).
- **Arithmetic to recompute in the final, not copy from this brief:** the 2.2815 percent differential, the 26 bp and 2.5 bp per-cycle figures, the $228,150 annual figure, and the "up to about 4.8 percent of the dollar duty" consequence of a 5 percent conversion spread (1 − 1 ÷ 1.05 = 4.76 percent).
