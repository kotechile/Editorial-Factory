# Angle Brief: multi_agent_enterprise_fabric — 2026-10-28

**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)
**Winner:** "Your agent fabric's port is a bet, and the model is not the dial."
**Scores:** E=9.5 A=9 S=9 → Composite=9.2

**Signal A (Anchor 1):** "Matched Capabilities, Different Behaviors: A Controlled Study of MCP and CLI Interfaces for Language Agents" (Zhenning Shi, Liang Pang, Yongqi Li — MICA; submitted 2026-10-25 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34298). The study renders MCP and CLI "from a shared semantic action contract and routes decoded actions through the same runtime", then compares six models across three interactive environments. With capabilities matched, "structured divergence in 94.3% of 2,004 episodes", and the difference is not noise: interface repair (DART) "improves benchmark score in all six evaluated settings by up to 9.0 points and reduces MCP token cost in all three environments by up to 27.1k tokens per task". The paper localizes the sensitivity to "capability presentation, realization, and feedback" and concludes that "interface representation" is "part of the closed-loop decision environment shaping agent behavior".

**Signal B (Anchor 2):** "Tool-Calling LLM Agents Under Realistic MCP Faults: Scale Does Not Purchase Fault Tolerance" (Antonio Ken Iannillo, Joshua Segun Owotogbe, Roberto Natella et al. — MCP-Injector; submitted 2026-10-23 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32555). A protocol-aware fault-injection middleware whose fault model is "calibrated to the observed incident distribution (failure mode x persistence) of the public MCP ecosystem, mined from 57,240 issue and pull-request threads across 1,774 open-source MCP repositories". In a 1,350-cell experiment crossing two backbone sizes (20B, 120B), three agent stacks and three fault conditions, faults "cause severe quality degradation (task-completion score falls by 60%, from 8.08 to 3.23 out of 10)". Scale does not rescue it: the 120B "retains no larger share of its fault-free score under faults (resilience-ratio model, p = 0.96)", and its fault-condition completion rate is "if anything, lower (91.1% vs. 95.6%; difference 4.4 points, 95% CI [-0.2, 9.3])". What does move the result is the stack: "Agent stacks differ in how well they absorb faults, which suggests that framework-level error handling matters more than model scale."

**Emergent Collision Point:** Neither paper varies the other's axis. MICA holds the environment healthy and varies the *representation* — same actions, two model-facing shapes — and finds the agent behaves differently in almost every episode, in both directions, with a repair worth up to 9 points. MCP-Injector holds the representation fixed (MCP) and varies the *backbone and the stack* under a fault profile mined from the ecosystem's own incidents — and finds the model's size buys nothing while the stack's error handling decides how much survives. Put them side by side and the enterprise fabric's outcome is set by two variables that both studies find decisive and neither party benchmarks: **which representation you standardize on, and what wraps it when a call fails.** The model is the one dial every architecture review scores and the only dial both experiments find irrelevant to the difference. That composition also dissolves a live contradiction the market cannot: the public MCP-versus-CLI comparisons disagree with each other — one vendor's 75-run test has CLI at 100% and MCP at 72% on TCP timeouts (https://www.scalekit.com/blog/mcp-vs-cli-use), another controlled 50-task comparison has the direction reverse by environment, CLI ahead 8.5% to 17.7% on data-heavy tasks and MCP ahead 7.3% to 11.8% on lighter ones (https://labs.scale.com/blog/mcp-vs-cli). MICA's mechanism is why: if the effect is conditional on presentation, realization and feedback, then a headline success rate is a property of one task mix, not of the protocol. The fabric vendors are meanwhile shipping exactly the plumbing that makes the choice invisible — a control plane for "AI agents, Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers, and APIs", with local A2A chat testing and per-LLM OAuth client credentials arriving as release notes (https://docs.mulesoft.com/release-notes/agent-fabric/agent-fabric-release-notes) — and the ERP vendors are wiring agents over the same interop protocol with the system of record's own authorizations inherited (https://news.sap.com/2026/10/sap-connect-sap-puts-autonomous-enterprise-to-work).

**Hook:** A task that scores 8.08 out of 10 falls to 3.23 when the tools fail the way the MCP ecosystem's own 57,240 incident threads say they fail — and a model six times larger holds no more of its score than the small one. In the same fortnight, a controlled study found that giving six models the same capabilities in two different shapes changed what they did in 94.3% of 2,004 paired episodes.

**Tension:** An enterprise standardizes its agent fabric once and inherits the consequences for every agent built on it. The standardization argument is governance: MCP is stateless since the 2026-07-28 revision, hosted by the Linux Foundation, shipped by every major vendor, and it is the only way to put a policy, an identity and a cost meter in front of a tool call. All true — and none of it is a measurement of what the port does to the agent. MICA says the representation is inside the decision loop, so the same tool presented two ways changes action selection, failure pathways and recovery. MCP-Injector says the failure profile the ecosystem actually has is permanent-heavy, that it costs 60% of task quality, and that buying a bigger model is not a mitigation. Who gets hurt: the teams that treat the fabric as a procurement line and the model as the engineering decision, because the two variables they are not testing are the two that moved the result. Who gets helped: the teams that can run their own task mix through both representations and inject their own provider's incident shape, because that test is cheap once the fabric exists.

**Target reader:** `ai_architect` (persona wants: MCP contracts and anti-corruption layers, RPC boundaries, state recovery patterns, tool schema discipline; owns the fabric standard and the model shortlist).

**Single claim to defend:** In an enterprise agent fabric, the interface representation and the stack that wraps it determine both normal-path behaviour and failure-path survival, while model scale determines neither — so a fabric chosen on protocol governance and model benchmarks has measured everything except the two variables that decide the result.
