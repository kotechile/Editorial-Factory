# Angle Brief: supply_chain — 2026-09-10

**Winner:** The reshoring boom is real but hollow — OEM reshoring activity hit a record (36%, up from 29%), yet satisfaction with the results collapsed from 96% to 65%, and the hard data shows manufacturing investment tripled over four years while capacity grew just 1.5%.

**Scores:** N=8 · A=9 · S=8 → Composite=8.3 (≥8 → proceed)

**Hook:** Two fresh primary datasets land the same punch. Kearney's 2026 Reshoring Index: US manufacturing investment roughly tripled over the past four years, but manufacturing capacity grew only ~1.5% — and imports hit a four-year high anyway. The Reshoring Initiative's 2026 USA Reshoring Survey (249 manufacturers): 36% of OEMs reshored or are actively engaged, up from 29% — yet only 65% are satisfied with the results, down from 96% a year ago, with dissatisfaction jumping from 4% to 25%.

**Tension:** The reshoring narrative sold a simple story — tariffs + geopolitical risk push production home, investment follows, capacity and jobs follow. The data says the first two steps happened and the third stalled. The binding constraint is no longer capital or policy: 35% of manufacturers name labor availability and overhead as the top challenge, and 57% say the real burden is policy *uncertainty* (the moving target), not the tariff rate itself. Who it hurts: ops leaders who underwrite reshoring on FOB/landed-cost math and discover vendor gaps, labor costs, and inflation after the capex is committed. Who it helps: leaders who re-cost on Total Cost of Ownership — a method only 40% of OEMs use (up from 30%) — and who treat the skilled-trades pipeline as the real capacity lever.

**Target reader:** ops_leader (VP of Supply Chain & Operations Leader — numerate, balance-sheet aware, ROI-first, grounded in physical physics).

**Single claim to defend:** Reshoring is at record engagement but producing thin new capacity and falling satisfaction — investment tripled yet capacity grew ~1.5%, and OEM satisfaction fell from 96% to 65% — which proves the constraint has moved from capital/tariff policy to skilled labor and physical execution, so an ops leader's edge is honest TCO re-costing and a locked-in labor pipeline, not chasing the next tariff headline.
