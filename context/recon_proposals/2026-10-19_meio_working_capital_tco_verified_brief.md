## Gate rulings for the drafter
- **Do not call 16.4% → 6.6% a measurement of the pooling law.** It is one optimiser's improvement over three *different* benchmarks on three network shapes. The honest sentence is that the gain thinned as the network stopped looking like a textbook chain, and the paper's own abstract frames it as linear / divergent / general cases.
- **Do not call 6.6% "the MEIO saving".** The benchmark it beat was the company's own base-stock policy that enforced 98% fill at every connection (a decentralized policy). Saving-versus-that-policy is the claim.
- **The correlation table is the desk's arithmetic, not a source figure.** State the identity — for N identical sites with pairwise correlation ρ, pooled σ = σ·sqrt(N(1+(N−1)ρ)) — and label the outputs as arithmetic: N=4 gives 50% reduction at ρ=0, 31.1% at 0.3, 16.3% at 0.6, 0% at 1.0. No source states these four numbers.
- **41.4% is also desk arithmetic** from the NIST multipliers (2.326 ÷ 1.645 − 1). Say so, and cite [2] for the two z values, not for the 41.4%.
- **The 15–30% band is folklore, not measurement.** Both rows [4] and [5] are vendor/secondary claims; the article must say that plainly. Never attribute the band to the peer-reviewed paper.
- **The $14.2M / six-node field note is an anonymized illustration** (`context/growth_os/customer-truth.md`, Anecdote 1). Use it at most once, attributed as the desk's own field note, and derive no new ratio from it (no "26% cost cut means X per node").
- **inventorypath.com is undated commentary**, not a measured study; cite it for the *law and its assumptions*, never for a result.
- **No figure may imply a dollar payback.** The article carries percentages, multipliers and one arithmetic table only; a payback estimate on the reader's own network would be an invented number.
- **Terminology:** "multi-echelon" means stock held at several tiers of one network (plant → central DC → regional DC → store); "fill rate" is the share of order lines a node can serve from stock. Both need a plain gloss at first use.
