## Gate rulings for the drafter (binding)

1. **Attribute every Hackett figure to the survey**, with its frame: 1,000 largest nonfinancial North American public companies, FY2025 results published 5 October 2026. Never write "companies hold $1.94 trillion" without the frame — it is the gap to top-quartile performance, not cash sitting in accounts.
2. **No annualised price arithmetic.** Do not compound 10–15% a quarter into a yearly rate, and do not compute a break-even between the price rise and a carrying-cost rate. State the quarterly projection and the survey's day figures side by side and let the reader do the comparison. The 20–30% carrying-cost rate is *not* in this brief and must not appear.
3. **TrendForce's percentages are 4Q26 projections**, not settled transactions. Write "is projected to" or "TrendForce expects", never "prices rose".
4. **The days are dollar days.** The survey reads public financial statements, so DIO/DSO/DPO are measured in dollars. That method may be stated; it may not be used to claim that physical units rose or fell.
5. **The Census ratio is not price-adjusted** — the release says so. It can support "the wholesale tier's dollar ratio is lower than a year ago"; it cannot support "wholesalers hold fewer units".
6. **Do not generalise memory to every industry.** Memory is the measured case; the article may say the same arithmetic applies to any input re-priced faster than the cost of carrying it, but must not claim all companies buy memory or that memory is the only input inflating.
7. **Do not present the three buckets as a sum the release makes.** Use them as components, not as 773 + 616 + 548 = 1.94 arithmetic in the body.
8. **Desk field note (owner `customer-truth.md` Anecdote 2)** — an electronics manufacturer holding $2.5 million of regional spare parts, about $575,000 a year of carrying cost against $85,000 a year of on-demand chartered air freight — may be carried once, labelled as the desk's own field note in `## Sources`. It is never a gate row and never a figure the article derives from.
9. **The counter-argument is mandatory:** a quarterly re-pricing cuts both ways, and for a SKU with short life or falling prices the same DIO cut is correct. Say so in the catch rather than only in passing.
