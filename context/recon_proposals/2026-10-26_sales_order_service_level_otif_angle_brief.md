**Emergent Collision Point:** The loss data relocates a third of retail's loss into the supply chain and names goods-receiving loss as one of its components — the physical event that also produces an in-full failure. The market data shows where the answering money is going: $2.1 billion of new order-management spend by 2029, almost all of it into SaaS/cloud, while the owned software and maintenance lines that hold the order record are cut by more than half. Neither document states the consequence, and that is the article: the promise layer is being funded and the receipt layer is being de-funded, so the OTIF percentage — the one number that touches both — keeps being argued about instead of being settled. A shortage is decided by a receiving record; a promise is decided by an availability calculation. Only one of them is in the growth forecast.
**Hook:** A retailer's 33% supply-chain loss block and its 95% in-full goal are the same missing case, counted twice — once by loss prevention in dollars, once by the order desk as a percentage that triggers a 3% chargeback.
**Tension:** The loss-prevention budget averages about 1.2% of sales and the only cohort that reported net declines across loss vectors was the one spending above 2% — so the fix is a budget fight, not a software purchase, and it lands on the operating leader who owns the dock rather than the CIO who owns the OMS. Retailers that buy an order-management platform to improve a receiving number are buying the wrong instrument, and the vendors' own trend list ("AI-driven operational intelligence") will not say so.
