# Angle Brief: smart_home_telemetry — 2026-10-27
**Angle Type:** Synthesis (Cross-Topic Fusion)
**Winner:** Your 4,292-Project Store Has No Auditors — the community catalog moved inside the platform in the same fortnight the consumer-security evidence showed a sixth of the makers behind that code have nobody to report a vulnerability to.
**Scores:** E=9.0 A=9.0 S=9.0 → Composite=9.0
**Signal A (Anchor 1):** Home Assistant absorbed HACS — the community store — into core. The integration PR was opened 2026-09-28 and merged 2026-10-01 (https://github.com/home-assistant/core/pull/183502), the HACS maintainer announced the move on 2026-10-16 (https://github.com/hacs/integration/issues/5590), and the installer was changed on 2026-10-17 to refuse the version that ships it (https://github.com/hacs/get/pull/67). The catalog it reads is `hacs/default`: 4,292 integration entries and 957 dashboard plugins, counted from the file on 2026-10-27 (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hacs/default/master/integration). The platform's own 2026.11 documentation states that nothing in it "is tested, audited, or supported by the Home Assistant project", that Marketplace content "can access your home, your data, and the system that runs Home Assistant", and that the store is administrator-only (https://next.home-assistant.io/integrations/marketplace/). The migration doc states that AppDaemon apps and Python scripts "are no longer managed" and that Home Assistant "blocks the custom integration" of standalone HACS (https://hacs.xyz/docs/marketplace).
**Signal B (Anchor 2):** Consumer Reports' third annual survey of smart-home disclosure practice, published 2026-10-15: 80 manufacturers, 84% with an easy-to-find security contact, 11 with no dedicated contact, 27 of 80 with no formal vulnerability disclosure program, only a third definitively tracking vulnerabilities across their products, 21% publishing a `security.txt`, and answers from 25 of 80 companies against 52 the year before (https://innovation.consumerreports.org/some-smart-home-companies-still-lag-on-security-basics).
**Emergent Collision Point:** Signal A turns 4,292 third-party projects into one-click installs inside the platform and — by the platform's own security policy — removes them from the class of vulnerabilities the project will even accept (https://www.home-assistant.io/security/ — attacks "that require the user to install a malicious other software, like a third-party integration"). Signal B measures the layer that is supposed to catch that code before it ships: the disclosure channel and the programme behind it. Neither document says the thing the pair says — that the marketplace centralises *distribution* while leaving *accountability* distributed across thousands of small maintainers, a sixth of whom the consumer tester could not even find a security contact for. The owner gains a faster install and inherits the review.
**Hook:** This November the largest local-first smart-home platform starts shipping its community store inside itself, and the only gate between an owner and 4,292 third-party projects is a warning dialog that says the project did not test any of them.
**Tension:** Local-first was sold as ownership: your box, your data, your automations. The Marketplace keeps every one of those promises and quietly adds a new job — deciding which of 4,292 projects is safe to run with access to the house. The platform says outright that it does not audit them and that a malicious integration is not a vulnerability it will accept; the consumer evidence says the disclosure layer behind small vendors is missing or unstaffed at a sixth to a third of the field. So the install got easier exactly as the review got harder.
**Target reader:** pro_homeowner
**Single claim to defend:** Moving the community catalog inside the platform made third-party code one click closer to the home without moving the audit with it, so the owner of a local-first home is now the reviewer of a 4,292-project supply chain and should treat a project's disclosure record — not its star count — as the install criterion.
