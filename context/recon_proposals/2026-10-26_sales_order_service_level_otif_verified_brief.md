## Gate rulings for the drafter

1. **"Total Retail Loss" is not inventory distortion and not a service level.** Ruling on row 9: the study excludes spoilage and overstock by scope. Never compare a *Beyond Shrink* figure with an out-of-stock rate, a fill rate, an inventory-distortion total, or the desk's earlier 63.6% OTIF figure. No sentence may imply the study measured OTIF, fill rate or stockouts — it measured *share of loss dollars by vector*.
2. **33% is a share of loss dollars, never a rate of failure.** Write "a third of their loss dollars", never "a third of orders failed". The three components named are supplier fraud, warehouse/sales-area shrink and goods-receiving loss, in that order of the source's own phrasing; the article may single out goods-receiving loss as the one an order desk owns, and must not imply the study separates its individual size.
3. **$103.40 is the average cost of an administrative process error, not the cost of an order exception.** Ruling on row 4: the study gives no order-exception figure. Attribute it to "administrative process errors" every time it appears; it may be used once, as the price of the paperwork class that follows a bad order, and never multiplied by an order count.
4. **The prior shrink index figure ($99.56 billion, 1.82% of sales) is store-level shrink from a different study and stays out.** Not in the signals file; do not introduce it.
5. **$4.3 billion is a software spend forecast for 2029, not the size of a market for fixing loss.** Never place it against a loss figure as a ratio, a share or a "the fix is cheaper than the problem" claim. The one permitted comparison is directional and self-evident from the two documents: SaaS orders nearly triple while on-premise software and maintenance are cut by more than half — and that comparison must be stated as the study's own forecast for its own category, with 2029 named.
6. **No derived dollar figures.** The brief contains no per-case penalty, no chargeback total, no cost-per-dock figure. If a number does not appear in rows 1–18, it is not in the article. Sums, spreads and intervals are forbidden by the figure audit; if the arithmetic is genuinely useful, name both inputs in the sentence.
7. **Sample size.** Print no sample count, or attribute 1,421 retailers in 12 countries to IHL's product page. Do not print 915 and do not average them (row 10).
8. **Attribution of the chargeback mechanics.** Rows 16–18 are a deduction-management vendor's reading of retailer programmes, and that publisher says its own cost range is not an independent study. The Walmart and Kroger thresholds may be stated as that publisher's statement of the programmes; no claim may rest on them alone, and the article must not assert a retailer's current terms in its own voice.
9. **Vendor naming.** The OMS study names eighteen vendors in its evaluation. Do not single any of them out — the piece is about a layer of the stack, not a vendor comparison.
