## Drafting rulings (each one exists because a gate cannot see the error)

1. **The $91 is two charges, not one.** $55 piracy risk + $36 Suez Canal. Write both amounts at least once. Do not call it an "MSC surcharge" in a way that implies a single fee.
2. **$91 per TEU is not $91 per container.** A 40-foot box is two TEU, so a 40-foot container carries $182 of it. State the conversion only as arithmetic ("$182 on a 40-foot box") and never as something MSC published per FEU.
3. **The advisory is lane-scoped, and that is the point.** MSC applies the charges "to all cargo moving from Asia to the following additional destinations". Nothing in the text conditions them on the vessel transiting Suez. Do not write "the surcharge pays for the canal transit" or "MSC charges more for Suez routing" — the charge is on the trade lane.
4. **"Until further notice" is the load-bearing phrase.** Quote it. The surcharge has no end date; the base rate has an annual negotiation. That asymmetry is the article's spine.
5. **The $2.18m is The Loadstar's computation, the $1m and $43 are Drewry's.** Attribute both. The comparison between them (the surcharge bill versus the routing saving) is the desk's arithmetic: state it as arithmetic and show the two inputs, exactly as "2.18 ÷ 1.0" would be shown. Do not write "Drewry says the surcharge exceeds the saving" — Drewry does not.
6. **The Drewry rows are FLAGGED for a reason.** Attribute to "Drewry, as reported by The Loadstar". Never present the $43 per slot or the $1.35m war-risk estimate as this desk's own reading of a Drewry document.
7. **$43 per slot is a rate per slot, not per container.** Do not convert it to FEU or turn it into a per-box figure. If a comparison is needed, put the two per-unit numbers side by side ($43 per slot against $91 per TEU) and say what each unit is.
8. **The 5% trap.** Do not compute the surcharge as a percentage of the Freightos Med rate unless you say the two numbers come from different documents and that Freightos publishes a lane rate while MSC publishes a surcharge: $182 ÷ $3,927 is 4.6% as desk arithmetic and the piece may state that with the inputs shown. Never state it as a rate MSC or Freightos reports.
9. **Direction of rates.** Freightos says Asia–N. Europe and Mediterranean spot rates rose last week (+6% and +3%) while Asia–US West Coast was level. Do not write "rates are falling" anywhere, and do not write "rates are falling on Asia–Europe" — the source says the opposite for the latest week even though it notes months of declines. If the piece needs the longer trend, say only what the source says: the rises came "after months of declines".
10. **Overcapacity is a 2027 risk for carriers, stated conditionally.** The source says "if geopolitics allow its completion". Keep the conditional; do not assert the return as a certainty.
11. **No war-risk figure for a specific carrier.** The 0.3% / $1.35m is a hypothetical Drewry constructs, not a billed number. Label it hypothetical.
12. **The 41 destinations.** The advisory lists Derince and Port Said West plus a named list of North Africa, West Mediterranean and Adriatic ports. "41 destinations" is the count of that list (2 + 39); if the count is used, say "more than 40" rather than a precise tally, or omit it. Do not invent port names.
13. **No payback, no per-voyage saving for the shipper.** Nothing in either anchor tells you what a specific shipper's surcharge bill is; the $2.18m is a full-ship theoretical maximum from The Loadstar, computed with "could, theoretically". Keep the hedge.
14. **The freightos figure is a weekly snapshot dated 2026-10-20.** Say "in the week to 20 October" when quoting it; it is not a contract rate and not a forecast.
15. **Persona framing (`ops_leader`)**: the decision is what to put in a 2027 ocean bid this quarter — the base rate, the surcharge, or both as separate lines with separate expiration dates. Report what carriers and shippers are doing; do not instruct the reader.
