**Signal A (Anchor 1):** On September 22, 2026 the two frontier leaders cut token prices on the same day. OpenAI reduced GPT-6 Sol ($4→$2 input, $20→$10 output) and GPT-6 Luna ($0.20→$0.10, $1.20→$0.50) — a 50% cut — https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-sol-and-luna/ — Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5.5 at $4/$20 (20% below Opus 5) with cache reads down 60%, "performs at the level of Claude Fable 5.1 on most work and costs 40% less to run than Opus 5" — https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5/

**Signal B (Anchor 2):** One day later, the Tokenomics Foundation (a Linux Foundation project) released the State of Tokenomics, September 2026, surveying 472 organizations across 11 industries ($4.6T combined revenue). It found "three in four enterprises cannot confidently prove AI business outcomes to the CFO" (39% not confident) — and, sharply, that "only 4% asked for cheaper prices" while "23% asked for more transparency and granular data." Seven percent wrote the FOCUS billing standard into free-text answers unprompted. — https://www.finops.org/insights/state-of-tokenomics-september-2026/

**Emergent Collision Point:** Neither source can state the combined finding. OpenAI and Anthropic race the price card toward zero, but the buyers' binding constraint is not price — it is attribution. The price card has stopped predicting the actual bill, because the bill is now driven by agent loops, cache-miss penalties, retries, tool calls, and unaccounted human review. Cheaper tokens make the per-token metric an even worse proxy for value, which is why the unit of account must move from cost-per-token to cost-per-resolved-work-unit (CRW).

**Hook:** OpenAI and Anthropic both cut frontier token prices on September 22. One day later, 472 enterprises told the Tokenomics Foundation that what they actually want is not a discount — only 4% asked for one — but a bill they can explain to a CFO.

**Tension:** The systemic friction is a unit-of-account failure. Two forces pull in opposite directions: supply-side price deflation (50% cuts, 100× price spreads inside a single model family) versus demand-side cost blindness (3-in-4 can't prove AI outcomes to the CFO). Who it empowers: the enterprises that already route models and attribute spend to business units — router users are 4× more likely to show CFO value; those with defined AI-economics ownership are 3.7× more likely. Who it threatens: every team still budgeting against a token rate card.

**Target reader:** enterprise_cai (CIO / CISO / Enterprise AI Governance Leader — balance-sheet literate, unit-economics focused).

**Single claim to defend:** The September 22 price cuts make cost-per-token a *worse* predictor of enterprise AI cost, not a better one — the CFO-facing gap is attribution and value (CRW), and the buyers are saying so out loud (4% want discounts, 23% want explainable bills).
